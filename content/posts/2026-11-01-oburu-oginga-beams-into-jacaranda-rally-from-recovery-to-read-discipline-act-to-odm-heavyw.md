Oburu Oginga took center stage without physically stepping onto the Jacaranda Grounds podium, beaming in via video link to close out the month-long *Mwezi wa Baba* celebrations while using his virtual appearance to issue a sharp reprimand to top party leaders assembled in Nairobi.

The Orange Democratic Movement (ODM) party leader, who has been recovering from a recent illness, turned his virtual address on Saturday, October 31, 2026, into both a public thank-you for medical well-wishes and a firm reminder of who holds the organizational reins.

Looking out over a crowd gathered for the emotional climax honoring his late brother, former Prime Minister Raila Odinga, Oburu acknowledged the outpouring of support during his hospital stay before pivoting directly to the political friction playing out across news broadcasts and rally platforms.

### Stern Warning to Assembled Stars

Seated on stage at Jacaranda were some of the party's biggest political figures, including Treasury Cabinet Secretary John Mbadi, Nairobi Governor Johnson Sakaja, Homa Bay Governor Gladys Wanga, Kisii Governor Simba Arati, and National Assembly Minority Leader Junet Mohamed.

Addressing the leaders and supporters directly, Oburu made it clear that personal opinions must not clash with official headquarters policy as the party navigates its ongoing relationship with President William Ruto's administration.

"If you want to express your own view, don’t express it against the policy of the party," Oburu said. "If you are a member of the party, please be disciplined and follow the policies of your party as we move towards the 2027 General Election."

The public lecture targeted recent public spats among party brass, where individual leaders have voiced contrasting stances on whether backing President Ruto's reelection bid should carry explicit conditions.

### Deliberate Alignment, Not Blind Faith

Oburu used the memory of Raila's political legacy to push back against claims that the party had stumbled blindly into the executive branch. He insisted that ODM's presence in the broad-based government was calculated and fully intentional.

"We are in this broad-based government, and we are not there blindly," he declared. "Raila did not go there blindly, and we are not ourselves there in this broad-based government blindly. We are there consciously, and we are there knowing what we are doing."

He drew a sharp distinction between the party's current working arrangement in government and a formal political coalition, explaining that the two operate under entirely different operational and statutory frameworks.

To navigate the current arrangement, the party has relied on a five-member technical team overseeing policy and constitutional matters. However, Oburu outlined a clear roadmap leading up to the next election cycle, confirming that a joint coalition negotiation team—appointed by both ODM and President Ruto's camp—will be formally established before February 2027.

### The Final Curtain on 'Mwezi wa Baba'

The Jacaranda rally brought an end to *Mwezi wa Baba*, a month-long series of memorial events held across the country to celebrate Raila Odinga's lifelong political contributions and legacy.

Closing his virtual appearance, Oburu described ODM as one of the most resilient political movements on the continent, expressing full confidence in its political trajectory as the February 2027 negotiation deadline approaches.
