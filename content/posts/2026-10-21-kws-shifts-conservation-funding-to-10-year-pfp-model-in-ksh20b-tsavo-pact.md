A new long-term financing framework launched at Tsavo West National Park aims to decouple Kenya's wildlife and ecosystem management from short-term donor cycles by anchoring conservation in a 10-year matching-grant structure.

The initiative, dubbed the Thamani Asili Programme, rests on a Project Finance for Permanence (PFP) model designed to secure predictable investment and institutional accountability across 31 counties. Under the financing agreement, the Kenyan government has committed Ksh10 billion (USD 80 million), matched by an equivalent Ksh10 billion mobilized through The Nature Conservancy and alliance partner Enduring Earth.

![Tourism Cabinet Secretary Rebecca Miano during the launch of the Thamani Asili Programme at Tsavo West National Park on Wednesday, October 21, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/YF3VwmY3/0c338c7e271b.webp)

Unlike traditional annual funding allocations, the PFP mechanism seeks to lock in permanent capital to expand protected land and streamline management. According to the Kenya Wildlife Service, the project establishes three core operational benchmarks over the next decade: creating over 2.7 million hectares of new conserved areas, upgrading management across 11 million hectares of existing reserves, and facilitating community-led conservation across an additional 6 million hectares.

These land-use adjustments underpin broader targets to bring at least 30 per cent of Kenya’s terrestrial, inland water, and near-shore ecosystems under formal conservation and management. 

![Head of KWS Erustus Kanga during the launch of the Thamani Asili Programme at Tsavo West National Park on Wednesday, October 21, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/RGksrqkG/584d56c85c30.webp)

The framework also projects broader economic integration through nature-based enterprises. Program documentation estimates support for approximately 7,800 direct jobs alongside 15,000 indirect positions spanning ecotourism, sustainable grazing initiatives, and carbon trading. Approximately 1.6 million people are slated to benefit directly from the investments, with another 3.9 million expected to gain from improved natural resource governance.

Implementation details, including disbursement timelines for the matched capital across the 31 participating counties, remain subject to the administrative roll-out managed by KWS and its conservation partners.
