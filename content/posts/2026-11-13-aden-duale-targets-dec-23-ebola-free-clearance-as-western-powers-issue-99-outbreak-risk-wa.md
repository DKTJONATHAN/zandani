Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale has marked out a strict 42-day calendar for Kenya to clear its Ebola monitoring cycle by December 23, 2026, even as a joint diplomatic offensive from six Western nations highlights a 99 percent risk of the deadly virus spilling across the border from South Sudan.

Duale’s public timeline follows Kenya’s containment of an October outbreak that began with a single imported index case before expanding to eight secondary infections. While two patients succumbed to the hemorrhagic virus, the remaining six recovered and were formally discharged from quarantine, triggering the mandatory countdown required under World Health Organization (WHO) protocols before the country can claim zero active transmission.

Addressing the clearance mechanics, Duale emphasized that the 42-day window represents twice the maximum 21-day incubation period of the virus. If no new cases are detected across surveillance points, Kenya will officially achieve Ebola-free status two days before Christmas.

### Western Allies Press Juba Over Border Exposure

However, Kenya’s health authorities face immediate external friction along the northern transit corridor. On Thursday, November 12, a joint diplomatic statement issued by the embassies of the United States, the United Kingdom, Germany, Japan, the Netherlands, and Norway underscored grave concern over South Sudan’s vulnerability to viral importation.

According to WHO estimates cited in the joint appeal, South Sudan faces a 99 percent probability of virus importation driven by active transmission along its 813-kilometer border with the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), where confirmed cases passed 10,900 as of November 9.

The foreign embassies directed sharp criticism at South Sudan’s leadership, demanding that public revenues be channeled directly into health sector funding, public health worker salaries, and unhindered access for emergency relief organizations.

### Cross-Border Trade and Screening Realities

For Kenya, the regional exposure carries immediate commercial and logistics implications. Between 800 and 1,500 travelers and traders move through shared Kenya-South Sudan border crossings daily, maintaining a continuous transit link that relies heavily on point-of-entry screening and clinical vigilance.

South Sudan health authorities have been actively investigating local alerts, including a fatal hemorrhagic fever case in Morobo County that cleared two Ebola tests before being confirmed as yellow fever on November 10, alongside a second pending alert in Yei County.

While South Sudan remains under international travel restrictions enforced by several nations—including the U.S., Canada, Saudi Arabia, and the United Arab Emirates—WHO officials have instructed Kenyan health units to maintain elevated facility-level screening, bolster laboratory diagnostic capacity, and keep frontline health teams on high alert through Duale's December 23 milestone.
