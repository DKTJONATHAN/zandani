The political realignment of Kenya’s executive branch has transitioned into active administrative control, with newly appointed Cabinet Secretaries systematically exercising their statutory powers to reshape the leadership of key economic, energy, and financial regulators.

According to official Gazette notices dated September 18, 2026, the sweeping board appointments and reappointments mark the first major wave of parastatal restructuring since the formation of the broad-based government. By filling critical seats on oversight boards, the reconstituted Cabinet is moving beyond high-level policy statements and anchoring its grip on the daily machinery of state-run corporations.

### Economic Regulators Under New Ministerial Guard

Among the most notable shifts are those directed by Cabinet Secretaries who recently entered the executive from the opposition, now utilizing their ministries' statutory appointment privileges to steer key financial and data institutions.

National Treasury Cabinet Secretary John Mbadi has initiated changes at the Kenya National Bureau of Statistics (KNBS) and the Retirement Benefits Authority (RBA). Mbadi reappointed Rose Towett to the KNBS Board and Ibrahim Mohamed Salat to the RBA Board, both for three-year terms. The appointments place trusted eyes over the national statistics agency and the regulator of Kenya’s multi-billion-shilling pension sector.

![Lamu port under construction, a part of the LAPSSET projects](https://i.ibb.co/k2jth5SL/4d01434c707e.webp)

Simultaneously, Energy Cabinet Secretary Opiyo Wandayi intervened in the country's utility watchdog, renewing Diba Daudi Huka’s membership on the Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority (EPRA) Board for another three years. The move ensures continuity within the energy regulator at a time when consumer tariffs and commercial power distribution agreements remain under heavy public and legislative scrutiny.

### Strategic Infrastructure and Financial Oversight

At the executive level, President William Ruto made key appointments targeting long-term infrastructural developments and financial sector stability.

President Ruto appointed Moses Narok as the Non-Executive Chairperson of the LAPSSET Corridor Development Authority Board for a one-year period. Narok takes the helm of the ambitious regional transport and infrastructure corridor during a crucial phase of foreign investor engagement and construction along the northern corridor.

At the same time, the President extended the tenure of Moses Njenga Chege as the Non-Executive Chairperson of the Insurance Regulatory Authority (IRA) for a three-year term, signaling a desire for stability within the domestic insurance underwriting market.

### Sectoral Resets Across Ministries

Other newly appointed Cabinet Secretaries used the September 18 Gazette notices to overhaul or reinforce boards within their respective dockets:

![President William Ruto signing key bills into law at Statehouse, Nairobi, on September 7.](https://i.ibb.co/PZLcsTtv/a402961ae3df.webp)

* **Tourism and Wildlife:** Cabinet Secretary Rebecca Miano executed a series of appointments and reappointments across the Tourism Fund, Kenya Utalii College, and the Kenya Wildlife Service (KWS) boards, resetting leadership in a sector vital for foreign exchange.
* **Water and Irrigation:** Water Cabinet Secretary Eric Murithi Mugaa appointed Lilian Anyango Yonga to the National Water Harvesting and Storage Authority and Silas Tochim to the Kenya Water Institute Governing Council, aligning the agencies with the ministry's focus on water security and sustainable infrastructure.
* **Education:** Cabinet Secretary Julius Migos Ogamba appointed Captain Antony Kiroken as the Non-Executive Chairperson of the Kajiado National Polytechnic Council for a three-year term. Notably, Ogamba’s directive revoked a previous Gazette Notice (number 14423 of 2026) concerning the council's leadership.

### Judicial and Security Alignments

Beyond the ministries, the administrative updates extended to specialized boards. Chief Justice Martha Koome appointed Maurice Murunga Osundwa to the Auctioneers Licensing Board for three years, effective retroactively to September 4, 2026.

In the security and correctional sector, Interior Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen appointed new members to the Boards of Visitors for both Shikusa and Shimo La Tewa Borstal Institutions for three-year terms.

The administrative sweep also cascaded to the local level, with the Gazette notices confirming appointments to four county-level boards overseeing municipal administration, healthcare, water services, and affordable housing in Tana River, Homa Bay, Bungoma, and Nyeri counties.

This systematic distribution of board seats across regulators and regional authorities indicates that the executive's transition phase is complete, shifting focus toward institutional execution under a newly aligned Cabinet.
