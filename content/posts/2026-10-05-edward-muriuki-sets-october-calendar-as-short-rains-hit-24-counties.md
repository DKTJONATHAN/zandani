The calendar is officially running for the October–December short rains, with acting Kenya Meteorological Service head Edward Muriuki locking in the seasonal timeline across the country's diverse zones.

Precipitation has already broken across 24 counties spanning western Kenya, the Rift Valley, and the coastal strip. The active wet corridor covers Kakamega, Vihiga, Bungoma, Busia, Siaya, Kisumu, Homa Bay, Migori, Kisii, Nyamira, Trans Nzoia, Uasin Gishu, Elgeyo Marakwet, West Pokot, Baringo, Nakuru, Kericho, Bomet, Narok, Nandi, Mombasa, Kwale, Kilifi, Lamu, and the Tana Delta.

For agricultural communities in the central highlands and eastern zones, the wait ends between the first and fourth weeks of October. Counties including Marsabit, Mandera, Wajir, Garissa, and Isiolo are slated for the initial window during the first and second weeks. Nairobi, Kiambu, Murang’a, Kirinigaga, Nyeri, Nyandarua, Laikipia, Embu, Meru, Tharaka Nithi, Machakos, Kitui, Makueni, Kajiado, Taita Taveta, and inland Tana River follow in the mid-to-late October bracket. The tail end of the onset cycle hits Turkana and Samburu during the third and fourth weeks of the month.

While the meteorological office projects that the enhanced precipitation will boost soil moisture, aid land preparation, and improve pasture and water availability for livestock, the forecast carries clear operational warnings. KMS highlighted the heightened risk of waterlogging, crop diseases, and transport snarl-ups, urging residents in floodplains and low-lying ground to prepare for potential flooding and landslides.

The seasonal shift is driven by a positive Indian Ocean Dipole coupled with developing El Niño conditions, accompanied by higher-than-average temperatures nationwide. Farmers have been advised to seek localized guidance from county meteorological offices due to expected variations in storm intensity and duration.

The seasonal persistence maps out uneven exits across regions. While central, western, and eastern areas will see the rains stretch into January 2027, northern pastoralist zones such as Mandera, Wajir, and Garissa are projected to experience a quicker wind-down by early December.
