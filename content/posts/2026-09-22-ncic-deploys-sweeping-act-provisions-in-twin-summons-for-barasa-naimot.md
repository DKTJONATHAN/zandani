The National Cohesion and Integration Commission has cast a wide statutory net in its latest enforcement push, summoning two sitting legislators to answer for public remarks that crossed from regional administrative patronage to local resource disputes.

Kimilili MP Didmus Barasa and Turkana South MP John Ariko Naimot have been ordered to present themselves at the commission's Britam Tower offices in Upper Hill, Nairobi, on Tuesday, September 29, 2026. Rather than relying on standard inquiry notices, the watchdog anchored its summonses across a dense block of twelve distinct sections of the National Cohesion and Integration Act—specifically sections 13, 25, 27, 28, 29, 30, 56, 59, 60, 61, 62, and 63.

![Kimilili MP Didmus Barasa at a past event.](https://i.ibb.co/svjSDPrc/3db4823eccd4.webp)

The inquiry into Barasa targets utterances delivered on September 19 in Sirisia Constituency, Bungoma County. According to the investigative file, the vocal lawmaker addressed senior government appointees hailing from the former Western Province—including Cabinet secretaries, principal secretaries, ambassadors, and parastatal chairpersons—urging them to leverage their state offices to directly advance the interests of their home region.

In contrast, the proceedings facing Naimot center on localized conflict dynamics. The Turkana South MP is required to explain remarks made on September 2 at Kaputir Location in Kapurir Ward. Investigators are examining allegations that the lawmaker utilized derogatory language targeting a specific community while debating perennial security challenges, livestock raids, and the distribution of economic benefits tied to the Lokichar Oil Basin.

![John Ariko Turkana South MP](https://i.ibb.co/jk7y71XZ/cda151f13d2b.webp)

While the commission stressed that the summonses do not constitute a formal finding of wrongdoing, the notices carry strict procedural teeth. Both lawmakers are slated to be given an opportunity to respond to the allegations, tender evidence, and make formal representations before any final determination is reached. However, the commission's directive maintains that failure to appear on the scheduled date leaves open the prospect of arrest warrants, criminal prosecution, or contempt proceedings.
