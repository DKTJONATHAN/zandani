When former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua stood before mourners in Naro Moru on Saturday, November 14, 2026, his emotional tribute centered on a deep personal debt: the steady guidance of his elder sister, Eunice Nyangari Gachagua, who stepped in as the family's mother figure seven years ago.

Nyangari, who passed away on November 6 at the age of 77 in Naro Moru, Nyeri County, was remembered by her brother as a pillar of strength whose warmth and wise counsel held the larger Gachagua household together following the death of their mother seven years prior. Gachagua noted that her sudden passing has left a profound void in their lives, marking the end of her quiet oversight of family affairs during years of significant public and personal change.

Beyond her central position within the family, tributes delivered during the funeral service highlighted a life spent balancing private enterprise with grass-roots advocacy in Nyeri. Relatives eulogized Nyangari as a devoted Christian, farmer, tailor, and businesswoman who dedicated a substantial portion of her life to social causes that directly impacted rural households in the Mount Kenya region.

Key among her community efforts was her long-standing work on behalf of Mau Mau survivor families. Nyangari spent decades championing the welfare of aging veterans and their dependents, ensuring their medical and social needs were recognized. Her public work also extended into land rights, where she actively advocated for land justice on behalf of communities displaced during historical land reallocations in the area.

The final send-off in Nyeri County drew a wide array of prominent national figures from across the political spectrum. Among those attending the service were Wiper Patriotic Front leader Kalonzo Musyoka, Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna, former Chief Justice David Maraga, Democratic Action Party-Kenya (DAP-K) leader Eugene Wamalwa, Siaya Governor James Orengo, and former Starehe MP Maina Kamanda. They joined Gachagua, his wife Pastor Dorcas Rigathi, immediate relatives, and hundreds of local residents to pay their final respects.

Saturday's funeral service capped several days of high-profile condolence visits to Gachagua's residence in Karen, Nairobi. On Wednesday, Kalonzo led a delegation of senior Wiper Party leaders to offer sympathies to the family, while former CS and Jubilee Party leader Dr. Fred Matiang’i arrived alongside members of the Jubilee Party National Executive Committee. On Thursday, Senator Sifuna—who leads the Linda Mwananchi Movement—also paid a separate condolence call to the Karen home.

As Nyangari was laid to rest in Naro Moru, family members and local leaders reaffirmed that her twin contributions—as the protective matriarch who anchored the Gachagua household for seven years and as a persistent defender of displaced families—form the enduring legacy of her 77 years.
