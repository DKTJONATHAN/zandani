Public health student representatives and union officials announced a 48-hour strike ultimatum on Monday, intervening to protect an understaffed national disease surveillance cadre whose three-month work stoppage has left Kenya’s preventive health defenses unmonitored.

Speaking at Kenyatta University, delegates representing university student leaders alongside the Kenya Environmental Health and Public Health Practitioners Union (KEHPHUPU) warned that without immediate government action, tertiary students will mount synchronized nationwide demonstrations beginning Wednesday, October 21.

The ultimatum marks an escalation of an industrial dispute that reached its 90th day on October 19. The protracted shutdown has paralyzed environmental health units, municipal inspection desks, and port-of-entry screening posts across the country.

### Surveillance Gaps Amid Outbreak Threats

Union and student representatives underscored that the prolonged withdrawal of labor directly undermines the country's border and community biosecurity, citing active regional concerns surrounding the spread of the deadly Ebola virus.

![Kenya Environmental Health and Public Health Practitioners Union (KEHPHUPU) leaders](https://i.ibb.co/3YBWYtmw/085bf37bad76.webp)

At the center of the dispute is a stark workforce deficit: approximately 4,200 registered public health officers currently serve a national population approaching 50 million. Union officials pointed out that natural attrition, impending retirements, and a sustained freeze on entry-level recruitment have stripped county disease surveillance systems of operational capacity.

Without routine field inspections, contact tracing, and vector monitoring, public health students argue that the country’s defensive wall against infectious outbreaks has been substantially compromised.

### Disputed Contracts and Ksh2,000 Risk Pay

KEHPHUPU’s industrial action stems from unfulfilled commitments to transition officers employed under short-term Universal Health Coverage (UHC) and Global Fund frameworks into permanent and pensionable terms.

Remuneration disparities remain another significant point of friction. Field officers deployed to handle hazardous waste, quarantine supervision, and outbreak hotspots receive monthly risk allowances ranging between Ksh2,000 and Ksh3,000. Union officials argue this allocation is disproportionately low compared to risk stipends awarded to doctors and nurses, despite equal or higher exposure to occupational hazards in field environments.

Union leaders have repeatedly accused both the national Ministry of Health and county governments of refusing to implement signed collective bargaining agreements, withholding comprehensive medical insurance coverage, and starving preventive public health of budgetary allocations in favor of curative care.

![A collage of the nurses' strike that rocked Nairobi's Central Business District on August 25.](https://i.ibb.co/9m7phb11/fd52b751f728.webp)

### Scrutiny on Oversight and Broken Timelines

During Monday’s briefing, student representatives directly petitioned President William Ruto to step in and break the administrative deadlock, while challenging Parliament to utilize its supervisory mandate to examine how public health budgets are appropriated.

"We are calling for Mr. President to intervene," one student representative stated. "For Parliament, do your work of oversight. We want you to oversee this allocation of the amount. Why was public health left behind?"

The standoff persists despite earlier executive commitments. During a town hall session at the Kenya Health Summit on August 19, President Ruto urged health unions to avoid disruptive work stoppages and proposed dedicated dispute-resolution committees to navigate collective bargaining.

At the same summit, the President directed state officials to clear outstanding health worker salary arrears within a week and ordered tighter administrative coordination with county governors to stop chronic payroll delays.

With those statutory timelines expired and the national strike entering its fourth month, the dispute now heads toward a street mobilization deadline expiring at dawn on Wednesday.
