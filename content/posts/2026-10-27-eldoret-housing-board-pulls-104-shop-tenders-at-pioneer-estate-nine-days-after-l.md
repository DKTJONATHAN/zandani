Local commercial investors eyeing retail spaces at Eldoret’s newly commissioned Pioneer Affordable Housing Estate have been forced into a financial holding pattern after the government abruptly pulled the plug on the sale and leasing tender for 104 shop units.

The Affordable Housing Board issued a formal cancellation on Tuesday, October 27, withdrawing Tender No. AHB/DISPOSAL/02/2026-2027 just nine days after President William Ruto officially handed over keys to 2,157 homeowners at the Uasin Gishu development.

![The Pioneer Affordable Housing Estate in Eldoret, Uasin Gishu County, October 18, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/affordable-housing-eldoret-october-2026.jpg.webp?itok=VbDj_fp_)

The canceled tender, originally floated on October 20, was designed to kickstart retail activity within the Ksh5.3 billion housing development. The offer included storefronts distributed across seven residential blocks, with purchase prices pegged between Ksh900,000 for small retail stalls and Ksh1.7 million for anchor commercial spaces. Separate lease terms were also being structured for facilities within the Pioneer Community Centre and Clubhouse.

By pulling the procurement notice, the board has effectively voided the upcoming November 10 submission deadline, which was scheduled to close at 11:00 am followed immediately by public bid opening.

While the board offered no official explanation for the sudden cancellation, the decision creates an immediate commercial gap for the estate's incoming occupants. The 104 shops, along with planned amenities including a kindergarten, playgrounds, green spaces, and a swimming pool, were planned to make the dense multi-family complex self-sustaining.

![President William Ruto pushes a wheelbarrow during the handover of houses at the Pioneer Affordable Housing Estate in Eldoret, Uasin Gishu County, October 18, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/ruto-pushing-wheelbarrow-affordable-housing-eldoret-october-2026.jpg.webp?itok=7Z124a_j)

For local entrepreneurs who had begun preparing financial bids, the unexplained withdrawal shifts attention back to regulatory portals. The board instructed prospective buyers and tenants to monitor its main website, the Public Procurement Information Portal (PPIP), *MyGov*, and national daily newspapers for a promised re-advertisement.

The timing leaves the commercial wing of Eldoret's largest government-backed housing project offline while residential occupation gets underway, pushing back the onboarding of essential estate services until a revised procurement framework is published.
