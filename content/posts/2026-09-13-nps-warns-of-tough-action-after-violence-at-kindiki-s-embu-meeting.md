One person is dead and six vehicles have been reduced to charred shells after violent clashes forced security teams to evacuate Deputy President Kithure Kindiki from a chaotic ruling party rally in Embu County on Saturday.

### What we know
* Rising political friction in Mt. Kenya East has breached basic security protocols, turning what should have been routine party consolidation into a fatal confrontation.
* The police response at Nembure will face immediate public scrutiny over whether lethal force was justified during an escalating gatekeeping dispute.
* Kindiki’s evacuation marks an embarrassing vulnerability for the ruling party’s grassroots outreach, exposing unresolved local factionalism that backfired publicly.
* Vigorous enforcement rhetoric cannot mask the tactical intelligence failure that permitted organized demonstrators to breach cordons and torch vehicles in broad daylight.

The mayhem erupted at Nembure Stadium during a scheduled United Democratic Alliance (UDA) engagement presided over by the Deputy President. Trouble flared when contingents of local youths were barred from entering the venue, triggering an immediate standoff that quickly devolved into open violence.

Confrontations pitted opposing groups against each other and against law enforcement units deployed to secure the gathering. As stone-throwing intensified and fires broke out across the parking grounds, tactical security officers scrambled to extract Kindiki to safety while dispersing the rioters.

By the time order was restored, one demonstrator had been killed, several participants and officers sustained severe injuries, and property running into millions of shillings lay ruined.

The National Police Service (NPS) confirmed the casualty figures in a statement released late Saturday, mounting a defence of its response while vowing a severe crackdown on perpetrators.

"The National Police Service condemns in the strongest possible terms the outrageous and criminal conduct of a group of individuals who attempted to storm a public meeting presided over by the Deputy President, H.E. Prof. Kithure Kindiki, in Nembure, Embu County," the statement read.

According to police headquarters, officers on the perimeter were compelled to deploy force when crowds attempted a hostile takeover of the perimeter.

"Upon the group's unlawful attempt to breach the meeting, officers acted decisively and repulsed the assailants. Several suspects were arrested at the scene, and law and order was swiftly restored," the NPS stated, adding that investigators have been deployed to identify organisers and financiers behind the skirmishes.

"Let this serve as an unequivocal warning: any individual found to have participated in this or any similar act of lawlessness will be pursued relentlessly. Stern action will be taken in accordance with the law. There will be no exceptions."

Kindiki denounced the skirmishes, directing Embu UDA leadership to carry out an immediate audit of the financial losses incurred by attendees whose vehicles were set ablaze and coordinate a response framework for victims.

Yet tough official statements cannot obscure the systemic failure that turned an open civic square into a battleground. For months, political tensions have simmered beneath the surface across regional strongholds; issuing retroactive threats after a citizen has died and motorcades have burned is an indictment of proactive policing, not an exhibition of it.
