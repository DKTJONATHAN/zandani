Radio presenter Alex Mwakideu has called out online factions attempting to turn the upcoming Bayern Munich versus Ajax Amsterdam exhibition match into a partisan political battleground, warning against the urge to brand every recreational arena in Kenya.

The debate emerged following President William Ruto’s announcement of the high-profile international fixture. In the wake of the news, a supporter of Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna publicly suggested that fans attending the match should wear "Linda Mwananchi" merchandise, effectively turning the stadium stands into a platform for political mobilization.

Speaking in a video shared on his Instagram page, Mwakideu pushed back against the proposal, arguing that dragging factional loyalties into sports strips fans of pure entertainment and ruins the spirit of neutral public gatherings.

Mwakideu expressed frustration over the rigid binary that dominates online discourse, where major events and public figures are routinely forced into the "Wantam" versus "Tutam" framing—referencing the ongoing debate around single-term or two-term political tenures.

"Kuna watu siwaelewi, you are either for Sifuna or you are spoiling for Sifuna," Mwakideu said, questioning the social pressure that forces citizens to declare political allegiances at non-political events.

The broadcaster argued that politicians themselves often enter sports venues strictly as enthusiasts rather than campaigners, pointing out that even Senator Sifuna would likely want to experience an international football match without the occasion being transformed into a political statement.

"You know mpaka nikajiuliza, even Sifuna himself cannot do this," Mwakideu stated. "Infact ata Sifuna mwenyewe ni fan wa ball, politics aside."

Mwakideu maintained that football functions best when it unifies people regardless of their political positions. By introducing political dress codes and campaign slogans to an international match, he argued, organizers and fans risk alienating supporters who simply want to watch elite European clubs play in Nairobi.

"Politics aside, it doesn't always have to be 1 term and 2 term," he added, urging followers to let the game remain about sport, entertainment, and shared enjoyment.

The debate over stadium attire underlines a growing fatigue among media personalities over the hyper-politicization of public life in Kenya. As online discussions continue ahead of the Bayern Munich vs Ajax match, Mwakideu's comments set a concrete marker for drawing boundaries between sports culture and political mobilization.
