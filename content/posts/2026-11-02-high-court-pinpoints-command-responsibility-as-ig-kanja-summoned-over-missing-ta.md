The High Court in Nairobi has dismantled the state's routine legal defense of agency disclaimers in enforced disappearance cases, ruling that ultimate responsibility for accounting for missing foreign nationals rests squarely on the head of the National Police Service rather than subordinate investigative departments.

Justice Alexander Muteti ordered Inspector General of Police Douglas Kanja to appear personally before the court on Tuesday, November 3, 2026, to address the missing status of Tanzanian activist Twaha Mwaipaya. Mwaipaya was reported forcefully taken by unknown individuals on Sunday, October 25, 2026, and has remained untraceable for over a week.

In issuing the directive on Monday, November 2, Justice Muteti specifically declined a prayer to separately summon Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) Director Mohamed Amin, establishing that organizational command cannot be fragmented when fundamental constitutional rights are at stake. The court noted that as head of the National Police Service, Kanja bears overall authority and is best positioned to state whether state agents orchestrated Mwaipaya’s abduction and unauthorized repatriation to Tanzania.

![Tanzanian Activist Twaha Mwaiyapa.](https://i.ibb.co/JRLrw6tD/bcf2a17ac24b.webp)

Justice Muteti reprimanded the State for failing to file a formal affidavit despite the gravity of the matter, while highlighting the constitutional duties of the Attorney General under Article 156. The judge underscored that the principal legal adviser to the government bears a clear responsibility to assist the court when human rights and Kenya’s international standing are jeopardized by allegations of extrajudicial removal.

"This court therefore finds merit in having the Inspector General of Police appear before it to explain whether State agents may have abducted and repatriated the subject to his homeland without due process," Justice Muteti ruled. "As the head of the National Police Service, the IG is hereby summoned to address the possibility of the subject’s irregular removal from the country and provide an update on his disappearance."

The ruling followed an application filed by Senior Counsel Martha Karua, representing civil rights organization Vocal Africa. Karua pushed the court to demand the personal appearance of both Kanja and Amin, arguing that state agencies demonstrated deliberate lethargy after being notified of the activist's disappearance on October 25.

![Inspector General of Police Douglas Kanja wearing the AFRIPOL lapel pin during a meeting with members from the Law Society of Kenya at his Jogoo House office, June 4, 2025.](https://i.ibb.co/HpHBydHF/a1c136670336.webp)

Karua, who leads the People Liberation Party, challenged the state's claim that formal investigations only commenced on October 26, asserting that police cannot hide behind bureaucratic service of court papers to justify delayed action.

"Having been notified on Sunday, they cannot say that they only began investigating on Friday unless they are telling the court that they have abandoned the duty we pay them for," Karua submitted during proceedings at the Milimani Law Courts.

State Counsel Marwa, representing both the Inspector General and the DCI Director, had opposed the personal summons, telling the court that investigations were actively underway and maintaining that Mwaipaya was not held in any police facility or under the custody of either police chief.

The case now turns to Tuesday morning's scheduled appearance, where Kanja is expected to present the court with an official accounting of the police service's efforts and clarify whether state security apparatus was involved in Mwaipaya's disappearance.
