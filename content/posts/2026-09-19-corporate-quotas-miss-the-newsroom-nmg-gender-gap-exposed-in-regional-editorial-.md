Nation Media Group has found itself defending its diversity credentials after a major regional leadership overhaul assigned top newsroom gatekeeping roles across Kenya, Uganda, and Tanzania almost entirely to male journalists.

The public backlash that followed the announcements forced Group Chief Executive Officer Geoffrey Odundo to issue a corporate statement on Friday, September 18, acknowledging a clear vacancy of women at the apex of its editorial decision-making operations.

The management's response highlights a striking institutional contradiction: while the media conglomerate meets or exceeds its internal gender quotas at the board and administrative department levels, its core journalism leadership remains heavily skewed toward men.

### The Numbers Behind the Disconnect

According to metrics released by the company, women make up 42 percent of NMG’s Executive Committee and 41 percent of its Heads of Department—surpassing the group’s internal baseline target of 40 percent representation in senior corporate leadership.

![A photo collage of NMG Journalists Joe Ageyo (left), Ben Kitili (centre) and James Smart.](https://i.ibb.co/C50HBC5r/599b43758a9d.webp)

However, those boardroom percentages fail to translate into newsroom decision-making power. Within the editorial operations, women account for 36 percent of the overall workforce and 33 percent of newsroom staff, with representation dropping sharply at the senior editorial desk level where final publishing decisions are executed.

In his statement, Odundo noted that public debate surrounding the appointments exposed a clear gap in senior newsroom structure, framing the criticism as an opportunity for the media house to re-evaluate how leadership is selected.

"We recognise the concern and the important role women have played in shaping NMG's journalism over the years," Odundo said, adding that increasing female representation in decision-making positions would strengthen newsroom output and better reflect regional audiences.

### Across Three Borders, an All-Male Lineup

The public scrutiny followed an extensive restructuring of NMG's newsrooms across East Africa. The changes saw Group Editor-in-Chief Joe Ageyo taking up the role of Editor-in-Chief for NMG Uganda, supported by Washington Gikunju as Acting Managing Editor.

In Kenya, the primary editorial seats were similarly allocated to male editors. Harry Misiko was named Acting Editor-in-Chief, while Dann Mwangi assumed the position of Acting Managing Editor for Broadcast and New Media. John Kiplagat was appointed Acting Managing Editor for Publishing, and Ben Kitili took over as Acting Lead Editor for Broadcasting.

The print and print-adjacent desks saw Michael Owuor appointed Acting Lead Editor for the *Daily Nation*, Michael Omondi named Acting Lead Editor for the *Business Daily*, and Justus Wanga assigned to oversee the *Weekend Edition*.

![Geofrey Odundo NMG](https://i.ibb.co/Zpz46Zpr/55c6d696a80d.webp)

At Mwananchi Communications Limited in Tanzania, James Smart was named Acting Managing Editor for Broadcast and New Media.

### Pressure on Editorial Gatekeeping

The absence of women from the new slate triggered immediate questions from media observers and readers regarding the criteria used during executive selection, particularly in light of long-standing industry commitments to gender equity in journalism.

While corporate governance frameworks often measure diversity through general workforce percentages or non-editorial executive positions, editorial leadership dictates story choice, perspective, tone, and resource allocation. Having women represented in executive committees without equivalent placement on the newsroom floor leaves actual editorial strategy driven predominantly by male perspectives.

Odundo conceded that closing the gap in senior newsroom roles remains an unfulfilled objective, promising that future interventions will focus on placing women into concrete decision-making positions.

Whether NMG will address this disparity through immediate corrective appointments or long-term promotion pipelines remains the primary operational question facing the region's largest media house as it attempts to align its newsroom reality with its corporate diversity targets.
