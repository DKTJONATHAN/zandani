Abel Mutua has pointed to a single family meeting as the turning point that worsened his late younger brother Raphael Mbuvi Musyoka’s battle with substance abuse. 

Speaking on *The Kisiangani Podcast*, the filmmaker and digital storyteller broke down how a well-intentioned decision by his family to withdraw direct financial support backfired, pushing his brother—popularly known as Rapho—into an accelerated downward spiral rather than toward recovery.

Rapho, the youngest of eight siblings, was an undergraduate student at Jomo Kenyatta University of Agriculture and Technology (JKUAT). With six older siblings already employed and earning, the family had instituted an informal monthly welfare pool to cater to his upkeep and tuition. 

Each working sibling contributed what they could spare, routinely pooling between KSh 1,000 and KSh 2,000 each to ensure the student never struggled for pocket money.

“So at the end of the month huyu kijana alikuwa ako in the chedas,” Abel recalled, noting that the steady stream of funds kept Rapho financially comfortable throughout his earlier campus years.

### The 2021 Discovery and the Shift at Home

The dynamic shifted during the COVID-19 pandemic, when university closures forced students off campus and back into residential neighborhoods. Rapho initially spent substantial time at home, but Abel noted that his presence gradually became erratic as his visits dwindled.

During an afternoon visit in 2021, Abel sat across from his brother and sensed something amiss. He later consulted another sibling close to Rapho in age, who confirmed that the student had been quietly wrestling with substance use for approximately two years.

“Hii time amebangaiza… hapa home manze wanapiga mihadarati,” Abel recounted being told.

Confronted with the reality of the addiction, the family stepped in to explore rehabilitation programs and offered to arrange dedicated accommodation to stabilize his living conditions.

### The Meeting That Changed the Trajectory

When treatment options stalled, the family convened a formal meeting to recalibrate their approach. It was during this session that they resolved to cut off all financial contributions to Rapho entirely, believing the lack of cash would force him to reconsider his choices.

Abel now identifies that consensus as the most damaging miscalculation they made.

“Makosa big tulifanya ni kuitana kikao…” Abel stated, describing the immediate aftermath as an unravelling they could not reverse. “It was a downward spiral.”

Rather than seeking rehabilitation, Rapho declined the offer to return to treatment. Deprived of the regular sibling stipend, he adapted by seeking money from people outside his immediate family circle.

“Si anajua ni janjez… anajua ananeed hii doo alikuwa anajua hapa kwa familia imekataa… anaenda kwa random person,” Abel said, describing how the financial freeze eliminated their leverage without addressing the underlying addiction.

### The Loss and 2024 Farewell

Abel publicly announced Rapho’s passing without immediately detailing the circumstances surrounding the death. At the time, the creator posted an image of a police vehicle transporting the body and shared an M-Pesa till number to help fund burial expenses, drawing public debate across social platforms.

Raphael Mbuvi Musyoka was laid to rest on January 5, 2024, in Machakos County, concluding a painful chapter that Abel is now parsing through publicly for the first time.
