Chebet Ronoh has pulled back the curtain on the turbulent financial and personal realities that mirrored her meteoric rise as one of Kenya’s earliest viral content creators, detailing how sudden seven-figure corporate checks fueled a severe alcohol addiction.

Speaking in an interview with the BBC, the comic and digital personality described reaching a peak where corporate partnerships yielded up to Ksh 1.5 million in a single month. Yet, behind the lucrative brand alignments, the rapid influx of capital and celebrity accelerated an unmanageable drinking problem that eventually derailed her finances and personal stability.

### The Ksh 1.5 Million Monthly Burn

During her peak commercial run, Ronoh stood as a primary beneficiary of corporate Kenya’s pivot to digital creators, juggling multiple commercial retainers simultaneously.

"In one month, I think I was doing four or five brand jobs, and it was like a Ksh 1.5 million salary," Ronoh said. 

Rather than translating into long-term commercial assets, however, that windfall became the primary budget line for heavy drinking, largely driven by an urge to sustain social approval among her circle.

"I remember a lot of my money went to alcohol. I was desperate for validation, and I wanted to prove so much to my friends," she said. "I wanted to say, 'I can buy for you guys this, we can do this, it’s on me.'"

### Spotting the Behavioral Split

As the cycle continued, the entertainer began to register that her relationship with alcohol diverged sharply from that of casual social drinkers in her company. While peers maintained composure during nights out, her own intake regularly produced erratic, high-friction episodes.

"I started to notice I’m a different drinker than the rest," Ronoh said. "Some people can drink, and they’re calm. Then I started to notice, okay, I kind of go crazy."

Accepting that alcohol had superseded her personal control proved to be a difficult admission, particularly as public expectations around her loud, comedic digital persona remained high while her private baseline deteriorated.

### The Breaking Point at Home

The turning point came not on a set or at an industry event, but inside her family home following an acute emotional breakdown.

After returning home in an agitated, distressed state, the disruption became too pronounced for her domestic circle to ignore. Her brother directly intervened, advising their mother that immediate clinical intervention was the only viable path forward.

"I remember one time I came home, and I was being very distraught. I was like really crashing out, and my brother told my mum, 'I think it’s time to take her to rehab,'" Ronoh recounted.

That family-led decision marked the end of her unmonitored spiral. Entering an addiction treatment facility forced an unavoidable reckoning with how deeply substance dependency had hollowed out her early career momentum.

### The Post-Rehab Stand

Having stepped through formal rehabilitation, Ronoh has turned her public platform toward candid discussions on recovery, stripping away the social glamour frequently attached to high-spending influencer culture.

Rather than framing her story solely around past financial losses, she now positions her experience as an open case study for others caught in similar dependency cycles.

"Anyone who is aspiring to be sober, I’m telling you, it’s beautiful on the other side," she said. "Choose yourself."
