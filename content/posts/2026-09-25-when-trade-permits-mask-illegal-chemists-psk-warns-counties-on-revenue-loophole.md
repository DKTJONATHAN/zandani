Across Kenya’s commercial corridors, the receipt pinned to a dispensary wall is frequently a county trade clearance rather than a clinical endorsement. That administrative blur has created a lucrative shield for unauthorized drug outlets, prompting the Pharmaceutical Society of Kenya (PSK) to formally demand an end to local governments monetizing illicit pharmacy operations.

Marking World Pharmacists Day on Friday, September 25, the national professional lobby drew a firm line between municipal revenue collection and statutory medical authorization. Issuing a standard Single Business Permit, the society warned, gives retail traders a veneer of official approval without verifying whether the personnel dispensing prescription medicines hold any medical credentials.

### The Municipal Revenue Conflict

The central tension lies in how local revenue officers assess pharmaceutical premises. Under devolved administrative structures, county governments routinely issue commercial trading permits to retail stores to meet local revenue targets. However, the legal mandate to inspect, accredit, and register both practitioners and premises rests exclusively with the Pharmacy and Poisons Board (PPB) at the national level.

![Members of the Pharmaceutical Society of Kenya gather to mark World Pharmacists Day, September 25, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/Jj6dZyJC/19c679bee2ee.webp)

“A County Government business permit is not a licence to practise pharmacy,” the society stated. “The statutory regulation of pharmacy practice and pharmaceutical premises belongs within the national pharmaceutical regulatory framework.”

By treating medicine counters merely as taxable storefronts, county administrations risk subverting public safety checks. The society pointed out that revenue collection must never inadvertently legitimize an unlawful pharmaceutical outlet, particularly when certified practitioners are subjected to stringent regulatory scrutiny while unqualified competitors trade unchecked.

### A 50 Percent Professional Drop-Off

The enforcement challenge is compounded by a stark drop in active statutory renewals among trained professionals. While the country has more than 5,600 registered pharmacists, PSK revealed that only about 2,800 currently maintain valid annual practice licences.

That 50 percent gap leaves thousands of qualified professionals administratively detached from formal practice registers, even as urban centers face a visible proliferation of drug kiosks staffed by unvetted attendants.

![Medicine](https://i.ibb.co/PZzSX2v2/e1364d2f4ff9.webp)

PSK argued that the sector’s primary challenge is not a numerical shortage of trained personnel, but poor distribution, irregular deployment, and an uneven regulatory baseline that penalizes compliant practitioners. The lobby maintained that healthcare access must be evaluated through the quality of pharmaceutical care delivered rather than the sheer volume of commercial outlets operating in a market.

### Harmonizing the Counter

To close the regulatory loophole, the lobby is pushing county revenue departments to integrate their permit vetting systems directly with the PPB database. Under the proposed alignment, no local authority would issue or renew a Single Business Permit for any business handling human pharmaceuticals without prior statutory clearance from the national regulator.

The push comes as the Ministry of Health and the PPB intensify joint crackdowns on counterfeit, substandard, and diverted medical supplies, urging patients to verify operational premises before purchasing prescription drugs.

Whether county administrations will agree to condition their municipal trade receipts on PPB approvals remains untested, particularly in secondary towns where retail business permits serve as an immediate source of daily local revenue.
