Democratic Party leader Justin Muturi has formally petitioned the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission to summon Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen, seeking an official inquiry into remarks encouraging Kenyans in Uganda to secure national identity cards and voter credentials ahead of the 2027 General Election.

In a statement released on Saturday, September 26, Muturi argued that remarks made by Murkomen during an official visit to Uganda risked blurring the boundary between routine diaspora consular services and unmonitored voter enrollment on foreign soil. The former Attorney General also drew Ugandan President Yoweri Museveni into the matter, demanding that Kampala clarify whether its administration has sanctioned any activities linked to Kenya's upcoming electoral cycle.

"Claims touching on voter registration activities in Uganda and Kenya's electoral process cannot be brushed aside. I call on the IEBC to demand clear answers, establish the facts, and protect the integrity of our elections," Muturi said. "We therefore demand that the IEBC immediately summon CS Murkomen to explain precisely what he meant and identify the alleged voter registration activities in Uganda."

### The Kampala Directive

![DP leader Justin Muturi during a past rally](https://i.ibb.co/5gRZrwBj/33ef01bcc11d.webp)

The dispute stems from Murkomen’s recent address to Kenyans residing across the western border, where he pledged easier administrative access for citizens seeking documentation. During the engagement, Murkomen stated that significant numbers of Kenyans based in Uganda lacked vital registration papers and directed them to approach the Kenyan embassy in Kampala or cross back over the frontier to enroll.

"I want to assure Kenyans who are here looking for their documents, we have made it easy for you to get your documents, just go to the Embassy in Kampala and get registered. But you can also cross the border and get registered easily," Murkomen stated, adding that the government would apply a non-discriminatory policy across all frontier zones, including borders shared with Tanzania, Ethiopia, and Somalia.

While executive officials frame such outreach as a basic constitutional entitlement ensuring marginalized frontier communities access national identity cards, the rhetoric has handed opposition figures a sharp line of attack. Voter registration along porous border corridors has long been an acute flashpoint in Kenyan election politics, where thin victory margins amplify sensitivity over who gets placed on the primary voter register.

### Sovereignty and Jurisdictional Friction

![Interior Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen addressing the National Cohesion and Integration Commission (NCIC) and the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) on August 26.](https://i.ibb.co/Z1cqXvf4/5faea271e843.webp)

By framing Murkomen's outreach as potential foreign interference, Muturi is seeking to place the electoral commission in a defensive position well before the official pre-election registration window opens. Under Kenyan law, voter registration remains the exclusive statutory mandate of the IEBC, making public declarations by cabinet secretaries regarding electoral documentation vulnerable to accusations of executive overreach.

Muturi insisted that any indication of extra-legal registration must trigger personal legal liability.

"Kenya's sovereignty is not negotiable, and our elections cannot be compromised. Claims of this magnitude demand evidence, transparency, and accountability," Muturi said, calling on the commission to establish whether any concrete threat to the roll had materialized.

He further expanded the diplomatic surface of the grievance: "I also call on President Yoweri Museveni to clarify whether his government is aware of, or has sanctioned, any activities in Uganda connected to Kenya's electoral process."

The demand shifts the spotlight onto the IEBC, which faces the procedural question of whether it will formally review statements delivered by a state officer on foreign territory, while State House in Kampala and the Interior ministry have yet to issue formal responses to Muturi's assertions.
