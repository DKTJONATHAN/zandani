Motorists absorbing the steepest fuel hikes in months are footing the cost of a deliberate policy shift by the state to abandon price stabilisation in favour of building a rainy-day reserve.

The Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority (EPRA) announced that super petrol will climb by Ksh12.93 per litre, diesel by Ksh9.90, and kerosene by Ksh28.49 for the October-November cycle. In Nairobi, super petrol now retails at Ksh226.96, diesel at Ksh227.76, and kerosene at Ksh219.87.

![A man fueling a car at a petrol station](https://i.ibb.co/2Yy2ydDN/32e12d8dd402.webp)

In Mombasa, maximum pump prices settle at Ksh223.81 for petrol, Ksh224.61 for diesel, and Ksh216.71 for kerosene. The increases eliminate the state-backed cushions that had moderated pump cycles since April.

Defending the decision to halt the intervention, EPRA Director General Edward Mwirigi Kinyua stated that the authority chose to withhold stabilisation funds so that unspent capital could be preserved for potential future crises.

“We will not introduce the stabilisation for this pricing cycle so that the amounts being saved today can be utilised in the future if the prices were to go burst,” Kinyua told the press on Wednesday, October 14.

![A man fueling a car at a petrol station](https://i.ibb.co/cS23H1PL/b66928bbe861.webp)

Underpinning the adjustment are severe external supply pressures tied to the U.S.-Israel war on Iran and shifting Middle East market demand. Kinyua noted that September registered the worst market conditions since the conflict began, with the price of super petrol surging to USD 1,300 per metric tonne, up from an initial baseline of USD 600. Crude oil prices also touched a high of USD 106 per barrel earlier in the week before easing.

While global crude benchmarks have shown signs of cooling, the decision to leave consumers entirely exposed to the recent spikes shifts immediate liquidity stress onto households and transport operators. The accumulated savings intended for a theoretical future shock arrive alongside peak retail rates that test transport and manufacturing budgets across the country.
