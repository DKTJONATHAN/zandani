When road crashes occur on Nairobi's congested corridors, the first person to arrive is rarely an ambulance or a traffic officer. It is almost always a commercial motorcycle rider. 

That street-level reality prompted a partnership between ride-hailing platform Bolt and the Kenya Red Cross Training Institute (KRCTI) to move riders past the hesitation of standing idle at accident scenes. On Wednesday, September 23, an initial cohort of 75 riders completed a rigorous four-hour practical session in Nairobi, earning certification as Community First Aid Ambassadors alongside their new emergency kits.

![red-cross-bolt-riders.jpg](https://i.ibb.co/BHtBMh58/1672bfed0aa9.webp)

Yet the instruction carried strict operational boundaries. Monica Orero, Director of Training and Capacity Building at KRCTI, emphasized that the curriculum did not transform riders into medical professionals. Instead, the focus centered on assessing chaotic environments, administering basic interventions within authorized safety limits, and knowing the precise moment to summon professional emergency services.

For many operators navigating daily traffic, the transition from helpless spectator to active responder addresses a persistent gap. Patrick Sakwa, one of the newly certified riders, noted that past accidents often left him paralyzed by uncertainty over what actions were safe or permissible. Jeremiah Githu recalled the helplessness of losing a friend to a heart attack without knowing how to perform CPR.

“Maybe if I could have had this training before, I could have at least saved a life,” Githu said.

![arthur-gacharia-senior-operations-manager-rides-kenya.jpg](https://i.ibb.co/KjHzTB5k/a12409bd31c0.webp)

Beyond basic triage and roadside stability, the rollout has exposed wider operational demands from within the transport ecosystem. Veteran riders participating in the initial feedback loop pointed to the psychological residue of daily exposure to severe trauma on the road. Vincent Kyagulani, a Bolt rider with two years of experience, urged organizers to incorporate mental health support and counseling into future sessions to help operators cope with the stress of witnessing crashes.

Meanwhile, riders like Alfred Sarinyo from Embakasi have called for specialized instruction targeting emerging risks, including handling accidents involving electric motorcycles.

Bolt and the Kenya Red Cross plan to use feedback from this initial group to refine the curriculum before scaling the initiative. The partnership targets training for more than 500 participants within its first year of operation.
