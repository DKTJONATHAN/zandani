Gladys Wanga did not sugarcoat her assessment when she stepped up to the microphone in Kakamega. Addressing supporters gathered to honor the memory of the late Raila Odinga, the Homa Bay Governor and ODM National Chairperson turned her sights straight at former President Uhuru Kenyatta, puncturing the enduring political belief that the retired head of state holds the keys to crowning Kenya’s next opposition leader.

Speaking on Friday, October 9, 2026, Wanga took direct aim at the figures quietly hoping for Uhuru’s endorsement ahead of the 2027 contest. With candidates across the political spectrum recalibrating their survival strategies, she laid down a blunt mathematical challenge that silenced diplomatic niceties.

“President Uhuru has played with us a lot as ODM,” Wanga said, recounting the turbulent era of the Handshake partnership. “When we used to walk with him, he said he would not give us anything because he will hand over presidency to Baba.”

That promised handover, as Wanga reminded the crowd, never materialized. Despite the full machinery of an incumbent president backing Raila during their alliance, the prize remained out of reach. Drawing on that bruising history, she turned the spotlight directly onto those waiting in line for a similar political blessing.

“If he couldn’t make Baba President, then how will he make Sifuna, Matiang’i and Kalonzo president?” she posed.

By naming Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna, former Interior Cabinet Secretary Fred Matiang’i, and Wiper Democratic Movement boss Kalonzo Musyoka, Wanga laid bare the uneasy fault lines forming within the wider opposition landscape. For Wanga, relying on Uhuru’s political backing to mount a serious challenge against President William Ruto is an exercise in repeating history’s mistakes. She maintained that Ruto would not be unseated through whatever influence the former president still claims to command.

Her Kakamega appearance was staged under ODM’s Mwezi wa Baba program, an extensive itinerary marking the first anniversary of Raila’s death in October 2025. Yet beneath the reflective tone of the memorial, the governor’s message was tailored for raw, practical party survival.

Bringing greetings from party leader Oburu Odinga, Wanga underscored that the Orange party had no intention of remaining an ornamental opposition outfit content with ideological sidelines. Power, she insisted, remains the ultimate currency.

“I have also brought greetings from our Party Leader Oburu Odinga, and as he says, *hatuwezi kuwa bila power*,” Wanga told the gathering, reinforcing the message that ODM's primary objective heading into 2027 is securing a seat at the governing table.

To keep the base intact as regional gladiators position themselves for elective tickets, Wanga paired her critique of outside kingmakers with an internal assurance to party loyalists: the upcoming nomination cycle will not be tainted by the internal chaos that historically sparked member walkouts.

“ODM will conduct free, fair and transparent nominations,” she pledged, assuring aspirants that internal democracy would serve as the party's foundation as it retools its machinery following the loss of its iconic founder.

The governor's fiery Kakamega outing wrapped up an intense week of political firefighting. Just three days earlier, on Tuesday, October 6, Wanga had to take to social media platform X to stamp out viral rumors that falsely claimed Oburu Odinga had died. Clarifying the situation after speaking directly with the ODM leader that evening, she confirmed Oburu was in good health, recovering from a routine medical review at The Nairobi Hospital following a trip abroad.

With internal party anxiety settled and outside alliances put under open scrutiny, ODM’s memorial calendar now heads toward its climax. The nationwide commemorations are set to conclude with a national memorial service at Kang’o ka Jaramogi in Bondo on October 15, 2026, where the debate over the party’s post-Raila identity is poised to take center stage.
