State infrastructure managers and the National Social Security Fund have moved to establish whether every shilling billed on the Ksh200 billion Rironi-Mau Summit highway project reflects actual ground progress, inviting external engineers to audit construction spending across the delayed corridor.

The joint tender published on Friday by the Kenya National Highways Authority (KeNHA), contractor China Road and Bridge Corporation (CRBC), and NSSF seeks an independent consulting firm to inspect completed and ongoing civil works and certify real costs across two critical arteries linking Nairobi to the Rift Valley.

The audit arrives at a precarious juncture for the public-private partnership. In August, Roads and Transport Cabinet Secretary Davis Chirchir publicly projected that dualling of the 58-kilometre Rironi-Maai Mahiu-Naivasha stretch would conclude by October 30, 2026. Yet National Treasury performance records paint a starkly slower reality.

### The Delivery Deficit

![Road construction and tarmac laying along the Rironi-Mau Summit highway near the Ngarariga Interchange.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/rironi-mau-summit_3.jpg.webp?itok=2-IC79QQ)

Treasury monitoring data compiled through September 30 shows early civil works on the Rironi-Naivasha corridor had reached only 34.96 per cent completion. Further west, progress on the 81-kilometre Gilgil-Mau Summit corridor stood at just 10.38 per cent.

With the state's self-imposed October delivery milestone now out of reach, the incoming independent engineer will scrutinise whether capital disbursements align strictly with work performed on site before further public and pension resources are committed.

The assignment covers early works verification and cost certification across both the Rironi-Gilgil segment of the Nairobi-Nakuru highway and the alternate Rironi-Maai Mahiu-Naivasha branch. Beyond measuring physical tarmac and earthworks, the consultant will inspect structural engineering on bridges, drainage installations, interchanges, road safety barriers, and planned toll collection plazas.

### Pension Money and Tolling Stakes

Scrutiny over project expenditure carries elevated financial stakes due to the commercial structure of the 30-year concession. NSSF's co-financing participation exposes workers' pension reserves to the capital efficiency of CRBC's construction schedule, while motorists face an estimated toll tariff of Ksh8 per kilometre once the expanded lanes open.

Treasury blueprints designate the Rironi-Naivasha section as a dual carriageway serviced by three toll stations. The downstream Gilgil-Nakuru section is slated for expansion into a six-lane highway to clear chronic freight bottlenecks toward western Kenya and the Great Lakes transit corridor.

![An image of ongoing construction works on the Rironi-Mau Summit captured on September 23, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/works-on-the-rironi-mau-summit_0.jpg.webp?itok=umck0Twd)

Because the road's commercial viability hinges on recouping capital costs through three decades of user fees, unverified cost escalation or unexplained expenditure during early civil works directly distorts the financial model used to set final toll tariffs.

### Procurement Terms

Under the joint tender requirements, participating consulting firms or consortia must present verified credentials in independent engineering services on large-scale public-private partnership (PPP) ventures.

KeNHA confirmed that technical competence will dominate the evaluation criteria, accounting for 80 per cent of each bidder's score, while financial proposals will carry a 20 per cent weight.

The selected expert will be tasked with issuing definitive cost certification for early works, establishing the verified financial baseline for the remaining expansion phases of Kenya's most expensive toll highway corridor.
