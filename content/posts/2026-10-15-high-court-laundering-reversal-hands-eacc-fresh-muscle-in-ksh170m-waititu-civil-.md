A decisive High Court intervention has tightened the legal net around former Kiambu Governor Ferdinand Waititu, handing the Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission (EACC) fresh evidentiary backing as it pursues Ksh170 million in alleged tender kickbacks through the civil courts.

Justice Rose Ougo on Thursday, October 15, dismissed Waititu’s appeal against his February 2025 graft convictions, while overturning a subordinate magistrate's decision that had cleared him of money laundering. The reversal establishes a direct judicial finding that illicit proceeds moved through the former governor's networks, creating a crucial bridge for anti-graft detectives seeking to seize the funds under civil asset forfeiture laws.

Waititu, alongside his wife Susan Wangari Ndung'u and their co-accused, now faces formal mitigation and sentencing on the reinstated money laundering charges on Monday, October 19, 2026.

### The Tender at the Core of the Clawback

![Ferdinand Waititu](https://i.ibb.co/HDfghPVS/e5e3b6d8fb4f.webp)

The financial footprint under scrutiny traces back to an inquiry launched by the EACC in 2019 into the Kiambu County government's procurement practices during the 2017/2018 financial year. Investigators zeroed in on an irregularly awarded Ksh588 million road contract intended to upgrade gravel roads to bitumen standards across several sub-counties.

On February 12, 2025, the trial court convicted Waititu and his co-accused on counts encompassing conflict of interest, dealing with suspicious property, abuse of office, and severe procurement irregularities. However, the trial magistrate's initial rejection of the money laundering charges left a legal loophole regarding how the proceeds were layered and converted.

Justice Ougo’s appellate judgment closed that gap on Thursday, confirming that the transaction structures met the statutory threshold for money laundering offences. According to the anti-graft commission, that formal criminal finding substantially elevates the state's posture in its separate, pending civil suit seeking to compel Waititu and an associated corporate entity to surrender Ksh170 million traceable to the road contract.

### Mounting Asset Liquidations

The pending Ksh170 million recovery is the latest phase in an expansive asset stripping process targeting the former county chief's wealth portfolio.

![EACC Integrity Centre](https://i.ibb.co/Z3hj0SJ/5b7e97627c2e.webp)

In December 2025, the Anti-Corruption Court executed a forfeiture order stripping Waititu and his associates of unexplained assets valued at approximately Ksh131.2 million. That forfeiture netted several high-value holdings for the state, including real estate properties situated in Thindigua worth an estimated Ksh64 million, a commercial Caterpillar machine valued at Ksh11 million, two motor vehicles, and assorted land holdings linked to the family.

While criminal proceedings target custodial sentences and statutory fines, the civil track operates on a balance of probabilities, designed to deny public officers the financial proceeds of procurement fraud even as appeals run their course.

### The Civil Threshold Ahead

Despite the boost delivered by Justice Ougo’s ruling, the EACC’s financial recovery is not instantaneous. Commission officials noted on Thursday that the Ksh170 million recovery remains an active civil matter requiring a final decree from the bench before bank accounts and underlying corporate holdings can be formally attached and liquidated into public revenue.

For Waititu, the immediate pressure shifts to the dock on October 19, where the High Court will hear mitigation before determining the custodial and financial penalties tied to the reinstated laundering counts—penalties that will arrive just as the civil court weighs the total forfeiture of the Ksh170 million tender loot.
