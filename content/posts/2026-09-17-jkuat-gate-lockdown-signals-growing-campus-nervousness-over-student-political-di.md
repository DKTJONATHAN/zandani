University campuses across Nairobi are facing heightened administrative sensitivity surrounding public political discourse, underscored by a high-profile lockout at the Jomo Kenyatta University of Agriculture and Technology (JKUAT) Karen campus where security officers barred Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna from entering for a student-led forum.

The abrupt decision by university management to shut the main gate upon the senator’s arrival reflects escalating caution among higher education administrators regarding political figures and student gatherings in the lead-up to the 2027 general election.

Sifuna had been invited by the JKUAT Students Association Karen Campus, headed by student governor Graham Mboya, to serve as the chief speaker at the institution's Inaugural Law, Policy and Governance forum. However, upon arrival at the facility, Sifuna and his delegation found the entrance locked and security personnel deployed to deny entry, leaving both the invited speaker and gathering students outside the gates.

Footage from the scene captured a university representative telling gathered students outside the perimeter that the event was halted over security and administrative considerations. The official explained that the administration postponed the assembly to prevent potential student unrest, while also attributing the decision to the absence of several faculty professors. The official assured students that the participants would be invited back to hold the meeting at a later date.

JKUAT management did not publicly identify the specific authority who issued the last-minute cancellation order, nor did the university provide an official timeline for when the forum would be rescheduled.

The incident comes amid an expanded campaign of political and civic engagements across Nairobi conducted by Sifuna alongside the Linda Mwananchi movement. Rather than dampening the political conversation, the campus shutdown has displaced it to higher official channels. Denouncing what he described as an pervasive atmosphere of fear constraining public engagement, Sifuna announced he would extend an official invitation to Mboya and the JKUAT student leadership to hold the governance session inside the Senate chambers.
