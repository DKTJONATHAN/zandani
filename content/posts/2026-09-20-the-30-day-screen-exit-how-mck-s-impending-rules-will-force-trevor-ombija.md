The double life of Kenya’s prime-time TV stars who spend their weekdays in high-definition newsrooms and their weekends on political podiums is about to be cut short. 

The Media Council of Kenya (MCK) is moving to close the regulatory loophole that has long allowed household media brands to build their campaign profiles while remaining on air. The council is finalizing strict new guidelines that will force any media practitioner harboring political ambitions to resign immediately and exit the airwaves.

This shift comes at a highly sensitive time for the country's top broadcasting talent. Senior Citizen TV anchor Trevor Ombija, who has been a prominent face at Royal Media Services (RMS) for over eight years, has already been off-air recently to focus on his political moves in Nyakach ahead of the 2027 General Election. 

However, the tension between a journalist's newsroom status and their campaign-trail rhetoric was laid bare during a Friday rally in Nyakach. Addressing residents in Luo, Ombija claimed that former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua had pressured RMS to remove him from his anchor position, suggesting the move was driven by ethnic and professional calculations. Ombija went on to claim that President William Ruto intervened to protect him and other journalists, framing his support for the President’s 2027 re-election bid as an act of personal gratitude.

The claim was swiftly dismantled by his own boss. RMS Editorial Director Linus Kaikai went public on X to reject Ombija’s version of events, stating unequivocally that no such call was ever made by Gachagua, and adding that such an intervention would not have carried weight at RMS anyway. Kaikai later revealed that when confronted, Ombija admitted the statement was a "mistake" he regretted, believing his comments wouldn't be picked up by the wider public.

While Ombija’s podium slip-up sparked internal newsroom friction, it also highlighted a much broader trend of high-profile journalists shifting their focus to elective politics. Citizen TV's Stephen Letoo has already announced his intention to contest the Kilgoris parliamentary seat, while political reporter John Wanyama recently resigned from the station to focus on his run for the Malava parliamentary seat.

Under the forthcoming MCK rules, the timing of these transitions will no longer be left to the discretion of the candidates or their employers. MCK Chief Executive Officer David Omwoyo confirmed that the council is wrapping up a regulatory framework that mandates an immediate halt to all on-air appearances once a journalist declares political interests.

To prevent stations from giving an unfair advantage to their former stars, the guidelines will also require media houses to maintain balanced, objective coverage of all candidates, including their own former colleagues.

Omwoyo indicated that the MCK plans to host a validation workshop with editorial leaders to refine the guidelines before gazetting them within 30 days. For the country’s most recognizable screen personalities, the countdown to a permanent choice between prime-time television and the campaign trail has officially begun.
