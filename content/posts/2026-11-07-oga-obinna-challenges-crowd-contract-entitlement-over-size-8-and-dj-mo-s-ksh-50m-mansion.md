Oga Obinna is tackling the unspoken assumption that public figures owe social media followers a say in their personal living rooms.

Addressing the commentary following gospel singer Size 8 and her husband DJ Mo’s detailed digital walkthrough of their newly finished residence, the media personality questioned why online audiences felt entitled to pass aesthetic judgment on a project funded entirely out of private pockets.

In a video published to his Instagram page on Friday, November 6, Obinna framed the online backlash as a humorous misunderstanding of who the house was actually built for.

“DJ Mo na Size 8, naona mliwajengea Wakenya walikuwa wamewapa contract muwajengee nyumba vile wanaitaka. Na mistake mlifanya ni nyinyi mkajenga nyumba vile munataka, na Wakenya hawajafurahia,” Obinna said.

![Silvia Of The Mziza Family Shocked After Dealing with Nairobi Landlords' Demands](https://i.ibb.co/RG1QZBdZ/0304a898747f.webp)

The satire targeted the volume of unsolicited architectural and interior design critiques that surfaced after the couple showcased their spacious living area, dining room, kitchen, lighting fixtures, and bathroom finishes. Critics quickly picked apart decorative choices, questioning color palettes and layout decisions despite having zero financial stake in the real estate development.

Obinna pointed out the absurdity of demanding bespoke standards without offering capital support, emphasizing the gap between digital commentary and real-life financial commitment.

“Kenyans would contribute one bob, not a cent, not a shilling. Hata sumuni mtu hajatoa,” he noted. “Umeenda umebuy shamba yako, ukajenga nyumba yako.”

Beyond defending the visual choices, Obinna anchored his position in his personal relationship with the couple, revealing that he had visited the construction site multiple times while work was underway. Having observed the multi-year progression firsthand, he pointed to the financial discipline and personal sacrifices required to pull off a project of that scale.

Based on his site visits and knowledge of the construction scope, Obinna estimated the total build expenditure at approximately KSh 50 million.

For Obinna, that multi-million shilling investment earns the couple complete immunity from public opinion regarding how their personal space looks or functions. He challenged dissatisfied followers to redirect their focus away from celebrity comment sections and toward their own physical assets.

“Build yours. Fagia kwako, jenga yako,” he stated.

Ending his remarks with a direct note to the couple, Obinna urged them to tune out the external noise and enjoy the outcome of their efforts.

“Size 8, I love the house. It came out exactly how you guys wanted it. And you guys love the house; that is what matters. Your family loves the house. Enjoy your house,” he added.

The defense comes as celebrity home unveils increasingly turn into battlegrounds between public figures seeking to showcase lifestyle milestones and audiences applying crowd-sourced standards to private residential builds.
