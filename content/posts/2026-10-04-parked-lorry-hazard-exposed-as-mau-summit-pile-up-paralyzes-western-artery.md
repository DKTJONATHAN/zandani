Traffic response teams moved to restore single-lane mobility at Mau Summit on Sunday morning after a three-vehicle pile-up blocked a vital stretch of the Nakuru-Eldoret Highway, scattering agricultural freight across the roadway and trapping hundreds of long-distance travelers.

The collision occurred minutes past 8:00 am when a commercial pickup traveling toward Eldoret struck a private saloon car from behind. Eyewitness accounts recorded at the scene indicate the pickup driver lost directional control immediately after the initial impact, veering off the active lane and slamming into a heavy commercial lorry parked along the highway shoulder.

Wreckage footage from the scene showed extensive structural damage to both the pickup cabin and the smaller passenger car. Consignments carried in the pickup bed ruptured upon impact with the stationary truck, discharging crates and goods across the tarmac and effectively halting movement along both directions of the western transit corridor.

### Roadside Obstacles Compound Impact

While traffic marshals worked to tow the mangled pickup from the stationary lorry's rear undercarriage, medical teams and local responders assisted occupants out of the crushed passenger compartments. Formal casualty counts remain unconfirmed by police commanders at the scene, though initial responders treated multiple persons for severe injuries before transferring them to regional healthcare facilities.

Witnesses cited high approach speeds and suspected driver impairment as factors in the initial rear-end collision. However, the presence of the immobilized lorry on the highway verge played a decisive role in converting an overtaking or following-distance error into a major containment barrier. Commercial freight corridors across the Rift Valley have frequently witnessed similar chain-reaction collisions, where unlit or improperly cleared shoulder vehicles leave errant drivers with zero run-off space.

### Scrutiny on Trunk Highway Safety

The Mau Summit gridlock comes barely 48 hours after 17 passengers were killed in a multi-vehicle disaster at Salama along the Nairobi-Mombasa corridor. In that incident, an out-of-control haulage truck suffered mechanical brake failure before cutting through a 14-seater public service vehicle carrying pilgrims toward Nakuru County.

National Transport and Safety Authority Director General Nashon Kondiwa confirmed that multi-agency technical squads have intensified spot audits and vehicle compliance inspections nationwide following the Salama fatalities. While the regulatory body has stepped up fleet-engagement forums with public transport operators to reinforce strict lane discipline and speed compliance, private and small-commercial utility vehicles remain a persistent enforcement challenge on high-speed bypasses.

Nakuru County traffic authorities are expected to release a detailed operational report detailing the formal casualty tally and determining whether the parked lorry complied with mandatory breakdown warning and shoulder clearance regulations.
