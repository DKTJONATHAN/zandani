A suspended police badge has emerged as the primary tool of deception for a highly coordinated highway syndicate, exposing how rogue officers can turn their insider tactical training into a weapon against the public.

On Thursday, September 24, prosecutors arraigned four suspects at the Makadara Law Courts. The group—comprising two interdicted police officers and two civilian accomplices—stands accused of running an aggressive extortion and robbery network along the busy Thika Superhighway corridor.

![A section of the Thika Super Highway at Muthaiga, Nairobi County](https://i.ibb.co/VckwFTYN/aff89b74168e.webp)

The suspects were intercepted by detectives in Kasarani on Tuesday, September 22, after they allegedly extorted Ksh15,000 from a member of the public. Investigators quickly established that the core of the operation relied on law enforcement backgrounds. One of the suspects was a constable formerly attached to the Kahawa Sukari Police Station, while the second officer was previously stationed with the Railways Police Unit before both were suspended.

According to police files, the suspects did not abandon their institutional training upon suspension. Instead, they allegedly leveraged their understanding of police patrol patterns, communication, and authority to stage highly convincing daytime operations. Using a grey Toyota Allion, the group posed as active-duty officers to intercept motorists and pedestrians across Kasarani, Kahawa, Githurai, and Mwihoko.

This was not a simple roadside extortion racket. Investigators at the Ruiru Police Station have linked the group to a sophisticated bank-trailing pipeline. In one incident captured on surveillance footage, members of the syndicate allegedly trailed a customer from a bank in Kahawa Sukari to a nearby petrol station. Once the victim parked, they used a specialized tactical tool to smash his vehicle's window, escaping with more than Ksh100,000.

![Kenyan Police Vehicle.](https://i.ibb.co/Cs4D7BLF/f9660caf5036.webp)

Detectives noted that the daytime execution of these break-ins and the precision of the tools used point directly to operational methods consistent with professional police training. By operating in broad daylight and mimicking legitimate police stops, the syndicate exploited the public’s natural compliance with law enforcement.

The prosecution is now examining the full scope of the network, with investigators tracking additional links to a string of unresolved violent robberies across Nairobi and parts of Central Kenya. Meanwhile, the Directorate of Criminal Investigations has urged motorists and bank customers who may have fallen victim to similar daytime intercepts along the Thika Superhighway to step forward and assist in tying the suspects to the wider regional network.
