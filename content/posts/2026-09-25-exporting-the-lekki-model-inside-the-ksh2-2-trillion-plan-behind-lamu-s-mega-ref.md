Kenya’s push to anchor East Africa’s petroleum supply chain is moving directly from the industrial zone of Lekki in Lagos to the coastal shores of Lamu.

When President William Ruto arrives at the Dangote Petroleum Refinery on Friday, September 25, the walkthrough serves as a live inspection of the operational scale and technical design about to be deployed in the Coast region. Five days later, on Wednesday, September 30, state officials and project leads will break ground on a Ksh2.2 trillion facility designed to process 700,000 barrels of crude oil per day in Lamu.

The project relies on a commercial and structural framework forged in West Africa and finalized in international diplomatic corridors. On Monday, September 21, during the 81st United Nations General Assembly (UNGA 81) in New York, President Ruto met with industrialist Aliko Dangote and Africa Finance Corporation (AFC) Chief Executive Officer Samaila Zubairu. That session locked in the final financing arrangements and project schedules required to move the complex into physical execution.

### Shared Contractors and Continental Scale

![Ruto-Dangote](https://i.ibb.co/zTxHMzt0/ef189067adca.webp)

The technical connection between the Lagos plant and the upcoming Lamu facility is built into the project's procurement structure. Kenya has approved a contract valued at more than Ksh58 billion to an Indian state-owned engineering firm, assigning it consultancy, engineering, procurement, and construction management oversight for the Lamu complex.

The Indian vendor’s expansion into Kenya directly follows its project management work on Dangote’s Lekki facility. By retaining the primary engineering firm that oversaw the technical assembly of the Lagos site, project partners are relying on an established construction team to manage early risk and prevent integration delays in Lamu.

The planned processing capacity places the project among the largest industrial developments in the region. The Lamu plant is designed for a daily throughput of 700,000 barrels. At the same time, Dangote Group is pursuing an expansion of its Lekki refinery from 700,000 to 1.4 million barrels per day. Combined, the two facilities will bring total planned refining capacity under this operational partnership to 2.1 million barrels per day.

### Regional Supply Shifts and Economic Impact

For Kenya, local processing capacity represents a structural shift away from total reliance on imported refined petroleum, a trade burden that repeatedly consumes foreign exchange reserves and leaves pump prices exposed to global market shocks. The administration projects that domestic output will stabilize regional supply lines and spur downstream industrial activity.

![An image of the Dangote Petroleum Refinery in Nigeria.](https://i.ibb.co/BK72rCc9/6769a1aa16de.webp)

The development is also pegged to deliver significant local employment along the coastal industrial corridor. Official estimates place projected job creation at more than 60,000 roles spanning heavy construction, logistical support, facility operations, and secondary chemical processing.

"We are ready to break ground on the East Africa refinery in Lamu, a transformative project that will enhance the region’s energy security, deepen local value addition, create jobs and advance our industrialisation agenda," Ruto said following the New York meetings.

### What Comes Next

With the funding alignment locked in alongside the AFC and project management contracts assigned, attention turns to initial site development. President Ruto’s Friday walkthrough at the Lekki plant with Aliko Dangote completes the pre-construction review before both leadership teams fly to Coast for the formal ceremony on Wednesday, September 30.

Once site works commence in Lamu, the immediate operational benchmark will shift to ground clearing, heavy equipment mobilization, and integrating civil infrastructure with the surrounding port transportation network.
