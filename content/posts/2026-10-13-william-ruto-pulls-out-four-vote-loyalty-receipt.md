William Ruto decided Monday was the day to open the old political ledger and read out the personal balance sheet between himself and his predecessor, Uhuru Kenyatta.

Addressing crowd stops across Nandi and Uasin Gishu counties on October 12, the Head of State stripped away any remaining diplomatic politeness about their famous fallout, accusing the retired president of outright political trickery after years of unreturned loyalty.

Ruto did not hold back on numbers, pointing squarely to the ballots he cast to elevate Kenyatta to State House in 2013 and through both rounds of the 2017 contest.

“Mimi William Ruto nilikupigia kura mara nne. Mimi nilisimama na wewe katika safari yako ya kutafuta uongozi. Nikasimama na wewe 2013, 2017. Wewe ukanitapeli,” Ruto said, airing the lingering grievance in front of cheering supporters.

### The Broken Covenant

The heart of the President’s grievance was the informal succession arrangement forged during the early Jubilee coalition days—a widely touted pact that promised a reciprocal ten-year handover once Kenyatta concluded his two terms.

Instead, Ruto reminded the crowd, when his turn arrived in 2022, the former Head of State pulled away and actively backed his rivals.

“Ukasema miaka kumi ya Uhuru na ya Ruto kumi. Wakati yangu ilifika ukanitapeli, ukanikwepa, ukaambia Wakenya wasinipigie kura,” Ruto told the gathering, recounting how the promised transition evaporated into a state-backed campaign to block his presidency.

The emotional post-mortem offered an unvarnished look at how deeply the 2022 snub still resonates with Ruto, who used the North Rift platform to replay the timeline of their alliance from brotherhood to bitter estrangement.

### A Cautionary Tale for New Suitors

Ruto did not limit the speech to reminiscing about his past bruised loyalty. He pivoted the personal drama into a buyer-beware advisory aimed directly at opposition politicians who are currently warming up to the retired president ahead of the 2027 General Election.

Using his own experience as exhibit A, Ruto cautioned any leader entering Kenyatta’s political orbit to look at the track record before committing their political capital.

His message to the opposition was direct: if four rounds of devoted voting and a decade-long partnership could end in abandonment, newcomers should hardly expect a different outcome.

### Tour Theater and Unsettled Grudges

The sharp exchange comes in the middle of Ruto’s intensive 10-day tour across the North Rift, an itinerary ostensibly packed with regional project unveilings and grassroots inspections.

Yet, as Monday’s stops proved, national succession politics and historic betrayals are never far from the podium.

By dragging the unfulfilled ten-year deal back into the spotlight, Ruto served notice that the 2022 score is far from forgotten—and that Kenyatta’s political footprint remains a central battlefield in the run-up to 2027.
