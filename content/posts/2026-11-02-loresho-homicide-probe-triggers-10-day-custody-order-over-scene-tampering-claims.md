Allegations of active crime scene tampering inside a Loresho residence have prompted a Nairobi court to grant police a 10-day custodial window, placing four suspects—including a veteran Kenyan television actress—in holding at Gigiri Police Station while investigators work to preserve essential forensic evidence.

Senior Principal Magistrate Barbara Akinyi issued the detention order on Monday, November 2, capping the prosecution's request for a 15-day hold. The ruling followed court disclosures regarding the death of 86-year-old Dorita Macharia, who was discovered on October 31, 2026, lying in a pool of blood with a deep cut across the left side of her neck. Macharia is the grandmother of the four detained suspects.

![A man's hand cuffed inside a police cell](https://i.ibb.co/67dLYYgd/204d392de31b.webp)

Central to the state's application for extended custody was an explicit submission by police that the actress and her brother were intercepted attempting to clean the room where the fatal attack occurred. Detectives informed the court that releasing the suspects at this stage presented an immediate risk of witness intimidation and evidence contamination, particularly as multiple technical legs of the investigation remain outstanding.

According to police submissions, investigators have yet to retrieve close-circuit television (CCTV) footage from Loresho estate, analyze latent fingerprints lifted from the scene, or secure mobile phone call data records from telecommunication providers. A formal postmortem examination to establish the exact cause of Macharia's death had also not been completed by the time of the court session.

Defense attorneys strongly opposed the continued detention, maintaining that their clients had already cooperated by recording statements and surrendering their mobile phones to police. The defense argued that the state lacked compelling grounds to curtail personal freedom, challenging the legality of the arrest process and invoking constitutional protections surrounding individual liberties.

![Image of Court Gravel](https://i.ibb.co/6RhfMRbP/d434f302882b.webp)

In balancing individual rights against procedural necessity, Magistrate Akinyi determined that the investigation remained at an early and sensitive phase, granting police 10 days instead of the requested 15.

The prominent suspect built her public career across broadcast media and stage productions, notably portraying Hope Baraka—the show's first female chief and Member of Parliament—in *Makutano Junction*. Her screen resume includes roles in *A Better Life*, *The Inside Story*, *A Merry X-Mess*, and *Kam U Stay*, alongside co-creating and presenting KTN's television game show *Who's Smarter Now?* between 1999 and 2006.

The 10-day custodial period sets a tight forensic timeline for homicide detectives to conduct the required autopsy, pull phone logs, secure CCTV recordings, and compile witness statements before submitting the investigation file to the Office of the Director of Public Prosecutions (ODPP).
