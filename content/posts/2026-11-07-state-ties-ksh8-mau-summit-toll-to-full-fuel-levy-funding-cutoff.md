The National Treasury's infrastructure financing desk has anchored its defense of the proposed Ksh8 per kilometre toll on the Rironi-Mau Summit Highway on a statutory funding cutoff: once the corridor is formally gazetted, it will be stripped entirely of state allocations from the fuel levy.

Responding to mounting public pushback that drivers paying the Road Maintenance Levy Fund (RMLF) at the pump will be taxed twice to ply the corridor, Public Private Partnerships (PPP) Directorate Director General Kefa Seda stated that official tolling legally severs an asset from public upkeep funds.

Under this administrative shift, the Ksh200 billion corridor will transition from a tax-supported public thoroughfare into a standalone commercial concession, where routine maintenance and capital investment recovery must be financed exclusively through direct user collections.

### The Statutory Severance From Fuel Levies

The dispute centers on the intersection between Kenya’s universal fuel levies and project-specific user tariffs. Motorists have questioned why they should shoulder an additional Ksh8 for every kilometre traveled along the crucial transit corridor while continuing to contribute to the national maintenance pool every time they refuel.

![Director General of the Public Private Partnerships (PPP) Directorate at the National Treasury during the Inter Agency Project Steering Committee meeting overseeing the implementat](https://i.ibb.co/0RDjSx80/38f613c31c80.webp)

Seda argued that the two revenue streams are legally separate instruments that do not overlap on a declared toll route.

"Tax is a compulsory extraction enforceable by law, while a toll is a fee charged for consuming a road service," Seda said on Saturday, November 7. "Therefore, tax is compulsory and applicable to the general public, while for tolls you only pay when you use the road and you can also avoid paying by using other alternative routes."

According to the directorate, that legal boundary becomes concrete the moment an infrastructure project undergoes formal gazettement.

“For a road to be tolled, there has to be a gazettement," Seda explained. "Once gazetted, it ceases to benefit from RMLF allocation, as the receivables from toll fees are deemed to be used to recover capital investment and maintenance costs.”

By cutting off RMLF funding, the state shifts the entire operational and financing burden of the highway to the private concessionaire, relying on projected vehicle throughput rather than Treasury transfers to keep the asset motorable.

### Transparency Demands and Statutory Barriers

The directorate's public clarification lands as civil society groups push for deeper exposure of the corridor's commercial numbers.

The Consumers Federation of Kenya (COFEK) recently challenged the Ksh8 tariff before the PPP Petition Committee, demanding that the Kenya National Highways Authority (KeNHA) release primary project documents. The lobby pressed for the disclosure of feasibility studies, procurement files, public participation reports, and the underlying mathematical models used to set the per-kilometre rate.

COFEK argued that existing project summaries failed to account for basic consumer protections, leaving questions unanswered regarding the economic affordability of the tariff, the allocation of commercial risks between the contractor and taxpayers, and the viability of toll-free diversionary roads.

The petition failed to secure those records, however, after the committee dismissed it on a technicality, ruling that the consumer group filed its challenge past the strict seven-day statutory limitation period set under the Public Private Partnerships Act.

While the dismissal insulated the concession's disclosures from tribunal scrutiny, the directorate's rationale places scrutiny back on practical highway mechanics. Because the state's legal justification relies on drivers retaining the choice to avoid toll booths, transport operators and private motorists are awaiting the gazettement notice itself—the formal legal instrument that will determine the final route classification, toll station locations, and the operational condition of free alternate routes across the Rift Valley corridor.
