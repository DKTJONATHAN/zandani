A 90-day government inquiry into recurring secondary school disruptions will scrutinize boarding school financing, uniform costs, and the state's historical failure to implement previous inquiry findings, under terms gazetted by Education Cabinet Secretary Julius Ogamba.

The 52-member team, chaired by former University of Nairobi Vice-Chancellor Peter Mulwa Mbithi, has been established following a wave of unrest and fire incidents that disrupted learning across approximately 204 secondary schools in 2026.

According to the Gazette notice published on Friday, October 23, the task team is not confined to arson investigations or student discipline. Instead, it has been given a statutory mandate to examine the systemic operational pressures that precipitate strikes, explicitly ordering members to investigate correlations between student unrest, institutional funding shortages, and school fees.

### Auditing Unimplemented Past Reports

A primary assignment handed to the Mbithi team is conducting an audit of earlier task force reports on school unrest to identify specific administrative bottlenecks that prevented past recommendations from being executed.

![Former University of Nairobi Vice-Chancellor Peter Mulwa Mbithi gives a speech during a past event.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/peter-mbithi.jpg.webp?itok=592k5Uy3)

Over the past two decades, successive education administrations have commissioned multiple inquiries into secondary school arson, producing detailed recommendations on dormitory safety, congestion, and student governance that frequently remained unfulfilled on ministry shelves.

Under the newly gazetted terms, the team must examine why those reforms stalled while establishing the immediate and underlying causes that forced nationwide school shutdowns this year.

The inquiry will also assess the commercial viability and financial strain of secondary education on families, with members directed to review the affordability of school uniforms, safety compliance, emergency preparedness, and the general adequacy of boarding facilities.

### Broad Stakeholder Composition

The panel pulls representation from across academia, state security, headteachers, and labor unions to balance institutional oversight with ground-level school realities.

Mbithi, who led the University of Nairobi between 2015 and 2019, will work alongside former Maseno University Vice-Chancellor Julius Nyabundi, Council of Legal Education chairperson Collins Odote, and Kenyatta University Associate Professor Jackline Nyerere. The panel also features Dr Purity Ngina, who earned her doctorate at age 28 in 2018.

Teacher and parental interests are represented by Kenya National Union of Teachers (KNUT) Secretary-General Collins Henry Oyuu and National Parents Association chair Silas David Obuhatsa.

![A partly burnt section of the dormitory building at Utumishi Girls in Nakuru, May 28, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/utumishi-school-building-burnt-by-fire.jpg.webp?itok=PTUni1xc)

Law enforcement and regulatory oversight have been integrated through Assistant Inspector General of Police George Lenny Kisaka, former Nairobi County education executive Janet Ouko, and former Teachers Service Commission official Leila Abdi Ali.

The broader membership includes educational researchers, administrators, and regional education figures, supported by an eight-member secretariat steered by William Sugut, the Head of the Directorate of Secondary Education.

### Reporting Timelines and Stakeholder Evidence

The gazetted framework requires the team to conduct public hearings and receive submissions from administrators, parents, learners, and education sector stakeholders before concluding its work.

Unlike ad-hoc commissions that only report at dissolution, the Mbithi panel must file monthly progress briefs directly to the Education Cabinet Secretary throughout its 90-day tenure.

The final dossier, expected in late January 2027, will test whether the ministry can convert diagnosis into policy enforcement across 204 volatile institutions.
