Nairobi’s stubborn electoral arithmetic has extracted its highest-profile casualty from the ruling coalition, compelling Governor Johnson Sakaja to surrender his United Democratic Alliance (UDA) colours in exchange for political survival on an Orange Democratic Movement (ODM) ticket.

Sakaja walked into ODM’s Chungwa House headquarters on Monday, November 2, where he was received by senior party officials led by Deputy Party Leader Simba Arati. The public crossover followed weeks of political signalling, culminating in a weekend address at Jacaranda Grounds where the county chief directly polled attendees on his ballot vehicle before requesting an official reception into the Orange ranks.

The defection abruptly alters the capital’s executive landscape. By yielding to ODM, Sakaja has effectively validated the opposition outfit's long-standing claim that Nairobi remains an Orange bastion where winning a countywide executive mandate on a ruling-party ticket is an uphill battle.

### The Arithmetic of Survival

![Nairobi Governor Johnson Sakaja during the ODM rally in Jacaranda on October 31, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/hFDHsVZG/2fcb1a42162a.webp)

For months, ODM strategists had applied sustained public pressure on the governor, openly arguing that his incumbency would count for little if he attempted to seek a second term under the ruling party. The party had consistently resolved to field candidates across all elective seats in Nairobi, framing the gubernatorial office as non-negotiable.

That pressure manifested bluntly during an empowerment forum in Kileleshwa Ward, where Suna East MP Junet Mohamed issued a candid warning to the governor regarding his reelection prospects.

"I want to tell Sakaja that if he still desires to be re-elected as Nairobi governor in 2027, then he should come to ODM," Junet said. "If he decides to remain where he is, he will wait until Jesus comes back, and nobody will elect him."

Junet’s calculation drew on Nairobi’s voting history, where opposition strongholds in the eastern and informal settlements have historically delivered decisive margins in countywide races. By accepting ODM's ticket, Sakaja secures institutional protection from the capital’s most disciplined voting machinery while preempting a bruising challenge from an ODM-backed rival.

### Roots of the Crossover

The groundwork for Sakaja’s migration had been laid well before Monday’s ceremony. In 2025, the late ODM leader Raila Odinga explicitly endorsed Sakaja’s performance at City Hall, calling on ODM loyalists to support the governor’s second-term bid when the time came.

![Nairobi Governor Johnson Sakaja, during a UDA Delegated meeting, 2022.](https://i.ibb.co/Jw1Df2Hx/a82227545091.webp)

Sakaja had drawn heavily on that relationship to manage a fractured county assembly, where ODM members of county assembly form a critical bloc. By August 2026, the governor was openly hinting at severing ties with UDA, citing broad consultations and his personal bond with the late opposition figurehead.

"I was very close with Raila Odinga. He was like my father, and I spent a lot of time with him," Sakaja said at the time, indicating that internal deliberations were steering him toward the Orange party.

### A Shifting Dynamic at City Hall

The formal migration leaves UDA without an executive figurehead in the country's wealthiest and most populous county. While the ruling coalition retained national power, its hold on Nairobi's devolved executive was built on a fragile compromise that Sakaja has now formally dissolved.

By taking shelter inside Chungwa House, Sakaja settles the uncertainty that has shadowed his administration’s legislative operations. Yet the move also binds his municipal agenda entirely to ODM’s party machinery, shifting the burden onto UDA to decide whether it will mount a fresh bid to reclaim the capital or recalibrate its urban strategy ahead of the 2027 vote.
