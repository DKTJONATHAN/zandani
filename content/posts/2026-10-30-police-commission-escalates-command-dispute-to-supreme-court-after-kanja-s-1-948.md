The jurisdictional battle over who controls the career progression and hiring of Kenya’s police service reached the Supreme Court on Friday, as the civilian oversight commission mounted an apex challenge against the expanding administrative reach of the Inspector General.

The National Police Service Commission (NPSC) lodged a formal Notice of Appeal on October 30, seeking to overturn an appellate ruling that stripped its authority over the recruitment, discipline, and promotion of uniformed personnel. The move effectively asks the country's highest court to arbitrate a direct institutional impasse between independent command and statutory civilian oversight.

The friction spilled into operational reality less than two weeks ago. On October 19—just three days after the Court of Appeal upheld curbs on the commission—Inspector General Douglas Kanja bypassed the agency to approve the elevation of 1,948 police officers who had completed mandatory advancement courses. That swift exercise of executive prerogative underscored the high stakes of the dispute, consolidating human resource management directly inside Jogoo House.

### The Thirteen-Month Judicial Impasse

![Inspector General of Police Douglas Kanja marches during a full dress rehearsal of the police recruits pass-out parade at the NPS Training College in Kiganjo, August 25, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/ig-douglas-kanja-and-police-recruits.jpg.webp?itok=DGTLJvXL)

The dispute centers on the boundary between Articles 245 and 246 of the 2010 Constitution. Article 245 vests the Inspector General with independent operational command over the service, while Article 246 establishes the NPSC to handle recruitment, appointments, promotions, and disciplinary control.

That balance fractured in September 2025 when the NPSC published regulations governing the intake of police constables and advertised countrywide vacancies. The move prompted legal challenges questioning whether the commission possessed the sole legal mandate to structure service entries and career ranks without the concurrence of the uniformed command.

On October 30, 2025, the Employment and Labour Relations Court declared the commission's recruitment framework unconstitutional, sharply curbing its oversight over uniformed officers. The Law Society of Kenya joined the NPSC in appealing that determination, arguing that subordinating the commission would hollow out civilian oversight established to prevent historic abuses inside the security apparatus.

However, on October 16, 2026, the Court of Appeal dismissed the consolidated petitions, cementing the lower court's finding that the Inspector General’s command structure retains operational primacy over internal personnel deployment.

### Clash of Mandates

Announcing the Supreme Court petition, NPSC Chairperson Amani Yuda Komora maintained that the commission is not attempting to micro-manage security operations, but rather seeking legal finality on where civilian administrative governance ends and operational command begins.

"The Commission has since filed a Notice of Appeal to obtain that clarification in the public interest," Komora said in a statement on Friday. The commission argued that leaving the appellate judgment intact permanently destabilizes the statutory architecture that separates operational policing from personnel administration.

While Kanja utilized the appellate clearance to unfreeze stalled departmental elevations for the 1,948 officers, nationwide recruitment remains paralyzed. The nullification of the 2025 intake regulations means thousands of prospective constables remain in administrative limbo, unable to enter training colleges until the courts establish which office holds the legal authority to sign their appointment letters.

With the Notice of Appeal now lodged, the dispute shifts to the Supreme Court registry. The seven-judge bench will have to determine whether the 2010 charter intended for the civilian commission to hold final veto power over human resource decisions, or whether operational command inherently vests the Inspector General with autonomous authority over the ranks.
