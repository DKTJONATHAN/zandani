The Kenya Airports Authority has directed that passengers must present national identification and sign clearance paperwork before reclaiming property left on aircraft, moving to formalise lost-and-found procedures following a viral terminal altercation at Jomo Kenyatta International Airport.

### What we know

* Aviation regulators are backing airline ground crews who enforce strict identity checks, transforming routine carrier liability procedures into an explicit, hub-wide security directive.
* The confrontation underscores persistent friction between harried travelers expecting immediate customer service and ground handlers strictly bound by item chain-of-custody rules.
* By clarifying the agent involved was airline personnel, the state authority protected its own workforce while endorsing the contested security requirement.
* Chronic gaps in passenger awareness at terminal arrival halls continue to turn straightforward lost-property collections into avoidable confrontations.

The directive, issued Tuesday, responds directly to video footage circulating across digital platforms showing an agitated passenger demanding the immediate return of a mobile phone reportedly left inside a cabin. In the recording, a staff member refused to release the device until the owner produced an original identification card and signed official property release logs.

KAA affirmed that the worker’s demand aligns with necessary aviation asset-protection standards designed to deter theft and misallocation.

"For security and accountability, passengers collecting items left onboard aircraft may be required to provide identification and complete the relevant documentation before an item can be released. These procedures are intended to ensure that recovered property is handed over to its rightful owner," the authority said in a statement.

The agency sought to insulate its own staff from the public backlash, pointing out that the confrontation involved airline ground personnel rather than airport authority employees.

"We wish to clarify that the interaction captured in the video involved airline personnel and not KAA staff. However, KAA recognises that passengers experience the airport as one journey and, together with our aviation partners, we remain committed to ensuring that such interactions are handled professionally," the statement added.

The authority stated it has opened discussions with the airline to refine frontline communication and improve public education around the recovery of misplaced baggage and personal effects.

While verifying identity before releasing personal property remains an indisputable security imperative, airport agencies often struggle to communicate routine compliance calmly. Enforcing rigid paperwork at arrival gates requires tact, and until terminal handlers master de-escalation alongside verification, routine compliance will continue to degenerate into public spectacles.
