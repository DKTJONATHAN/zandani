Gospel minister Mercy Masika has pushed back against social media critics questioning the focus of her upcoming marital programme, asserting that women hold the primary emotional leverage required to shape domestic outcomes.

Appearing on the *Family Dynamics* podcast hosted by Kerry Kagiri, Masika addressed online pushback triggered by the announcement of her 'Becoming a Wife' masterclass. Following her initial promotional posts, several female commentators responded by suggesting that relationship seminars and marital coaching should be directed toward men instead of wives and prospective brides.

Masika rejected that stance during the interview, arguing that shifting accountability outward misses a fundamental reality of marital dynamics.

"Most women think their husbands are the problem, but one of the things I realise is we have emotional intelligence more than our men," Masika said.

![Mercy Masika // Faceboook](https://i.ibb.co/S4ggHC4J/67d78bb8e038.webp)

Rather than viewing relationship maintenance as a zero-sum debate over who requires instruction, the gospel singer framed domestic stability around female perception. Citing scriptural principles on homebuilding, she argued that women possess an inherent capacity to read situations and manage tension without resorting to blame or public confrontation.

"That's why the Bible doesn't say a wise man builds, it's a wise woman, why, because we can see through things, we have emotional intelligence more than our men," she stated.

She urged women to abandon combative approaches when navigating domestic friction, taking the position that patient diplomacy yields far better long-term results than insistence on equal conflict.

"You win with wisdom, you win with how you being respectful, it always works," Masika explained.

Her podcast reflections serve as the foundation for the 'Becoming a Wife' masterclass, which is scheduled to officially launch on 21 November. Designed to accommodate both local participants and an international audience, the programme's format comprises three physical sessions alongside four online classes.

The masterclass will bring in high-profile guest facilitators, including Bishop Kathy Kiuna and Dr Sue, with sessions slated to cover a broad spectrum of topics ranging from emotional maturity to physical intimacy.

Beyond direct communication between spouses, Masika also addressed the broader structural pressures that affect modern Kenyan households. She placed specific emphasis on setting firm operational boundaries with extended family networks to safeguard the nuclear unit.

"If you let your other family rule over your family, you will not have a family," she said, warning that unmonitored external interference frequently destroys marital foundations.

Masika concluded the sit-down by advising couples to guard their homes against anxiety by prioritizing their immediate domestic environment and seeking internal contentment as the masterclass sessions draw near.
