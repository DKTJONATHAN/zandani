The Nairobi Expressway is set to offload its high-speed traffic onto the already congested Waiyaki Way this weekend, as the operator begins a 40-hour maintenance cycle on the elevated overpass.

Starting Saturday, September 19, at 8 pm, the elevated deck running from the James Gichuru Junction toward the Central Business District will be closed to all traffic. The closure, which is scheduled to last until Monday at noon, effectively forces motorists who typically bypass surface-level bottlenecks to rejoin the city's traditional, high-volume grid.

For commuters, the diversion represents more than a simple detour; it marks a return to the traffic conditions that characterized the Westlands corridor before the expressway's completion. The shift is particularly sensitive given the recent history of congestion in the area. Just ten days ago, a similar closure at the ABC Place-Red Hill Road junction triggered significant delays, with motorists reporting prolonged gridlock as vehicles from the expressway were funneled into the lower lanes.

MOJA Expressway Company, the private firm managing the toll road, maintains that the Westlands entrance will remain open, though the utility of this access point is diminished for those whose primary destination is the CBD. Drivers exiting the expressway toward the overpass are expected to see operations normalize by 5 pm on Saturday, but those heading into the city center will have no choice but to navigate Waiyaki Way.

While the company has deployed traffic marshals to manage the flow of diverted vehicles, the reliance on the underlying road network highlights the limited redundancy in Nairobi’s current transit infrastructure. When the expressway’s elevated deck requires attention, the city’s surface-level roads-already operating near capacity-are forced to absorb the entirety of the traffic volume.

Motorists are being advised to anticipate longer transit times throughout the weekend. The recurring nature of these maintenance windows suggests that until the underlying surface roads undergo their own capacity upgrades, the expressway’s operational updates will continue to serve as a bellwether for wider traffic instability in the Westlands-CBD corridor.
