A strict statutory timer built into Kenya’s public-private partnership laws has shut down an attempt to force the state road agency to expose its financial modeling and risk-sharing deals for the Ksh200 billion Rironi-Mau Summit highway project.

By throwing out a challenge by the Consumers Federation of Kenya (COFEK) on procedural grounds, the Public Private Partnership (PPP) Petition Committee effectively validated the road's planned toll structure without compelling the government to publish underlying project documents.

The ruling hinges on a tight statutory limit: the Public Private Partnerships Act mandates that any administrative challenge to a PPP project decision must be submitted within seven days.

### The Procedural Bar

In its determination, the tribunal found that COFEK missed the seven-day deadline to challenge the project's documentation. The committee noted that the lobby failed to state when the Kenya National Highways Authority (KeNHA) had released its project disclosure document.

![A model of the Rironi-Mau Summit highway](https://i.ibb.co/yx6FV8n/e416ab165345.webp)

However, the tribunal established that the document was in existence prior to September 2, 2025—the date when formal stakeholder consultations began. Because the lobby filed its challenge long after that timeline, the committee ruled the petition time-barred and struck it out.

The procedural dismissal closes an administrative avenue that sought to unseal core commercial details of the 30-year concession.

### Unanswered Transparency Demands

COFEK’s petition had sought an order compelling KeNHA to produce critical project records for scrutiny. The lobby requested access to:

* Complete tolling plans and tariff calculation methodologies.
* Comprehensive feasibility studies and procurement records.
* Environmental and public participation reports.
* Official documentation detailing the allocation of financial risks between the state and private concessionaires.

The consumer group argued that KeNHA’s public disclosures provided insufficient justification for the toll tariff structure and failed to demonstrate the project's long-term affordability for motorists. It also raised questions about whether viable, un-tolled alternative routes would remain fully accessible for drivers who choose not to pay.

Because the committee threw out the petition on statutory time limits, it did not examine the merits of those disclosure requests or determine whether KeNHA complied with the broader transparency mandates of the PPP Act.

### Concession Framework and Toll Structure

The ruling solidifies the operational roadmap announced by KeNHA in its June 23, 2026 disclosure notice, which established that the major transport artery will operate as a fee-paying corridor under a 30-year PPP arrangement.

Under the framework, motorists travelling along both the main Rironi-Naivasha-Gilgil route and the alternative Rironi-Mai Mahiu-Naivasha alignment will pay distance-based charges:

* **Rironi to Naivasha:** Ksh8 per kilometre.
* **Naivasha to Mau Summit:** Ksh8.50 per kilometre.

The Ksh200 billion corridor expansion is split into two construction phases under separate Chinese contractors. China Road and Bridge Corporation (CRBC) is executing the first phase, expanding the existing two-lane road into a four-lane dual carriageway between Rironi and Naivasha. Shandong Hi-Speed Road and Bridge International Engineering is handling the section extending from Gilgil to Mau Summit, which will be expanded to six lanes.

With the legal challenge dismissed on procedural grounds, the statutory precedent highlights the tight operational windows civil society groups face when contesting major infrastructure concessions, leaving KeNHA free to move forward under its existing tariff framework.
