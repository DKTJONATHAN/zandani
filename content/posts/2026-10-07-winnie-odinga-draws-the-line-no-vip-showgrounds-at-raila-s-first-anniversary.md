East African Legislative Assembly MP Winnie Odinga has drawn a hard line against political grandstanding as preparations peak for the first anniversary of her father’s death.

Speaking on Wednesday during the handover and dedication of the Kang’o Ka Jaramogi mausoleum in Bondo, Siaya County, Winnie issued a direct warning to political camps planning to attend the upcoming October 15 memorial at Migwena Cultural and Sports Ground.

“Please let October 15 be a day of remembrance and celebration,” Winnie said during the ceremony. “It should not become a political showground, a competition over who owns the crowd, who receives the loudest cheers, or who can embarrass another guest.”

The appeal follows the formal dedication of the family mausoleum in a restricted ceremony attended by President William Ruto, members of the Odinga family, and invited guests. The site, which houses the final resting places of both Jaramogi Oginga Odinga and Raila Odinga, is slated to open for public visits ahead of the broader memorial.

While politicians from various divides have been invited to pay their respects, Winnie emphasized that the family expects decorum rather than political posturing. She noted that guests would not be required to abandon their political convictions at the gate, but insisted the event must remain a commemoration of Raila’s legacy rather than a proxy battleground for rival political factions.

Shifting focus away from the high table, Winnie made a specific appeal for the inclusion of ordinary Kenyans who walked alongside the veteran opposition leader during his decades-long political career.

“Many of Baba’s people had no titles,” she noted. “They had no escorts, and they had no access to the high table. They stood in the rain. They travelled far. They prayed for him.”

She urged those grassroots supporters to bring their songs, dances, poems, and personal memories to the Migwena grounds, stating that the family wants the people who truly filled her father's life to anchor the day.

October 15, 2026, marks exactly one year since Raila died at the age of 80 while receiving medical treatment in India. His burial on October 19, 2025, at Bondo drew thousands of mourners from across East Africa. The upcoming gathering is expected to bring together family, political allies, and the public starting at 8 am, with the National Museums of Kenya overseeing the preservation of the mausoleum as national heritage.
