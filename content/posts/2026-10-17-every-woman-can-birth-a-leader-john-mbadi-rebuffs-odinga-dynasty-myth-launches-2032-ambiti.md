John Mbadi has pulled down the curtain on political reverence in Luo Nyanza, choosing a funeral gathering in Homa Bay to declare that devotion to the late Raila Odinga ended with the man himself—and cannot be passed down like family heirlooms.

Speaking on Friday, October 16, 2026, the National Treasury Cabinet Secretary directly challenged the long-held assumption that the Odinga household holds a hereditary mortgage over the region’s ballot boxes. Rather than dancing around traditional deference, Mbadi made it clear that nobody is inheriting regional loyalty on family credentials alone.

“I want to tell you this, we followed the late Raila Odinga because of the stand he had and the sacrifices he did for us as a nation,” Mbadi told mourners. “The love we had for Raila cannot be transferred to somebody else, unless you convince us that you match that respect and the honour we had for him.”

### The Bondo Absentee Roll

Mbadi’s blunt podium outing arrived barely twenty-four hours after a telling boycott rattled the region. On Thursday, October 15, crowds gathered at Migwena Grounds in Bondo, Siaya County, for the first anniversary of Raila’s death. 

Yet the memorial exposed a widening chasm between the Odinga family—led by Raila’s widow, Ida Odinga—and the senior figures now anchored inside President William Ruto’s broad-based administration. 

Mbadi stayed away. So did Homa Bay Governor Gladys Wanga, Mombasa Governor Abdulswamad Nassir, and Mining Cabinet Secretary Hassan Joho. Of the senior leaders allied to the broad-based government, only Energy Cabinet Secretary Opiyo Wandayi and Kisii Governor Simba Arati turned up in Bondo.

The ground at Migwena was instead dominated by Linda Mwananchi, the faction of Orange Democratic Movement politicians firmly set against cooperating with the Ruto administration. Government insiders quickly linked the memorial’s platform to the Linda Mwananchi camp, signaling that the late patriarch’s household had chosen its side of the factional divide.

### Breaking the Hereditary Lock

Mbadi seized on that brewing tension to demolish the notion of regional royalty. Taking aim at the idea that top leadership belongs to specific genealogies, he delivered a pointed rebuttal to dynastic exclusivity.

“There are people who think that they are the only ones who can give birth to leaders. Every woman has the capacity to bring forth leaders,” Mbadi stated. “That is why I have problems with those who think the president should only come from their areas. Even here in the lake region, we are capable of producing a president.”

Having discarded the notion of waiting for an anointed blessing, Mbadi took an audacious leap: he announced that he is running for State House in 2032.

“Don’t be lied to that you can remove some Luos from President William Ruto; you are wasting your time,” he argued, framing Ruto’s lake region development footprint as the transactional anchor for 2027. “Luos have already decided that, per what the president is currently doing for them, they are going to re-elect him for his second term, and when they are done with him, they will produce their own as president in 2032, and Mbadi is on the front line, I want to be president.”

### Demanding Sides in 2027

Mbadi's double-barreled declaration serves a dual purpose: carving out his own political autonomy while forcing hesitant colleagues off the fence ahead of the next general election. 

He insisted that the transition era leaves no room for neutrality, daring regional figures to stop flirting with multiple camps.

“In politics, there is no sitting on the fence,” Mbadi said. “If you belong to ‘two-term’, be frank and declare it, and if in ‘one-term’, also be brave enough to declare it.”

By laying out his personal 2032 presidential ambition while shutting the door on hereditary political estates, Mbadi has signaled that the post-Raila era in Nyanza will not be managed by proxy or past pedigree. The contest for regional command, once sealed behind family doors, is now being fought on open podiums.
