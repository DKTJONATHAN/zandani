Eyewitness accounts and preliminary incident reports from Makindu confirm that four people died on Sunday after two commercial trucks collided head-on and burst into flames at Kiunduani along the Nairobi-Mombasa Highway.

The early afternoon collision trapped occupants inside the cabins before an intense fire engulfed both vehicles, leaving two truck drivers and a pregnant female passenger dead from severe burns. The resulting inferno closed the main lanes of Kenya’s primary logistics corridor, forcing long-distance haulage trucks and public service vehicles to bypass the burning wreckage via the unpaved road shoulder.

The fatal crash adds to a catastrophic pattern across the 461-kilometre highway, where single-carriageway sections continue to expose commercial and passenger transport to catastrophic head-on impacts while multi-billion-shilling infrastructure interventions navigate bureaucratic reviews.

### Unchecked Flames and Roadside Friction

![A collage showing the aftermath of the accident at Kinunduani, along Mombasa Road on Sunday, November 8.](https://i.ibb.co/Z6DsVygj/6aac5dc33ce7.webp)

Initial footage and witness accounts from the Kiunduani stretch captured bystanders attempting to suppress the spreading flames without the support of visible emergency fire engines or specialized recovery personnel in the immediate aftermath of the crash. The lack of on-site containment allowed the blaze to consume the cabin structures entirely, stalling transit movement across both directions.

While traffic marshals eventually routed vehicles onto the highway shoulder to ease building snarl-ups, the operational disruption reverberated along the lower Eastern transit link, where heavy freight operations between the Port of Mombasa and the hinterland rely almost entirely on single-lane overtaking windows.

Sunday’s fatalities raise the death toll along the corridor to more than 40 people since the beginning of October alone. The stretch has become a focal point in a 9.2 per cent nationwide surge in road fatalities documented by the National Transport and Safety Authority (NTSA).

The Kiunduani crash closely mirrors a series of recent mass-casualty events on the same corridor: the October 25 disaster at Kanga near Mtito Andei that killed 22 people, the October 3 Salama collision that claimed 17 lives, and earlier fatal wrecks at Taru and Kapiti Plains that killed two and five people, respectively.

![Flames after two trains collided at Kiunduani, along Mombasa Road, on Sunday, November 8.](https://i.ibb.co/KjnNzb8G/377b73b43499.webp)

### The Ksh300B Procurement Lag

While freight operators and transit passengers bear the immediate physical danger of undivided road surfaces, the state’s structural remedy remains locked in administrative processes. The government recently abandoned proposals to build an independent, greenfield expressway, opting instead to widen the existing 461-kilometre highway into a four-lane dual carriageway.

The project, carrying an estimated price tag of Ksh300 billion, is designed to eliminate head-on risk through grade separation, controlled access ramps, dedicated interchanges, and tolling infrastructure. 

However, delivery timelines remain contingent on commercial talks. The National Treasury is currently evaluating private sector bids, while the Kenya National Highways Authority (KeNHA) continues structured negotiations with local financiers to assemble the capital required for construction.

Until those contracts reach financial close and earthworks begin separating opposing lanes of heavy haulage, motorists navigating the Kiunduani corridor remain dependent on narrow two-lane pavement that leaves zero margin for mechanical error or driver miscalculation.
