Cleophas Malala has drawn a firm line against joining Kalonzo Musyoka’s newly minted Ukombozi Alliance, laying out a distinct counter-proposal that prioritizes a separate partnership with Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna over early umbrella unity.

Speaking at the Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP) headquarters in Lavington on September 22, 2026, the party deputy leader firmly shut down suggestions that DCP would melt into any reincarnated opposition coalition structure. Instead, Malala outlined a multi-stage strategy: building an independent front alongside Sifuna’s Linda Mwananchi movement first, before approaching Musyoka’s outfit as a standalone negotiating power.

"We as DCP will not join the Ukombozi Alliance," Malala stated, rejecting any association with the coalition or its Azimio predecessor. "Let them join there, and then we and Sifuna unite, and then we negotiate with them."

The refusal directly challenges the timetable set by Musyoka, who unveiled the Ukombozi Alliance on September 21, 2026, at the SKM Command Centre in Karen. Positioning the vehicle as the official successor to former President Uhuru Kenyatta’s Azimio la Umoja-One Kenya coalition, Musyoka framed Ukombozi—Swahili for liberation—as an open platform designed to rally Eugene Wamalwa’s DAP-K and Rigathi Gachagua’s DCP around a single presidential candidate against President William Ruto in 2027.

Reinforcing Malala’s stance on September 28, 2026, DCP Advisory Council Chairperson Maina Kamanda made it clear that party structures would take precedence over coalition sign-offs. Kamanda insisted that DCP must conduct a comprehensive self-assessment and build grassroots machinery across all 47 counties before making any formal commitments.

According to Kamanda, former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua has already consolidated substantial political ground, unifying the Mount Kenya electorate under the DCP banner while extending operational reaches into the Maa and Kipsigis communities. Kamanda calculated that this grassroots footprint gives the newly formed party the organizational leverage to target over eight million votes independently, removing any immediate pressure to seek cover under Musyoka’s canopy.

"Gachagua has brought together many Mount Kenya voters under DCP," Kamanda said during his briefing. "So, telling us to join some alliance will first require us to do our research and self-assessment."

Despite holding back on structural union, Kamanda outlined a very specific hierarchy for ongoing political dialogue. He explicitly listed only four leaders whom DCP recognizes as carrying genuine weight in the contemporary opposition landscape: Gachagua, Sifuna, Wamalwa, and Musyoka himself.

By ring-fencing those four figures while demanding an independent bilateral pact with Sifuna, DCP’s leadership has signalled that any opposition coalition built for 2027 will have to be negotiated between co-equal blocs rather than dictated from Karen.
