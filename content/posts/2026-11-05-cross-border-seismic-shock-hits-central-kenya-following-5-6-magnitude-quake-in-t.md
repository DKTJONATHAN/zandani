Seismic vibrations triggered by a magnitude 5.6 earthquake near the Lake Victoria basin in northern Tanzania propagated across a 986-kilometer expanse within five minutes on Thursday afternoon, jarring high-rises and residential neighborhoods in Kenya’s capital and surrounding central counties.

Independent real-time monitoring by the European-Mediterranean Seismological Centre (EMSC) confirmed that the tectonic rupture occurred in Tanzania at approximately 12:22 pm East African Time. The earthquake’s epicenter was pin-pointed 17 kilometers east of Ushirombo, a township located south-west of Lake Victoria.

Despite the substantial geographic distance from the source, seismic energy moved rapidly north and north-east, reaching the Nairobi metropolitan region and adjoining counties by 12:27 pm.

### Spatial Reach Across Central Counties

The physical tremors were registered across a continuous regional belt encompassing Nairobi, Murang’a, Kiambu, and Kajiado counties. In high-density commercial districts and elevated office structures within Nairobi, occupants experienced brief shaking of floor plates and office fixtures.

The five-minute interval between the origin shock in Ushirombo and the arrival of vibrations in central Kenya highlights the rapid propagation of low-frequency seismic waves through the regional crust. EMSC data categorized the event as a moderate earthquake, yet its broad geographic footprint underscored the structural connectivity of fault networks across the East African Rift system and Lake Victoria basin.

### Verified Seismic Data

![An aerial view of Nairobi City](https://i.ibb.co/F4ws36q5/b252c39852d5.webp)

Data published by seismic detection networks detailed the exact operational sequence of the tremor:

- **12:22 pm EAT:** Primary rupture registered 17 kilometers east of Ushirombo, Tanzania, at magnitude 5.6.
- **12:27 pm EAT:** Vibrations and technical alerts recorded across Kenyan monitoring locations.
- **Distance Covered:** Waves traveled approximately 986 kilometers from the epicenter to Nairobi and adjacent counties.
- **Affected Footprint:** Surface tremors recorded across four major Kenyan administrative regions including Murang’a, Kiambu, Nairobi, and Kajiado.

No immediate structural collapse or critical utility disruption was reported along the northern extremity of the wave path in Kenya. However, the event prompted swift administrative checks across commercial buildings in Nairobi and Kiambu to verify building integrity.

As global seismological desks analyze subsurface depth and fault-slip mechanics for the Ushirombo quake, regional monitoring stations continue to observe telemetry across the Lake Victoria basin for any secondary settling activity.
