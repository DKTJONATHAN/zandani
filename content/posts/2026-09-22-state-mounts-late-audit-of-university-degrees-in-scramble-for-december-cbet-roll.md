Kenyan universities face a high-stakes curriculum and institutional inspection barely three months before the government begins piloting competency-based degrees in higher education, as the state moves to uncover how far behind institutions are in overhauling their programmes.

In twin procurement notices issued on Tuesday, September 22, the State Department for Higher Education invited bids from independent consultants to inspect degree programmes across public and private universities, while simultaneously assessing whether individual campuses possess the faculty and practical infrastructure required for Competency-Based Education and Training (CBET).

The sudden procurement drive reflects a compressed calendar for the Ministry of Education. The government has scheduled the inaugural university-level competency rollout for December 2026, ahead of an intended system-wide transition by 2029. Yet, with the first intake looming, state officials still lack comprehensive baseline data on whether existing bachelor's degrees meet competency standards or whether lecturers are equipped to teach them.

### Dissecting Academic Degrees

![Beatrice Muganda Inyangala](https://i.ibb.co/DHX1zJ1N/a15c9d456524.webp)

The first tender tasks external experts with a granular review of current academic offerings across all accredited universities. The appointed firm must determine whether existing degree programs are designed around practical problem-solving and industry-demanded abilities rather than passive, lecture-heavy memorisation.

Consultants will inspect stated learning outcomes against workplace competencies, verify whether practical skills are formally integrated into course outlines, and propose structural revisions to drag non-compliant curricula into alignment with the competency architecture. The findings are expected to serve as a blueprint for universities forced to redesign, merge, or scrap courses that fail to deliver demonstrable market skills.

### Gauging Campus Readiness

The companion audit focuses on institutional capacity—a point of longstanding friction between university administrations and education planners. Under this brief, the consultant will survey both public and private universities to measure the true extent of CBET adoption to date.

![cs-ogamba.jpg](https://i.ibb.co/FLD6J4zs/d3687e3f2f32.webp)

Crucially, the assessment will investigate two chronic bottlenecks: staff preparation and physical facilities. Evaluators will measure how many university lecturers have received formal retraining in competency delivery and assessment, alongside an audit of specialized workshops, laboratories, and technology assets available on each campus. Without adequate physical infrastructure, universities cannot realistically execute practical, skill-centered instruction.

### Compressed Timeline to 2029

Prospective consulting firms have a narrow window to submit bids, with the procurement process closing on October 6. Bidders must meet baseline statutory criteria, including valid tax compliance certificates from the Kenya Revenue Authority and formal corporate incorporation papers.

Once contracted, the consultants will enter an exceptionally narrow evaluation window to review scores of public and private institutions before the December launch date. While the full institutional rollout is anchored to 2029—when the pioneer cohort of the Competency-Based Curriculum progresses into tertiary institutions—the December 2026 pilot will immediately expose which universities have restructured their faculties, and which remain bound to traditional instructional models.
