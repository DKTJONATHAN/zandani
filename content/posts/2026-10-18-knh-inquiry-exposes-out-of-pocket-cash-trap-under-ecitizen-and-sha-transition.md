A formal management inquiry opened at Kenyatta National Hospital on Sunday has turned an admitted patient’s viral testimony into an institutional test of how public referral care handles digitised revenue collection and statutory insurance.

The referral hospital confirmed on October 18, 2026, that it had initiated an internal review to verify claims made by an inpatient who documented weeks of stalled care, outsourced diagnostics, and mounting out-of-pocket charges that culminated in a rejected Social Health Authority (SHA) claim upon discharge.

While KNH described the step as a fact-finding exercise and invited the patient or her family for direct dialogue, the grievance outlines specific transactional failure points across the facility’s clinical wards and finance desks.

### Disputed Scans and Outsourced Prescriptions

According to the patient’s public account, multi-week admission at the national facility failed to guarantee essential clinical investigations even after fees were deducted or settled in cash.

![KNH](https://i.ibb.co/B599scbg/9f525f204963.webp)

The patient reported paying Ksh4,600 for an ultrasound after its listed charge jumped from an initial quote of Ksh1,500, yet the resulting diagnostic report was never delivered to inform her ward management. Similar gaps emerged around specialised gastroenterology procedures; she incurred an additional Ksh45,000 for a colonoscopy and endoscopy, only to be left without confirmed clinical findings or verifiable follow-through.

Ward routines broke down further when secondary complications surfaced. Following medical observations of an enlarged heart, doctors recommended an emergency chest X-ray. Despite her family processing a Ksh3,500 deduction through insurance approvals, the scan was never conducted, leaving the patient to navigate outsourced private imaging centres and purchase basic pharmaceuticals externally while occupying an inpatient bed.

### The eCitizen and SHA Clearance Breakdown

Beyond diagnostic delays, the patient's case highlights deep friction in how statutory health coverage interfaces with the national government's unified digital payment gateway.

All hospital-related payments were routed through eCitizen paybills rather than directly credited ledger accounts under KNH’s name, compounding accounting opacity for the patient. The final financial hurdle occurred at clearance: the hospital reportedly declined her SHA cover at the exit desk, forcing an emergency out-of-pocket settlement of Ksh50,000 in cash before release.

![An undated image of the dyalisis section of the Murang'a Level 5 Hospital](https://i.ibb.co/k6x3BTsy/4941c4049ef8.webp)

She was ultimately discharged with instructions to attend an outpatient follow-up clinic, despite her protests that the underlying pathology that prompted admission had not been systematically investigated or resolved.

### Referral Oversight Under Scrutiny

In its formal communication on Sunday, KNH acknowledged the circulating footage and pledged administrative accountability, though it stopped short of addressing the specific billing entries or procedural backlogs.

“The Hospital takes these concerns seriously and has initiated investigations to establish the facts and determine any appropriate action,” the facility stated, adding that its executive team is prepared to meet the complainant to resolve the disputed ledger.

The inquiry places KNH under acute public pressure at a moment when Kenya’s broader shift from legacy insurance to SHA frameworks has faced persistent operational scrutiny. For thousands of lower- and middle-income households relying on the country's main public referral hub, the episode exposes the precarious reality of public healthcare access: where platform billing glitches, missing laboratory results, and eleventh-hour insurer rejections can abruptly shift the full burden of specialized hospitalisation onto private pockets.

KNH has not yet established a timeline for concluding the internal audit or clarifying whether patients facing administrative service deficits are eligible for institutional refunds.
