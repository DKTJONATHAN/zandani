Rigathi Gachagua has trained his sights on the grand opening of the 60,000-capacity Talanta Stadium, questioning why the state is rolling out the red carpet for European football royalty while local teams watch from the sidelines.

Speaking on Wednesday, October 28, 2026, the former Deputy President took issue with President William Ruto’s announcement that German giants Bayern Munich and Dutch powerhouse Ajax will headline the official launch of the facility—also named the Raila Odinga International Stadium—on January 5, 2027.

For Gachagua, the decision to feature foreign clubs in the curtain-raiser for a project bankrolled by domestic citizens strikes a discordant chord with national pride.

“How can a stadium that will cost Ksh145 billion be paid for by the taxpayers, the people of Kenya, and the first game is played by foreigners?” Gachagua posed during an interview. “Don’t we have teams in Kenya?”

The government has framed the high-profile European friendly as a strategic marketing milestone to showcase Kenya’s sports infrastructure ahead of the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations, which the country is co-hosting alongside Uganda and Tanzania under a self-sustaining corporate model.

However, Gachagua maintained that an iconic national asset financed by public coffers demands a local heartbeat on opening day. He pointed to domestic heavyweights Gor Mahia and AFC Leopards, or the national side Harambee Stars, as natural choices to break in the pitch.

“Why couldn’t you bring one team to play against Harambee Stars? At least we would have some local component,” Gachagua said. “What happened to our patriotism? Our identity as a nation? Sixty-two years after independence, the first match in a stadium in Kenya features foreign teams.”

Beyond the team selection, Gachagua also looped the stadium's massive financial scale back into the conversation, pointing to prior audit queries regarding procurement and expenditures.

The clash underscores an unfolding tug-of-war between international commercial ambitions and domestic cultural identity as Nairobi counts down to the 2027 continental showcase.
