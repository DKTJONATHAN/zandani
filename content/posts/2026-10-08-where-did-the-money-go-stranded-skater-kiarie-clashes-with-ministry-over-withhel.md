A diplomatic scramble to fly skater Kelvin Kiarie to Paraguay has escalated into a public confrontation over state sports financing, with the continental champion accusing the Ministry of Youth Affairs, Creative Economy and Sports of withholding approved competition funds while he turned to harambees to finance his own national duty.

Kiarie remains stuck in Nairobi nearly a week after the World Skate Games began on October 2, having already missed his opening competitive events in Asunción. While the sports ministry presented the standoff as a routine visa hiccup caused by the athlete's itinerary, Kiarie pushed back forcefully, accusing Cabinet Secretary Salim Mvurya of fabricating claims of state backing.

At the center of the administrative logjam is a missing transit visa for Panama, a country with no resident diplomatic mission in Nairobi. Kiarie had independently booked a multi-leg flight path routed through Panama City to reach Paraguay, leaving Kenya’s Ministry of Foreign and Diaspora Affairs attempting to negotiate clearance through non-resident diplomatic channels days after his scheduled departure.

### The Funding Contradiction

![Skater Kelvin Kiariu displaying his gold medal after winning the Benin International Skate Challenge in April 1.](https://i.ibb.co/4wW8CsFR/68a3888e0945.webp)

In a formal briefing issued on October 8, the Sports Ministry defended its record, asserting that Kiarie had received sustained state backing across his international assignments, including the Africa Skating Championships in Egypt, and remained an active beneficiary of government cash rewards for medal-winning performances.

Kiarie rejected that narrative within hours, characterizing public statements by CS Mvurya as baseless.

"I have noted a social media post from CS Sports Salim Mvurya claiming to have supported me, another lie," Kiarie said, disclosing that he has not seen a single shilling from official state reward schemes despite securing podium finishes across the continent.

According to Kiarie, competition prize monies won on international circuits are traditionally routed through the Ministry of Sports for administrative processing before disbursement. That pipeline, he argues, has yielded nothing.

"Money was approved, but I have never received a coin. Where is the money going?" Kiarie said. "And in my case, you did not even have to give me the funds. Recognising my wins from Benin and other wins could have been enough; everything falls into place after recognition. I have never been supported in any of the competitions by the Ministry of Sports, not even the African Championship."

### Self-Funded National Duty

![Sports CS Salim Mvurya meets with his Ugandan and Tanzanian counterparts and CAF representatives to review AFCON 2027 preparations, August 25, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/nq3nBVfk/19c5ba58e69c.webp)

The financial dispute casts a sharp light on the operational realities facing non-traditional sports in Kenya. To secure his ticket and lodging for the Paraguay championship, which runs through October 18, Kiarie relied on personal savings and private fundraising appeals after official paperwork stalled.

The ministry has countered by pointing out procedural missteps in Kiarie’s travel planning. Officials stated that because Kiarie arranged his own ticketing without pre-clearing the transit logistics through the state, he walked into a complex visa requirement that Kenyan consular authorities could not easily resolve overnight.

The ministry confirmed it had instructed Kiarie to ditch the Panamanian route entirely, purchase a fresh itinerary that circumvents Central America, and submit the new travel plans to government offices for potential intervention.

For an athlete who had to crowdfund his initial ticket, purchasing a second long-haul ticket to South America on short notice remains an insurmountable hurdle. Kiarie faulted senior officials, including the Director of Sports, noting that administrative inquiries yielded bureaucratic evasions while his competitive calendar slipped away.

With half the World Skate Games calendar already exhausted, Kiarie is now demanding a formal audit of sports ministry books to disclose exactly how much financial support was approved under his name for previous continental tournaments, and why those funds never reached his pocket.
