A mounting death toll along the Northern Corridor has shifted the government’s infrastructure timeline, forcing executive intervention to accelerate long-stalled negotiations for the dualling of the Nairobi-Mombasa Highway.

Speaking on Wednesday, November 4, at the Industrialisation Summit at the Kenyatta International Convention Centre (KICC) in Nairobi, President William Ruto confirmed that talks to widen the vital trade route are being expedited. The announcement follows intense public pressure from county leadership and motorists' associations demanding immediate safety interventions after a series of fatal accidents, including a crash at Salama in Makueni County that claimed the lives of 17 pilgrims, and an October 25 incident that left 22 passengers dead.

![ruto-nairobi-mombasa-highway.jpg](https://i.ibb.co/J42x4V7/9eb5f2db1a70.webp)

Makueni Governor Mutula Kilonzo Jr. had earlier pressed the Kenya National Highways Authority (KeNHA) to replicate the rigorous safety infrastructure seen at blackspots like Salgaa and Nithi Bridge along the treacherous Salama stretch. Simultaneously, the Motorists Association of Kenya accused authorities of sluggishness in addressing structural hazards on the two-lane highway, which handles the bulk of heavy commercial freight moving inland from the Port of Mombasa.

The political and public safety fallout has injected urgency into financing discussions that previously languished in bureaucratic assessments. Just a day prior to the President's remarks, KeNHA Director General Luka Kimeli held discussions with local financial institutions regarding funding models for the highway expansion. These talks build on a prior initiative by the National Treasury to procure a consultancy firm to evaluate traffic volumes, environmental impacts, engineering requirements, and project costs to convert the existing route into a four-lane dual carriageway.

![An artistic impression of the Nairobi-Mombasa expressway upon its completion.](https://i.ibb.co/4RLL0C5c/de5a8793909f.webp)

Beyond the immediate corridor, the administration aims to tie the highway overhaul to a broader 2,500-kilometer trunk road expansion strategy linking Mombasa, Nairobi, Mau Summit, Malaba, and Kisumu. Officials argue that opening these inland arteries is essential for lowering transport costs and supporting manufacturing growth, while complementary plans to expand the Port of Mombasa seek to ease regional trade bottlenecks.

Despite the renewed political commitment, specific timelines and financing structures for the Nairobi-Mombasa project remain unreleased, leaving motorists and regional leaders watching to see whether the accelerated negotiations will translate into physical groundworks before the next major travel surge.
