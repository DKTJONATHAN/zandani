Trans Nzoia Governor George Natembeya has waded straight into the public rift splitting the young opposition ranks, framing Embakasi East MP Babu Owino's recent maneuvers as a deliberate attempt to skip the 2027 presidential contest and shield his long-term ambitions for 2032.

Speaking during a radio interview on Sunday, November 22, 2026, Natembeya—a key principal supporting Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna’s 2027 State House bid—publicly shut down any residual claims that Owino belongs to the newly rebranded Linda Mwananchi Movement party. In doing so, the governor laid bare the power dynamics and tactical disagreements that broke apart the duo's post-ODM alliance.

At the heart of the feud is a contested power-sharing arrangement that surfaced publicly earlier in the month. During a television interview on November 13, 2026, Owino revealed details of private talks following his and Sifuna's exit from the Orange Democratic Movement (ODM). Owino claimed the two had struck an understanding that would see him register and head The Mwananchi Party as its party leader, while Sifuna would fly the outfit’s presidential flag in 2027.

Sifuna’s camp swiftly rejected that structure, questioning the logic of sponsoring a presidential candidate who exercises no executive control over the sponsoring political party. Sifuna’s allies subsequently took control of the Equitable Party, rebranding it into the Linda Mwananchi Movement party to secure an independent vehicle.

Natembeya dismissed Owino's proposed setup as politically suicidal, drawing a direct parallel to the current ruling administration.

“Babu is saying he sat with Sifuna, so maybe I was not there myself,” Natembeya said during the Sunday broadcast. “I can’t know what they discussed, but I can assure you, if Sifuna had come up with that proposal to the Linda Mwananchi team, we would not have agreed. Can you imagine someone telling Ruto that you are going to be the presidential candidate of UDA and not be the party leader?”

Beyond the mechanics of party governance, Natembeya explicitly separated Owino from the Linda Mwananchi party hierarchy, clarifying that the Embakasi East lawmaker remains outside their official party framework despite his high-profile opposition stance.

The Trans Nzoia governor attributed Owino’s reluctance to back Sifuna’s immediate campaign to calculated self-preservation and peer rivalry among young opposition leaders, arguing that Owino is deliberately avoiding a direct clash with President William Ruto in the upcoming election.

“Babu Owino is in the opposition, but he is not in Linda Mwananchi, because Linda Mwananchi is a political party,” Natembeya stated. “From his math, he doesn’t want to face the incumbent president. That’s why he’s pushing his ambition to 2032.”

That math, Natembeya argued, carries a significant strategic risk for Owino. Should Sifuna pull off a victory against Ruto in 2027, Sifuna would enter the 2032 cycle as the sitting head of state—effectively upending Owino’s projected timeline and altering the balance of power within their political generation.

With Owino holding onto his registered Mwananchi Party and Sifuna’s allies solidifying the Linda Mwananchi Movement party, the public fallout underscores how fast the post-ODM opposition bloc is fracturing into competing personal machinery ahead of the 2027 campaign trail.
