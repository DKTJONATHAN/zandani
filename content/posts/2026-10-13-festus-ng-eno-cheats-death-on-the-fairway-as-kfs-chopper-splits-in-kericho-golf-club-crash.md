Environment Principal Secretary Festus Ng’eno cheated death on Monday morning after a Kenya Forest Service helicopter carrying him and four others came crashing down during takeoff at the Kericho Golf Club.

What was meant to be a swift executive lift-off from the manicured fairways of the upscale club quickly unraveled into a chaotic scene on the turf. The aircraft, registered under tail number 5Y-FSK, suffered a catastrophic failure seconds into getting airborne, hitting the grassy grounds near Famous Hotel with enough force to rip the machine apart.

Photographs emerging from the grounds showed the sheer violence of the impact. The chopper's main cabin lay mangled on the grass, while the tail assembly was sheared clean off the fuselage, leaving debris scattered across the green.

### Rushed to Hospital from the Fairway

Despite the extensive structural destruction, Ng’eno and his four travel companions survived the violent landing. First responders and local security details pulled the five occupants from the battered wreckage before rushing them for urgent medical attention.

Kericho County Police Commander Kivindu Kilonzo confirmed the mid-morning incident, noting that the five individuals were admitted to a nearby medical facility in stable condition.

"All five individuals on board were evacuated to a local hospital in stable condition," Kilonzo confirmed, noting that emergency crews on the ground reported no fatalities from the crash site.

Within minutes of the impact, multi-agency security personnel moved in to secure the boundary between the golf grounds and the neighboring hotel, cordoning off the twisted metal to keep curious onlookers back and preserve the scene for technical assessors.

### Scrutiny on Official Chopper Flights

Ng’eno’s narrow survival comes at a particularly tense moment for VIP and regional aviation across the country, where recent crashes have put light aircraft and helicopter safety under intense public examination.

In August 2026, a tourist charter flight ended in complete tragedy after slamming into a rock face near Mount Ololokwe in Samburu County, catching fire and claiming the lives of all seven passengers and crew on board. Just weeks later, in September 2026, a Kenya School of Flying trainer craft went down in Kipeto, Kajiado West, killing an instructor and student pilot during a low-visibility morning flight.

Monday’s escape at Kericho Golf Club spares the state another grim casualty list, but it immediately raises serious questions about the mechanical reliability and maintenance schedules of government-operated rotary wings.

Accident investigation teams and civil aviation authorities have taken over the Kericho fairway to determine whether engine trouble, tail rotor failure, or erratic local winds caused 5Y-FSK to stall on departure.
