Kalonzo Musyoka did not wait for the projector screens to light up before staging one of the sharpest boardroom exits of the political season. 

Flanked by former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua and Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna, the Wiper Democratic Movement leader arrived at Nairobi’s consultative forum on electoral preparedness on Thursday, delivered his grievances directly to the top brass of the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC), and walked straight out the door before substantive talks even began.

The standoff left IEBC Chairman Erastus Ethekon facing an empty row of opposition seats and holding a formal presentation that was never delivered to the delegation.

### The Boardroom Showdown

Kalonzo framed the abrupt departure as an unavoidable response to what he described as an unprepared electoral referee. 

“We came to the IEBC today in good faith, and we expected the same in return,” Kalonzo said as the leaders made their exit. “Good faith is shown in deeds: an independent, impartial and prepared Commission. Today, we did not find it. So we have walked out.”

The opposition contingent took issue with the official agenda, arguing that the commission had bypassed critical electoral reforms that political parties wanted put on the table. Rather than staying to hear Ethekon and his team make their formal pitch on the roadmap to the August 10, 2027 General Election, the political heavyweights delivered their preliminary remarks, tabled their demands, and headed for the parking lot.

The walkout drew direct inspiration from the People’s Coalition on Electoral Reforms, a grouping steered by former Chief Justice David Maraga, former Attorney General Justin Muturi, and ODM’s Caroli Omondi. Ahead of the Nairobi engagement, that coalition had already compiled four non-negotiable reform benchmarks.

### The Four Non-Negotiables

Maraga’s coalition had laid down four irreducible minimums they insisted must guide the next electoral cycle:

* A thoroughly cleaned and verifiable voters’ register.
* The outright abolition of the national presidential tallying centre at Bomas of Kenya.
* The establishment of electronic voting mechanisms for Kenyans living in the diaspora.
* Mandatory, end-to-end livestreaming of voting, ballot counting, and result announcements at all polling stations and tallying centres.

Maraga had underscored the gravity of those demands prior to the meeting, declaring that opposition formations would not sit back while administrative lethargy crept into election planning. 

“We will not rest until the Commission is freed from state capture and Kenyans have a free, fair and credible election,” Maraga said, underscoring that the demands had been raised in earlier sessions without securing satisfactory answers.

### Ethekon's Counter-Punch

By Friday, Ethekon fired back with an official commission statement, expressing deep frustration over what he described as missed dialogue. He revealed that the commission had even shifted its scheduled start time backward purely to accommodate the arrival of the opposition leaders.

“The Commission, however, regrets that a section of the Opposition leaders opted to leave the meeting after making their preliminary remarks and presenting their demands, before the Commission could make its presentation and substantive dialogue could commence,” Ethekon said.

According to the IEBC chairman, the early departure prevented the commission and the Kenyan public from examining the feasibility of the opposition’s proposals while robbing the political leaders of a firsthand briefing on the commission’s Strategic Plan and Election Operations Plan.

“Walking away from dialogue before substantive discussions begin does not advance the resolution of electoral concerns,” Ethekon stated, reminding political actors that constitutional independence under Article 249 does not insulate the commission from public scrutiny, but rather invites structured, sustained engagement.

Ethekon pointed to the commission’s track record of conducting 32 recent by-elections across Kenya as evidence of operational capacity, adding that the statutory Electoral Code of Conduct under Article 84 of the Constitution is already active.

The chairman also addressed friction inside the venue, explicitly condemning separate reports of harassment and intimidation directed at certain participants during the consultative session. Ethekon confirmed that the commission is reviewing the incidents and coordinating with security agencies to enforce orderly engagements as the countdown to 2027 accelerates.
