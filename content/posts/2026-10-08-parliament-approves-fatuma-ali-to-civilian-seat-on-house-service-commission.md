Parliament has completed the bicameral approval of former Wajir Woman Representative Fatuma Ibrahim Ali, handing the former regional lawmaker administrative oversight of legislative staff and lawmaker welfare as the new civilian commissioner on the Parliamentary Service Commission (PSC).

The National Assembly’s vote concluded a dual-house vetting cycle triggered after the Senate cleared her nomination on the basis of a vetting report by its Standing Committee on Justice, Legal Affairs, and Human Rights, which conducted her formal confirmation hearing on September 28.

Tabling the approval motion in the National Assembly, PSC Commissioner Patrick Makau reported that Ali emerged as the top-ranked candidate following competitive interviews conducted on September 10. The vetting evaluated academic records, management credentials, grasp of parliamentary operations, and compliance with Chapter Six leadership and integrity standards.

Seconding the motion, Eldas MP and PSC Commissioner Adan Keyna stated that Ali’s administrative remit directly influences the operational machinery supporting legislators and parliamentary workers across both houses. Keyna noted that her prior tenure in the National Assembly and service as a member of the East African Legislative Assembly (EALA) equips her to balance staff administrative needs against the working requirements of lawmakers.

### Competitive Vetting and Scorecard

The vacancy opened following the expiration of the term of former commissioner Rachel Ameso, who previously held the female civilian seat. The commission gazetted the vacant office on August 14, drawing 75 formal applications from public administration practitioners nationwide.

From the initial pool of 75 applicants, the PSC shortlisted 12 candidates for oral interviews and statutory vetting. State security and ethics agencies conducted background verifications alongside formal public participation rounds before the commission submitted Ali’s name as the highest-scoring candidate to both chambers for statutory debate.

Makau told the House that the evaluation specifically tested the candidates' capacity to handle institutional governance without legislative conflicts of interest, given the distinct administrative mandate of non-parliamentarian commissioners.

### The Article 127 Mandate

Ali's entry satisfies Article 127(2)(d) of the Constitution, which reserves two positions on the 11-member commission for individuals with extensive experience in public affairs who do not hold active seats in either the National Assembly or the Senate. One of those two slots is constitutionally designated for a woman.

The non-member commissioners sit alongside lawmakers nominated by parliamentary parties, serving as an external governance counterweight within the body that controls Parliament's budget, oversees the parliamentary service bureaucracy, and directs capital developments across the legislative precincts.

With both chambers having adopted the committee reports without amendments, Ali is slated to take the oath of office to formally succeed Ameso, assuming responsibility for institutional welfare policies, human resource management, and administrative budget oversight for the remainder of the commission's operational term.
