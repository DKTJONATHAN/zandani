A private consortium has secured a long-term lease for land owned by Kenya Railways in central Nairobi to construct a $294.5 million (Ksh38.2 billion) indoor arena. The project, backed by Pan-African investment firm Zaria Group and US-based Helios Sports & Entertainment Group, will anchor the Nairobi Railway City urban renewal plan.

The 10,000-seater multipurpose facility represents a shift toward private-sector-led sports and entertainment infrastructure in Kenya, bypassing traditional state-funded development models. Under the agreement, the developers will build and operate the arena on the leased central Nairobi site, integrating it into a wider commercial and entertainment district around the city's Central Railway Station.

Regulatory filings cited by Bloomberg indicate the arena is designed to host basketball tournaments, international music tours, cultural programs, and corporate conferences. The developers project the construction phase will generate approximately 3,500 direct jobs, followed by 1,500 permanent operational roles. Additionally, they estimate that annual events at the venue could support up to 25,000 temporary positions across the retail, hospitality, and live production sectors.

The Nairobi project is part of a broader commercial strategy by Zaria Group—co-founded by Masai Ujiri—and Helios to establish a network of privately operated sports hubs across major African urban centers. The partners are using a model similar to their work in Rwanda, where they developed the $26 million (Ksh3.37 billion) Zaria Court Kigali and managed the commercialization of the 10,000-seat BK Arena and Amahoro Stadium.

Following the Nairobi and Kigali developments, the consortium plans to expand its sports and entertainment infrastructure portfolio to Lagos and Johannesburg. According to Ujiri, the regional expansion aims to build commercial platforms for athletes, artists, and businesses within the continent's creative economy.

In Nairobi, the private arena will operate alongside state-backed sports projects, including the under-construction Talanta Sports City. Together, these facilities are expected to increase the capital's capacity to bid for and host major international sporting events and entertainment tours.
