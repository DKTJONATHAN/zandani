Public Health Principal Secretary Mary Muthoni has announced a nationwide El Niño response plan targeting 18 high-risk counties across Kenya. The intervention aims to shield over four million citizens in flood hotspots like Nairobi, Mombasa, and Kisumu from cholera, malaria outbreaks, and severe infrastructure damage caused by predicted torrential downpours.

* Public Health PS Mary Muthoni confirmed 18 counties face imminent flood and health risks.
* The Ministry of Health will distribute 10 million treated mosquito nets starting November 2026.
* High-risk zones include Nairobi, five coastal counties, and five Lake Victoria Basin counties.
* State officials proposed a monthly national cleanliness day modeled after Rwanda's Umuganda.

Addressing health partners during planning for the Kenya Public Health International Conference, PS Muthoni explained that the ministry is coordinating with donor agencies to stock temporary medical structures, water purification systems, and basic drugs. Past disasters taught Afya House hard lessons. In 2023, El Niño deluges paralyzed 43 counties, destroying clinics and contaminating freshwater sources.

Data from the Interior Ministry singles out urban estates in Nairobi along clogged riverways, alongside coastal zones in Kwale, Kilifi, Lamu, Tana River, and Mombasa. Rising sea levels and choked storm drains pose immediate threats. Further west, Kisumu, Busia, Siaya, Homa Bay, and Migori face waterborne diseases as Lake Victoria expands into nearby villages.

To prevent malaria spikes, Afya House plans to dispatch 10 million long-lasting insecticide-treated nets to vulnerable families in November. PS Muthoni also confirmed that her department has forwarded a Cabinet memorandum seeking to institute a compulsory monthly national cleaning day, borrowing directly from Rwanda's environmental model.

### What it costs you

If you are stuck in traffic near the Allsops interchange along Thika Road during a sudden drizzle, you already know this story. Ministry plans on paper sound crisp in Nairobi air-conditioned boardrooms, but floodwater does not read Cabinet memos. Every rainy season, local contractors collect public funds for drainage clearance, yet stormwater channels from Githurai to Eastleigh remain choked with plastic waste. When Afya House flags 18 high-risk counties, the real risk lands on the estate dweller in Mukuru or the boda boda rider in Nyali. They pay for clogged culverts through lost business, flooded living rooms, and clinic bills for typhoid treatment. Setting aside a monthly cleaning day works in Kigali because enforcement is tight and public waste collection actually functions. Until Nairobi City County unclogs the drains along Waiyaki Way and coastal municipalities clear municipal waste, ten million mosquito nets are just a bandage on an open wound. The real cost is paid by taxpayers who fund emergency response twice over.
