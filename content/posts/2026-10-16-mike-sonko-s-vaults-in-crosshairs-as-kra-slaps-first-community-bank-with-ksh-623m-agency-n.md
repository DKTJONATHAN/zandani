Mike Sonko’s celebrated reputation for unrestricted liquidity has run into the cold, mechanical reality of the taxman’s most potent enforcement weapon: a direct agency notice aimed squarely at his commercial bank balances.

The Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA) has moved to recover an estimated KSh 623 million in disputed unpaid taxes linked to the former Nairobi Governor, issuing an enforcement directive directly to First Community Bank. The move, reported on Friday, October 16, 2026, bypasses the usual protracted court confrontations to tap directly into funds held within the lender's vaults.

For a public figure whose political and celebrity brand was built on visible displays of ready wealth, the maneuver represents a sharp, institutional squeeze on his private banking network.

### The Mechanics of Section 42

Rather than waiting for voluntary compliance or engaging in standard demand exchanges, the tax authority activated Section 42 of the Tax Procedures Act, 2015. The provision grants KRA statutory power to declare a commercial bank an agent for tax collection when an account holder faces an outstanding liability.

Under this framework, First Community Bank is legally instructed to remit funds held on Sonko’s behalf directly to the national revenue ledger. While the notice establishes an aggressive debt-recovery action, tax law does not treat an agency order as a finding of criminal culpability.

The specific terms of the bank directive, including the exact volume of liquid capital currently available inside Sonko's accounts for immediate remittance, remain unconfirmed. Commercial lenders served with such notices are required to freeze and transfer matching funds up to the declared liability, provided the deposits exist.

### Fresh Pressure After Tribunal Defeat

This latest KSh 623 million push lands against the backdrop of an already hostile legal season for Sonko’s personal balance sheet. 

In August 2026, the Tax Appeals Tribunal dismissed Sonko’s contest against a separate KSh 385 million assessment. That earlier dispute centered on an exhaustive review of deposits funneled through his various personal and business bank accounts between 2013 and 2019, spanning his tenure as Nairobi Senator and his term at City Hall.

Following that August tribunal defeat, Sonko announced his intention to mount a fresh challenge in the High Court, disputing the authority’s calculations. 

Legal and financial documentation treats the current KSh 623 million agency notice as an independent enforcement development rather than an automatic extension of the August tribunal judgment, unless subsequent filings establish a formal cross-link between the two files. While an assessment establishes a contested liability, an agency notice serves as an active enforcement order to seize assets on the ground.

### Silence From the Sonko Camp

Sonko, who routinely uses his massive digital platforms and broadcast media appearances to push back against institutional pressure, had not issued a formal response to the latest bank enforcement notice by Friday.

The immediate question rests on First Community Bank’s compliance timeline and whether the former county chief will scramble his legal team to petition the courts for an emergency stay of execution. Until a superior court intervenes to halt the Section 42 order, the KSh 623 million agency notice stands as an active claim against any funds standing to his credit.
