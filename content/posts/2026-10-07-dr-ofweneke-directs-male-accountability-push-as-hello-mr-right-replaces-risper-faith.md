Dr. Ofweneke took the stage at the Season 5 launch of *Hello Mr. Right* on Tuesday, October 6, 2026, setting down a strict thesis for modern relationships: a man cannot effectively support or guide his household if he remains consumed by past guilt and starved of personal peace.

Addressing guests during the official unveil of the dating reality show, the veteran television host combined traditional views on headship with a direct plea for male mental health. While reiterating that men retain an ongoing duty to shield and provision for their partners, Ofweneke argued that self-preservation and internal emotional stability are mandatory prerequisites for that work.

“Men should always love themselves. A man is expected to always take accountability,” Ofweneke stated, framing personal well-being as the baseline for household leadership. “When in a relationship, always know you are in charge of your wife and children. You must do everything to make sure you remain the best.”

That expectation, he noted, breaks down rapidly when men neglect their own mental state under constant economic and societal pressure.

“Your peace is also more important, because without peace you won’t be able to provide fully for your family,” he explained.

Crucial to maintaining that balance, according to Ofweneke, is confronting the silent shame that many men carry from previous financial or relationship missteps. He challenged men to break the cycle of self-condemnation before attempting to build new family structures.

“As a man, forgive yourself,” Ofweneke urged. “A lot of men have not learnt to forgive themselves for everything, and your past doesn’t determine how you value your family in the future.”

That message on accountability and emotional health signals an intentional editorial turn for *Hello Mr. Right* as it enters its fifth cycle. Rather than serving strictly as a surface-level matchmaking format, the upcoming season will deliberately lean into controversial real-world dynamics affecting modern couples.

According to Ofweneke, the show will force contestants and viewers to confront explicit societal friction points, including wealth gaps, personal grooming standards, age differences, and financial expectations within relationships.

“We will be very keen when incorporating basic social society conversation,” Ofweneke said. “From money, to age, to looks and grooming and conversation about finances, it’s all put in the show so that we have an open conversation.”

He conceded that inviting bold, unfiltered contestant personalities to debate those issues on camera will inevitably divide opinion among viewers across social platforms.

To navigate that expanded format, the production is executing a key change on the main stage. Digital creator and actress Trisha Khalid officially steps in as co-host alongside Ofweneke, replacing socialite Risper Faith, who co-hosted Season 4. Veteran relationship counselor Getrude Mungai returns to the show to offer expert psychological guidance during on-screen discussions.

The revamped format makes its screen debut on Saturday, October 17, 2026, with an auditions special, before launching its main broadcast season on Saturday, October 31, 2026.
