Kenya's digital push to issue and distribute national identity cards has hit an administrative wall, forcing the Interior Ministry to pivot from automated text alerts to door-to-door tracing powered by state intelligence databases and local administrative police.

Appearing before the National Assembly’s Departmental Committee on Administration and Internal Security on Thursday, Principal Secretary Belio Kipsang disclosed that 557,930 processed identity cards currently sit abandoned in registration offices nationwide. To break the deadlock, the government is bypassing standard passive collection protocols and mobilizing grassroots administrators to physically hunt down applicants.

### The Limits of Digital Alerts

Until now, the ministry relied heavily on eCitizen-driven short messaging services, firing automated text prompts to phone numbers provided during registration whenever a card cleared the production line. However, churn in mobile numbers, rural connectivity blind spots, and applicant relocation have blunted the reach of digital pings, leaving over half a million vital records unclaimed.

![Processed IDs at Nyayo House in Nairobi County.](https://i.ibb.co/v4pGJdd8/5d3bbdeff94d.webp)

To bridge the gap, civil registration authorities are executing an aggressive data-matching operation. The state plans to cross-reference uncollected files in the Integrated Population Registration System (IPRS) against registries maintained by the tax authority, the electoral commission, and state social welfare programmes. By extracting current addresses, workplace filings, and active welfare delivery points, authorities intend to pinpoint where delinquent applicants actually reside.

### Expanding the Grassroots Net

Once digital tracing generates actionable locations, enforcement falls to the provincial administration. Chiefs and assistant chiefs will be tasked with identifying, sensitising, and physically tracking down cardholders within their jurisdictions to ensure the credentials reach their hands.

The assignment coincides with a rapid expansion and retooling of the state's local administrative arm. Just days prior to the committee hearing, a cohort of 5,892 chiefs and assistant chiefs completed paramilitary and administrative courses at the National Police College’s Embakasi 'A' Campus, a program intended to standardize community-level security and civil command.

![The Principal Secretary for Immigration and Citizen Services Belio Kipsan'g, before the Senate Committee on National Assembly's Departmental Committee on Administration and Interna](https://i.ibb.co/tMCHBM2Z/512c5dbe97dd.webp)

Reinforcements are already in the pipeline. On Wednesday, November 4, the Public Service Commission authorized a fresh recruitment drive for chiefs and assistant chiefs, expanding the state's eyes and ears in sub-locations that have historically slipped through centralized civil registration radars.

### Alternative Channels and Frontline Hardware

While administrators prepare to fan out across villages and urban wards, authorities are running parallel self-service lookup mechanisms to encourage voluntary walk-ins before administrators intervene.

Applicants holding an active waiting slip can verify production status via mobile phone by sending their serial card number—omitting the final digit—to the SMS short code 20031. For Kenyans who have relocated since their initial biometrics were logged, the state is routing document re-routing requests through the Huduma Telecommunication helpline on 919, enabling cards to be transferred from original sub-county registries to the nearest Huduma Centre.

To prevent fresh print runs from swelling the 557,000-card pile, Kipsang told lawmakers the ministry is procuring live capture biometric stations. By digitizing verification at the immediate point of intake, officials aim to compress production turnarounds and establish immediate, verifiable contact records that survive the gap between application and handover.
