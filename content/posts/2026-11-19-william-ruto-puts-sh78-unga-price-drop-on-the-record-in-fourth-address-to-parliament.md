President William Ruto stepped to the podium for his fourth State of the Nation Address with a very specific domestic arithmetic ready for lawmakers: the everyday cost of preparing a meal of ugali.

Addressing a joint sitting of Parliament, the Head of State chose to anchor his administration’s economic scorecard on the country's most sensitive kitchen staple. He reported that a standard two-kilogram packet of sifted maize flour, which commanded an average peak retail price of KSh 230 when he assumed the presidency in September 2022, has dropped to approximately KSh 152.

The KSh 78 price correction served as Ruto's primary exhibit to demonstrate that state-driven agricultural interventions have begun altering daily consumer reality.

### The 27-Million-Bag Supply Shift

Rather than treating the price dip as an isolated market fluctuation, Ruto tied the downward shift on supermarket shelves directly to domestic production volumes. Official production numbers shared during the address placed the country’s annual maize harvest at 71 million bags in 2025, up significantly from the 44 million bags recorded in 2022.

That leap of 27 million bags represented the bedrock of the administration's argument. According to the President, flooding local distribution networks with locally grown grain removed the import pressures and structural deficits that had pushed retail prices beyond the reach of ordinary households during his first months in office.

He credited the harvest expansion to a deliberate push toward input access, specifically pointing to the nationwide distribution of subsidized fertilizer and structured logistical backing for domestic grain producers.

### Balancing the Farm Gate and the Shelf

For Ruto, the data presented in the chamber was framed as a dual achievement rather than a single victory for urban consumers. Lowering the retail ceiling for urban shoppers often risks depressing farm-gate returns for rural growers, yet the Head of State maintained that the policy design protected both ends of the supply chain.

By cutting the cost of production at the planting stage through cheaper fertilizer, Ruto argued, farmers were able to expand acreage and secure viable earnings even as the final consumer paid substantially less per packet.

The presentation marked a defining checkpoint of his tenure, contrasting the peak inflationary pressures of September 2022 with the current 2026 retail landscape as his administration continues to defend its broader economic agenda before Parliament.
