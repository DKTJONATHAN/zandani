The State’s push to hit rigid tree-planting quotas faces growing logistical strain after Cabinet Secretary Davis Chirchir formally asked the Environment Ministry to declare an extra national public holiday to recover lost ground from an unexpected dry spell.

Speaking at Makutano Forest in Kipkelion East on Sunday, the Roads and Transport minister confirmed he will lobby Environment, Climate Change and Forestry Cabinet Secretary Deborah Barasa to gazette a second nationwide planting exercise. The move follows a disrupted Mazingira Day in Kericho County, where an unseasonal dry period frustrated an official bid to plant 300,000 seedlings within an hour.

“I will ask my colleague, the Minister for Environment, Deborah Barasa, to give us another day nationally so that we can continue striving towards our target of planting the 15 billion trees,” Chirchir said, arguing that the country must maintain its momentum despite unfavourable weather.

### Chasing the 15-Billion Target

![CS Chirchir](https://i.ibb.co/60RXmqx8/eb2684adfd55.webp)

The call for another gazetted public holiday—on top of the October 10 Mazingira Day established to mobilize citizens for conservation—reflects the mounting pressure on state agencies to show concrete tallies toward the 2032 target of 15 billion trees.

Across the country, Mazingira Day activities yielded 5.3 million planted trees, a modest sum against the overarching 10-year quota. To close the operational gap, the executive has begun shifting from manual hand-planting toward centralized, large-scale forestry interventions.

President William Ruto announced plans to deploy aerial seeding across cleared and demarcated public forests. The initiative is backed by a directive deploying 15,000 National Youth Service personnel to work alongside the Kenya Forest Service, aimed at raising annual seedling output to two billion.

The administration is also moving to establish 17 mega-nurseries, each projected to yield 25 million seedlings annually to sustain state-directed planting drives.

### The Catchment Contradiction

Yet the proposal to shut down public offices and commercial operations for another planting exercise arrives amid simmering public frustration over commercial tree harvesting in vital catchments.

While state officials at Makutano Forest urged communities to accelerate seedling coverage, county leaders and residents in the central highlands have spent the week protesting intense commercial logging in the Aberdare Forest.

Heavy haulage trucks have been recorded transporting timber from the Geta and Njabini Kiburu forest stations, prompting Nyandarua Governor Kiarie Badilisha and Kipipiri MP Wanjiku Muhia to challenge the operations. Local authorities warn that clearing canopy cover in the Aberdares threatens downstream water security, destabilizes local farming systems, and damages rural transport arteries already weakened by heavy freight.

Whether CS Barasa will grant Chirchir's request and gazette an additional work-free planting day remains an open administrative question. The Environment Ministry must weigh the economic disruptions of a second unscheduled holiday against the reality of ground moisture deficits, even as communities in critical basins question why state-backed logging continues while citizens are summoned to replenish cleared soils.
