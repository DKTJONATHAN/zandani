President William Ruto stepped before a State House audience on Tuesday and delivered an unusual public concession: when it comes to the grueling grind of grassroots mobilization, First Lady Rachel Ruto has outmaneuvered him.

Speaking during the 17th-anniversary celebrations of the Joyful Women Organisation (JOYWO) on November 24, 2026, the Head of State bypassed standard ceremonial platitudes to revisit his formative years in elective politics, offering a rare, self-deprecating comparison between his own legislative record and his wife’s nationwide network.

For a politician whose career has been anchored on an image of obsessive ground-level organization, Ruto admitted that his early attempts to rally women in his former parliamentary backyard hit a wall.

### The Eldoret North Confession

Before taking the national stage, Ruto served as the Member of Parliament for Eldoret North. That tenure established his reputation as a formidable political operator, yet he told guests at State House that grassroots women's organizing completely confounded him within the boundaries of a single constituency.

“I tried to organise, when I was a member of parliament, I tried to organise women in my constituency, and it was not an easy task,” Ruto said, recounting the frustration of trying to build a cohesive grassroots collective at the local level. 

Looking across the State House lawns at the delegation representing JOYWO’s chapters, the President noted that Rachel succeeded on a scale he failed to execute even within his home constituency.

“I am amazed that Rachel can organise women across 45 counties,” he remarked.

### Surrendering the Organizing Crown

That admission set the stage for Ruto’s central declaration of the afternoon. Acknowledging that the First Lady had accomplished what eluded him as a constituency lawmaker, the President openly yielded the high ground on grassroots enterprise.

“When I see this great organisation that has brought together thousands of women, built thousands of businesses and supported thousands of families, I confirm without any fear or doubt that what a man can do, a woman can do better,” Ruto told the gathering.

The public nod arrives as JOYWO marks 17 years since its inception. Founded by Rachel Ruto on August 31, 2009, the initiative was launched during the tail end of Ruto’s parliamentary years, starting as a focused intervention before swelling into a national movement.

Over the past nearly two decades, the organisation has centered its operations on table banking, financial inclusion, micro-enterprise funding, and community development. By providing women with direct access to revolving credit without the steep collateral barriers of commercial lenders, the model quietly built an expansive constituency of its own across 45 of Kenya’s 47 counties.

### A Seventeen-Year Grassroots Contrast

While elective campaigns rely on cyclical mobilizations that often disperse after ballots are counted, JOYWO’s structure was built to endure routine financial cycles. The 17th-anniversary event in Nairobi highlighted how the table-banking framework expanded capital access to thousands of households, establishing self-sustaining micro-enterprises far away from the spotlight of mainstream party politics.

By drawing a direct contrast between his single-constituency struggles in Eldoret North and the First Lady’s cross-county footprint, Ruto offered a rare glimpse into the household’s competing organizing resumes.

For Rachel Ruto, Tuesday’s State House milestone placed her long-running economic project at the center of the executive complex, celebrated not merely as a supportive civic initiative, but as an operational feat that even the President admitted he could not replicate.
