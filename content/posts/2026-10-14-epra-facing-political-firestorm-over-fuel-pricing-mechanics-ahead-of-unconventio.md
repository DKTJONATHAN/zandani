Pre-emptive political accusations targeting Kenya's fuel pricing mechanism have thrust the Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority into an intense institutional spotlight, forcing government officials to defend the regulator’s legal independence hours before a critical price announcement.

The statutory deadline for the October 15 to November 14 pricing cycle arrived amid intense political maneuvering, after Democratic Citizens Party leader Rigathi Gachagua publicly alleged that the administration intended to force a pump price hike of Ksh12 per litre for petrol and Ksh9 per litre for diesel. Gachagua claimed the proposed increases were designed to generate political capital ahead of the 2027 General Election.

Speaking during a development tour in West Pokot County alongside President William Ruto on Wednesday, Energy Cabinet Secretary Opiyo Wandayi rejected the claims, insisting that monthly pump tariffs remain strictly governed by statutory formulas rather than executive preference.

![A collage of the Energy and Petroleum Cabinet Secretary Opiyo Wandayi (left), with that of the Former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua (right).](https://i.ibb.co/mrPTD5PH/149ca33f643b.webp)

Wandayi argued that the former Deputy President lacked a basic grasp of the regulatory architecture, emphasizing that individual political actors cannot dictate prices managed independently by EPRA. 

"There is nothing that you know regarding fuel prices in this country because you were a one-term MP," Wandayi said, referring to Gachagua as "Mr Wamunyoro" while counter-alleging that Gachagua had solicited bribes from petroleum importers during his tenure as Deputy President.

The sharp political exchange coincides with an unusual operational shift at Anniversary Towers, where EPRA announced plans to deliver the October-November pricing verdict through a live media press briefing rather than its traditional written statement.

![An image of a fuel pump.](https://i.ibb.co/qMpRkRvg/153a9922c49c.webp)

The regulatory agency faces a complex calculation for the upcoming cycle. During the previous September 15 to October 14 window, maximum retail prices in Nairobi were held flat at Ksh214.03 per litre for Super Petrol, Ksh217.86 for Diesel, and Ksh191.38 for Kerosene. However, recent disruptions in Middle East transit corridors have driven up international crude prices and shipping freight costs, creating upward pressure on landed import costs.

Under Kenya’s established petroleum framework, EPRA is legally mandated to calculate retail ceilings based on landed cargo costs, pipeline transmission tariffs, distribution margins, and statutory levies. The decision to hold a live press conference indicates a deliberate push by the regulator to publicly defend its mathematical inputs against growing political scrutiny.

All eyes now turn to EPRA's afternoon briefing to see whether international supply shocks will push domestic retail prices higher or if the regulatory formula will absorb the global freight surge.
