Blanket travel restrictions enforced by Riyadh are threatening the employment timelines of a massive Kenyan diaspora workforce, following the abrupt return of more than 350 workers who were blocked from entering Saudi Arabia over health concerns.

The group touched down at Jomo Kenyatta International Airport at approximately 1:30 p.m. on Monday, carrying valid work visas that failed to secure them passage past Saudi border control. Their return followed a four-day ordeal stranded at Sharjah Airport in the United Arab Emirates as carriers and agencies scrambled to coordinate repatriation flights.

![A photo collage of the main entrance of the Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (JKIA) and a sign of Ebola, October 6, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/fzbRN9Tt/992cfb8b41ad.webp)

The disruption stems from strict entry measures announced by Saudi Arabia on October 30. Under the directive, the Kingdom suspended travel for its citizens to Kenya, halted the issuance of all visa categories to travelers from the country, and barred entry to any individual who had visited Kenya or an active outbreak zone within the preceding 21 days—even when transiting through third countries.

For the returning workers, the immediate anxiety centers on administrative deadlines. Affected individuals have warned that prolonged diplomatic stalemates risk causing their newly acquired work visas to lapse, jeopardizing months of preparation and financial investment tied to their overseas employment plans.

“We have come back, and it is very much frustrating. We had a lot of hopes, but when we arrived at the nation we were very much disappointed,” said Mary Njau, one of the returnees, calling for urgent state intervention to salvage their job placements.

![Saudi Arabia Police](https://i.ibb.co/2YcRqDVT/cfa6444427a3.webp)

The confrontation highlights the vulnerability of Kenya's substantial labor migration pipeline to the Gulf. Government figures show more than 350,000 Kenyans were living and working in Saudi Arabia as of July 2026, making the Kingdom one of the primary destinations for the country's overseas labor force.

In response to the blockade, Nairobi lodged a formal diplomatic protest, dismissing the restrictions as disproportionate and lacking scientific backing. The Ministry of Health maintained that all nine confirmed Ebola cases recorded during the current cycle are tied to a single, imported cluster with no evidence of community transmission.

Bilateral discussions are currently underway, with Kenya engaging authorities in Saudi Arabia and Kuwait to review the border curbs. Technical talks have been scheduled to present epidemiological data to Gulf regulators, even as the public remains directed to report any suspected health emergencies through the Ministry's designated lines.
