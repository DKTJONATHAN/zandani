Political commentator Herman Manyora has delivered a blunt diagnostic check to opposition strategists, prematurely declaring President William Ruto a "Tutam" (two-term) president ahead of the 2027 general elections—framing his verdict not as personal praise for the incumbent, but as the natural result of an alternative policy void.

Speaking during a sit-down interview with *Nairobi Review*, the veteran communications expert urged Kenyans to adjust early to the prospect of a full two-term mandate for the current administration. Manyora argued that opposition factions have failed to generate any fresh political momentum or present a realistic counter-vision for the country's economic governance.

"I am not seeing anything new to convince me," Manyora declared during the sit-down. "So I’ll just call the election in favour of President William Ruto. Na mzoee mapema."

At the core of Manyora's argument is what he views as a fundamental flaw in opposition strategy: a heavy reliance on anti-government rhetoric without backing it up with concrete financial alternatives. While public debate continues to center on high taxation and economic pressure, Manyora noted that opponents of the administration have not put forward a clear, painless alternative tax and economic plan.

Without a viable roadmap showing how an alternative administration would fund government operations, manage national debt, and relieve taxpayer pain, Manyora warned that changing national leadership carries unnecessary systemic risks. In his assessment, voters faced with economic uncertainty will likely choose the predictability of the incumbent over an opposition that offers criticism without statutory solutions.

Manyora has consistently challenged the opposition to move beyond public rallies and press statements to do the heavy lifting of shadow governance. His use of the popular "TUTAM" tag reflects a pragmatic view of power dynamics: incumbents rarely lose elections to fragmented opponents who fail to present organized, credible policy alternatives.

By calling the 2027 race early, Manyora shifts the spotlight away from State House and onto the opposition retreat rooms. Until anti-government leaders organize effectively and table a workable economic agenda, the veteran analyst insists the path to a second term for President Ruto remains open and uncontested.
