A quiet administrative consolidation of domestic power bands has collided with a potential legal challenge, centering on how households are automatically bucketed for electricity billing.

Under a gazette notice published on September 18, the Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority (EPRA) dismantled the traditional mid-tier structures by wiping out the distinct 30–60 kilowatt-hour (kWh) and 60–100 kWh brackets. In their place, the regulator introduced a broad, single category spanning 30 to 100 kWh.

![Kenya Power token and an electricity meter](https://i.ibb.co/HDJS6CTW/f1988ba28663.webp)

Under the new architecture, domestic consumers are split into three tiers: Domestic Consumer 1 (DC1) for lifeline users consuming 0 to 30 kWh, Domestic Consumer 2 (DC2) for those using 30 to 100 kWh, and Domestic Consumer 3 (DC3) covering heavy users between 100 and 15,000 kWh. EPRA mandated that customers be automatically assigned to these tiers based on a rolling three-month moving average of their consumption.

This structural streamlining has sparked an immediate pushback from consumer advocates who argue that merging the bands masks hidden price increases for households near the margins of the old thresholds.

Engineer Isaac Ndereva, Executive Director of the Electricity Consumer Society of Kenya (ECSK), stated that the regulatory shift was enacted without statutory public consultation. Ndereva announced that the consumer group is preparing legal proceedings to challenge the validity of the September 18 gazette notice, contending that altering consumption brackets without public input violates legal frameworks.

![A collage of a token meter displaying the 'connect' error (left) and several meter token (right).](https://i.ibb.co/JWS118g9/6d562f2b4405.webp)

"They have done it silently without consulting the public because they understood that we are going to get concerned," Ndereva said, asserting that the amendment will effectively drive up electricity costs for segments of domestic users.

The dispute unfolds against a backdrop of ongoing friction over power costs. Earlier in the year, the government announced the withdrawal of Kenya Power's formal application to review retail tariffs for the 2026/27 to 2028/29 control period following inter-agency consultations led by Energy Cabinet Secretary Opiyo Wandayi to shield households from cost escalation. Yet, even with that application shelved, EPRA's September billing cycle adjustments added upward pressure on consumer tokens.

With the ECSK preparing its filings, the core legal question remains whether the regulator’s automated three-month moving average reassignments constitute a procedural bypass of public participation requirements.
