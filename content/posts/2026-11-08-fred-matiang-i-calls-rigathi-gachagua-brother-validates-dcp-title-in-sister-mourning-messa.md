Dr. Fred Matiang’i chose an intriguing blend of personal fraternity and meticulous protocol to break his silence over the bereavement hitting the Gachagua household.

Reaching out following the death of former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua’s eldest sister, Eunice Nyangari Gachagua, at the age of 77, the former Interior Cabinet Secretary bypassed casual sympathies. Instead, he framed his dispatch around a distinct public salute, explicitly acknowledging Gachagua not only as a peer but by his emerging partisan mantle.

### The 'Brother and Colleague' Framing

In his formal dispatch, Matiang’i opened with an unusually warm, fraternal register, describing Gachagua as both a personal contemporary and an institutional equal.

“I extend my heartfelt condolences to my brother, colleague, former Deputy President and DCP Party Leader, H.E. Rigathi Gachagua, following the passing of his beloved elder sister, Eunice Nyangari Gachagua, aged 77,” Matiang’i stated.

The catalog of honorifics stood out immediately. Beyond referencing Gachagua’s tenure as former Deputy President, Matiang’i went out of his way to explicitly validate his standing as the "DCP Party Leader." In Kenyan political circles, where naming conventions and formal recognitions carry calculated weight, the deliberate inclusion of the party title signaled an open posture of mutual respect at a moment of heavy domestic grief.

### Party Fraternity Meets Personal Loss

Matiang’i, who issued the message wearing his hat as Jubilee Party leader, did not restrict the gesture to an isolated, one-on-one phone call. He anchored the sympathy directly within institutional channels, offering condolences on behalf of his entire political outfit.

“On behalf of the entire Jubilee Party fraternity and on my own behalf, I extend our deepest condolences to the Gachagua family,” he noted, bridging party boundaries to address the emotional shock confronting the wider family.

By positioning the Jubilee apparatus behind the message, Matiang’i established an official channel of solidarity toward Gachagua, treating the loss of the senior matriarch as an event warranting cross-party executive dignity rather than cold partisan distance.

### Honoring a Four-Generation Matriarch

Central to the condolence was the acknowledgment of Eunice Nyangari's foundational presence at the head of an expansive family tree. Matiang’i directed his message toward the generations she leaves behind, specifically highlighting her lineage.

He offered words of comfort directed “particularly [to] Eunice’s children, grandchildren, and great-grandchildren,” recognizing her 77-year journey and her legacy anchored across four distinct generations.

“May Almighty God grant the family strength, comfort, and peace as they mourn this profound loss,” Matiang’i concluded. “May her soul rest in eternal peace.”

For Gachagua, navigating the loss of his eldest sibling, the message from the Jubilee boss offered a rare public moment where solemn family mourning and mutual political protocol converged seamlessly.
