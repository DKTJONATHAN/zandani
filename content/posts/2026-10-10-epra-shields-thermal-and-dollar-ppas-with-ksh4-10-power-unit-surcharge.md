The Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority (EPRA) has issued a tariff review that effectively insulates thermal plant operators and foreign-currency power suppliers from operational inflation, shifting a cumulative Ksh4.10 per kilowatt-hour (kWh) burden onto retail electricity bills.

The regulatory notice, dated Friday, October 9, 2026, activates adjustments across three distinct statutory pass-through lines: a fuel energy charge of Ksh3.22 per kWh, a foreign exchange rate adjustment of 87 cents per kWh, and an expanded water resource management levy of 1.34 cents per kWh.

“Pursuant to the 2023 Schedule of Tariffs, all electricity prices will attract an additional Water Resource Management Authority levy of 1.34 cents per kWh for meter readings taken in October 2026,” EPRA stated in the regulatory notice detailing the revised billing schedule.

### The Tripartite Pass-Through Structure

The regulator structured the charges across staggered billing windows, applying the 87-cent foreign exchange adjustment retrospectively to meter readings taken in September 2026, while the Ksh3.22 fuel energy cost and the 1.34-cent water levy take effect across October meter readings and immediate token sales.

Rather than drawing from state stabilisation reserves, the mechanism functions as an automatic pass-through conduit under Kenya's tariff architecture, ensuring that utility bulk suppliers recover fluctuating production overheads in real time.

At Ksh3.22 per unit, the fuel energy cost forms the bulk of the latest increase. The charge compensates heavy-fuel-oil and diesel thermal stations for the volatile expense of run-time fuel, alongside cross-border electricity imports dispatched onto the national grid to cover demand variations.

### Hedging Dollar Contracts Against Local Currency

The 87-cent foreign exchange adjustment protects independent power producers whose long-term power purchase agreements (PPAs) are denominated in foreign currencies, predominantly the United States dollar.

Because the underlying capital investments, foreign loan repayments, and offshore operational contracts of generation plants require hard currency, the tariff framework transfers exchange-rate variances directly into consumer invoices during monthly billing and token vending transactions.

This automatic insulation guarantees that currency depreciation or offshore payment spikes do not erode the cash margins of contracted power producers, leaving domestic households and commercial enterprises to absorb the differential.

### Hydro Basins and Statutory Levies

Beyond private thermal and independent generation plants, public water management institutions are also set to draw increased yields from the revised schedule. The Water Resource Management Authority (WRA) levy of Ksh0.0134 per kWh captures revenues tied to raw river throughput powering the country’s primary hydro stations.

Under applicable energy tariff rules, bulk power off-taken from hydroelectric stations boasting capacities of at least one megawatt incurs a statutory charge of five cents per kilowatt-hour, which is scaled into retail consumer formulas.

Data published by EPRA indicates that the national grid off-took approximately 304.82 million kWh of electricity in September 2026 from qualifying large-scale hydropower facilities.

That off-take was pooled from major generation installations across the country, including the Seven Forks cascade comprising Gitaru, Kamburu, Kiambere, Kindaruma, and Masinga, alongside the Tana and Turkwel hydropower stations.

### Operational Impact on Retail Users

For industrial operators, small businesses, and domestic consumers purchasing prepaid tokens, the Ksh4.10 per unit increment will translate to fewer units per transaction throughout October.

Because electricity tariffs function on a compounded structure where value-added tax and statutory percentage levies apply to base rates and pass-through adjustments alike, the actual cash deduction at the vending terminal will rise slightly above the nominal unit increase.

With diesel thermal plants remaining active on the dispatch curve and dollar-denominated contracts holding firm, power users will remain tethered to wholesale generation cost dynamics until EPRA's next monthly adjustment window in mid-November.
