Bien Baraza has disclosed that his decision to cast model Aisha Saringe as the female lead for his *'I’ll Be Okay'* music video hinged entirely on an unpolished agency passport photograph, bypassing established industry figures in favor of what he described as a "global face."

Speaking during an interview on the *Oga Obinna Show*, the singer outlined the agency-led talent acquisition process behind the visual project, which officially debuted on October 9, 2026. Rather than scouting active music video regulars or leveraging familiar entertainment personalities, Bien requested formal portfolio options from a modeling agency to find a precise visual look he had envisioned for the shoot.

"I got a bunch of models from an agency, and I selected her because she fit the description," Bien said during the interview, detailing how he sifted through candidate options before settling on Saringe.

According to Bien, the decision was finalized before any in-person screen test or studio meeting took place. While examining the candidate documentation submitted by the agency, Saringe's standard headshot stood out instantly.

"Actually, when I saw her the first time, I saw her passport picture, and I was like, ‘E! Hu ni mrembo.’ Like, her face is global. So, I actually said yes to her based on seeing her face," Bien explained.

The singer's decision highlights a deliberate pivot in East African music video production. In a market where high-profile pop releases frequently rely on established online creators or familiar social media figures to drive immediate promotional traction, Bien chose to greenlight an agency submission based strictly on visual suitability for the song's aesthetic.

Saringe’s placement as the lead character subsequently made her a prominent focal point following the video's October release, driving online discussion around her performance and expanding her public profile beyond agency modeling circles.

With *'I’ll Be Okay'* continuing its streaming run, Bien’s account of the audition phase confirms that an official passport image proved decisive in launching one of the quarter's most talked-about music video debuts.
