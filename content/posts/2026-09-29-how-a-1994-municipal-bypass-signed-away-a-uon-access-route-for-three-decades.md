A three-decade administrative blind spot protecting a grabbed public corridor in Mombasa has been unpicked by the Environment and Land Court, laying bare how municipal routines were flouted in the autumn of 1994 to carve private parcels out of active urban infrastructure.

In a judgment delivered on September 24, 2026, Justice Yuvinalis Angima voided the 99-year lease for parcel Mombasa Municipality/Block XIX/305, ruling that the allocation of the Machakos Road reserve was executed entirely outside the statutory framework governing public road closures.

![A collage of the parcel of land adjacent to the Mombasa UoN Campus that was illegally grabbed.](https://i.ibb.co/NkCpY6y/d7de5fd23cb2.webp)

The disputed 0.0-hectare excision sits directly against the access route serving the University of Nairobi’s Mombasa Campus. According to court records, the alienation process launched on June 1, 1994, bypassed every mandatory safeguard required for public property conversion. Investigators from the Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission (EACC) established that no Gazette notices announcing an intent to close the road were ever published, no approved Part Development Plan existed, and neither the local government ministry nor the municipal council passed the necessary committee resolutions to authorize the transaction.

Justice Angima noted that the allocating authorities at the time lacked the legal capacity to convert part of a public street into commercial-cum-residential holdings. The court directly tied the unlawful facilitation to former municipal executives, pointing to Town Clerk David Kalume Randu and Mayor Ahmed Mwidhani alongside the initial beneficiary, the late councillor Ezekiel Luyali.

![land-eacc-mombasa.jpg](https://i.ibb.co/9kdX1Mm3/746c23da6458.webp)

Despite the lease being registered on September 2, 1994, the land escaped physical development. Evidence tendered during the trial confirmed that the parcel remained unbuilt and continues to function as an open public parking lot.

Following the ruling, the Chief Land Registrar has been directed to cancel all lease entries, while the Director of Surveys must expunge the survey plan that brought the irregular parcel into existence. The court's permanent injunction shields the property from any future transfers, sales, or charges by its purported holders, returning the corridor to its intended civic utility without triggering the complexities of vacant possession.
