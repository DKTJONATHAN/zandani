Busia Senator Okiya Omtatah has locked in a strict November 2026 timeline to determine whether he will contest the 2027 presidency, formally restructuring the research apparatus tasked with testing his national viability.

Through a Kenya Gazette notice published on Friday, October 2, the lawmaker overhauled the leadership of his Presidential Exploratory Committee and officially prolonged its operational lifespan. The decree sets an explicit sunset date for the advisory unit, mandating that it conclude its feasibility work exactly when Omtatah makes a formal public declaration on whether to challenge President William Ruto at the ballot.

Under the newly gazetted appointments, David John Bwakali assumes the role of committee chairperson, while Dr. Dancan Otieno Onyango steps in as deputy chairperson.

The changes formally revoke the mandates of previous chairperson Mary Kathomi Ringu and former deputy chairperson and diaspora coordinator Charles ole Kabaiku, who were appointed under Gazette Notice No. 15372 when the committee was first unveiled in November 2024.

### Statutory mandate and viability review

Unlike traditional political formations that rely on informal party caucuses, Omtatah has maintained a formal, gazetted structure for his pre-campaign groundwork. The 10-member exploratory team has spent nearly two years gauging public sentiment, analyzing policy priorities, and assessing logistical requirements for a nationwide campaign.

According to the gazetted directive, the committee’s primary brief remains testing whether the first-term senator's anti-corruption and public-interest litigation record can translate into a competitive national electoral machinery.

"The Committee's tenure has been extended and shall remain in office until November 2026," the notice states. "It shall be dissolved immediately upon the aspirant formally announcing his candidacy for the presidency or his decision not to run."

### Rejecting coalition shortcuts

The leadership shakeup comes as Omtatah reaffirms his decision to navigate the 2027 landscape outside conventional opposition alliances. The lawmaker has deliberately kept his distance from both the united opposition formation and the Linda Mwananchi Movement, maintaining that existing political groupings have failed to present coherent, policy-driven solutions for the public.

Instead, Omtatah is banking on direct public engagement and youthful voter demographics to establish an independent platform rather than negotiating pre-election power-sharing pacts.

With the committee's mandate now extended through November 2026, the newly appointed leadership under Bwakali and Onyango will spend the coming months executing public outreach and finalizing feasibility reports that will dictate Omtatah's final ballot decision.
