The Registrar of Companies has wiped 72 entities from Kenya's official business roll and initiated the legal extinction of 68 more, deepening an aggressive administrative audit of corporate registers across the country.

The simultaneous actions, authorized by Deputy Registrar of Companies Hiram Gachugi through twin Gazette notices published on Friday, October 23, immediately strip the 72 businesses of their corporate legal personalities while setting a firm 90-day survival clock for dozens of others.

Invoking statutory powers under Section 897(4) of the Companies Act, the Registrar notified the public that the 72 entities were formally dissolved and their names expunged from the registry effective on the date of the notice.

### The Mechanics of Entity Extinction

![An aerial view of companies in Industrial Area in Nairobi](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/industrial_area.jpg.webp?itok=p5hP7uIN)

Under Kenya’s corporate statutory regime, striking a firm off the register under Section 897 terminates its legal existence. A dissolved company loses the right to hold assets, enter enforceable contracts, maintain banking operations, or initiate litigation in its corporate name, unless interested parties successfully pursue formal legal restoration through the courts.

While deregistrations under the Companies Act can arise either from voluntary winding-up applications filed by shareholders or through compulsory enforcement by the Registrar against defunct and non-compliant operations, the published notices did not specify the individual trigger for each business.

Crucially, the statutory declarations also offered no baseline data on whether the dissolved entities were operating concerns or abandoned paper shells, obscuring their real-world payroll and economic footprints.

### Broad Multi-Sector Footprint

The culling cut across both technical capital projects and consumer-facing enterprises. Among the 72 dissolved businesses are companies registered to undertake property development, solar power systems, water purification, healthcare delivery, textiles, business consulting, food processing, and media production.

### Back-to-Back Corporate Cleansing

The October purge marks an intensification of registry housekeeping rather than an isolated administrative action. Just four weeks earlier, in September 2026, the Registrar carried out an even larger sweep, dissolving 176 companies outright while earmarking 155 others for removal.

Taken together, the two consecutive enforcement rounds have removed 248 commercial entities from Kenya's corporate rolls within 60 days, while subjecting another 223 businesses to imminent regulatory termination.

For the 68 firms targeted in the latest gazette, the three-month statutory window offers a final administrative recourse. The Registrar indicated that any director, creditor, or interested party has until late January 2027 to show cause why the entities should not be permanently struck off.
