Vehicle registrations across Kenya crossed 207,000 units during the first six months of 2026, playing out against a backdrop of sweeping digital administrative changes and a court-mandated extension for imported cargo.

Data compiled by the Kenya National Bureau of Statistics (KNBS) and the National Transport and Safety Authority (NTSA) places total registrations between January and June at 207,259 vehicles. The figures reveal deep monthly fluctuations, opening with a yearly high of 37,650 units in January before sliding sharply to 28,137 vehicles in February—a 9,513-unit drop that marked the slowest registration period of the half-year.

![Kenyans lining up inside the National Transport and Safety Authority (NTSA) office in Nairobi in 2024.](https://i.ibb.co/Z6p9C9C6/8a1ef8ba3666.webp)

Activity rebounded through March to 35,798 units, dipped slightly to 34,041 in April, climbed to 36,299 in May, and settled at 35,334 units in June.

Two-wheelers continued to dominate the national fleet expansion. In June alone, motorcycles accounted for 22,696 of the 35,334 total registrations recorded that month. Station wagons and estate cars—led by models like the Toyota Fielder and Subaru Outback—formed the second-largest category with 6,717 units, followed by 1,254 three-wheelers.

Heavy commercial and public transport categories showed smaller volumes for June, including 895 lorries, 707 trailers, 608 pick-ups, 373 saloon cars, 296 vans, 160 minibuses, and 148 buses.

![Motorists on a Colossal Traffic Jam Along Busy Uhuru Highway in Nairobi](https://i.ibb.co/nsXK5Mdt/64bf93a1f4e4.webp)

This volume tracking coincided with a major internal shift at NTSA: the deployment of the electronic motor vehicle registration certificate, or eLogbook, designed to replace traditional paper certificates for initial registrations and ownership transfers. Despite the digital migration, June registrations fell by 965 units compared to May.

The administrative rollout ran parallel to a significant legal adjustment regarding imported vehicles. Following a High Court challenge that deemed the previous seven-day compliance window unfair, NTSA extended the mandatory registration period for incoming vehicles to 60 days from the date of arrival.

The extended timeline and ongoing digital integration set the framework for compliance as transport authorities monitor how vehicle owners adapt to the eLogbook system through the remainder of the year.
