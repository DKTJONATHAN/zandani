The executive branch entered an unshielded period of constitutional vulnerability on Thursday after the Court of Appeal refused to freeze a High Court order requiring President William Ruto to reconstitute his cabinet to comply with the two-thirds gender rule.

The decision strips the State Law Office of an interim protective buffer, leaving the administration operating under a standing judicial order after the 120-day compliance timeline expired on Wednesday, October 28.

Delivering the ruling on Thursday, October 29, the appellate bench dismissed the government's application for an injunction to stall the orders pending the determination of its full appeal. The state had argued that any reconstitution should pause until the appellate court conclusively decides the substantive petition. By denying the stay, the judges left the High Court decree fully enforceable and directed Attorney General Dorcas Oduor to serve the underlying appeal papers as substantive hearings proceed.

### The Arithmetic Behind the Breach

The dispute traces to a June 30 judgment by a three-judge High Court bench comprising Justices Eric Ogola, Stephen Githinji, and Jairus Ngaah. The bench established that the cabinet formed following the dissolution of the executive during the July 2024 nationwide protests fell below mandatory constitutional thresholds.

Under Article 27(8) of the Constitution, the state is barred from allocating more than two-thirds of appointed or elective bodies to a single gender. 

In scrutinising the 25-member cabinet—a lineup encompassing the President, Deputy President, Attorney General, and Cabinet Secretaries—the High Court documented only seven female members against 18 male members. That 28 per cent female representation fell short of the minimum legal baseline, which demands at least nine women in a 25-member cabinet to satisfy the one-third requirement.

While the High Court verified the legality of several individual nominations, it ruled the cumulative gender balance invalid and issued a 120-day transition clock for the appointing authority to realign the cabinet structure.

### Mounting Executive Pressure

The State Law Office’s failure to secure a stay alters the procedural dynamics for the presidency. With the October 28 deadline officially surpassed, the executive branch no longer holds a temporary legal pause while its appeal is heard.

The development places Attorney General Dorcas Oduor under immediate procedural obligations to process and serve court records, even as the administration confronts a binding judicial decree requiring changes to at least two cabinet portfolios to achieve the mandatory nine-woman quorum.
