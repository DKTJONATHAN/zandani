Prime Cabinet Secretary Musalia Mudavadi has announced the formal establishment of bilateral diplomatic relations between Kenya and the Solomon Islands, creating a direct operational link to the South Pacific intended to open trade, maritime, and knowledge channels for Kenyan entrepreneurs.

The pact was finalized through the signing of a Joint Communiqué with Solomon Islands Minister for Foreign Affairs and External Trade Rick Nelson Houenipwela on Tuesday, September 22, during the 81st session of the United Nations General Assembly (UNGA 81) in New York.

According to Mudavadi, the formalization of relations establishes an administrative framework for joint initiatives spanning the blue economy, climate resilience, vocational skills transfer, and cross-border commercial networks.

“This opens a new chapter for Kenya in the Pacific, creating a foundation to explore cooperation in trade, the blue economy, climate action, skills and knowledge exchange, while strengthening our collaboration on global issues that affect our people,” Mudavadi stated, noting that the agreement is designed to deliver direct commercial pathways for young Kenyan innovators and businesses.

![Prime Cabinet Secretary Musalia Mudavadi meets with Solomon Islands Minister Rick Nelson Houenipwela during UNGA 81, September 22, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/b4TfYfy/e42dd3722f81.webp)

### Moving Beyond Canberra Accreditation

The bilateral pact marks an operational pivot in Kenya’s management of its diplomatic footprint across Oceania. Historically, Nairobi’s foreign policy engagements in the region have been handled through third-party accreditation via its High Commission in Canberra, Australia, alongside representation in New Zealand.

By executing a direct bilateral treaty with Honiara, Kenya circumvents the administrative distance of proxy representation, allowing both states to engage directly on maritime jurisdiction, ocean resource management, and international environmental lobbying.

The move aligns with Nairobi’s broader diplomatic strategy of expanding sovereign bilateral ties with small island developing states (SIDS), which frequently form pivotal voting blocs on global climate finance and ocean governance within multilateral bodies.

### Expanding Island Alliances at UNGA 81

The agreement with the Solomon Islands formed part of a coordinated push by Kenya’s foreign affairs desk to deepen ties across island economies during the New York summit.

![Prime Cabinet Secretary Musalia Mudavadi meets with Commonwealth Secretary-General Ayorkor Botchwey and other officials during UNGA 81, September 23, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/mFHR0GhT/37b3fbb08453.webp)

In a parallel bilateral engagement, Mudavadi met with Antigua and Barbuda Minister for Foreign Affairs Chet Greene to structure joint trade, investment, and blue-economy frameworks between East Africa and the Caribbean.

“For Kenya, stronger Caribbean partnerships can open new markets and connections for our businesses, entrepreneurs and young people while strengthening Africa’s voice on global issues that directly affect our future,” Mudavadi said of the Caribbean talks.

Mudavadi also held consultative talks with Commonwealth Secretary-General Ayorkor Botchwey to align Kenya's national trade and youth employment priorities with the secretariat's operational programs.

“We discussed strengthening Kenya’s engagement within the Commonwealth. Our focus is on practical outcomes: expanding trade and investment, creating opportunities for young people through skills, entrepreneurship and innovation, advancing climate action and strengthening cooperation on sustainable development, peace and security,” he noted.

Following the signing of the communiqué, both foreign ministries are expected to initiate technical-level discussions to map out specific bilateral trade instruments and cooperation protocols in ocean management.
