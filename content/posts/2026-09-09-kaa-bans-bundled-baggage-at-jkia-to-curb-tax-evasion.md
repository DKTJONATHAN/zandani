The Kenya Airports Authority has outlawed the practice of bundling multiple passengers' luggage together on inbound flights to Jomo Kenyatta International Airport. Travellers caught arriving with tied-together bags will have their luggage separated and returned at their own expense, following a directive aimed at halting commercial cargo smuggling masquerading as personal baggage.

**What we know**
* Tied or conjoined bags arriving at JKIA will be decoupled and returned to the origin port at the passenger's personal cost.
* Airlines that facilitate unmanifested cargo through passenger terminals risk suspension by the Kenya Civil Aviation Authority.
* Advanced Passenger Registration and the Automatic Terminal Information Service will go live at the hub within four weeks.
* JKIA handles 8.8 million passengers and 358 kilotonnes of cargo annually, with a KSh 154.2 billion terminal expansion underway over a 54-month timeline.

State aviation officials confirmed that the crackdown targets traders who bypass cargo tariffs by combining heavy parcels under single passenger tickets. The practice has allowed importers to skirt Kenya Revenue Authority customs duties on goods that ought to be declared and cleared through commercial freight channels.

Airlines flying into Embakasi must now enforce strict luggage segregation before departure. Carriers are required to route all commercial shipments through dedicated cargo sheds rather than passenger carousels. Ground handling staff who accept bundled luggage will face sanctions under International Air Transport Association and KCAA safety protocols.

Airport management has begun reconfiguring the terminal floors to support single-stream passenger screening and centralized check-in desks. The Ministry of Transport is also installing the Automatic Terminal Information Service alongside an Advanced Passenger Registration platform to track incoming passenger manifests against actual baggage counts before flights touch down on the runway.

These operational shifts run parallel to the KSh 154.2 billion modernization project. The 54-month construction contract aims to raise the airport's annual handling ceiling from eight million to 12 million passengers, easing congestion across arrival halls that currently process 358,000 tonnes of freight each year.

### What it costs you

If you run a boutique in Dubois Street or an electronics stall in Eastleigh, the cheap luggage run from Guangzhou or Dubai just ran out of road. For years, small-scale importers beat KRA tax bands by hiring mules or convincing seatmates to pool luggage weight allowances, stuffing three cartons into one plastic-wrapped bundle. That loophole is shut. KAA wants the cargo cleared through proper freight forwarders at the Swissport and Mitchell Cotts sheds, where duty assessors wait with calculators.

A trader will now pay freight rates per kilo alongside standard customs duties instead of splitting passenger baggage fees. Those costs will land directly on the retail shelf. When you buy a replacement alternator along Kirinyaga Road or pick up a dress off Jamia Mall, expect the price tag to reflect the formal cargo tariff. KRA gains revenue, and the airport clears up its clogged arrival belts. The small trader standing in traffic along Airport North Road is the one left holding the bill.
