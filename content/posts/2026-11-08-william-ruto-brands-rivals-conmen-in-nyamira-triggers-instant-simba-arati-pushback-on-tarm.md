President William Ruto dropped the polite diplomatic script on Sunday, using an interdenominational church pulpit at Nyamaiya Stadium in Nyamira County to openly brand his political competitors "conmen" who have zero clues about building national infrastructure.

Addressing the congregation on November 8, 2026, the Head of State warned the Gusii region against entertaining political rhetoric that offers no tangible roadmap for the bread-and-butter issues shaping ordinary households ahead of the 2027 General Election.

"They must tell us where we want to go as a nation. The other guys do not have a plan; they have no ideas about roads," Ruto said, pitching his administration as the sole camp anchored on practical delivery. "Kenyans want roads for the cost of living to go down, education to be streamlined, they want the prices of fertiliser to come down and brokers to be eliminated in agriculture."

Taking aim directly at regional voters, the President urged the crowd to interrogate the credentials of anyone pitching alternative governance: "Do not entertain conmen. They must tell you what they will do for you, how agriculture will be improved, jobs created and infrastructure upgraded."

### Simba Arati’s Podium Reality Check

If the executive playbook aimed to leave the crowd focused purely on the opposition’s alleged deficits, Kisii Governor Simba Arati ensured the morning did not pass as an uncritical endorsement rally.

Taking the microphone in front of the presidential delegation, Arati offered a sharp, unvarnished counterpoint on the very subject Ruto raised: tarmac. Pointing directly to the disparities on the ground across the Gusii belt, the outspoken county boss reminded the President that national treasury allocations must mirror citizen contributions, regardless of political affiliation.

"There are some places where people have not seen tarmac. We must have equity in Kenya; we all pay taxes," Arati emphasized, cutting through the church podium pleasantries with a blunt demand for visible executive accountability.

Arati’s intervention shifted the afternoon’s energy, highlighting the uneasy tension between the national government's broad promises and the everyday frustrations of local leaders who face voters demanding paved corridors and basic public works.

### Allies Rally Around Non-Tribal Politics

While Arati pressed on tarmac equity, parliamentary allies from the region stepped forward to cushion the President’s political message. South Mugirango MP Silvanus Osoro backed Ruto’s aggressive posture, urging the electorate to discard ethnic voting blocs and hold politicians accountable strictly on policy merit.

"We want to compete on ideas, not tribes, and then the people will decide," Osoro said, echoing the executive argument that political legitimacy heading into 2027 should rest entirely on verifiable delivery.

Nyaribari Chache MP Zaheer Jhanda followed suit, praising the rollout of grassroots state projects throughout Kisii and Nyamira and urging residents to stay aligned with national government initiatives rather than leaning into political skepticism.

Responding to the room's contrasting political undercurrents, President Ruto sought to assure the Gusii leadership that his administration would not weaponize development against perceived political strongholds.

"We do not want politics that brings division and hatred. There is no part of this country that is in Kenya by accident. We want every part of the country to be developed," the President declared.

### The Gusii Gathering Lineup

The Nyamaiya Stadium service drew an influential cross-section of cabinet executives and regional lawmakers. Flanking the President were Education Cabinet Secretary Julius Ogamba and Transport Cabinet Secretary Davis Chirchir, both key figures overseeing the school reforms and road construction portfolios placed at the center of the morning’s debate.

Host Governor Amos Nyaribo of Nyamira joined the dais alongside several Gusii lawmakers, including North Mugirango MP Joash Nyamoko, Bonchari MP Charles Onchoke, and Nyaribari Masaba MP Daniel Manduku.

With Ruto drawing a sharp line against his critics and Arati placing unpaved roads on the national ledger, the Sunday gathering signaled that the battle for the Gusii vote heading into 2027 will be fought not just over sweeping political labels, but on the exact tarmac laid across local wards.
