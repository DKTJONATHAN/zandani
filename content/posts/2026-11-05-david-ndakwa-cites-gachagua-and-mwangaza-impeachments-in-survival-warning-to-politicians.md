Member of Parliament David Ndakwa has turned the country's recent wave of high-profile political exits into a public warning for colleagues, arguing that backing the wrong camp in Kenya's current political climate is a direct fast-track out of government.

Speaking on the dynamics shaping realignments across the political divide, Ndakwa used the ousted leadership of former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua, former Meru Governor Kawira Mwangaza, and former Agriculture Cabinet Secretary Mithika Linturi as concrete proof that factional loyalty carries immediate operational risks.

Rather than treating political alliances as abstract party discipline, Ndakwa framed them as a matter of raw career survival. He argued that politicians who tie their stock to struggling leaders routinely share in the collateral damage when those key figures fall from grace.

“Look at the political dynamics around us: those who aligned with Kawira Mwangaza saw her impeached, those who walked with Gachagua saw him impeached, and the same fate met Linturi,” Ndakwa stated. “Political alliances carry real consequences, and those who choose the wrong side ultimately fall out of governance.”

The three cases cited by the lawmaker represent some of the most dramatic power shifts in recent political memory. Mwangaza faced multiple impeachment pushes in Meru before her final exit, while Gachagua's historic impeachment reshuffled the executive hierarchy. Linturi similarly lost his cabinet footing following intense political scrutiny and legislative proceedings.

For Ndakwa, the trend across these administrative collapses shows a clear pattern: politicians who fail to read executive shifts or who align with isolated factions risk losing both political standing and operational power long before voters head to the polls.

Building on his warning to colleagues, the legislator pivoted directly to President William Ruto's administration, making a case for political stability under the Head of State. Ndakwa argued that supporting the sitting President offers the clearest path for leaders looking to deliver on development promises while maintaining their political longevity.

He urged Kenyans and political actors alike to grant President Ruto a complete two-term mandate, maintaining that ten years are required to fully implement the administration's policy vision.

“When you look at everything President William Samoei Ruto has accomplished for this country, the results speak for themselves,” Ndakwa said. “Given his track record of delivery and political resilience, we should stand behind his vision and grant him a full ten-year mandate to complete the transformation of Kenya.”

Ndakwa's public stance comes at a time when politicians across regions are weighing their positions ahead of the 2027 General Election. As party figures assess potential coalitions and executive lineups, his remarks underline how previous impeachment votes continue to cast a long shadow over political decision-making, serving as a reminder of how quickly political fortunes can change in Nairobi.
