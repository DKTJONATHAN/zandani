Musician Bahati knew his high-cost parenting experiment had run off course the moment his firstborn son, Morgan, served his mother with a written grievance demanding privacy in his bedroom.

The note to Diana Marua was polite, phrased in fluent English, and unmistakably firm: *“I don’t like it mom when you come to my room and you don’t knock.”*

For Bahati, who grew up as an orphan navigating the hardline realities of Mathare before building a commercial music and media profile, the formal complaint was not an amusing milestone of adolescent independence. It was the breaking point of a two-year standoff.

Speaking on the *Obinna TV* podcast, the singer laid bare the private domestic friction that prompted him to withdraw Morgan from an international school costing KES 500,000 per term and send him to a rural boarding institution in Makueni County.

### The Half-Million Shilling Bubble

Bahati explained that the decision to enroll Morgan into the elite school circuit was initially driven by the desire to afford his son every opportunity he had lacked in his own youth. But the environment quickly introduced unintended cultural side effects.

Surrounded by the children of governors and affluent Kenyan dynasties, Morgan began adopting the detached habits of privilege, gradually shedding basic personal duties around the house.

“You see in these group of schools, these kids wanapatana na watoto wa governor, watoto wa the who is who and I think at some point, he forgot where he came from,” Bahati explained during the podcast session.

The shift manifested in simple daily refusals. Morgan stopped making his bed and showed growing indifference toward basic household chores, treating personal staff and family routines as services to be taken for granted.

Bahati spent more than twenty-four months trying to counter the drift using patient, conversational parenting. He repeatedly sat Morgan down to remind him of their family foundation and the financial discipline required to fund his elite lifestyle. But gentle persuasion yielded no measurable shift.

“He became too comfortable to a point he forgot small, small chores like making his bed,” Bahati said. “So that became an issue and I sat down, trying to talk to him and reminding him about the background, but at some point we realized we were losing our firstborn.”

### The Makueni Detour

Convinced that domestic conversations could not compete with the peer pressures of an ultra-wealthy school ecosystem, Bahati sought a complete environmental break.

A return to Mathare, where Bahati spent his earliest years, was ruled out as counterproductive. Instead, the musician looked upcountry. He placed a direct call to Makueni Governor Mutula Kilonzo Jr, seeking a personal recommendation for a strict, top-performing public boarding school located far away from Nairobi's social circuits.

Once the school was identified in the rural interior of Makueni, Bahati handled the admission process with unusual urgency. Worried that Morgan's academic complacency would see him fail the mandatory entry interview, Bahati personally engaged the school principal to secure the placement, asking the administration to overlook initial performance gaps in exchange for a clean institutional slate.

The shift was immediate and austere. Stripped of Nairobi comforts, private bedrooms, and elite peer benchmarks, Morgan was thrust into the communal demands of traditional boarding life.

### The Lenana Turnaround

The environmental shock produced the exact recalibration Bahati had failed to achieve across two years of living room discussions. Realizing the security of his privileged trajectory was contingent on discipline and output, Morgan adjusted his study habits.

His grades climbed consistently across successive terms, exceeding the family's baseline expectations and reversing his earlier academic slump.

That upcountry performance ultimately positioned Morgan to compete for placement in prestigious national schools, culminating in his admission to Lenana School in Nairobi.

The character turnaround followed him back to the capital. At Lenana, Morgan was appointed head prefect for Grade 10, completing a disciplinary arc that began with an unmade bed and a written bedroom complaint in a Nairobi suburb.
