Corporate marketing expenditure across Kenya is undergoing a rapid financial migration, with online advertising revenue projected to nearly double from approximately Ksh 40.6 billion in 2025 to Ksh 78.2 billion by the end of the decade.

According to data from PwC’s Africa Entertainment and Media Outlook 2026–2030, this commercial reallocation is part of a broader household and business shift where internet connectivity will account for more than 75 percent of the country’s total entertainment and media spending. By 2030, this connectivity outlay is expected to touch roughly Ksh 589.1 billion, driven largely by mobile broadband adoption and the growing economic weight of online services.

![A person about to click an app on a phone.](https://i.ibb.co/Ldvsn1Sn/8a822110c669.webp)

Video advertising is emerging as the primary catalyst for this digital windfall, poised to outpace traditional display formats by 2027. Platforms such as TikTok and YouTube are absorbing the bulk of corporate marketing budgets as brands aggressively compete for screen time among digitally native consumers. For content creators, digital marketers, and e-commerce enterprises, the expanding online ecosystem promises lucrative brand partnerships and targeted sponsored-content streams.

At the same time, the transition is forcing legacy channels to adapt rather than perish. Traditional television revenue is projected to post moderate gains, moving from approximately Ksh 43.1 billion in 2025 to Ksh 49 billion by 2030, even as high-speed 5G networks expand to cover more than half the market.

![File image of Kenyans using smartphones](https://i.ibb.co/N2xdnkNy/4462f2a4f81a.webp)

With households dedicating three-quarters of their digital consumption budgets to keeping connected, the commercial battleground has permanently shifted from physical print and billboards to algorithms and mobile feeds as businesses race to capture consumer attention online.
