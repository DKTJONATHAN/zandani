The High Court has affirmed the investigative autonomy of the Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission, clearing detectives to pursue a Nairobi advocate accused of soliciting Ksh10 million from former Cabinet Secretary Raphael Tuju to sway an active commercial case.

In a determination rendered on October 7, the court dismissed a constitutional petition filed by the advocate to halt the inquiry, ruling that regulatory agencies cannot be restrained absent exceptional proof of administrative misconduct.

“The Petitioner has failed to demonstrate that the EACC’s probe has crossed the high threshold of malice, clear lack of factual foundation, illegality, or ultra vires action to warrant judicial interference,” the court held.

The judgment directly benefits anti-graft prosecutors by reinforcing the legal threshold required to stop active white-collar inquiries. By declining to intervene before formal charges are registered, the bench preserved the commission’s mandate to investigate allegations of judicial interference arising from high-stakes corporate debt battles.

### The Karen Operation and Marked Currency

The dispute originates from a formal grievance submitted by Tuju on March 9. The former Cabinet Secretary alleged that the advocate, acting in concert with a businessman and a now-deceased former High Court judge, demanded Ksh10 million to broker a favourable outcome from a sitting judge.

At the center of the alleged arrangement was a bitter row before the High Court’s Commercial and Tax Division, where lenders sought to auction prime assets linked to Tuju, including the Dari Business Park and Entim Sidai hospitality properties, to recover defaulted commercial loans.

Following Tuju’s report, EACC operatives initiated surveillance, equipping the former minister with recording devices to capture conversations detailing the financial demands. The covert operation culminated in an ambush at a wellness club in the Karen suburb of Nairobi, where detectives arrested the advocate alongside two associates and seized Ksh1 million in specially treated currency supplied for the sting.

### Limits of Pre-Charge Intervention

In his challenge against the anti-corruption agency, the lawyer argued that the covert setup constituted unlawful entrapment, asserting that the commission overstepped statutory boundaries and infringed on his fundamental rights during the Karen raid. He asked the bench to declare the entire probe unconstitutional and bar any subsequent prosecution.

The High Court rejected that reasoning, finding no evidence that the anti-graft body acted outside its statutory mandate. The court clarified that challenges concerning the manner of evidence collection, including entrapment claims and procedural propriety during arrests, belong within the trial court once formal criminal proceedings commence, rather than through preemptive constitutional petitions.

With the constitutional challenge dismissed, the investigative file remains under active review at Integrity Centre. EACC detectives are finalizing inquiries to determine whether to transmit the docket to the Office of the Director of Public Prosecutions for formal indictment, while the underlying commercial proceedings involving Tuju's properties continue before the Commercial and Tax Division.
