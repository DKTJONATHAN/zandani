Vehicle owners in Kenya face direct criminal prosecution if unlicensed minors gain access to their steering wheels, as the National Transport and Safety Authority (NTSA) moves to enforce statutory liability provisions governing motor vehicle custody ahead of the end-of-year travel surge.

In a road safety directive issued on November 2, 2026, the transport regulator warned that permissive oversight or failing to prevent a child under 18 from operating a vehicle constitutes an explicit offense under national traffic laws. The warning arrives as school holidays release students back home, coinciding with heightened highway travel where unsupervised minor driving historically risks spiking.

### Statutory Age Boundaries

![NTSA mounts a multiagency roadblock along a major highway in Kenya](https://i.ibb.co/8DtMQ5d5/d7b878b31d06.webp)

Under the Traffic Act (CAP 403), the law establishes strict age thresholds for motor vehicle operations across public roads. While individuals aged 16 may qualify for provisional licenses restricted strictly to motorcycles, a full or provisional motor vehicle driving license cannot be issued to anyone under 18 years.

Because minors under 18 lack legal qualification to hold a car license, any instance of an underage driver behind the wheel transfers primary legal jeopardy to the registered owner or custodian who enabled access.

"Don't let your child become the next statistic. The minimum driving age in Kenya is 18 years. Allowing, or failing to prevent, an underage or unlicensed person from driving is an offence," NTSA stated.

![The National Transport and Safety Authority (NTSA) Director of Road Safety, Andrew Kiplagat, addressing the press in November 2025.](https://i.ibb.co/9HHQ16ZK/414c862297a2.webp)

### Key Custody and Vehicle Tracking

The authority instructed vehicle owners to maintain physical control over car keys at all times, regardless of whether a minor has acquired practical driving knowledge. Alongside physical key restriction, NTSA advised car owners to maintain active tracking of vehicle locations during the holiday period and hold explicit household conversations regarding the legal and physical hazards of unauthorized driving.

Statutory enforcement against permissive vehicle owners carries established precedent in Kenyan courts. In an earlier prosecution, police arrested and charged a parent after finding their seven-year-old child driving a vehicle on a public road. The court convicted the parent, imposing a Ksh 20,000 fine with a three-month custodial sentence alternative for failing to prevent the minor from driving.

As travel traffic builds across national corridors, NTSA has called on motorists to enforce strict internal vehicle accountability, with multiagency enforcement units monitoring compliance as holiday movement increases.
