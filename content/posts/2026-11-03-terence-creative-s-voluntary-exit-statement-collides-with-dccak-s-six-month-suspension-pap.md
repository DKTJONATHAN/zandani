Comedian Terence Creative stepped onto his social media handles on Monday, November 2, 2026, to publish a formal departure letter, framing his exit from the Digital Content Creators Association of Kenya (DCCAK) as an immediate personal resignation from his post as Secretary General. But within hours of his announcement, the creators' body released its own accounting of the executive desk—disclosing that the comedian had been suspended from office six months earlier.

In a letter addressed directly to DCCAK's chairperson and executive committee, the content creator—known for his viral "Kamami" sketch persona—thanked the organisation for the opportunity to hold executive power before declaring his role vacant.

“I hereby tender my resignation as Secretary General of the Digital Content Creators Association of Kenya (DCCAK), effective immediately,” Terence wrote in the statement. “Thank you for the opportunity to serve the Association.”

He added that despite stepping down, his commitment to the sector remained active beyond the formal secretariat.

“My commitment to creative empowerment remains strong. I will continue supporting creators through skills development, mentorship and opportunities that promote sustainable livelihoods in Kenya’s creative economy,” he wrote.

The polished, voluntary tone of the comedian's statement was promptly matched by a clarifying counter-narrative from DCCAK leadership. The association issued a public response confirming Terence's departure, but reframed the entire timeline around his tenure.

According to DCCAK, Terence was officially suspended from his Secretary General duties on May 2, 2026—six months prior to his public resignation post. The association further revealed that Joan Wanjiku Waweru was installed as acting Secretary General immediately following that May action to manage executive operations.

The rapid succession of statements exposed a clear disconnect in how the two sides framed the end of Terence’s leadership term. While Terence presented his departure as a fresh, independent decision to step back and focus on creator mentorship, DCCAK’s public statement treated his Monday post as the formal closing of a half-year administrative procedure that had already stripped him of executive authority.

Terence’s transition into content advocacy had marked one of the most high-profile leadership appointments within Kenya’s digital creative sector. Having built a massive online brand through character-driven skits and satirical entertainment, his elevation to DCCAK’s secretariat was designed to give mainstream credibility to institutional representation for online talent.

With Joan Wanjiku Waweru remaining in place as acting Secretary General, DCCAK’s administrative ledger now reflects a complete transition, even as neither party has publicly detailed the internal issues that triggered the initial May 2 suspension.
