Commercial airlines operating routes to the United States face an abrupt operational overhaul after American federal regulators ordered carriers worldwide to refuse boarding to any traveller who fails to sign a mandatory declaration concerning recent movement in the Democratic Republic of Congo.

The directive issued by the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) turns airline departure gates into a binding biosecurity checkpoint. Under the rules taking effect at 11:59 pm on November 3, 2026, foreign carriers must compel every US-bound passenger to certify—either on paper or through digital portals—that they have not set foot in the DRC within the preceding 21 days.

Travellers who decline or fail to supply the attestation cannot be issued boarding passes or cleared onto planes.

### Strict Six-Week Paper Trail

![Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale, during a meeting at Afya House, with an inset of an Ebola Virus Bundibugyo Strain under a microscope.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/aden-duale-ebola-virus.jpg.webp?itok=6K0Ay4qz)

Beyond screening passengers at check-in desks, the CDC order imposes an extended administrative burden on airline operations: carriers are legally required to archive a complete copy of every traveller’s attestation for at least 42 days after the flight touches down on American soil.

The 42-day retention window mirrors the standard double-incubation surveillance period used in viral containment, creating an auditable contact tracing registry should an infection surface weeks after entry.

The mandate leaves no exemptions based on point of origin. The CDC confirmed the directive applies universally to all flights leaving foreign soil for the United States, including international services routing through overseas pre-clearance ports.

### Regional Cross-Border Transmission

![trump-deport-plane](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/trump-deport-plane.jpg.webp?itok=Abdu25cE)

The American enforcement push follows weeks of heightened bio-surveillance across East Africa. Kenya has been working to contain an active chain of transmission that began when an infected traveller crossed into the country from the DRC via Uganda, arriving in Nairobi on October 3.

According to the Ministry of Health, that patient subsequently died from the haemorrhagic fever. Health surveillance teams have since identified nine secondary infections among immediate contacts, forcing dozens of individuals into strict institutional quarantine.

The cross-border transmission has triggered broader diplomatic and mobility headwinds for the region. On Tuesday, October 20, the US Department of State elevated its travel advisory for Kenya to Level 3, urging American citizens to reconsider travel to the country due to combined health concerns over Ebola and ongoing domestic security risks.

At Kenyan border points and Jomo Kenyatta International Airport, health desks have already stepped up physical thermal scanning and passenger screening protocols. The impending November 3 US timeline now shifts significant compliance liability directly onto international ticketing systems and airline check-in staff, who must manage boarding gate verifications before departures leave African transit hubs.
