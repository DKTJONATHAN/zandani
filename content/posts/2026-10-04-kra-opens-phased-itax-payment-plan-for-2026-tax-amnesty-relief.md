The Kenya Revenue Authority has opened a phased settlement pathway for distressed taxpayers, allowing individuals and businesses carrying legacy arrears to stagger principal tax payments through an automated portal without immediately losing statutory debt write-offs.

Under the directive, taxpayers unable to settle accumulated arrears in a single lump sum can schedule structured instalments using the Automatic Payment Plan on the iTax platform. The provision is designed to cushion cash-strapped enterprises and individual filers, granting them breathing room to manage liquidity while remaining eligible for complete relief on punitive state surcharges under the 2026 Tax Amnesty Programme.

"Unable to clear your principal tax in one go? Apply for an Automatic Payment Plan on iTax and pay in instalments," the tax authority announced in an advisory to taxpayers.

![Customers being attended to at KRA's offices](https://i.ibb.co/TsBwrrm/bd2e41f781dd.webp)

### The All-or-Nothing Deadline

The installment option, however, carries a strict compliance condition: the entire principal debt must be settled in full before December 31, 2026. Any taxpayer who enrolls in the structured schedule but fails to extinguish the principal balance before the cutoff date will lose entitlement to the relief.

"Pay in full by December 31, 2026, and penalties and interest will be waived automatically," the authority stated, underscoring that incomplete payments will void the statutory concession.

Under the framework of the amnesty, taxpayers who satisfy the full-settlement criteria will receive a 100 percent waiver on all fines, interest, and penalties that accumulated on tax liabilities accrued up to December 31, 2025. The relief applies retroactively across historical debts, including unpaid principal sums dating back before January 2026, provided the core liability is completely cleared during the current amnesty window.

### Automatic Relief for Return Filers

The authority also clarified the operational terms for taxpayers who do not owe core principal taxes but face administrative sanctions. Filers burdened solely by statutory late-filing fines—including the routine Ksh2,000 charge levied for submitting returns past statutory dates—qualify for the 100 percent waiver once they file all missing declarations.

For this group, the write-off operates without requiring an active payment schedule or formal debt application. Once the outstanding returns are logged on iTax and the system registers zero principal exposure, the associated penalties are written down automatically.

The taxman has advised taxpayers across all categories to access their iTax accounts directly and review their digital tax ledgers. Because the system applies waivers through programmatic ledger updates, filers must verify that their historical penalty balances reflect the write-off before the December 31 closeout seals the amnesty register.
