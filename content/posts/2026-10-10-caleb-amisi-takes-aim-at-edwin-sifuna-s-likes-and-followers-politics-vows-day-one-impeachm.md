Saboti MP Caleb Amisi is not buying into the online hype surrounding Nairobi’s political heavyweights, warning that social media vanity metrics will do little to stop President William Ruto in 2027.

Taking aim at Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna’s presidential posturing, the People’s Renaissance Movement (PRM) party leader questioned whether the senator’s inner circle has any realistic nationwide apparatus beyond chasing online applause.

According to Amisi, relying on viral engagement and high follower counts is a losing formula against an entrenched incumbent.

Amisi argued that Ruto would comfortably brush aside Sifuna in a head-to-head contest if the opposition settles on the outspoken senator as its flagbearer, accusing those managing Sifuna's campaign of mistaking internet popularity for genuine political ground game.

He contended that Sifuna's handlers have spent their energy obsessing over algorithms and social validation rather than building the disciplined party structures required to run a viable 47-county presidential operation.

### The 233-Seat Threshold and the Swearing-In Altar

Yet while Amisi dismissed the influencer style of politics, his own alternative playbook relies on an equally bold ambition: vacuuming up an outright supermajority in the National Assembly to wield over the executive like a guillotine.

Amisi declared that PRM is formally setting its sights on capturing 233 seats in the National Assembly during the 2027 General Election—the exact two-thirds constitutional threshold required to pass a presidential impeachment motion without needing outside coalition partners.

Rather than putting his energy into fronting an opposition flagbearer, Amisi insisted his party’s sole mandate will be policing whoever takes the oath of office at State House, promising no grace period for the winner.

“What we are saying as a party is that the opposition is making mistakes. They are not united,” Amisi said. “If they make a mistake and William Ruto comes back, our party has got you. Why? Because we want to have 233 members of parliament.”

His warning for Ruto was blunt about what happens the moment the chief justice steps away from the podium.

“William Ruto comes back, he puts down the Bible, then we impeach him,” Amisi declared. “Whether or not he has stolen or done what, we will impeach him.”

### Scrutiny Across the Political Divide

Amisi made it clear that his legislative crosshairs are not reserved exclusively for the current head of state. Turning toward the wider field of opposition hopefuls auditioning to replace Ruto, the Saboti lawmaker insisted that no contender would enjoy a honeymoon period if PRM delivers on its ambitious legislative slate.

He explicitly named Wiper Democratic Movement leader Kalonzo Musyoka, former Chief Justice David Maraga, and former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua alongside Sifuna as figures who would instantly find themselves subject to the same aggressive parliamentary pressure.

Winning an election, Amisi stressed, is not a shield against accountability, especially if power alters a new administration's conduct.

“You bring a new person, say Kalonzo, Sifuna, Maraga, or Gachagua, then they fail in their work, start killing youths, power gets into their heads. We will impeach them,” Amisi warned.

### Confronting Opposition Fractures

Behind Amisi's sweeping promises sits a stinging diagnosis of the current opposition landscape. He maintained that fragmented ambitions and internal competition among leading anti-government figures are directly clearing a path for Ruto's second term.

By positioning PRM as an independent legislative check rather than another player in the endless opposition coalition talks, Amisi is pitching voters on a strategy of parliamentary containment.

Whether a newly minted party can realistically organize nationwide to win 233 seats remains a massive operational hurdle. Under Kenya’s constitutional architecture, unseating a sitting president requires not just raw numbers on the floor of the National Assembly, but an airtight legal threshold detailing gross violation of the law or gross misconduct, followed by an equally demanding trial in the Senate.

For now, Amisi is betting that voters disillusioned by both State House and opposition clout-chasing will buy into his promise of legislative muscle over social media stardom.
