According to the National Treasury, commercial transporters and regional motorists along Kenya’s busiest transit artery are slated to gain a minimum four-lane, access-controlled carriageway, as the government advances the procurement of a transaction adviser to guide the expansion of the 461-kilometre Nairobi–Mombasa highway.

A ministerial status report reveals that the state concluded receiving submissions for an Expression of Interest published on August 11, 2026, and has commenced the evaluation of prospective consulting firms. The chosen transaction adviser will be tasked with determining the technical, economic, and environmental viability of converting the two-lane corridor into a tolled public-private partnership (PPP) asset.

Rather than carving out a separate expressway footprint across unsettled terrain, the state has formally anchored the initiative on the existing right-of-way, requiring designers to minimize disruptions to towns and markets strung along the arterial.

### The Advisory Scope

The incoming consultant faces an extensive analytical remit before civil works can be tendered to concessionaires. According to Treasury documents, the team must assess traffic projections, structural engineering requirements, social and environmental impact profiles, and bankability metrics.

![Director General of the Public Private Partnerships (PPP) Directorate at the National Treasury during the Inter Agency Project Steering Committee meeting overseeing the implementat](https://i.ibb.co/gZrkMkVM/a615340c7424.webp)

Treasury outlines that the core scope requires expanding the existing 461 kilometres into at least four lanes with restricted access points. Tolling gantries will be built into the layout to recover private capital investments, transforming the free primary highway into a commercialized toll facility.

“The project scope comprises expanding it to a minimum of four lanes and converting it into a tolled road, while incorporating cost-effective designs to minimise impact on local connectivity,” Treasury noted in its status update.

State planners view the dualing as an economic imperative for regional logistics. The corridor carries the bulk of freight moving from the Port of Mombasa inland, serving as a critical artery of the Trans-African Highway network connecting Kenya to landlocked economies across Central and Eastern Africa.

### The Greenfield Pivot

The push for an advisory tender reflects a decisive institutional departure from an earlier Ksh365 billion private initiative that sought to build an entirely new alignment.

In 2024, American private equity consortium Everstrong Capital tendered a 2,500-page privately initiated proposal to construct the "Usahihi Expressway"—a six-lane, 440-kilometre greenfield bypass intended to run alongside the existing corridor. That pitch was struck down by the National Treasury’s Public Private Partnership Committee, which ruled that the submission failed to meet statutory conditions outlined in the PPP Act, 2021.

![The Nairobi Expressway Mlolongo Toll Station along the Nairobi-Mombasa highway.](https://i.ibb.co/kV52p6M3/9b2c3fb8f7b1.webp)

Regulators subsequently instructed the American consortium to discard the greenfield concept and restructure any prospective engagement around the widening of the existing two-lane route.

The regulatory rejection triggered formal resistance. In April 2026, the PPP Petitions Committee dismissed an administrative petition filed by Everstrong challenging the state's evaluation of its feasibility documentation, prompting the firm to indicate plans to seek judicial review in the High Court.

### Infrastructure Priority

Despite the friction with previous proponents, the state has absorbed the expansion into its Medium-Term Plan priorities, resolving to procure the infrastructure under government-led PPP provisions.

By converting the existing road into a four-lane tolled bypass, Treasury is balancing heavy freight throughput against local access needs. Controlled entry and exit ramps will be introduced to maintain high-speed logistical flow while accommodating localized traffic that depends on the corridor for daily trade.

The conclusion of the transaction advisory evaluation will clear the way for full feasibility sign-offs, followed by commercial contracting to identify the consortium that will finance, widen, and toll the route.
