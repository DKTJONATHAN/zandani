NAIROBI - Pastor Victor Kanyari has publicly confirmed the passing of a former partner with whom he had previously intended to settle down, marking a moment of personal reflection shared during a church service held on Wednesday, September 9, 2026.

Addressing his congregation, the preacher disclosed that the woman, who had been battling cancer, was a significant figure during the formative years of his ministry before his rise to national prominence. Kanyari stated that she had provided essential support during his period of financial hardship, noting, “I don’t forget my friends. Even if they have married or not married, I always remember my friends.”

According to Kanyari, his historical connection with the deceased was deep enough that he had formally introduced her to his mother with the expressed intent of marriage. The preacher attributed the eventual dissolution of their relationship to his mother’s disapproval, which he stated was rooted in concerns regarding the woman’s struggles with alcohol consumption.

Kanyari elaborated that despite their separation and the woman’s subsequent marriage to another man, he maintained a supportive role in her life. He claimed to have financed the education of her three children at a private institution and stated he had attempted to facilitate her recovery through professional rehabilitation services, though he noted these efforts were ultimately unsuccessful. During his address, Kanyari linked her long-term alcohol use to the development of the illness that resulted in her death, though this claim remains his own assertion.

The preacher expressed his intention to remain involved in the final arrangements for the deceased, confirming that he has already cleared her outstanding mortuary fees and plans to be in attendance at the burial. “That’s my msichana,” Kanyari stated, as he recounted her history of assisting with the upkeep of his church premises, including the cleaning of his footwear during his early days as a minister.

This public acknowledgment follows the mourning period for his former wife, the gospel musician Betty Bayo, who passed away in November 2025. Kanyari and Bayo, who had shared two children, had been separated for several years prior to her death, though they continued to co-parent until her passing.
