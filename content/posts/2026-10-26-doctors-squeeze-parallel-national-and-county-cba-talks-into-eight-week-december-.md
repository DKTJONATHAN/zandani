The Kenya Medical Practitioners, Pharmacists and Dentists Union (KMPDU) has opened a high-stakes, two-front bargaining round with national and county health employers, giving negotiators barely eight weeks to deliver a harmonised 2025–2029 Collective Bargaining Agreement before the end of December.

The push compresses talks for doctors across all 47 counties and the national government into a single calendar stretch. It formally moves the union from public agitation into structured technical sessions, following a joint resolution reached by its National Advisory Council and National Executive Council on October 16.

Yet the accelerated timetable collides directly with a fiscal squeeze in the devolved units. Just weeks before talks convened, the Salaries and Remuneration Commission (SRC) suspended reviewed pay and benefits for top county executives, service boards, and county attorneys over mounting distress regarding the sustainability of devolved wage bills.

### Twin Bargaining Tables

![The Kenya Medical Practitioners Pharmacists Dentists Union (KMPDU) Secretary General Davji Atellah during an interview on April 17, 2023](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/the_kenya_medical_practitioners_pharmacists_dentists_union_kmpdu_secretary_general_davji_atellah_during_an_interview_on_april_17_2023.jpg.webp?itok=HVwI7UaR)

To prevent the talks from fracturing across individual counties, KMPDU has structured the push as a parallel, coordinated exercise rather than 47 separate disputes.

The national track launched on Monday, October 26, in Mombasa, where an inter-ministerial negotiation committee led by Public Health and Professional Standards Principal Secretary Mary Muthoni met the union’s team. Ahead of that session, the Ministry of Health submitted an official counter-proposal to the union’s original claims, establishing the baseline for national-level terms of service and workplace conditions.

Three days later, the devolved track begins. The Council of Governors and the SRC agreed to union demands for a centralised bargaining format, with intergovernmental talks set to open on October 29 to tackle county-level employment terms.

"The parallel negotiations would help establish a coordinated approach to securing improved terms of service for doctors employed by both levels of government," the union stated, confirming that its National Advisory Council mandated the negotiating team to guide both pacts to completion by December.

### The County Payroll Bottleneck

Securing concurrent signatures by the end of the year requires the union to resolve the structural divide between national health facilities and devolved hospitals.

In September, SRC Chairperson Sammy Chepkwony halted scheduled pay adjustments for senior county officials following consultations with the Council of Governors. The commission cited submissions from county leadership indicating that local personnel spending was already overrunning statutory limits.

By insisting on centralised terms, KMPDU is attempting to lock in baseline working conditions across devolved facilities without allowing individual counties to derail negotiations through fiscal distress or administrative delays. The union has cited the fallout of the 2017–2021 CBA cycle—a prolonged period of industrial action, delayed implementation, and litigation—as the model it is trying to avoid.

With counter-proposals now formally exchanged at Afya House and intergovernmental sessions opening this week, negotiators face an immediate test: translating two distinct employer mandates into a unified agreement before their self-imposed December cutoff.
