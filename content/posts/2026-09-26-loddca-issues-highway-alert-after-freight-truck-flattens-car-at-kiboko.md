The Long Distance Drivers and Conductors Association of Kenya (LoDDCA) has instructed long-haul operators and private motorists to exercise heightened vigilance along the Nairobi-Mombasa corridor, following a severe midday collision at Kiboko where an overturned freight hauler pinned a personal vehicle with its occupants trapped inside.

The alert, issued on Saturday afternoon, comes as roadside emergency crews and local residents scrambled to cut through the crushed wreckage before heavy specialized towing units could reach the scene.

According to initial scene assessments, the commercial truck lost stability along the Kiboko stretch, rolling directly onto the smaller passenger car. Bystanders immediately launched manual extrication efforts to pull the vehicle’s occupants from beneath the truck's chassis, working ahead of formal emergency dispatch teams. While the exact passenger count and the extent of injuries or fatalities remained unconfirmed as operations progressed, the sheer structural weight resting on the passenger cabin raised immediate fears of critical trauma.

### Recurring Corridor Collisions

The Kiboko incident represents the latest in a rapid succession of catastrophic multi-vehicle accidents along the primary transit artery connecting the Port of Mombasa to the hinterland.

Just three days prior, on September 23, a Dreamline passenger bus collided head-on with a freight trailer near Taru and Mackinnon Road. That impact sheared the front section of the coach, leaving several commuters severely injured and trapped within the bent metal until police and emergency medical workers ferried them to nearby sub-county hospitals.

Earlier in the month, a multi-vehicle pile-up paralyzed traffic at Kwa DC, situated between Salama and Sultan Hamud. In that incident, an out-of-control long-distance truck slammed into the rear of a passenger vehicle, triggering a secondary chain-reaction crash that shut down the carriageway for hours.

### The Dualing Pressure

For commercial logistics operators and public service vehicle drivers, these repeated rollover and rear-end incidents highlight the narrow margin of error on undivided two-lane highway sections carrying high volumes of heavy cargo.

Transport operators note that single-lane configurations force heavy haulers and smaller private vehicles into dangerous proximity during overtaking maneuvers, where unexpected braking or tire blowouts leave drivers without escape shoulders. The recurring collisions have prompted renewed joint demands from driver associations and freight logistics firms for the national government to accelerate the long-delayed full dualing and lane expansion of the Mombasa-Nairobi corridor.

Traffic police and local administration teams have maintained an active perimeter around the Kiboko crash site to divert oncoming traffic, with formal casualty figures pending the full removal of the overturned truck.
