Texas Representative Keith Self has formally introduced legislation to terminate the United States Diversity Immigrant Visa Programme, targeting the statutory pathway that allocated green card eligibility to 3,949 Kenyan entrants and their dependents in the current lottery cycle.

The draft measure seeks to amend the U.S. Immigration and Nationality Act by expunging the core statutory provisions that govern the annual allocation of up to 55,000 permanent resident visas to nationals of countries with historically low immigration rates to the United States.

“I just introduced a bill to terminate the Diversity Immigrant Visa Lottery, a programme that gives 55,000 green cards a year to foreigners from third-world countries and is riddled with fraud and weak identity checks,” Self said when tabling the document.

### Committee Review and Statutory Route

![U.S. House Representative for Texas, Keith Self, speaks at a past event, September 22, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/us-representative-texas-keith-self-september-2026.jpg.webp?itok=-HSRhM4J)

The legislative proposal—formally titled "A Bill to terminate the diversity immigrant visa programme"—has been transmitted to the U.S. House Committee on the Judiciary for preliminary consideration. From there, it is expected to be directed to the House Judiciary Subcommittee on Immigration Integrity, Security, and Enforcement, the panel exercising jurisdiction over federal border and immigration statutes.

Should the measure advance through committee, it would require majority approval on the floor of the House of Representatives and the Senate before reaching the President’s desk for signature.

Self’s legislative push comes as the lottery faces overlapping administrative and judicial crosswinds in Washington. Consular processing under the programme had already run into complications after the U.S. State Department instituted a temporary pause on all Diversity Visa issuances to re-evaluate identity verification and vetting protocols, leaving qualified candidates facing strict fiscal-year processing deadlines.

That administrative freeze unfolded against the backdrop of an August federal court ruling, which temporarily enjoined several executive directives from the Trump administration that had restricted immigrant visa adjudications.

### The Stake for Kenyan Applicants

For Kenya, the legislative challenge strikes at a substantial and well-established immigration corridor. Official U.S. State Department tabulations show that 3,949 prospective applicants from Kenya—comprising primary selectees alongside spouses and children—were picked in the DV-2026 lottery drawing.

Across the wider region, the programme remains heavily subscribed. Qualified candidates were selected from neighbouring East African nations, including Uganda, Tanzania, Ethiopia, Rwanda, Burundi, South Sudan, and Somalia, forming part of a global pool of approximately 129,516 selectees filtered from 20,822,624 submitted entries.

Securing a lottery selection does not confer automatic permanent residency. Under existing federal regulations, selectees must clear strict educational or skilled-labour thresholds, furnish verified civil documentation, undergo extensive criminal and security vetting, and complete an in-person consular interview before an immigrant visa can be stamped against the statutory ceiling.

For the thousands of Kenyan beneficiaries currently preparing civil records and scheduling embassy appointments, the bill introduces fresh political uncertainty to a process already constrained by strict annual statutory expirations.
