Motorists traversing Nairobi’s eastern corridors face an overlapping wave of disruptions after road authorities pushed back the completion of a vital river crossing by three months while scheduling a fresh partial shutdown along the peri-urban freight belt.

The Kenya Urban Roads Authority (KURA) announced on Monday that the section of Eldoret Road closed four months ago to construct a vehicular bridge over the Nairobi River will remain off-limits until December 30, 2026. The corridor had initially been scheduled to reopen on September 18, but supply chain and technical hurdles on the bridge deck forced engineers to seek a 103-day extension.

The prolonged closure means the alternative routes absorbing local Eastlands traffic will carry heavy spillover through the end-of-year peak, just as separate works begin redirecting commercial haulers between Mlolongo and Kamulu starting Thursday.

### Material Delays on the River Deck

KURA initially cordoned off Eldoret Road on May 18, setting what was supposed to be a four-month window to erect the new vehicular crossing across the Nairobi River. 

![Jogoo Road](https://i.ibb.co/W496vdMy/4fd7c49833b2.webp)

That timeline collapsed midway through September. In its Monday update, the authority attributed the failure to meet the September 18 reopening target to logistical snags and procurement bottlenecks beyond the contractor's immediate control.

"The extension has become necessary due to unforeseen logistical and site-related challenges encountered during the construction of the Vehicular Bridge across the Nairobi River, including delays in the delivery of critical construction materials and equipment," KURA said in a public notice.

Under the revised schedule, the road is expected to reopen at 6:00 PM on Wednesday, December 30, keeping local connectors under elevated pressure for a cumulative seven months.

To cushion the area from chronic paralysis, KURA directed motorists to continue using internal neighborhood grids within Eastlands to feed into Jogoo Road and adjacent regional connectors. Depending on commuter destinations across town, the agency also pointed drivers toward the Ngong Road network, Mbagathi Road, Lang'ata Road, and intermediate linking arteries, supported by deployed traffic marshals and police officers.

### Freight Diversions on Mlolongo–Kamulu

![A photo of recarpeted City Hall Way Taken on July 8, 2021.](https://i.ibb.co/RkHw2r0F/82f467b12648.webp)

The bridge delay coincides with an immediate two-month partial shutdown on the Mlolongo–Kamulu corridor, widely known as Quarry Road, which carries substantial construction and industrial transport.

KURA confirmed the Quarry Road works will take effect at 6:00 AM on Thursday, October 1, and run through Tuesday, December 1, forcing a temporary division between passenger vehicles and heavy cargo trucks operating along the Machakos-Nairobi boundary.

Rather than directing all traffic onto a single detour, the agency has split the alternate flow by vehicle tonnage:

* **Light and medium vehicles:** Diverted through Syokimau–Katani Road and the Syokimau–Parliament Road link.
* **Heavy commercial vehicles:** Barred from residential bypasses and restricted to Tuffoam Road throughout the 61-day repair window.

The twin interventions place significant demand on alternative infrastructure on both sides of the city. While Eastlands relies on already saturated arteries like Jogoo Road to compensate for the stalled Nairobi River bridge, peri-urban estates in Syokimau will absorb an influx of mid-sized traffic navigating around the Quarry Road work zone.

KURA maintains that both contractor teams have been instructed to fast-track construction schedules, leaving December 1 and December 30 as the definitive recovery benchmarks for the two disrupted transport links.
