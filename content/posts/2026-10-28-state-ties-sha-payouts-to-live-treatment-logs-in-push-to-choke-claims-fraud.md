Hospitals submitting medical claims to the Social Health Authority (SHA) will no longer be allowed to prepare standalone billing paperwork after the state moved to lock reimbursement directly to patient charts logged at the bedside.

The Digital Health Agency (DHA) informed the National Assembly on Wednesday that it is decommissioning the traditional window where health facilities recreate clinical data on separate billing sheets. Under the incoming architecture, claims will generate automatically from the medical information entered while a doctor or nurse attends to a patient.

The procedural shift aims to remove the operational seam between clinical care and administrative invoicing—a gap that has historically enabled inflated or fictitious claims in public health financing schemes.

DHA Chief Executive Officer Eng. Antony Lenaiyara presented the system changes during an oversight session with the Parliamentary Health Committee, chaired by Seme MP Dr James Nyikal.

### Eliminating the Second Entry

Under the existing routine across public and private hospitals, clinical teams record diagnoses, prescriptions, and procedures in primary hospital files. Administrative clerks later extract and re-enter that data onto external forms or separate portals to bill the national insurer.

Lenaiyara told lawmakers that this two-step model introduces data discrepancies, slows down settlement timelines, and complicates audits. By unifying medical records with the claims engine, a facility's reimbursement claim will depend entirely on records established in real time during consultation, admission, or theatre procedures.

"The transition is expected to improve efficiency in claims processing while enhancing transparency and accountability in the management of public healthcare funds," the agency noted, emphasizing that integrating clinical and administrative procedures creates an unbroken audit trail for every shilling claimed.

The agency expects the automation to lift a substantial clerical load off hospital personnel, who currently expend operational hours retyping patient histories to satisfy verification thresholds.

### Digital Traceability and Fraud Pre-checks

Beyond administrative efficiency, the overhaul targets fraudulent billing practices that have plagued national health coverage schemes. In previous configurations, third-party reviewers often evaluated paper or digital invoices detached from contemporaneous clinical notes, making post-facto verification slow and porous.

By pulling data straight from the point of care, DHA plans to enforce automated validation checks before a file ever enters the settlement queue. The unified trail allows regulators to track the exact clinical actions, timestamps, and service delivery points backing every invoice.

Inconsistencies—such as billings for procedures not reflected in primary patient notes or services billed outside the treatment window—can be flagged or rejected at source.

The committee appearance forms part of DHA’s push to align the technology backbone of Universal Health Coverage with strict public finance rules, moving public healthcare funds into a framework where clinical reality dictates administrative settlement.
