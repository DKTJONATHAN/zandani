Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna has stepped back from his usual public social calendar, revealing that heightening personal security concerns forced him to abandon public entertainment spots, skip national football fixtures, and resort to carrying packed home-cooked food to public functions.

Speaking during a Thursday morning broadcast on Radio Citizen on November 19, 2026, the legislator detailed how security warnings altered his daily habits following his vocal public pushback against several of President William Ruto’s policy stances. Sifuna stated that the initial stream of alerts, which included warnings of potential road travel ambushes and assassination plots, arrived almost daily at their peak.

The resulting advisory from his security team triggered an immediate overhaul of his off-duty routine. Sifuna explained that he was instructed to clear his schedule of public social outings, forcing him out of regular entertainment venues and keeping him out of the stands for a recent Harambee Stars match he had planned to attend.

Beyond keeping away from crowded social venues, the security advisories extended directly to his dining table. Sifuna disclosed that at the height of the security alert, he began packing his own meals for public gatherings due to specific concerns regarding food contamination and poisoning. While he noted that some of the strict self-catering protocols have since been relaxed, his personal protection detail remains enlarged.

During the live interview, Sifuna did not publicise the identities of the individuals behind the reported threats, nor did he present physical evidence or formal security documentation to validate the claims.

He nevertheless connected his heightened precautions to wider political friction in Western Kenya, pointing to recent security incidents involving Trans Nzoia Governor George Natembeya and members of the Linda Mwananchi Movement.

That connection follows a formal statement issued on Wednesday, November 18, by the Linda Mwananchi Movement demanding swift police action over what they described as systematic attacks against the governor's inner circle. The group cited a violent Monday, November 16 raid in which the vehicle of Natembeya’s communications officer, Amos Boaz Masika, was set ablaze at his residence, leaving Masika hospitalized in critical condition. The movement also referenced separate arson incidents targeting affiliate properties across Kakamega and Bungoma counties, urging law enforcement to track down both the field operatives and financiers.

While Sifuna continues to navigate his political duties under enhanced guard, his immediate routine remains governed by the reinforced security posture established by his team.
