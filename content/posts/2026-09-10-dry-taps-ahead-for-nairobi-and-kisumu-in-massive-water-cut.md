Nairobi City Water and Sewerage Company and Kisumu Water and Sanitation Company have issued notices warning millions of residents across both cities of major water supply disruptions starting Friday. Engineers will shut main treatment plants and distribution lines to carry out emergency pipe repairs and install new meters.

### What we know

- Nairobi Water will shut down the Sasumua Water Treatment Plant for 26 hours, from Friday 6:00 AM to Saturday 8:00 AM.
- Affected Nairobi areas include Westlands, Lavington, Kileleshwa, Kilimani, Upper Hill, Kawangware, and Kenyatta National Hospital.
- Kisumu Water will interrupt supply on Friday morning to repair trial pits, install valves, and fit new meters.
- Major institutions hit in Nairobi include Daystar University, Coptic Hospital, St George's School, and the University of Nairobi main campus.
- Kisumu estates facing dry taps include Milimani, Nyamasaria, Nyalenda A and B, Dunga Estate, and the central business district.

In the capital, the shutdown focuses on emergency repairs along the Sasumua-Kabete transmission pipeline. NCWSC announced that the 26-hour closure at the Sasumua plant will cut off supply to critical commercial hubs and residential zones. Piping networks along Riverside Drive, Argwings Kodhek Road, Gitanga Road, and Valley Road will remain completely depressurised throughout the repair period.

Over in Kisumu County, KIWASCO technicians plan to construct meter chambers and trial pits while installing control valves across the lake city's main distribution grid. The utility firm confirmed that the maintenance cycle aims to improve pressure management and curb non-revenue water losses across the CBD and surrounding residential estates.

The disruption forces major referral facilities, including Kenyatta National Hospital and Coptic Hospital, to rely on emergency water reserves or expensive bowser deliveries. Estates in Dagoretti, Kawangware, and Waithaka, including Sohill City, Kagira, and Upper Kabiria, must also store water in advance as feeder lines drain empty.

Both water companies urged households and business owners to fill storage tanks before Friday morning. Operational teams expect supply to normalize gradually after the valves reopen, though high-altitude areas like Hillview and parts of Lavington may face lower pressure through Saturday afternoon.

### Why it matters

When dry taps hit Nairobi and Kisumu at the same time, the real bill falls on small businesses and ordinary households. In Upper Hill and Kilimani, office managers will pay up to KSh 10,000 per 10,000-litre tanker to keep flush toilets running. Mama mboga in Kawangware and Nyalenda will spend double the daily rate on 20-litre jerrycans, paying up to KSh 50 each from private vendors who always profit off public utility failures.

Emergency maintenance is necessary when pipelines age, but short notice leaves families scrambling. Hospital administrators face real anxiety trying to keep surgical wards clean while waiting on bowsers navigating Friday traffic along Waiyaki Way and Mbagathi Way. These utility shutdowns expose how thin the margin is between normal city life and chaotic survival in Kenya's primary urban centres. Fixing Sasumua and Kisumu's meter chambers is good for the grid, but until municipal providers build adequate storage redundancy, every repair cycle remains a costly crisis for residents.
