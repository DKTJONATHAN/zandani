Meshack and Ninah Mpendwa have found an unexpected fix for the age-old studio friction of trying to hum, tap, and explain a half-formed melody to a sound engineer: letting artificial intelligence sketch out the rough draft first.

The gospel couple behind Wapendwa Muziki revealed during an appearance on *Obinna TV* that they have integrated the generative audio platform Suno AI directly into their songwriting pipeline. Rather than stepping into a commercial studio cold or relying on crude voice notes to convey musical arrangements, the duo now feeds their lyrical concepts and structural ideas into algorithms to assemble functional audio blueprints before approaching human sound engineers.

The technical disclosure offers a practical look at how digital tools are altering independent music production in Nairobi, moving AI out of theoretical copyright debates and into day-to-day workflow problem-solving.

### The Breakdown Between Vision and Mixing Desk

For years, independent recording artists without formal composition training have faced the same creative roadblock when working with third-party audio producers. Conveying specific chord progressions, tempo shifts, and instrumentation by ear frequently leads to misunderstood briefs, misdirected studio hours, and repeated project re-records.

![Wapendwa Muziki // Instagram](https://i.ibb.co/k6Sw1JPY/9adf17a94ebc.webp)

Meshack, widely known as Mesh, and his wife Ninah recounted that their previous production cycle was defined by this exhausting trial-and-error loop. The pair would record raw vocal fragments on their phones and spend extended sessions trying to guide producers toward the exact sonic textures and progressions they had envisioned.

"The process was previously more demanding, especially when we had to communicate our musical ideas to producers," the couple explained during the broadcast, demonstrating how they now prompt Suno AI to assemble complete musical mockups from written lyrics and stylistic guidelines.

By arriving at the studio with an AI-generated demo in hand, the artists effectively bypass the initial phase of acoustic translation. The generated track acts as a sonic reference sheet, providing sound engineers with immediate clarity on song tempo, harmonic direction, and mood without requiring multiple warm-up sessions to find common ground.

### Drawing the Line at Demo Assembly

Despite handing over their preliminary composition scaffolding to artificial intelligence, Wapendwa Muziki emphasized that the software stops at the drafting table. The generated audio tracks are treated strictly as working demos rather than commercially release-ready masters.

The duo was explicit that the technology does not substitute human instrumentalists, studio vocal takes, or professional audio engineering. Instead, the AI output serves as an interactive mood board, preserving their creative intent while leaving the final tracking, live vocal layering, and acoustic refinement in the hands of seasoned producers.

This distinction marks a pragmatic compromise in the entertainment sector. While major global labels and artist advocacy bodies wrestle with the intellectual property threats posed by automated music generation, independent acts are quietly adopting the same software as low-overhead studio assistants.

By generating their own audio proofs-of-concept at home, artists retain tighter creative control over the sonic direction of their catalog before committing financial resources to live tracking sessions.

### Budgetary Relief for Independent Creators

Beyond solving creative miscommunication, the workflow provides clear logistical breathing room for independent gospel creators operating without label-funded studio budgets.

Traditional commercial studio time in Kenya commands hourly rates or project-based fees that quickly escalate when song arrangements are worked out live in the booth. By shifting the exploratory phase out of commercial studios and onto digital software, independent performers eliminate the costly preliminary hours typically burned while experimenting with different keys, styles, and tempos.

For Wapendwa Muziki, the pivot is fundamentally about creative efficiency. As gospel artists navigate an increasingly competitive digital landscape, the ability to rapidly test musical arrangements outside a studio booth gives them a sharper, more focused presentation the moment the red recording light turns on.
