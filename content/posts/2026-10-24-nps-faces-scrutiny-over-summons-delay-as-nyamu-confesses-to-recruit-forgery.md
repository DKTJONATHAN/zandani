While eighteen recruits at the National Police College in Kiganjo were promptly arrested and expelled when forged academic credentials were confirmed within their ranks, the sitting legislator and advocate who publicly claimed complicity in one of those admissions has yet to face formal questioning.

The National Police Service (NPS) confirmed on Saturday, October 24, that it had opened an administrative examination into remarks made by nominated Senator Karen Nyamu during a live radio broadcast. During the interview, the lawmaker described acquiring a forged Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education (KCSE) document to push a political campaigner through the police enlistment barrier.

In an official communication issued Saturday evening, NPS spokesperson Muchiri Nyaga acknowledged the circulation of the broadcast footage, noting that police headquarters was reviewing statements in which a senior nominated politician acknowledged knowing an enrollee had used fabricated credentials in a bid to become a recruit constable.

### The On-Air Admission

![nyamu-kanja.jpg](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/nyamu-kanja.jpg.webp?itok=ihgOSH3o)

The inquiry stems from Nyamu's appearance on *Radio Generation* on Friday, October 23, where she recounted attempting to secure state employment for an associate following campaign operations.

"I recently tried to help a very staunch campaigner of mine join the police force. We forged a Form 4 certificate," Nyamu stated during the broadcast. "She went to Kiganjo for training; we were so happy, but after investigations, she was arrested for forgery alongside 17 others."

The lawmaker offered the narrative as an anecdote, but the assertion directly implicated her in the commission of an offense under Kenyan law—tampering with and uttering false official documents to circumvent national recruitment standards.

In response, the police service termed the remarks "both regrettable and unacceptable," drawing specific attention to Nyamu’s status as a practicing advocate of the High Court and a sitting member of the Senate.

" recruitment into the police service is subject to strict verification procedures designed to detect falsified documents and prevent unqualified applicants from joining the force," Nyaga said, reiterating that internal auditing mechanisms at the Kiganjo main campus were what unmasked the cohort of 18 suspects.

### Institutional Pressure on Oversight

The admission poses a direct procedural challenge for internal police investigators and oversight bodies. While the junior recruits caught up in the scheme were immediately detained and removed from the training rosters, the police service has limited its formal response to public condemnation.

Falsification of academic records and procurement of public office through fraudulent misrepresentation carry strict criminal penalties under the Penal Code. , admissions of complicity in felony offenses by officers of the court routinely trigger scrutiny from statutory bodies overseeing legal practice and legislative ethics.

Police headquarters maintained that fraudulent entry avenues into the disciplined services remain heavily policed, warning prospective applicants that verification systems actively filter out counterfeit papers before commissioning.

Yet by Saturday evening, the service had not clarified whether detectives had formally invited Nyamu to record an evidentiary statement or initiated a parallel criminal file regarding the origin of the forged Form Four certificate.
