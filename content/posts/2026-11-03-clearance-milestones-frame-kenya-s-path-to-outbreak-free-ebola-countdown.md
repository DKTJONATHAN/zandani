Kenya’s public health system reached a decisive milestone in its containment of the Bundibugyo Ebola virus on Tuesday, discharging its first cohort of recovered patients while maintaining a strict surveillance calendar that governs when the country can officially be declared free of the disease.

The Ministry of Health approved the exit of five male patients following consecutive laboratory tests confirming they had entirely cleared the virus from their systems. The men had been undergoing isolation and active treatment across two primary referral facilities in the capital—two at Kenyatta National Hospital (KNH) and three at The Nairobi Hospital.

All five were released after showing full symptom resolution and satisfying public health safety criteria, marking the first clinical recovery batch since the outbreak was detected.

### Dual Surveillance Tracks

Tuesday's discharges operated along two parallel containment pipelines: clinical recovery for laboratory-confirmed cases and quarantine clearance for individuals exposed to infected patients.

![Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale, during a meeting at Afya House, with an inset of an Ebola Virus Bundibugyo Strain under a microscope.](https://i.ibb.co/V0ZyNWC4/5c9d0552aa49.webp)

Alongside the five discharged men, health authorities cleared 46 individuals who had been undergoing mandatory monitoring after coming into direct contact with confirmed cases. Having completed their full observation window without exhibiting symptoms, the group was cleared to return home.

This follows an earlier cohort of 20 contacts released from quarantine on October 25. 

According to official health data, the overall case count stands at nine confirmed infections, including two fatalities, yielding a case fatality rate of 22.2 per cent. Crucially, public health monitoring has recorded zero new infections since October 19.

### What Stands Between Kenya and Outbreak Termination

While the discharges substantially relieve high-level isolation wards in Nairobi, public health rules prevent health officials from starting the final countdown to declare the outbreak over.

Under international health protocols adopted by the ministry, declaring an Ebola outbreak officially over requires a continuous 42-day period—representing twice the virus's maximum 21-day incubation window—with no new cases reported.

However, that 42-day clock cannot begin until every active patient in isolation either tests negative and is discharged or passes away. 

![Red Cross Society workers wearing personal protective equipment gathered after handling the coffin of a man who died in Ebola.](https://i.ibb.co/0j5pSjVc/2d78b74476dc.webp)

Two confirmed Ebola patients remain under strict clinical care—one at KNH and another at The Nairobi Hospital. The countdown clock remains paused until both satisfy exit parameters.

Simultaneously, 52 contacts remain in mandatory isolation. This group is slated to complete its 21-day observation schedule by Wednesday, November 11, provided no symptoms develop.

### Reintegration and Community Protocols

To ensure safe reintegration, the ministry confirmed that outgoing patients and contacts are receiving structured psychosocial support from multi-disciplinary health teams prior to returning to their neighborhoods.

Public health officials warned against social exclusion, instructing families, employers, and local communities to receive returning individuals without discrimination, noting that cleared patients present zero transmission risk to the public.

The ministry maintains active emergency communication channels for public reporting and inquiries through toll-free number 719, USSD code *719#, and WhatsApp line 0700 719 719.
