By committing to an aggressive solo pace from the gun, Agnes Ngetich took the women-only half marathon world record down to 1:05:15 in Copenhagen on Sunday, shaving a single second off the historic mark set by compatriot Peres Jepchirchir in 2020.

The 25-year-old’s run at the 2026 World Athletics Road Running Championships was built on a tactical gamble rarely seen in unassisted distance events. Without a cluster of competitors or male pacemakers to shield against the wind, Ngetich broke away in the early kilometres of the 21.1-kilometre loop through the Danish capital, maintaining full mechanical cadence entirely on her own to hold off both fatigue and the clock.

Her finishing time of 1:05:15 unseated Jepchirchir’s 1:05:16 performance from the World Half Marathon Championships in Gdynia, Poland, completing a demanding front-running display that left the chasing field fighting for minor placings.

### The Pure Distance Benchmark

The Copenhagen performance sits in the distinct "women-only" statistical ledger maintained by World Athletics. While female athletes have logged faster half marathon splits during commercial mixed-gender city races—where elite men are deployed to set a rhythmic pace and break air resistance—the women-only standard strictly excludes external drafts.

![Agnes Jebet Ngetich in the Copenhagen race in September, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/3yg2XNTT/36d7bff80ab0.webp)

Running isolated at the front under championship regulations requires a precise internal clock. Rather than monitoring an escort runner, Ngetich had to calibrate her mile splits entirely by feel, absorbing the physical toll of wind resistance over the second half of the course.

Fellow Kenyan Veronica Loleo crossed the line 50 seconds later in 1:06:05, securing silver and confirming a dominant Kenyan 1-2 podium finish.

### Dual Global Titles in 2026

The record run cements an exceptional competition calendar for Ngetich, who has transitioned seamlessly across disparate running environments over the past nine months. 

Earlier in the year, she captured the senior women’s individual crown over turf and ruts at the World Athletics Cross Country Championships in Tallahassee, Florida. Taking that raw cross-country power onto the flat asphalt in Denmark transformed her from a proven pack racer into an undisputed solo record breaker.

Before taking the start line on Sunday morning, Ngetich acknowledged the pressure of entering the Danish capital as a marked runner. Her stated goal had been simple championship execution—securing the world gold medal—yet her aggressive early splits immediately put the six-year-old global record in jeopardy.

![Agnes Jebet Ngetich in the Copenhagen race in September, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/C374zbq3/a86317411313.webp)

### Kenya's Copenhagen Haul

Ngetich’s record-breaking run capped a busy morning for the 16-member Kenyan contingent across road events in Copenhagen.

In the men’s half marathon, Nicholas Kipkorir secured silver in 58:11 after a tight closing sprint, finishing five seconds behind Sweden’s Andreas Almgren (58:06), while Uganda’s Joshua Cheptegei took bronze in 58:26.

Kenya’s middle-distance runners also picked up hardware in the shorter formats, with Caroline Gitonga running 14:48 for silver in the women’s 5km, and Mirriam Cherop collecting bronze in the women’s road mile with a time of 4:25.71.

The Danish medal sweep carries immediate symbolic weight back in Nairobi. Five days earlier, on September 15, the World Athletics Council formally awarded the 2029 World Athletics Championships to the Kenyan capital, marking the first time Africa will host the marquee track and field gathering. 

With preparations now set to begin for 2029, Ngetich’s relentless solo run through Copenhagen ensures Kenya enters that hosting cycle holding the sharpest road marks in the sport.
