Comedian and media personality Dr. Ofweneke has directed an unexpected assignment at Kenya’s youth: step in as household gatekeepers and physically stop rural parents from accepting face-branded underwear from political campaign caravans.

In a video published across his Instagram page on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, the entertainer bypassed generic political commentary to challenge millennials and Gen Z directly. Rather than treating campaign handouts as harmless village giveaways, Ofweneke argued that the emergence of bras, panties, and boxers stamped with candidate portraits crosses into outright indignity—one that younger family members must actively police back home.

"Giving your potential voters panties and boxers as campaign materials is a stupid, foolish idea," Ofweneke said in the address. "It is childish, it does not make sense at all, and it is a total degradation to your voters. So we better stop our mothers and our fathers who are in the village receiving bras, receiving panties and boxers that are actually printed with the face of an aspirant on them, all in the name of campaign merchandise."

### Targeting the Campaign Handlers

Beyond urging young Kenyans to intercept the deliveries, Ofweneke leveled sharp criticism at the public relations desks and communications consultants steering political contenders ahead of the 2027 General Election. The comedian questioned why campaign machinery with substantial war chests would settle on personal undergarments as an outreach vehicle, advising aspirants struggling for traction to seek out experienced professionals instead of resorting to embarrassing gimmicks.

He pushed the awkward reality of the giveaway to the forefront, asking strategists to reflect on what putting political slogans on undergarments signals to the women receiving them.

"You cannot give our mothers panties that have their faces on, all in the name of campaigning," he said. "What do you take our mothers for? Can we upgrade our style of campaigning? Can we start sounding sensible?"

### The Kisii Campaign Catalyst

Ofweneke’s public rebuke lands directly on the heels of a storm created by Kevin Nyamari, an aspirant running for the Kisii County gubernatorial seat in 2027. Nyamari became a focal point of online debate after rolling out women’s underwear branded with his portrait alongside the campaign tagline 'Governor Kisii County 2027'.

While critics condemned the branded apparel as an unwelcome breach of personal boundaries, Nyamari has mounted an unapologetic defense of the merchandise. The gubernatorial hopeful linked the distributed undergarments to his ongoing menstrual hygiene and sanitary towel outreach, arguing the initiative was designed to uplift women’s welfare in the county.

Nyamari has since stated that the national attention did little to derail the strategy. According to the aspirant, the public visibility generated thousands of additional distribution requests across the county, prompting him to maintain that the branded intimate wear served its intended goal of setting his bid apart in an increasingly crowded political field.

With early 2027 mobilization already underway across county circuits, Ofweneke’s appeal places the burden squarely on families to decide whether branded undergarments become a normal fixture of Kenyan electoral merchandising or a line voters refuse to let candidates cross.
