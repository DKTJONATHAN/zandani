Socialite and entrepreneur Huddah Monroe has issued a firm directive to men approaching her in public spaces. In a video posted to her social media platforms this week, the businesswoman stated she no longer wishes to be accosted by strangers. She cited a persistent lack of respect and inappropriate comments as her primary motivation for demanding personal space.

Huddah claimed that interactions with men in Nairobi often lack the basic formalities she has grown accustomed to while traveling abroad. She described a preference for environments where men seek explicit permission before initiating a conversation. According to her, this buffer allows a woman the agency to refuse engagement before an interaction turns sour.

The entrepreneur took issue with men who assume a level of familiarity that does not exist. She specifically addressed those who approach her as if they share a deep history, despite not having spoken in decades. Even when she attempts to remain polite, she alleged that many of these exchanges inevitably devolve into disrespectful or vulgar commentary.

She ended her statement with a blunt request for silence. She asked men who see her out in public to refrain from greeting her entirely. She noted that she would prefer to be left alone rather than endure the risk of being insulted by someone seeking her attention.

This is not the first time Huddah has voiced grievances regarding the dating scene in Kenya. She has previously stated a preference for remaining single rather than marrying a local man, citing recurring behavioral issues. She attributes these views to her own past, including a marriage at age 19 that ended in divorce due to her former husband’s struggle with drug addiction.

### The take

Huddah Monroe is not asking for a favor; she is setting a boundary that many Kenyan men seem incapable of processing. There is a specific kind of entitlement that runs through certain circles in this city. It is the belief that any woman in the public eye-or simply sitting at a cafe in Westlands-is public property. When Huddah says "msinisalime," she is cutting off the air supply to the amateur pickup artist.

The reaction online has been predictable. Some call her arrogant, while others recognize the exhaustion of a woman who just wants to drink her coffee in peace. She has the capital and the independence to demand a level of decorum that most local men are not being taught at home. Whether you like her brand or not, she is pointing out a rot in our casual social interactions. You don't have to agree with her dating history to see that she is tired of the disrespect. For the men who think a "hello" is an opening for a lewd remark, the message is loud: you are being ignored. She is not losing anything by avoiding your company. You, however, are losing the benefit of a doubt you never really earned in the first place.
