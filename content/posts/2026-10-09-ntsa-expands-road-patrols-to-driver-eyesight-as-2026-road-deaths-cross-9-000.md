Highway safety enforcement in Kenya is widening beyond speed traps and mechanical roadworthiness stickers to scrutinise the biological fitness of the person behind the wheel.

The National Transport and Safety Authority (NTSA) confirmed on Friday that its roadside enforcement teams will begin questioning drivers during regular stops to verify whether they have completed designated optical examinations. The directive links driver licensing and active road surveillance directly to vision assessments, as state regulators attempt to arrest a sharp spike in deadly crashes.

The policy pivot comes against a grim statistical backdrop. Internal data from the transport safety regulator shows that 9,002 people were killed on Kenyan roads between January and September 2026. Public alarm has mounted further following a lethal seven-day stretch leading into October that claimed more than 40 lives across multiple transit corridors.

### The 90 Percent Perception Metric

![A collage of the mandatory eye-testing procedure of a driver in Nairobi(left), and an NTSA pick-up.](https://i.ibb.co/3VjtJfm/2c4159cc306f.webp)

According to NTSA evaluations, human error is responsible for between 20 and 30 per cent of all road crashes nationally. Agency specialists have isolated visual impairment as a decisive contributor to misjudged overtakes, pedestrian collisions, and late reactions to oncoming traffic.

Regulators estimate that roughly 90 per cent of driving decisions rely directly on sensory inputs processed through the eyes, ranging from tracking motorbike movements in side mirrors to calibrating braking distances in heavy rain.

"The eyes are the ones which help to see and make judgments," an NTSA officer noted during the Friday briefing, explaining why physiological tests are now moving to the frontline of traffic policing. Officers deployed on patrol duties will actively query motorists on whether they have cleared the optical evaluation baseline.

### Targeting the Public Transport Chain

![NTSA officers assisting drivers with free motor vehicle inspection services](https://i.ibb.co/q36WD2Qg/78d49c8ef23e.webp)

The vision-vetting push runs alongside a wider mandatory curriculum rolled out across the public transport network. For the past three weeks, NTSA trainers have been processing more than 4,000 public service vehicle (PSV) drivers, Sacco executives, fleet supervisors, and terminal marshals.

That capacity-building drive was formally initiated on September 21 in Mombasa and Taita Taveta counties before spreading inward along major arterial transit corridors. The intervention seeks to dismantle the unstructured, fare-chasing operational culture common in urban matatu routes, nudging operators toward institutional management models where driver wellness and shift schedules are formally audited.

### Four-Stage Compliance Framework

The eye-screening programme is scheduled to unfold across four distinct phases: an initial baseline design assessment, an operational pilot, a comprehensive national rollout, and eventual long-term institutionalisation into statutory licensing requirements.

By integrating optical screening into routine highway checks, the authority is raising the compliance bar for commercial and private operators alike. Whether the visual verification drive can deliver immediate safety dividends remains tied to how rigorously patrol units enforce the requirement across high-speed upcountry highways, where multi-vehicle pileups continue to drive up Kenya's road toll.
