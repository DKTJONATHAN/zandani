The Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA) told the National Assembly Finance and Planning Committee that it is expanding its compliance net by integrating third-party mobile transaction channels and mandatory payroll records directly into its audit system.

Under the strategy presented to lawmakers, the exchequer seeks to systematically pull financial logs from commercial mobile payment channels—specifically Merchant Till Numbers and Pochi La Biashara accounts—and match them against individual taxpayer filings and eTIMS electronic invoicing records. The cross-matching mechanism aims to identify undeclared business income and compel non-filing enterprise operators to register as active taxpayers.

### Dual-Front Data Reconciliation

KRA’s reliance on third-party commercial feeds marks a structural shift from self-declaration reliance toward automated ledger verification. The authority indicated that surveillance on mobile payment channels will focus on variance cases where reported annual returns fail to align with real-time electronic tax receipts, high-credit transaction accounts, and total non-filers.

![KRA Commissioner General Adan Mohamed speaking to the National Assembly’s Departmental Committee on Finance and National Planning on June 12 in Kiambu.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/adan-mohamed-kra.jpg.webp?itok=Ig33u-nn)

According to the submissions, this mobile transaction sweep is projected to directly impact 42 per cent of Kenya's total domestic Value Added Tax (VAT) register, bringing previously untracked commercial activity into formal tax reporting workflows.

Beyond small business transaction channels, the tax collector is deploying similar data-matching frameworks against corporate payrolls. KRA informed legislators that it will cross-check employer payroll declarations with Housing Levy remittance logs to flag companies that maintain large reported workforces while submitting minimal or zero payroll tax obligations.

Initial findings from this targeted payroll compliance push have already identified an estimated Ksh5.3 billion in potential uncollected revenue stemming from non-remitting employers and understated deduction statements.

### Revenue Shortfalls Drive Enforcement

![Traders conducting business in a town in Kenya](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/traders%20conducting%20business%20in%20a%20town%20in%20kenya.jpg.webp?itok=YQf0LLyS)

The compliance aggressive stance follows consecutive revenue underperformances that have squeezed Treasury disbursements. Official authority figures disclose that KRA collected Ksh2.844 trillion in the financial year ending June 2026, missing its statutory baseline target of Ksh2.977 trillion by Ksh132 billion.

The shortfall has extended into the current fiscal calendar. During the first quarter of the 2026/2027 financial year, KRA generated Ksh749.678 billion against a set target of Ksh775.953 billion, leaving a quarter-one deficit of Ksh26.275 billion.

By leveraging automated reconciliation across both informal transaction rails and formal payroll records, KRA intends to narrow the revenue deficit without introducing new statutory tax rates, focusing instead on enforcement against non-compliant entities operating within existing payment infrastructures.

Parliamentary scrutiny of the proposed compliance framework will continue as lawmakers review the operational safeguards governing access to third-party financial data.
