Hospitals and clinics across Kenya must immediately restart their applications to be contracted under the new national health insurance scheme, after the Social Health Authority (SHA) announced a complete reset of its registration system.

![The Social Health Authority building in Nairobi.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/sha-build.jpg.webp?itok=NnGajx8P)

The SHA officially launched its new E-Contracting Portal on Thursday, September 17, 2026, at 3:00 PM. Along with the launch, the authority delivered a major administrative surprise: all applications submitted by healthcare facilities before this official launch time have been permanently archived and discarded.

This means any healthcare provider that pre-emptively registered or attempted to secure contracting during the transition period must start the process again from scratch. The authority clarified that these early submissions were treated as testing data and will not be carried forward into the live system.

### A Nationwide Clean Slate

The directive, issued by SHA Chief Executive Officer Dr. Mercy Mwangangi, places immediate administrative pressure on the country’s vast network of public, private, and faith-based healthcare facilities. Hospitals cannot offer services funded by the new medical schemes without active, approved contracts through this newly launched portal.

The system is designed to handle licensing and contracting across four distinct public health funds:
* **Primary Health Care Fund (PHCF):** Aimed at supporting basic healthcare services.
* **Social Health Insurance Fund (SHIF):** The core insurance pool replacing the defunct NHIF framework.
* **Emergency, Chronic and Critical Illness Fund (ECCIF):** Designed to handle specialized, high-cost emergency care.
* **Public Officers Medical Scheme Fund (POMSF):** Dedicated to civil servants and public officers.

"All healthcare providers are therefore required to submit fresh applications through the E-Contracting Portal from 3:00 pm on September 17, 2026," Dr. Mwangangi directed in a notice distributed to health facilities nationwide.

### The Administrative Burden of "Archived" Data

By archiving all prior submissions, the SHA has drawn a hard line between its pilot phase and the live rollout. For hospital administrators, this means any time spent navigating the portal and uploading credentials prior to Thursday afternoon has been invalidated.

The decision to erase pre-launch data underscores the technical challenges that have accompanied the transition to the SHA. The authority has warned healthcare providers to exercise extreme caution with their new submissions, noting that incomplete or inaccurate documents will stall the contracting process.

"Ensure everything is accurate and complete before submitting," the authority advised, emphasizing that errors could lead to immediate rejection or prolonged delays in contracting.

### High Stakes for Healthcare Access

The transition from the legacy National Hospital Insurance Fund (NHIF) to the SHA remains one of the most critical and heavily scrutinized policy shifts in Kenya’s social sector. Because these contracts determine which facilities can receive government payouts and treat insured patients, any delay in getting accredited on the portal could leave hospitals temporarily locked out of the national insurance network.

For patients, the speed at which their local hospitals complete this fresh application process will directly impact their ability to access subsidized care. With the portal now live, the burden shifts entirely to hospital management to successfully upload their credentials and secure their places in the new national healthcare architecture.
