The National Computer and Cybercrimes Coordination Committee (NC4) has directed all public and private network operators to formally justify any decision to conceal data compromises from the public, introducing a structured four-tier reporting protocol designed to protect Kenyan consumers from unmonitored financial fraud and identity theft.

Under the newly clarified oversight rules, corporate entities and public agencies managing computer systems can no longer submit generic technical notifications following an intrusion. Instead, network custodians must calculate the direct human impact of every security incident, providing state cybercrime investigators with actionable data regarding how individual citizens will be shielded from ensuing criminal exploitation.

### The Four Mandatory Filing Requirements

According to instructions issued by the coordination agency, an acceptable cyber incident filing must satisfy four distinct informational thresholds before regulators can assess the severity of an intrusion:

![Cybersecurity](https://i.ibb.co/bMKpG7yh/31055fdf701b.webp)

* **Incident Nature:** Detailed technical records establishing what transpired, isolating the attack vector, and defining the precise mechanics of the breach.
* **Exposure Census:** A verifiable estimate of the total number of individuals affected, determining the operational scale where personal records or public-facing digital services were compromised.
* **Harm Assessment:** A formal risk matrix outlining individual vulnerability to subsequent financial theft, SIM manipulation, synthetic identity duplication, or public data exposure.
* **Non-Disclosure Justification:** Documented administrative reasons explaining why affected consumers or system users may be restricted or blocked from accessing details about the breach.

This fourth requirement targets a long-standing corporate practice of quietly patching exploited systems without notifying affected account holders, leaving everyday users vulnerable to downstream fraud.

### The 24-Hour Statutory Clock and Criminal Penalties

The compliance guidelines operate alongside statutory obligations that govern electronic infrastructure across the country. Operators managing both public and private computer networks are legally bound to lodge an official report with the NC4 within 24 hours of detecting any cyber threat or breach.

Failure to meet the 24-hour deadline or comply with the comprehensive four-part evidentiary standard carries direct criminal consequences for corporate leadership and technical administrators. The statute prescribes fines of up to Ksh 200,000, imprisonment for up to two years, or both for non-compliant network custodians.

### Escalating Attack Volumes Across Networks

The enforcement push comes against an unprecedented surge in hostile electronic activity targeting Kenyan infrastructure. Official regulatory figures from the Communications Authority of Kenya (CA) show that the country recorded 11,124,632,684 cyber threats during the 2025/2026 financial year.

The volume represents a 29 per cent escalation compared to the 8,622,876,858 security events tracked across the 2024/2025 period. Attacks intended to overwhelm online network bandwidth—known as Distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS) disruptions—more than doubled over the same twelve-month span.

The scale of hostile domestic activity was independently documented in the Interpol African Cyberthreat Assessment Report 2026, which singled out Kenya as East Africa's leading target for coordinated electronic syndicates. Interpol's findings noted that hostile groups are actively capitalising on the rapid expansion of mobile financial services and uneven telecom safeguards, recording 46,786 DDoS attacks against major Kenyan telecommunications carriers during the first half of 2025 alone.

With national systems facing billions of automated probes each quarter, the NC4's reporting framework shifts regulatory focus from basic perimeter defense to post-intrusion accountability, requiring system owners to substantiate the safety of consumer data on the clock.
