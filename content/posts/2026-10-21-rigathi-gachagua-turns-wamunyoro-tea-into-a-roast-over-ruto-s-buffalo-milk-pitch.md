Rigathi Gachagua did not wait to  his bags from the United States before turning state agricultural policy into a stand-up routine on his Wamunyoro patio.

Hosting Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna and delegates from the Linda Mwananchi movement on Wednesday, October 21, 2026, the former Deputy President used the local wildlife of Nyeri County to deliver a sharp comic dismissal of President William Ruto’s recent dairy crisis directives.

Rather than serving standard farm milk to his high-profile visitors, Gachagua joked that he had dispatched scouts toward the tree line of nearby Mount Kenya forest to source the exact beverage recommended by State House.

“I came back home and found that you have been told to start taking buffalo milk,” Gachagua told the assembled delegation. “I am at my home, Wamunyoro. Because there are so many buffaloes at Hombe Forest, I have sent young men to see if they can get some milk so that I can get enough to prepare for my visitors.”

### The Jamhuri Park Punchline

The punchline took direct aim at remarks President Ruto delivered on October 8, 2026, during the Agriculture and Food Security Transformation Summit at Jamhuri Park. Facing nationwide dairy shortages triggered by persistent drought, the Head of State had floated the untraditional alternative to an audience of farmers and dignitaries.

“If others are milking them, why can’t we do the same? What is wrong with that?” the President had asked during the summit session. “We need to diversify our sources of milk.”

For Gachagua, who has repeatedly leaned into rural domestic theatrics since exiting office, the summit declaration provided immediate comedy material for his re-entry into national politics. Wamunyoro sits within striking distance of the Hombe Forest belt, allowing the former Deputy President to frame national economic policy as a backyard chore gone absurd.

### Lounge Diplomacy with Sifuna

Behind the forest humor, the living room gathering marked Gachagua’s formal return to local political circulation following his prolonged American trip. The Wednesday session brought together key figures from the Linda Mwananchi lobby alongside Sifuna, testing cross-regional alliances ahead of the 2027 General Election.

Gachagua made it clear that his living-room hospitality was designed to reboot an opposition engine that he claimed had run out of steam while he was away.

“I returned after our people said politics had stalled and needed to be re-ignited,” Gachagua said. “Our cousins from Abaluhya visited me. They said that they would like us to unite to look for power so that we can kick out Kasongo because the country has continued to deteriorate.”

The Wamunyoro discussions touched on fielding a unified opposition contender for the presidency. Gachagua confirmed that consensus talks remain wide open, highlighting himself, Sifuna, and Wiper leader Kalonzo Musyoka as viable contenders to challenge the Kenya Kwanza administration.

### Building the DCP Machine

To anchor his own standing in those coalition negotiations, Gachagua used the gathering to sketch the organizational road ahead for his political outfit, the Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP).

He set an aggressive benchmark of securing more than 100 parliamentary seats at the next polls, describing the numerical target as a necessary legislative shield for his political base. He noted that a robust party contingent would protect friendly leaders—expressly including former President Uhuru Kenyatta—from administrative pressure.

With his tea-time broadside delivered, Gachagua signaled that the Wamunyoro circuit will stay active. He closed the day by teasing that more delegations are scheduled to make the trek to his Nyeri home in the coming weeks, promising that his forest-scouting routine would remain ready for whichever political leaders come calling next.
