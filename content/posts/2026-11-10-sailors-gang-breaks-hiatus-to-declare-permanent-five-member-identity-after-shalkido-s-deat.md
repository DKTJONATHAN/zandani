The surviving members of Sailors Gang have stepped out of an extended public hiatus, confronting months of silence to settle the identity of the group following the death of their former member Shalkido.

In their first joint statement released on Tuesday, November 10, 2026, via their verified Instagram account, the four artists acknowledged the turbulent period away from stage lights, framing their absence not as an abandonment of their craft, but as an overwhelming private reckoning with loss.

“A lot has happened behind the scenes,” the group stated. “Life took us through seasons we never imagined, and along the way, we lost someone more than just a group member. We lost a brother, a friend, and a part of our family.”

Rather than treating their re-emergence as a standard quartet reshuffle, the artists drew a hard line around the group's historical makeup, insisting that the stage lineup will not erase the original blueprint that anchored their music.

“We may be four standing here today, but Sailors will always be five in our hearts,” the statement read. “His memories, his laughter, his contribution and the moments we shared will forever be part of our story.”

### The Thika Road Crash and Hospital Battle

The Tuesday dispatch marks the collective's most direct public engagement with the loss of Shalkido, born Kevin Mburu Kinyanjui, who died at the age of 28 on October 6, 2025.

Shalkido sustained fatal injuries during a late-night motorcycle crash along Thika Road near Githurai as he traveled home from a live performance. Paramedics rushed the artist to Kenyatta University Teaching, Referral and Research Hospital (KUTRRH), where medical teams treated him for severe head trauma and acute internal bleeding before he succumbed to his injuries.

The crash abruptly cut short a determined individual comeback. Before the fatal October night, Shalkido had begun laying the groundwork to revive his recording career, drawing support from industry colleagues and fans who had rallied behind him following Sailors Gang’s initial creative pause.

### Carrying the Gengetone Legacy Forward

Sailors Gang broke into national prominence at the crest of Kenya’s Gengetone explosion, churning out a succession of street anthems that dictated urban radio and club playlists across East Africa. Anthems including *Wamlambez*, *Wainame*, and *Pekejeng* transformed the raw, Eastlands-bred sound into an unavoidable cultural wave, positioning the original five-man outfit at the center of a generational sonic shift.

Subsequent management disputes and structural fractures eventually slowed that early momentum, pushing the individual members into parallel solo paths and sporadic appearances. Shalkido’s death in late 2025 compounded those existing strains, casting uncertainty over whether the collective would ever convene under the shared banner again.

In addressing their long-standing supporters on Tuesday, the group made a point of recognizing the audience that carried their breakout tracks into mainstream dominance, thanking fans who stood by them during the height of their success and through the quiet months of grief.

While the quartet stopped short of announcing immediate release dates for new music, their formal return to public communication establishes a clear operational baseline: moving forward as four working performers, while keeping Shalkido permanently counted in the group's record.
