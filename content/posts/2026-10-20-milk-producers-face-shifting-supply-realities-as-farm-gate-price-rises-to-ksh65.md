Dairy producers grappling with constrained national yields are set to receive an official farm-gate price of Ksh65 per litre, backed by an added quarterly bonus structure as the administration moves to incentivize local output.

The revised pricing tier was made public by President William Ruto during the 63rd Mashujaa Day celebrations hosted at the Kipchoge Keino Stadium in Eldoret on Tuesday, October 20. Under the new directive, farmers will not only secure the higher baseline per-litre rate beginning this month but will also draw a Ksh2 quarterly bonus for every litre delivered.

![The Pioneer Affordable Housing Estate in Eldoret, Uasin Gishu County, October 18, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/qMYZdzjb/8dffa241abca.webp)

The adjustment marks an upward shift from previous producer baselines. According to state figures, the farm-gate price previously hovered around Ksh35 before climbing to Ksh60, with the latest intervention pushing returns to the Ksh65 mark as the country continues to navigate a persistent milk deficit.

While national production figures cited by the presidency point to an aggregate growth of one billion litres, the domestic market remains under pressure from supply constraints. In the North Rift hub of Eldoret, state-backed processors such as the New Kenya Cooperative Creameries (New KCC) anchor the collection and processing networks tasked with absorbing raw output from smallholder and commercial producers.

![A File Image of Citizen TV Studios](https://i.ibb.co/QFLBbBVh/0d8d1d007c57.webp)

Whether the increased per-litre return will sufficiently offset production costs and close the persistent domestic shortage gap remains to be seen as processing plants roll out the new payment schedule this month.
