If you were hoping the cooling global oil market would translate to cheaper city traffic runs this festive season, the latest numbers offer cold comfort.

Motorists across Nairobi will continue parting with KSh226.96 for a litre of Super Petrol, KSh227.76 for Diesel, and KSh219.87 for Kerosene after the Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority (EPRA) locked maximum retail prices for the November–December 2026 cycle. 

The flat pricing line hides a frantic behind-the-scenes financial rescue. To keep pump numbers from climbing further, the government dipped into the Petroleum Development Levy Fund (PDLF) to inject KSh4.6 billion, absorbing a shock that would have otherwise added up to KSh10.54 more per litre for diesel users.

Without that state intervention, the arithmetic dictated a punishing climb. EPRA’s review revealed that the average landed cost of imported refined petroleum spiked heavily between September and October. Super Petrol jumped 6.11 per cent to hit US$1,037.30 per cubic metre, while Diesel climbed 6.73 per cent to US$1,137.09 per cubic metre. Kerosene held relatively flat with a modest 0.71 per cent rise to US$1,106.11.

Paradoxically, the local stabilization measures collided with softening figures on international trading floors. Central Bank of Kenya bulletins tracking Murban crude showed prices easing to US$90.07 per barrel by November 12, down from US$94.04 a week prior, as Middle Eastern escalation fears subsided and global supply concerns weighed down Brent and WTI benchmarks.

Yet, because Kenya relies entirely on imported refined products rather than crude, local pumps remain tightly bound to foreign processing costs and the stability of the shilling, which held steady at KSh129.96 against the US dollar during the review window.

For drivers navigating the capital, the current stability follows a brutal October adjustment that saw petrol leap by nearly KSh13 and kerosene spike by over KSh28. While the KSh4.6 billion injection has successfully frozen the scoreboard through mid-December, it leaves consumers tethered to high baseline costs as the holiday travel season swings into gear.
