Kenya is moving to replace finished automotive imports with domestic industrial lines under a proposed Ksh390 billion green mobility agreement designed to assemble up to 150,000 electric vehicles each year.

The memorandum of understanding, signed at State House in Nairobi on Tuesday, sets terms between the national government and Endelevu Enterprise Corporation to construct two specialized assembly facilities, roll out nationwide off-grid charging infrastructure, and build localized supply networks.

Under the framework witnessed by President William Ruto, the manufacturing plan divides production across two separate facilities. One plant is earmarked for four-wheeled passenger and commercial vehicles with an annual output ceiling of 50,000 units. A second facility will focus on the country’s high-volume commuter transit segment, carrying an annual production capacity of up to 100,000 electric two-wheelers and light mobility units.

### Industrial footprint and charging grid

![President William Ruto witnessed the signing of an MoU between the Government of Kenya and Endelevu Enterprise Corporation at State House, Nairobi, on October 6, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/bRqZcPj8/8c9217f5b652.webp)

Beyond assembly lines, the commercial agreement outlines the deployment of 1,000 solar-powered charging hubs across the country, linked to a centralized digital management system engineered to track and support up to 100,000 active electric units.

State House officials project that operationalizing the twin assembly plants and the supporting supply chain will generate roughly 2,000 direct manufacturing jobs. An additional 20,000 indirect roles are slated for domestic parts suppliers, logistics handlers, and service contractors, alongside an estimated 80,000 ancillary opportunities within fleet operations and maintenance.

Speaking during the signing ceremony, President Ruto characterized the Ksh390 billion proposal as an intentional pivot away from finished foreign vehicle shipments, arguing that domestic fabrication is necessary to capture industrial value that currently leaves the country.

"Producing vehicles locally would help keep more economic value in the country while creating opportunities for technicians, engineers, suppliers, and other businesses involved in the automotive sector," Ruto said, noting that the project represents one of the largest single capital commitments to regional green transport.

### The scale gap: 39,000 imports to 150,000 annual builds

The pact accelerates an aggressive official drive to build an electric transport sector from the ground up, formalised in February 2026 with the gazettement of the National Electric Mobility Policy.

Ministry of Roads and Transport data illustrates the steep scaling curve the new factories are expected to address. By the close of 2025, Kenya’s cumulative electric vehicle registrations stood at 39,324 units. While that figure marked substantial expansion from the 1,378 clean units logged on local roads in 2022, the bulk of that inventory relied on imported conversions, completely built units, and pilot fleet shipments.

To prime consumer demand ahead of factory-level domestic production, the state previously eliminated value-added tax on electric buses, motorcycles, bicycles, and lithium-ion battery packs, while the transport registry introduced distinctive green registration plates to identify compliant clean-energy vehicles.

The capital investment outlined in the State House memorandum now shifts the burden from consumer-side tax waivers to execution on factory floors, where Endelevu and state agencies must turn outline production targets into operational assembly bays.
