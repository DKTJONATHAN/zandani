President William Ruto has directed Kenya's diplomatic and economic advocacy toward a dual overhaul of global institutions, urging international lenders to transition from direct debt issuance to risk-sharing guarantees while Nairobi moves to expand its physical footprint as a United Nations hub.

Co-hosting the Partners for Multilateralism Summit in New York alongside European Council President António Costa and Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney, Ruto argued that the 80-year-old global financial architecture must be restructured to unlock Africa’s domestic capital rather than trapping the continent in high-interest external debt cycles.

![President Ruto addressing the High-Level Reflection on the Pact for the Future, convened by Namibia, Zambia, the African Union, and the Economic Commission for Africa in New York o](https://i.ibb.co/qM25SJv1/04b06b7a6166.webp)

The summit, held on the sidelines of the 81st United Nations General Assembly (UNGA), serves as the launchpad for Kenya’s dual-track campaign. On one front, the administration is demanding that multilateral development finance institutions (DFIs) pivot their operational models. Instead of relying primarily on direct lending—which Ruto argues is restricted by inaccurate risk-pricing that inflates borrowing costs for developing nations—DFIs must act as market catalysts.

According to the President, this shift requires global institutions to deploy guarantees, credit enhancements, and risk-sharing instruments. The objective is to make it safer and easier for African countries to channel their substantial domestic savings directly into development projects, reducing reliance on expensive foreign-currency loans.

On the political front, Kenya is leveraging its position as the host of the UN's only headquarters in the Global South. As Ruto calls for systemic reforms—including permanent African seats on the UN Security Council under the Ezulwini Consensus and Sirte Declaration—Nairobi is actively acquiring additional land in Gigiri. This land acquisition is designated for the construction of new offices for UN missions, physically cementing Kenya's leverage within the multilateral system it seeks to reform.

![United Nations Secretary-General Antonio Guterres](https://i.ibb.co/q3YpdWxF/bd411885f7fb.webp)

This structural push aligns with support from UN Secretary-General António Guterres, who previously backed Africa’s demand for permanent representation on the Security Council. Guterres noted that the current Council, which lacks a permanent African member despite the continent's population and geopolitical weight, remains a historical injustice originating from the colonial era.

The immediate focus now shifts to how multilateral development banks respond to the demand for risk-mitigation tools. With African nations facing severe debt pressures and escalating climate finance needs, Kenya's push to alter credit-risk pricing remains a critical test of whether the 80-year-old financial system can adapt before domestic capital reserves are exhausted.
