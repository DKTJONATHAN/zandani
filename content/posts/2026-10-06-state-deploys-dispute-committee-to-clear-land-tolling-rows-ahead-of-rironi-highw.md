A multi-agency dispute-resolution committee has been deployed to resolve outstanding land acquisition, compensation, and highway tolling conflicts along the Ksh200 billion Rironi-Mau Summit road, just weeks before the first major section opens to motorists.

The Kenya National Highways Authority (KeNHA), working with the Public Private Partnership (PPP) Directorate and contractor China Roads and Bridges Corporation (CRBC), launched the Grievance Redress Mechanism (GRM) specifically for the Rironi-Gilgil section. The deployment is a direct response to rising community friction along the 175-kilometer transit corridor, which is one of Kenya’s most expensive infrastructure undertakings.

The timing of the committee's launch is critical. According to Roads Cabinet Secretary Davis Chirchir, the Rironi-Naivasha section of the highway is scheduled to open to the public on October 30, 2026. The state is racing to clear administrative and physical bottlenecks on the upper side of the A8 corridor before shifting construction equipment to other high-traffic segments.

![An image of ongoing construction works on the Rironi-Mau Summit captured on September 23, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/N6CvRWNt/c9db8c397ed0.webp)

Led by Lucy Wainaina, KeNHA’s Environmental and Social Safeguards (ESS) team will oversee the GRM committee. The panel brings together several state agencies that hold regulatory keys to the project’s friction points. Representatives from the National Land Commission (NLC) will handle title disputes and valuation issues, while the National Environment Management Authority (NEMA) will address environmental disruptions.

The Ministry of Labour, Kenya Power, and Children’s Services are also represented on the committee, alongside local Project-Affected Persons (PAPs).

By placing these agencies into a single, localized committee, the state is attempting to bypass the slow, bureaucratic channels that traditionally stall large infrastructure projects. KeNHA stated that the GRM committees will provide a structured process for receiving, addressing, and resolving complaints, aiming to prevent active conflicts between local communities and the contractor.

Three primary pressure points dominate the committee's immediate agenda:

![An impression of how the Rironi-Mau Summit highway is expected to look after it is expanded.](https://i.ibb.co/JwDXX6BF/f5505c84b159.webp)

* **Proposed Tolling:** The government’s plan to charge motorists for using the expanded highway has met steady resistance from local transport operators and commuters.
* **Land Acquisition:** The expansion of the corridor into a multi-lane highway requires significant corridor widening, affecting commercial and agricultural land.
* **Compensation Delays:** Many local landowners have demanded prompt payouts from the NLC before heavy machinery begins work on their properties.

The urgency to address these complaints is heightened by the project's aggressive phased schedule. Once the Rironi-Naivasha section opens at the end of October, the government plans to redirect traffic onto this newly completed upper stretch of the A8 corridor.

This diversion will allow engineers to begin major works on the A8 South section, which covers the Rironi-Mai Mahiu-Naivasha road. Chirchir confirmed that this sequential approach is designed to maintain traffic flow on the busy Western-bound transport corridor while construction continues toward Mau Summit.

The entire 175-kilometer expansion is scheduled for completion by June 2027. By then, the corridor is expected to establish a high-capacity link between Nairobi, Naivasha, and the wider East African region. However, the success of this June 2027 target hinges on how quickly Wainaina’s team can resolve the localized disputes now threatening to slow down construction on the remaining stretches.
