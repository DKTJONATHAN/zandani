While early morning drizzles are set to slow commuter traffic in Nairobi and Kiambu, the sharpest edge of Kenya's next weather system is tracking further west, where heavy downpours and afternoon electrical storms are forecast across lake and highland counties through Thursday.

The latest five-day advisory from the Kenya Meteorological Service Authority (KMSA), running from Sunday, October 4 to Thursday, October 8, maps an uneven distribution of rainfall across six key geographical belts. The system signals an active start to the broader October-to-December seasonal rains, which forecasters project will coincide with an El Niño weather pattern.

According to the meteorology department, the most intense atmospheric activity will concentrate over the western agricultural zones and the Lake Victoria depression. Counties within the lake basin—specifically Kisumu, Siaya, and Homa Bay—are projected to sustain heavy rainfall over all five days, with the strongest precipitation concentrated in the afternoons.

### Thunderstorm Corridor Across the Rift and West

For western and central Rift Valley counties, the meteorological agency warned that moisture surges will bring sharp, localized convection rather than steady drizzles.

![Motorists using a flooded highway during cold and rainy weather in Nairobi](https://i.ibb.co/rGJcrYdM/61a3cf90d86a.webp)

Trans Nzoia and Elgeyo-Marakwet are forecast to see thunderstorms develop from Monday through Wednesday. Similar storm conditions are expected across Kericho, Bomet, Nandi, and Baringo during the same window.

Further west, the weather office expects afternoon thunderstorms to persist across Kakamega and Vihiga from Monday through Thursday. Neighbouring Bungoma is slated for afternoon rainfall on Monday, Tuesday, and Thursday, while Busia will likely record its wettest periods on Tuesday and Thursday.

This pattern places significant runoff into regional river systems early in the season, contrasting sharply with the intermittent showers anticipated along the country's eastern transport spine.

### Morning Moisture for Nairobi and the East

Across the capital and the central highlands, the forecast describes a much lighter, morning-skewed precipitation profile.

![Nairobi residents crossing a flooded road in the CBD (left) and floods in Parklands estate.](https://i.ibb.co/yFjKTW4M/6efd5e817af9.webp)

Nairobi and Kiambu counties are expected to experience morning rains across all five days, though moderate afternoon showers will pick up toward midweek on Wednesday and Thursday. In Nyeri, light rains will punctuate Monday, Wednesday, and Thursday, with a single burst of moderate rain projected for Tuesday morning. Kirinyaga and Murang’a are set for persistent morning rains across the entire five-day outlook.

In the lower eastern highlands, Kitui, Machakos, and Makueni will similarly navigate light morning rains throughout the week, complemented by afternoon showers from Monday onward. Meru, Tharaka-Nithi, and Embu will experience a parallel sequence of wet mornings and lighter afternoon activity.

### Coastal Influx and Arid Showers

Along the Indian Ocean coastline, moderate rains will roll across Mombasa, Kilifi, and Kwale between Sunday and Thursday, peaking during the afternoons and extending into the night. Lamu will see lighter showers bookending its days in the early mornings and late nights, while Taita Taveta remains largely dry until light rains arrive on Wednesday and Thursday.

Even arid and semi-arid tracts in the north are drawn into the five-day footprint, though at lower volumes. Wajir is forecast to receive morning rains through Thursday, Mandera will register light rains on Monday and Tuesday, and Marsabit will see light morning precipitation matched by nighttime showers throughout the cycle. Isiolo's exposure remains limited to afternoon showers on Sunday and Monday.

The widespread geographical coverage over the next five days serves as the initial test for local infrastructure and drainage systems ahead of projected peak volumes later in the October-December cycle.
