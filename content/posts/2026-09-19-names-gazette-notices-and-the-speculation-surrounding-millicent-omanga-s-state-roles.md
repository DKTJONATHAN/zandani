A simple middle name has become the latest line of demarcation in the highly scrutinized world of Kenyan state appointments. 

When Interior Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen published a Gazette Notice dated August 26, 2026, listing the new members of the Board of Visitors of the Shikusa Borstal Institution, the inclusion of "Millicent Kerubo Omanga" immediately set off online speculation. In a political landscape where state appointments are closely parsed for patterns of reward and patronage, many assumed the former Nominated Senator had quietly returned to the public payroll.

However, the former lawmaker moved to clear the administrative confusion on Saturday, September 19, 2026, revealing that the gazetted appointee was a different individual altogether.

"My full name is Millicent Nyaboke Omanga… and to the gossipers: sorry," Omanga stated on social media, distancing herself from the three-year appointment that took effect on August 14. 

### The Shikusa Appointment

The position in question is relatively obscure compared to the high-profile offices usually associated with Omanga. Pursuant to Section 20 (1) of the Borstal Institutions Act, the Board of Visitors oversees the welfare and rehabilitation programs at the Shikusa Borstal Institution, a juvenile correctional facility in Kakamega County. 

While the gazetted Millicent Kerubo Omanga is one of ten individuals appointed to the board, the intense focus on her name reflects the persistent public interest in the political fortunes of the former Senator. 

### A Turbulent History with State Roles

The readiness of the public to believe Omanga had secured a new state role stems from a highly volatile administrative record over the last three years, characterized by rapid appointments, judicial roadblocks, and public rejections.

Omanga’s career in public boards began relatively quietly with a directorship at the Kenya Electricity Generating Company (KenGen) between 2013 and 2017, which preceded her term in the Senate. However, her subsequent appointments under the current administration have faced repeated hurdles:

*   **March 2023:** Appointed as Chief Administrative Secretary (CAS) in the Ministry of Interior. This deployment was cut short in July 2023 when the High Court declared the entire CAS office unconstitutional, rendering her position void.
*   **October 2024:** President William Ruto appointed her to the Nairobi Rivers Commission. In a highly publicized move, Omanga turned down the offer.
*   **January 2025:** Appointed to the Board of Directors of Kenya Shipyards Limited. Her tenure there was short-lived, with her appointment being revoked in April 2026.

This cycle of appointments and exit strategies has made Omanga’s name a lightning rod for speculation whenever a new Gazette Notice appears. 

With the identity issue now clarified, the actual appointee, Millicent Kerubo Omanga, remains on course to serve her three-year term on the Shikusa board, while the former Senator remains outside the formal machinery of the state—at least for now.
