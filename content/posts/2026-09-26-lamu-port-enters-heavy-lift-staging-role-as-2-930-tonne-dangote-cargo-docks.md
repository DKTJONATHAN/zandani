The Port of Lamu moved into an active industrial staging phase on Saturday as the cargo ship *MV Da Yang* discharged 2,930 metric tonnes of heavy machinery destined for the planned Dangote East Africa Refinery.

The offloading marks the initial physical logistics run into the coastal port for the Ksh2.2 trillion ($17 billion) refinery and petrochemical development, establishing Lamu as the primary freight terminal days before earthmovers officially break ground on the complex.

Docking at Lamu's deep-water quayside, *MV Da Yang* delivered earthmoving and industrial equipment intended for immediate site establishment. Kenya Ports Authority (KPA) Managing Director Captain William Ruto received the vessel, presenting its master, Captain Wang Shengli, with a commemorative plaque and a certificate of first call to recognize the maiden industrial drop.

"The arrival of this vessel is very critical and shows the Government’s commitment to ensuring this project succeeds," Captain Ruto said at the quayside. "It will be a game changer for the entire region."

### Industrial Staging at Lamu Quayside

For the Port of Lamu, handling heavy project freight of this scale shifts its operational profile from periodic container and transshipment handling to servicing specialized engineering logistics. Under the development plan, the port is designated to manage all inbound maritime movements of specialized refinery modules, heavy processing equipment, and construction inputs before eventually pivoting to liquid cargo and refined petroleum export handling.

The machinery delivery lands as Dangote Group finalizes technical coordination for the 700,000-barrel-per-day processing complex. Once fully built, the installation is designed to refine crude yields from northern Kenya’s Lokichar basin in Turkana alongside supplies sourced across East and Southern Africa, positioning Lamu as an integrated energy refining terminal.

### Indian State Oversight Contract

Behind the dockside machinery deployment, Dangote Group has locked in formal supervisory architecture to prevent cost and schedule overruns on the massive project. An engineering consultancy firm operating under India’s Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas secured a project management contract valued at over Ksh58 billion ($450 million).

![dangote-refinery-ship.jpg](https://i.ibb.co/Q3k5bVM3/ec3d34ffdd98.webp)

The Indian state-owned entity will act as both Project Management Consultant and Engineering, Procurement, and Construction Management (EPCM) consultant. Under its mandate, the firm is charged with overseeing detailed engineering design, procurement tenders, contractor synchronization, and quality benchmarks during construction.

Crucially, the Ksh58 billion management and advisory allocation sits outside the estimated Ksh2.2 trillion baseline construction budget, creating a dedicated layer of technical accounting to monitor timelines and material specifications independently from site contractors.

### Site Groundbreaking Set for Next Week

The maritime arrival follows political clearance secured during President William Ruto’s recent bilateral tour of the Lekki-based Dangote Petroleum Refinery in Lagos, Nigeria. Following the tour, the presidency confirmed that the national government had completed land allocations for the Lamu footprint and cleared administrative hurdles to facilitate an uninterrupted buildout.

With the initial 2,930 tonnes of plant machinery secured on the quayside, logistics teams are preparing to move the equipment inland to the designated project acreage ahead of the official groundbreaking ceremony scheduled for next week.
