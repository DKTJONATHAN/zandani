Patients seeking specialized care at Kenyatta University Teaching, Referral and Research Hospital face a looming intake freeze as unionized health workers draw a hard line over unfulfilled administrative reforms.

Under a 21-day strike notice served by the Kenya National Union of Nurses and Midwives (KNUNM), operations are slated to shift into a restrictive emergency-only footing at 12:01 am on November 9. While union leadership confirmed that staff currently admitted will continue receiving attention, the directive explicitly bars the facility from accepting any new admissions once the industrial action takes effect.

![Kenyatta University Teaching, Referral & Research Hospital Main entrance](https://i.ibb.co/Ldgtvpwd/ec940586bb1e.webp)

The standoff follows months of friction between union representatives and the hospital's administration, including an earlier walkout threat in September that was defused before services were disrupted. This time, KNUNM General Secretary Seth Panyako has instructed branch officials to mobilize workers for a complete labour withdrawal unless management settles seven outstanding disputes.

At the center of the dispute are long-standing employment regularisation issues. The union is demanding the immediate conversion of eligible nurses from temporary terms to permanent and pensionable contracts, alongside payment of accrued salary arrears and outstanding gratuity. Other unresolved demands include the finalization and signing of a Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA), a review of temporary contract rates, recognition of higher academic and professional qualifications, standardization of working hours, compensation for public holidays, and the proper alignment of employees within common cadres.

![A collage of the nurses' strike that rocked Nairobi's Central Business District on August 25.](https://i.ibb.co/Jj0zhCW7/0bf21e84a57f.webp)

According to the union, repeated attempts to resolve the grievances through direct engagement with hospital executives have stalled, leaving industrial action as the remaining recourse to enforce compliance.

With the three-week countdown underway, hospital administrators face a narrow window to clear the administrative backlog and avert a service paralysis that threatens to compound patient backlogs across the metropolitan referral network.
