As variable renewable energy climbs past a third of Kenya’s daily electricity consumption, unmanaged rooftop solar systems feeding back into the distribution network have crossed the line from private cost-saving measure to operational grid hazard.

To curb uncoordinated supply spikes, the Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority (EPRA) has introduced a dumping surcharge that targets commercial and residential solar users exporting surplus electricity into the grid without formal authorization.

Under amendments to the 2023 tariff schedule, any unauthorized electrical feed-in pushed back into the Kenya Power distribution system will be categorized as dumping. Rather than earning credits or offsets, producers making unapproved feed-ins will be billed directly at the applicable base tariff for the unauthorized energy pushed into the lines.

### The Net-Metering Boundary

![Kenya Power CEO Joseph Siror (left) and the company's staff repair a transformer.](https://i.ibb.co/CKPSyTJd/be9e1b0d570f.webp)

The regulatory push aligns with the Energy (Net-Metering) Regulations 2024, designed to integrate small-scale renewable generators into the central grid. Under these rules, systems with capacities up to one megawatt (1 MW) are legally allowed to export surplus power, provided the owner executes a binding net-metering contract with Kenya Power.

Where no contract exists, or where connected systems endanger operational stability, the regulations grant Kenya Power explicit authority to physically disconnect non-compliant installations from the network.

Beyond technical compliance, the utility has warned that illegal or uncoordinated solar connections present severe physical hazards to line crews working on distribution infrastructure and risk triggering localized transformer overloads.

### Frequency Spikes and Off-Peak Swings

The enforcement comes as Kenya Power manages increasingly volatile frequency and voltage swings driven by weather-dependent generation sources. 

![British SAS soldier Chris Craighead (right) holding a rescue operation at Dusit D2 in January 2019.](https://i.ibb.co/cSw6y8yx/4822822babb9.webp)

Data released by the utility on August 11, 2026, illustrates the scale of renewable penetration on the central grid. Variable renewable energy—primarily solar and wind—accounted for 34 percent of total generation during daytime peak demand, which reached 1,900 megawatts.

During low-load periods when overall demand drops to 1,200 megawatts, the share of solar and wind leaps to 36 percent of the total supply mix. Sudden cloud cover or rapid bursts of sunlight cause immediate dips and surges in generation, forcing grid controllers to quickly fire up thermal plants or adjust hydro generation to maintain system frequency and prevent localized brownouts.

### What Private Solar Owners Face

For commercial facilities and residential properties operating off-grid or hybrid solar setups, the new policy eliminates the practice of using the national grid as a passive dumping ground for excess generation.

Systems fitted with export-capable inverters must now either be reconfigured with reverse-power relays to block feedback or undergo formal inspection and approval under the 1 MW net-metering framework before sending a single kilowatt-hour across the transformer.
