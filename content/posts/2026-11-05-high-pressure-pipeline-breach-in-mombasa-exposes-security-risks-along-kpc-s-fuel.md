Tapping directly into a high-pressure petroleum main requires specialized engineering tools, technical knowledge, and physical access to restricted rights-of-way. The discovery of an illegal bypass along Kenya Pipeline Company's (KPC) main distribution network in Mombasa has brought those operational security vulnerabilities into sharp focus, forcing detectives to examine how unauthorized crews penetrated one of the country's most heavily guarded energy corridors.

Automated monitoring systems first registered an unexpected pressure drop on Tuesday, November 3, along the primary pipeline segment connecting two major delivery pumping stations in Mombasa. KPC security and maintenance teams dispatched to investigate found that suspects had drilled directly into the steel line under active hydraulic pressure, installing a custom valve connection designed to draw off refined petroleum without triggering immediate automated shut-offs. Law enforcement agencies secured the site on Wednesday, November 4, before establishing a perimeter.

![A collage of an illegal fuel connection in Mombasa uncovered by KPC on November 2, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/PZr8Rzbp/e0d330f23086.webp)

The mechanics of the breach have turned police attention toward perimeter controls and potential insider access. Live petroleum transport lines operate under significant force, making unassisted manual tapping hazardous and technically complex. In a press briefing on Thursday, November 5, Mombasa detectives confirmed they are examining how suspects breached the restricted zone, declining to rule out internal involvement while investigators work to determine whether KPC staff or former contractors aided the setup.

The foiled breach highlights an ongoing financial and physical drain across Kenya’s fuel transport routes. Disclosures presented to the National Assembly Committee on Energy indicate that illegal siphoning costs KPC approximately Ksh600 million every year. That loss reflects roughly 3.2 million liters of diverted fuel annually, accounting for about 0.04 percent of the company's total volume capacity.

![A photo collage of fuel tanks allegedly siphoned in Nairobi](https://i.ibb.co/XxZKQtqs/1b6ea5b8cba8.webp)

The Mombasa discovery follows a series of similar interventions targeting organized fuel diversion across the country over the past year. In April, the Energy Petroleum Regulatory Authority (EPRA) alongside the Energy Police Unit (EPU) raided an illegal siphoning yard along Nadume Road in Nairobi's Industrial Area, seizing 800 liters of diverted product. Earlier enforcement operations uncovered illicit siphoning operations in Kathangaita, Machakos, as well as a January operation at Sultan Hamud Railway Station where thieves tapped fuel directly from rail tank wagons.

Police officers remain stationed at the Mombasa breach point while KPC technical crews complete line restoration and integrity testing. Investigators are currently conducting forensic analysis on the recovered drilling equipment and custom valve fittings to trace their origin and identify the logistics network intended to haul the stolen fuel.
