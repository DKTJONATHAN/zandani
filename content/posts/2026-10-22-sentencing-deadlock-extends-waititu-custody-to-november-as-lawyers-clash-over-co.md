A sharp procedural contest between State prosecutors and defence counsel over the exact scope of sentencing has paralyzed the conclusion of Kiambu County’s Ksh588.7 million graft trial, locking former Governor Ferdinand Waititu and his co-accused in remand until late November.

Sentencing proceedings collapsed on Thursday at the Anti-Corruption Court after prosecutor Vincent Monda and defence advocate Chris Mutuku took conflicting positions on which convicts are legally due for mitigation and punishment. The deadlock forced Chief Magistrate Harrison Barasa to halt the session and formally escalate the file back to the High Court registry for clarification.

With formal directions stalled in the judiciary’s electronic filing pipeline, the dispute immediately stretched the custodial clock for the former governor and his wife, Susan Wangari Ndung’u, who will now remain behind bars through at least November 27, 2026.

### The Subordinate Court Impasse

The standoff in Barasa’s courtroom emerged from friction surrounding a recent High Court intervention that drastically altered the legal posture of the case. 

![Waititu Court](https://i.ibb.co/zhXQpxFb/96a214642398.webp)

State and defence teams failed to agree on how to apply punishment after High Court Judge Rose Ougo overturned the earlier acquittal of the accused persons on money laundering counts while simultaneously affirming their convictions on multiple other economic crime charges. 

Rather than proceeding with mitigation on the established counts, the disagreement over who must be subjected to sentencing in the lower court forced the magistrate to seek guidance from the High Court through the Deputy Registrar.

Barasa noted on Thursday that while superior court directions had already been formulated, they had not yet been formally published on the judiciary's digital system for immediate extraction by the advocates.

“I have since been advised that the directions have been issued and are awaiting publication on the court system for the parties’ further action,” Barasa told the courtroom before pushing the sentencing timeline.

### Life Behind Prison Gates

The administrative delay directly penalizes the personal liberties of the convicts, whose bail cushions have entirely evaporated. 

Following Justice Ougo’s ruling a week ago, the High Court cancelled all existing bond terms, sending the former county executive to Nairobi Remand Prison while remanding Wangari to Lang’ata Women’s Prison. That order wiped out a brief period of freedom Waititu secured in February 2026, when the High Court had temporarily reduced his appellate bail to Ksh20 million.

![Ferdinand Waititu](https://i.ibb.co/Kpk5dW9v/1c70063dee66.webp)

The original convictions date back to February 12, 2025, when the anti-corruption trial court found Waititu, his spouse, and their co-accused guilty of conflict of interest, abuse of office, dealing with suspect property, and executing fraudulent procurement schemes.

The criminal docket centers on a 2019 investigation into the irregular award of a Ksh588.7 million road improvement tender during Kiambu County’s 2017/2018 fiscal cycle, where gravel routes across several sub-counties were contracted for upgrading to bitumen standards.

### Parallel Forfeiture Demands

The stalled sentencing timeline in the criminal division runs alongside an equally intense financial dragnet managed by anti-graft detectives. 

Outside the magistrate's sentencing dock, the Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission (EACC) is running an active civil recovery suit demanding Ksh170 million from Waititu and an enterprise linked to the road tender. The commission maintains the contested sum constitutes proceeds of corruption that must be surrendered back to public accounts regardless of the jail terms or fines imposed by the criminal court.

For now, the trial court has set an interim mention for Tuesday, October 27, 2026, to confirm whether the High Court’s directives have officially loaded onto the court management system, even as substantive sentencing remains deferred to late November.
