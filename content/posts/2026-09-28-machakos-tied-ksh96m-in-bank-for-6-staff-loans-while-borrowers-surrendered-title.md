A credit arrangement that locked away Ksh96 million of Machakos County funds in a commercial bank for seven years has delivered loans to only six individuals, even while requiring those same borrowers to pledge their private property as collateral.

The terms of the staff car loan and mortgage scheme, established in 2019, came under scrutiny when the Senate Committee on County Public Investments and Special Funds questioned Governor Wavinya Ndeti and her executive team over the lopsided arrangement.

Auditors established that out of the Ksh96 million deposited with SBM Bank to anchor the facility, the lender disbursed just Ksh55.3 million across six accounts. Despite the public cash serving as institutional security, the bank still required borrowing employees to surrender personal logbooks and land title deeds to access the credit.

### The Double-Security Trap

The dual collateral requirement drew sharp rebuke from lawmakers, who pointed out that public money remained trapped in a non-interest-earning account without shielding staff from private financial exposure.

![Wavinya Ndeti](https://i.ibb.co/tPF1qt4j/6b1803f23f39.webp)

"We cannot have Ksh96 million in public funds sitting in an account without earning interest while beneficiaries are also required to provide their own security," said Senate Committee Vice-Chairperson Beth Syengo, who presided over the session. "The county must review this arrangement and demonstrate that it gives value to the public."

Governor Ndeti maintained that the deposit was not sitting idle, explaining that the county funds acted as a guarantee mechanism that prompted SBM Bank to issue advances from the lender's own balance sheet rather than directly drawing down the public deposit.

"The Ksh96 million is security for the scheme, not money that has simply been left unused," Ndeti told the committee. "However, we accept the concerns raised and are engaging the bank so that the arrangement can be reviewed and improved."

### Access Gap for County Workers

Machakos Senator Agnes Kavindu pressed the county administration on why so few workers had benefited from a multi-million-shilling facility over nearly a decade of operation.

"County resources must serve employees fairly," Kavindu said. "If this scheme is sustainable, then its structure should allow more eligible staff to benefit while protecting public funds."

![A file photo of an empty county assembly](https://i.ibb.co/nqKPVK5v/504f0d67e8aa.webp)

Senator William Kisang instructed the county executive to produce clear paper trails, including ongoing renegotiation correspondence with SBM Bank, a revised draft agreement, and the active loan book detailing how the Ksh55.3 million was apportioned.

The inquiry into the credit facility coincided with broader friction across Machakos County's fiscal management. In April 2026, Senate scrutiny of the county's 2024/2025 books flagged Ksh4.3 billion in trade payables, with auditors highlighting debts that had aged past 365 days.

### Compounding Cash Strains

The loan scheme dispute also lands against an active operational squeeze at county headquarters. On September 22, county workers staged protests over unpaid August salaries, unremitted statutory deductions, and frozen medical insurance benefits.

Those payroll disruptions parallel an ongoing institutional standoff over the county’s Ksh17.8 billion budget for 2026/2027, which Ndeti declined to sign after the County Assembly amended allocations totaling Ksh853.96 million—adjustments the governor argued would disrupt frontline public services.

Beyond the bank facility, the Senate committee directed the Machakos administration to finalize missing valuation registers for municipal assets, verify compliance across all revenue collection accounts, and correct shortfalls in statutory quotas for ethnic diversity and persons living with disabilities in future staff hiring.
