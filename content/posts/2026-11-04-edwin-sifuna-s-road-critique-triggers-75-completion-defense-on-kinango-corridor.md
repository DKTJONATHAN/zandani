Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna’s public spotlight on the Kinango Road has drawn a sharp response from government defenders, who claim the critical Coast transport corridor is already 75 per cent complete despite ongoing political commentary.

The rebuttal, aimed at counteracting criticism raised by Sifuna and his political counterparts, argues that focusing strictly on unpaved construction zones misrepresents a highway project that has remained largely neglected since independence. According to project details outlined in the statement, major segments along the route have already transitioned from unpaved tracks to completed tarmac, changing transit conditions between Kwale and neighboring trade hubs.

### Samburu to Moyeni Progress Audit

Central to the project defense is a breakdown of completed civil works along the historical route. The stretch connecting Samburu directly towards Kinango Town is now fully tarmacked, resolving what local motorists had long considered one of the most difficult transit stretches in the region.

In addition to the Samburu segment, contractors have completed a 23-kilometre tarmac section surrounding the Moyeni area. Active civil engineering works are currently concentrated on the remaining unpaved portions, with project managers projecting full completion by February 2027. Defenders of the project maintain that highlighting active construction zones ignores decades of total inactivity on a road that was virtually impassable under previous administrations.

### Coast Region Policy Metrics

The political friction over the Kinango corridor has expanded into a broader defense of President William Ruto’s development footprint across the Coast region. The statement accompanying the road update links infrastructure progress to social welfare initiatives currently operating in surrounding counties.

On public health, the statement highlights shifts in medical financing across the region, particularly for low-income families managing high-cost conditions such as kidney disease and cancer. It asserts that households that previously defaulted to selling livestock or land to fund emergency medical care now access institutional health coverage without facing the same financial devastation.

Similarly, the defense points to agricultural interventions in neighboring Kilifi County, where the national fertilizer subsidy scheme has capped input costs at KSh 2,000 per bag. Supporters argue that the reduced retail price has directly enabled smallholder farmers to secure necessary planting supplies, framing both the input subsidy and the Kinango Road works as direct evidence of policy execution.

### Timelines and Political Benchmarks

As the February 2027 completion date approaches, the pushback surrounding the Kinango Road reflects an ongoing debate over how infrastructure delivery is evaluated by opposition figures and government communicators.

By demanding that leadership performance be judged on physical evidence—such as finished tarmac kilometres and lower agricultural input costs—project advocates are pushing back against Sifuna's commentary, setting up the completed Kinango corridor as a key test case for development claims in Kwale County.
