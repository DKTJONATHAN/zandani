Former Attorney General Justin Muturi has turned Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen's recent diplomatic trip to Uganda into a cross-border confrontation, calling on Ugandan President Yoweri Museveni and election regulators to answer for the minister's public voter registration pitch in Kampala.

In an official statement issued on Saturday, September 26, 2026, the Democratic Party (DP) leader insisted that Murkomen’s public remarks advising border-region residents on acquiring Kenyan identity documents and voter cards cannot be treated as routine administration. Muturi demanded that the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) immediately summon the Youth Affairs, Creative Economy, and Sports Cabinet Secretary to give a formal account of his Kampala remarks.

“Claims touching on voter registration activities in Uganda and Kenya’s electoral process cannot be brushed aside,” Muturi stated, calling on the electoral body to protect the integrity of the voter register ahead of the 2027 General Election.

Muturi specifically urged the electoral agency to compel Murkomen to explain what he meant and identify any specific voter registration operations taking place on Ugandan soil.

Taking his challenge beyond domestic politics, the former Attorney General extended his petition directly to State House in Kampala, requesting President Yoweri Museveni to state clearly whether the Ugandan government had prior knowledge of or gave official backing to any activities tied to Kenya’s upcoming polls.

“I also call on President Yoweri Museveni to clarify whether his government is aware of, or has sanctioned, any activities in Uganda connected to Kenya’s electoral process,” Muturi added.

Muturi emphasized that any concrete evidence pointing to illicit voter listing or external influence must be investigated by state security and electoral bodies, with individuals held personally accountable under Kenyan law.

The political storm stems from remarks Murkomen delivered during a public engagement in Uganda, where he addressed administrative delays facing Kenyans living in border zones who lack essential civil documentation. Seeking to assure residents, Murkomen encouraged affected citizens to utilize state consular services in Kampala or visit border registration posts.

“I want to assure Kenyans who are here looking for their documents, we have made it easy for you to get your documents, just go to the Embassy in Kampala and get registered. But you can also cross the border and get registered easily,” Murkomen told the crowd during the event.

By linking Murkomen’s administrative advice directly to foreign interference concerns, Muturi has transformed a speech about border consular services into an international talking point. For the former Attorney General, any public statement by a Cabinet Minister regarding foreign-soil voter registration demands explicit clearance from both the electoral commission and foreign governments to ensure full public confidence.

Neither CS Murkomen nor representatives from the Ugandan government have publicly responded to Muturi's demands. Attention now turns to the IEBC and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs to see whether electoral officials will issue a summons or seek formal diplomatic communication with Kampala.
