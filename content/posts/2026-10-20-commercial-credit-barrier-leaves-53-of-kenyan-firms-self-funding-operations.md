More than half of Kenya’s formal private sector is running on internal cash flow rather than commercial debt, caught between restrictive bank lending conditions and an escalating regulatory overhead.

Findings published by the Central Bank of Kenya (CBK) from its September 2026 survey of 600 chief executive officers show that 53 percent of enterprises relied entirely on their own generated resources to fund baseline operations. Only 28 percent turned to commercial bank borrowing as their primary financing stream, underlining a sustained withdrawal from formal credit lines as high interest rates and compliance checks deter corporate borrowing.

The lending retreat is driven largely by institutional hurdles at the counter. Over a quarter of the surveyed executives—26.7 percent—classified loan access as difficult or very difficult, pointing to strict collateral coverage requirements and cumbersome underwriting intervals as primary roadblocks to securing capital.

### Bottlenecks at the Credit Counter

![Central Bank of Kenya Headquarters along Haile Selassie Avenue, Nairobi CBD](https://i.ibb.co/F4sF3hMG/6e97c1817366.webp)

The financing gap is falling heavily on small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), where thinner balance sheets make conventional bank collateral benchmarks harder to meet. The executives asked the monetary authority and commercial lenders to streamline loan documentation, introduce flexible amortization schedules, and pull down benchmark lending charges.

Without broader access to external capital, companies report limited capacity to expand production lines or cushion their balance sheets against external shocks, including regional geopolitical friction that continues to inflate import costs.

### Dual Licensing and Production Overheads

Beyond access to credit, the survey details cumulative administrative and operational friction inside Kenya's borders. Chief executives pointed to duplicate compliance costs generated by parallel national and county regulatory frameworks, proposing an immediate merger of national permits with county-level business licenses.

Import delays and fragmented inter-agency oversight across transport nodes were cited as secondary friction points driving up operating expenditures. Business leaders urged the state to coordinate trade documentation and reduce clearance turnaround times at entry points.

![President William Ruto hosting a meeting with stakeholders in the manufacturing industry on March 12, 2024](https://i.ibb.co/fY8cvfHY/a3c4afce4b4a.webp)

Factory floors and logistics corridors face parallel utility strains. The executives asked the government to prioritize lower energy tariffs and repair instability across the power grid, noting that power disruptions and costly fuel continue to inflate unit production expenses above regional competitors.

### Demands for Policy Predictability

The survey reflects corporate unease with frequent shifts in public revenue strategies. The 600 executives urged fiscal planners to commit to predictable tax horizons, establish structured consultation pipelines before introducing new revenue statutes, and cut overall levy rates to revive enterprise competitiveness.

In tandem with fiscal discipline, the surveyed executives tied operating stability directly to governance standards, calling for sustained anti-corruption enforcement, tighter controls on non-priority public spending, and development frameworks insulated from five-year electoral cycles.

With commercial credit channels constrained for roughly three-quarters of surveyed enterprises, the private sector's immediate expansion plans now hinge on whether forthcoming monetary and fiscal decisions translate into cheaper institutional credit and consolidated regulatory oversight.
