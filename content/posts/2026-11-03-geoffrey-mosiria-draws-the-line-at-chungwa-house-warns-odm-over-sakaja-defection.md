Geoffrey Mosiria has stepped directly into Nairobi's shifting political crosscurrents, firing a sharp warning shot at the Orange Democratic Movement over its willingness to roll out the red carpet for Governor Johnson Sakaja.

Sakaja officially traded his United Democratic Alliance colors for ODM on Monday, walking into Chungwa House where senior party figures—including Deputy Party Leader Simba Arati and National Chairperson Gladys Wanga—welcomed him into the fold. To justify the high-profile switch, the Nairobi governor pointed straight to the top, claiming the late former Prime Minister Raila Odinga had personally advised him to anchor himself in the Orange camp to keep the capital politically stable.

That claim, however, has run straight into a fierce internal brick wall.

Stepping forward as an unabashed defender of the late opposition chief's ideological footprint, Mosiria publicly questioned whether the party's current executive organ is sacrificing its foundational soul for short-term electoral convenience as the 2027 General Election looms.

“Baba believed in good leadership, integrity, accountability, and ethical governance,” Mosiria stated in a blistering reaction to the defection. “Admitting individuals with questionable reputations and integrity into ODM is an insult to Baba’s legacy and everything he stood for. His legacy deserves to be protected, respected, and upheld.”

Rather than framing the governor's arrival as a straightforward numbers game for the upcoming ballot, Mosiria's critique zeroes in on the moral cost of welcoming figures carrying heavy administrative baggage. By dragging the debate back to core principles of integrity and accountability, the former county official challenged the party leadership to decide whether it intends to police its own standards or simply open its gates to any incumbent looking for a safer ticket to defend their seat.

“To the ODM leadership, I appeal to you: Please allow Baba to rest in peace by preserving the values he fought for. Do not betray his legacy for the sake of political expediency,” Mosiria warned, pushing the party to clarify its identity post-Raila.

With Sakaja securing an Orange ticket maneuver to protect his political future in Nairobi against an increasingly crowded field of challengers, the immediate fallout leaves ODM's executive hierarchy balancing the pragmatic gains of an incumbent governor against sharp internal reminders of what the party's founders left behind.
