Nairobi’s unseasonable daytime heat is projected to persist through the final quarter of 2026 and well into early 2027, upending expectations that the arrival of an El Niño cycle would deliver an immediate, cooling reprieve to the capital.

Seasonal climate projections indicate that Nairobi’s temperatures will sit between 0.5°C and 1.5°C above historical averages over the coming months. According to assessments by weather expert Maithya wa Vilivū, the median increase is tracking at roughly 1.1°C, keeping daytime highs across the city between 28°C and 30°C, with several pockets across the metropolitan area forecast to cross the 30°C threshold.

While the October-November-December rainy window is formally underway, the anticipated cooling has been blunted by complex atmospheric and marine interactions over the Indian Ocean.

### Oceanic Interference Defers Wet Weather

![An undated aerial image of City Hall Way with City Hall and Holy Family Basilica.](https://i.ibb.co/0yxKFWNt/149c70f29e33.webp)

The assumption that El Niño automatically produces continuous downpours and low temperatures overlooks competing tropical dynamics that dictate moisture distribution. Vilivū pointed out that current weather patterns are heavily mediated by several intersecting phenomena, including the Indian Ocean Dipole, the Madden-Julian Oscillation, Kelvin waves, and localized tropical storm systems across the Indian Ocean basin.

These systems dictate the movement of moisture corridors. When they pull convective activity away from the East African landmass or scatter cloud cover, solar radiation heats the surface uninterrupted, driving up daytime mercury levels even during a declared wet season.

The Kenya Meteorological Service Authority (KMSA) echoed this assessment, noting that delays in rainfall onset in specific localities do not signal an alteration to the broader seasonal forecast. KMSA specialists clarified that the standalone intensity of an El Niño event does not dictate the exact volume or day-to-day cadence of precipitation in any given area. In its outlook for the final quarter, KMSA projected above-average rainfall across wide swaths of the country alongside higher-than-average temperatures—a dual-track scenario where heavy showers and acute heat coexist.

### Broad Continental and Regional Disparities

The IGAD Climate Prediction and Applications Centre (ICPAC) similarly modeled enhanced rainfall across much of the Greater Horn of Africa for the October–December window, while cautioning that both the timing and physical distribution of the rains will remain uneven.

In the interim, the heat has registered acutely across Kenya’s arid and semi-arid zones. For the week running October 20 to 26, the Kenya Met flagged daytime maximums surpassing 35°C in portions of Turkana, Samburu, Marsabit, Isiolo, Tana River, Kilifi, Garissa, and Wajir. Projected highs place Lodwar at 38°C, Mandera at 37°C, Wajir at 36°C, and Garissa at 35°C.

Nairobi’s forecast for the same weekly window mirrors this broader thermal loading, with projected daytime highs of 29°C against nighttime minimums dropping to 12°C.

### Global Thermal Pressures

Kenya’s prolonged sunshine and delayed cooling align with a wider escalation in worldwide baseline temperatures. Data from the Reuters Climate Monitor’s Heat Storm Tracker indicated that extreme heat conditions were forecast to cover 32.1 per cent of the planet’s surface, placing roughly 1.7 billion people—representing 21 per cent of the global population—under heat stress.

Historical metrics tracked by the monitor show that over the past five years, heat storms and sustained waves have encompassed an average of 18.1 per cent of the Earth’s surface daily, compared to a baseline of just 2.1 per cent recorded between 1961 and 1990.

For residents in the capital, the convergence of global baseline warming and local oceanic dynamics means the onset of seasonal showers will not signify a rapid return to cool conditions. With the median 1.1°C thermal elevation expected to hold into the early months of 2027, Nairobi will continue navigating hot, high-radiation afternoons punctuated by sporadic and localized rainfall events.
