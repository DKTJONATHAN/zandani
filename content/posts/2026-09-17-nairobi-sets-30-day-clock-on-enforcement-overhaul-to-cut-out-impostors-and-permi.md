Nairobi City County has placed its compliance machinery on a 30-day notice to curb field harassment and permit extortion, committing to issue verifiable credentials to all enforcement officers while demanding an immediate halt to third-party brokerage in business licensing.

The push, unveiled Thursday during an engagement between county executives and the Kenya National Chamber of Commerce and Industry (KNCCI), targets the informal network of brokers and predatory impostors who have long inserted themselves between City Hall and enterprise owners.

Addressing traders at the Nairobi Business Breakfast Forum, Governor Johnson Sakaja laid out the enforcement timeline, noting that rampant impersonation of county personnel requires structural safeguards rather than routine administrative warnings. Under the planned system, every field inspector and compliance officer deployed across the city must carry verifiable identification that traders can authenticate on-site before permitting inspections.

"We are putting in place a verifiable identification system for county compliance and enforcement officers within one month to eliminate impostors and enhance accountability," Sakaja said.

The credentialing drive runs parallel to a full operational pivot toward self-service licensing. City Hall is directing all commerce operators to bypass informal fixers and process their permits directly through its centralized portal, Pay Nairobi. Under the system, businesses submit their applications, clear statutory payments electronically, and generate their permits without physical visits to county offices or reliance on intermediaries.

"You do not need a middleman to obtain your county licence," Sakaja stated. "Apply directly through our website, pay via Pay Nairobi and print your licence. That alone is enough."

For street-level enterprises, the measures touch on an entrenched operational headache. Business owners across commercial hubs from the central business district to industrial nodes have persistently reported dual exposure: navigating rent-seeking brokers who inflate statutory charges, followed by inspections from unvetted individuals brandishing ambiguous municipal authority. In many retail corridors, distinguishing legitimate revenue personnel from organized impostors has historically proved nearly impossible without formal intervention.

The private sector assembly convened by KNCCI provided a platform for operators to air these administrative friction points directly to county leadership. Business lobbies have consistently cited arbitrary compliance raids and opaque licensing channels as major barriers to formalization and capital investment in the capital.

By tethering legitimate licensing strictly to digital printouts and giving field inspection a verifiable audit trail within four weeks, City Hall is seeking to shut down the physical touchpoints where extortion thrives. The effectiveness of the initiative will now rest on how rapidly county inspectorate units are cataloged and whether the public authentication mechanism proves tamper-proof once deployed across Nairobi's busy retail streets.
