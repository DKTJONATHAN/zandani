Candidates sitting the November 2026 Advocates Training Programme (ATP) examinations have been granted a final seven-day registration reprieve, an administrative adjustment arriving just as judicial challenges bring incoming admissions to a standstill.

The Council of Legal Education (CLE) announced on Tuesday, October 20, that registration for the upcoming examination series will now close on Tuesday, October 27, 2026. The regulator explicitly cautioned that the revised cutoff is definitive, warning prospective examinees that no additional extensions will be granted once the portal closes.

Alongside the extended registration cutoff, the council introduced an administrative exit valve for trainees unable to sit the November series. Candidates can formally request a deferral through their individual student portals until the October 27 cutoff, provided they submit verifiable supporting documentation.

### The Two-Year Deferral Tether

The relief granted to deferring students carries strict operational limits. Under the rules outlined by the regulator, examinees granted postponement must clear their deferred units within two calendar years—a window spanning four consecutive ATP examination cycles.

Candidates who postpone will not transition automatically into subsequent exam rosters. When ready to sit, they must actively re-register for the specific deferred units during the relevant series. Inquiries regarding portal validation require candidates to include their formal registration numbers in subject headings when contacting the regulator’s support desk.

For an earlier cohort, the credentialing process moves to its closing formalities. CLE confirmed that candidates gazetted in October 2026 must collect their official Certificates of Compliance and final academic transcripts from its offices between October 26 and October 28. To secure the documents, graduates must appear in person unless they provide formal written authorization designating an accredited representative.

### Pipeline Friction at Karen

While current ATP candidates navigate the tight October 27 deadline, the upstream pipeline that feeds the exam room remains paralyzed by litigation over who qualifies to study law.

The Kenya School of Law (KSL) halted application processing for its 2027/2028 academic intake on October 15. That suspension complied with interim orders issued by the High Court on October 6, which barred both KSL and CLE from administering student admissions under contested entry criteria.

With admissions stalled pending further directions from the High Court, the legal sector faces an unusual structural divergence: an orderly, time-limited sprint to register and defer candidates already inside the system, contrasted against an indefinite legal blockade on the next generation of law graduates seeking entry.
