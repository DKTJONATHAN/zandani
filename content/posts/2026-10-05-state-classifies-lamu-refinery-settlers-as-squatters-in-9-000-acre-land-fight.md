The Ministry of Lands has drawn a firm legal boundary between long-term occupancy and private land rights in Lamu, asserting that the vast majority of the 9,000-acre tract designated for the Dangote East Africa Refinery remains unalienated public property.

Addressing a simmering legal battle with coastal communities, Lands Cabinet Secretary Alice Wahome stated that the physical presence of residential settlements does not confer private title over land marked for strategic national infrastructure.

### The 9,000-Acre Footprint

The dispute centers on two major contiguous blocks in Lamu County: a primary 7,000-acre site earmarked directly for the industrial refinery complex, and an adjoining parcel exceeding 2,000 acres also logged in state registries as government land.

![Signs held by Lamu residents during protests against the proposed Dangote refinery, September 29, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/5h53hxpG/41b1da01a117.webp)

Wahome acknowledged that establishing legal clarity on the ground is complicated by generations of informal occupation. In several affected sections, families have inhabited and farmed the parcels for 50 to 60 years, passing land down across generations without formal deeds or statutory registration.

“The government was dealing with different categories of people in the area, including genuine squatters who may have occupied particular pieces of land for decades and may have no other place to go,” Wahome explained, maintaining that long-term occupation would be handled through administrative vetting rather than conceded as automatic private ownership.

### Court Action and Ancestral Claims

The ministry’s public-land stance directly collides with proceedings filed in court by Lamu residents, who argue that the earmarked zones constitute communal and ancestral heritage. The petitioners have sought judicial intervention over displacement risks and demanded clear compensation frameworks before any construction commences.

Wahome pushed back against the litigation, claiming state intelligence and land records show that certain litigants challenging the development were “planted by enemies of development” to stall the enterprise. She insisted, however, that verified historical occupants would not be disenfranchised.

![An undated Image of the Dangote Refinery with an insert of Land Cabinet Secretary Alice Wahome](https://i.ibb.co/0jfd3KT3/cc5ac83731f5.webp)

According to the CS, the ministry maintains historical baseline data identifying original occupants and legitimate claimants across the Lamu corridor to guide any impending compensation or relocation packages.

### Multi-Agency Machinery and High Stakes

Because of the scale of potential displacement, the state has activated a multi-agency framework combining land administrators, physical planners, and security organs to coordinate resettlement logistics.

The stakes surrounding the site are immense. The Ksh 2.2 trillion Dangote East Africa Refinery, officially launched on September 30, represents one of the largest single industrial investments in the region. National economic planners project that the facility will generate up to 60,000 direct and indirect jobs once operational, positioning Lamu as a central refining and petrochemical hub for East Africa.

Formal legal proceedings challenging the project have been placed under the direct management of the Office of the Attorney General, which is preparing the state's defense against ancestral title claims while inter-agency teams proceed with occupant screening on the ground.
