East African Community citizens, including Kenyans, have been granted a full waiver from Tanzania’s newly operationalised inbound travel insurance mandate, insulating regional travellers from a $44 (Ksh5,700) entry levy imposed on international arrivals.

![Tanzania President Samia Suluhu Hassan](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/samia-suluhu_2.jpg.webp?itok=F8cXNv-J)

The framework, formalised under the Insurance (Inbound Travel Insurance) Regulations, 2026, requires foreign travellers entering mainland Tanzania via land, air, or maritime border posts to secure a state-backed policy before admission. However, the legal notice specifically carves out exemptions for citizens of both the EAC and the Southern African Development Community (SADC) trading blocs.

For non-exempt foreign nationals, the entry requirement introduces a mandatory compliance check at border controls. Travellers must obtain the cover prior to departure or at the point of entry, with immigration officers empowered to deny admission to visitors who fail to produce a valid certificate.

### Scope and Underwriting Terms

The inbound policy carries a validity term of up to 92 days from the date of initial entry, accommodating multiple trips into mainland Tanzania within that window. Visitors intending to remain in the country past the three-month threshold must renew the cover.

State-owned National Insurance Corporation (NIC) has been designated as the primary underwriter, operating alongside certified private insurance partners. The statutory package provides standard emergency protections, covering:

- Emergency medical treatment during the stay
- Medical evacuation and emergency repatriation
- Compensation for lost or mishandled luggage

The mainland rollout expands a model first tested in October 2024, when the semi-autonomous archipelago of Zanzibar made local inbound insurance mandatory for international tourists arriving on the island.

### Diverging Paths Across the Border

Tanzania’s implementation highlights a sharp regulatory contrast with neighbouring Kenya, which attempted a broader inbound health insurance mandate earlier this year before running into legal barriers.

In July 2026, Kenya gazetted regulations requiring non-Kenyan visitors staying under 12 months to hold inbound health cover with a minimum cumulative benefit limit of Ksh6.48 million ($50,000). The Kenyan blueprint included specific caps: Ksh2.59 million ($20,000) for hospital medical expenses, Ksh3.24 million ($25,000) for medical evacuation, Ksh129,620 ($1,000) for mental health care, and Ksh38,886 ($300) for prescription drugs.

That rollout was brought to an abrupt halt in August 2026 when the High Court intervened. Justice Francis Rayola Olel issued interim conservatory orders stopping the implementation of the Kenyan framework following a constitutional challenge filed by two petitioners.

While Kenya's inbound health insurance programme remains tied up in litigation pending a full bench hearing, Tanzania's mainland entry checks are now fully operational, leaving international tourism operators adjusting to dual-tier compliance rules across the regional corridor.
