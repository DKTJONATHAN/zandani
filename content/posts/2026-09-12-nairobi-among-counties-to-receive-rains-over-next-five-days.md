Nairobi and vast stretches of the country will plunge into a five-day spell of moderate to heavy rainfall alongside biting night temperatures starting Sunday, September 13, weather authorities warned today.

### What we know
* **Duration:** The wet spell runs from Sunday, September 13 through Thursday, September 17, 2026.
* **Regions affected:** Nairobi, Central Kenya, Western, the Lake Victoria Basin, the Rift Valley, Northern Kenya, and the Coastal strip.
* **Precipitation patterns:** Coastal areas will endure recurring morning showers, while interior counties face afternoon downpours.
* **Cold snap:** Night temperatures will drop drastically, bottoming out at 6°C in Nyandarua and Samburu.

According to a five-day advisory issued by the Kenya Meteorological Service Authority (KMSA), the pattern will be characterized by morning precipitation along the Indian Ocean coastline and widespread afternoon storms across the highlands, western valleys, and the capital.

In the coastal belt, Mombasa will experience light morning showers daily through Thursday, with skies clearing for sunny afternoons on all days except Tuesday. Neighbouring Kwale, Kilifi, Lamu, and Taita Taveta are scheduled to experience morning precipitation on Sunday, Tuesday, Wednesday, and Thursday.

Across the capital and the central highlands, rainfall will persist from Sunday through Wednesday. The forecast maps light to moderate afternoon showers over Nairobi, Kiambu, Murang’a, Nyeri, Kirinyaga, Nyandarua, Meru, and Tharaka Nithi.

Western Kenya and the Lake Victoria basin-spanning Kisumu, Kakamega, Bungoma, Vihiga, Busia, Siaya, Homa Bay, and Migori-will record afternoon showers across Sunday, Monday, and Tuesday. In the Rift Valley, Uasin Gishu, Nandi, and Elgeyo Marakwet will see moderate downpours on Monday, flanked by light showers on Sunday, Wednesday, and Thursday. West Pokot will register intermittent showers on Sunday, Wednesday, and Thursday afternoons.

Even typically arid northern counties will receive rainfall. Garissa, Mandera, and Marsabit will see light showers on Sunday, Tuesday, Wednesday, and Thursday, while Wajir will register rain on Sunday and Tuesday afternoons.

Alongside the precipitation, KMSA flagged an acute cold front set to hit the central and eastern highlands. Nyandarua will register the coldest conditions in the country, with nighttime temperatures sinking to 6°C on Monday, Tuesday, and Thursday. Samburu will match that 6°C low on Sunday night before climbing to 10°C on Monday. Nairobi will record minimum temperatures between 12°C and 15°C, Kiambu and Murang'a will hover between 11°C and 13°C, while Machakos and Embu will drop to lows of 10°C.

"Residents in low-lying zones, urban centres with poor drainage, and frost-prone agricultural pockets must prepare for both waterlogged surfaces and sharp drops in nocturnal temperatures," KMSA noted in its advisory.

The five-day forecast presents an immediate operational test for urban municipal authorities-most notably in Nairobi and Mombasa-where routine downpours consistently overwhelm clogged storm drains, stall arterial traffic, and displace informal settlements. If county disaster teams fail to clear blocked waterways before Sunday afternoon, predictable seasonal rain will once again be treated as an unforeseen emergency.
