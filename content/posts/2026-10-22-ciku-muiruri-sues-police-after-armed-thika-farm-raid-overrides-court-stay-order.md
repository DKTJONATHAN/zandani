Former radio broadcaster Ciku Muiruri has filed suit against the National Police Service, challenging law enforcement's refusal to enforce a subsisting court stay order after an armed gang seized control of her family's farm in Thika.

The lawsuit follows a violent takeover last week in which roughly 100 armed individuals breached the property, forced entry into the main residence, and established an ongoing occupation that local police have yet to disperse.

According to Muiruri, the raid resulted in physical violence and the confiscation of private assets. The attackers broke down the house door and forced her to surrender cash alongside personal electronics, including a laptop and mobile phone.

### The Attack at the Farmstead

![media-journalists-camera](https://i.ibb.co/99RkSLJt/dd527d34a17f.webp)

The confrontation turned physical when Muiruri returned to the homestead accompanied by a police officer to recover vehicle keys left behind during the initial breach.

"After walking out, this guy grabs a crowbar, and he hits me on the head. I've been hit in my eye; I've been hit on my skull," Muiruri stated in a public account detailing the incident, displaying facial injuries sustained in the assault.

Despite recording an official police statement and formally identifying the individual who struck her, authorities have effected no arrests. The armed group remains on the property, where Muiruri’s father and grandmother are buried.

### Conflicting Rulings and Statutory Inaction

The standoff stems from a protracted title dispute pitting Muiruri’s family against a corporate entity laying claim to the adjacent parcel and seeking ownership of the ancestral holding.

While litigation was initiated at the business tribunal court, proceedings have been marred by contradictory directives from different magistrates, creating legal confusion over the parcel’s custody. However, Muiruri holds an active court order granting an absolute stay against any eviction notices pursued by the rival claimant.

![odee-francis.jpg](https://i.ibb.co/fYWk8xzw/331e62776e98.webp)

Under statutory procedures, police officers are obligated to maintain public order and uphold explicit court injunctions preventing forced evictions. In her petition against the police service, Muiruri contends that officers on the ground actively abdicated their constitutional duty by standing down during an illegal forced entry, leaving private citizens exposed to armed violence while valid conservatory orders remained in effect.

### Mounting Legal Pressures

The legal filing places direct scrutiny on local precinct commanders in Kiambu County regarding how private land evictions are policed when commercial interests clash with registered family occupants.

While media industry associations—including the Kenya Editors Guild—have expressed mounting concern over physical threats facing current and former journalists ahead of the 2027 electoral cycle, Muiruri's litigation isolates a distinct administrative breakdown: the routine failure of the security apparatus to honor judicial stay orders during contentious real estate seizures.

The petition now moves before the courts to determine whether police inaction during the Thika raid constitutes a contempt of judicial directives and a compensable breach of constitutional rights.
