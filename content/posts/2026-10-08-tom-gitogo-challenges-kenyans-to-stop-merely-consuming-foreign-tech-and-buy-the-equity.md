Tom Gitogo does not want Kenyans to spend another decade simply footing the bill for Silicon Valley’s balance sheets.

The Britam Group Managing Director and CEO has laid down a direct challenge to the continent’s digital habits, arguing that while millions of Africans power the global knowledge economy every day as end-users, they remain entirely locked out of the financial windfall created by those platforms.

“Every day, Africans participate in the global economy as consumers, businesses and users of technology created around the world,” Gitogo said. “We believe they should also have the opportunity to become investors in the companies driving that economy, own a stake in their growth and share in the value they create.”

That critique anchors Britam’s latest expansion outside local borders. Following formal clearance from the Capital Markets Authority (CMA) in August, the insurer’s asset management wing is rolling out two tailored offshore portfolios: the Britam Enhanced Global Equities Special Fund and the Britam Multi Asset Special Fund.

### The Shift to Offshore Tech and Commodities

The move marks a deliberate pivot away from traditional local securities, offering retail and institutional players direct exposure to the foreign sectors driving international markets. 

Under the Britam Enhanced Global Equities Special Fund, the mandate concentrates explicitly on the frontiers of global industrial transformation. Portfolio managers will deploy capital directly into artificial intelligence, digital economy ecosystems, life sciences, healthcare, and transition energy.

To balance that exposure for savers wanting multi-market cushions, the parallel Britam Multi Asset Special Fund combines multiple asset classes under a single roof. Instead of confining portfolios to Nairobi-based paper, the vehicle blends local and international equities, fixed income securities, and physical commodities into a single structure.

Britam Asset Managers Chief Executive Officer Barack Obatsa explained that the product design reflects a maturing appetite among Kenyan wealth-builders who have outgrown basic savings vehicles.

“These funds are the next step in widening the choices available to investors as their needs and ambitions evolve,” Obatsa said. “We have built our offering from accessible local investments to a wider range of opportunities, and these funds extend that choice across global markets and asset classes, backed by the research, expertise and risk management of a professional investment manager.”

### Chasing Kenya's Ksh 948 Billion Pool

The offshore push comes amid an aggressive liquidity rush into managed collective investment funds across Nairobi.

Official data from the CMA shows that collective investment scheme assets in Kenya touched Ksh 948.7 billion in June, marking an explosive 59.1 percent jump compared to the previous year. As middle-class savers search for higher yields and hedge options against domestic inflationary pressures, asset managers are competing fiercely to absorb those inflows.

Britam Asset Managers currently controls a portfolio exceeding Ksh 300 billion, cementing its status among the country’s top five wealth houses. The firm maintains a commanding grip on local paper, leading the industry's Fixed Income bracket with over Ksh 55 billion in assets as of July.

By packaging global technology giants and commodity plays into regulated domestic funds, Gitogo and Obatsa are betting that the next wave of local financial ambition will not settle for domestic yield alone.
