A legal ultimatum fired by Nairobi Governor Johnson Sakaja against Embakasi East MP Babu Owino has laid bare a deeper institutional gridlock at City Hall, exposing how municipal authorities flagged illegal works at the fatal Eastleigh site months before it crumbled without securing prosecutorial backing.

In pushing back against social media claims linking him to the development, Sakaja released internal records indicating that City Hall had formally documented illegal construction on LR/Parcel No. 36/11/40 as far back as July 16, 2026. The police notifications identified named developers and cited direct breaches of physical planning bylaws along Yusuf Haji Avenue (9th Street). 

Yet the county's own paper trail revealed that enforcement had stalled long before the multi-storey residential tower gave way on October 23. Correspondence released alongside the governor's legal rejoinder showed Sakaja had appealed to the Director of Public Prosecutions (ODPP) two months prior to the July breach, protesting a complete standstill in prosecuting county law violations that had persisted since 2024.

### The Prosecutorial Bottleneck

![Aftermath of the Eastleigh building collapse in Nairobi on October 23, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/collapsed-building-eastleigh-nairobi-october-2026.jpg.webp?itok=r1ziJ8S4)

According to the county documents, City Hall sought urgent intervention from the DPP to unfreeze stalled planning enforcement across the capital. Sakaja had petitioned the state prosecutor to gazette designated special municipal prosecutors, revise stagnant charging frameworks, and formally approve six nominated county enforcement officers.

The absence of those gazetted powers left City Hall relying on standard police reports without an expedited court mechanism to padlock active, non-compliant sites—including the specific parcel in Kamkunji Sub-county that ultimately collapsed on Friday.

The administrative disclosure formed the backbone of a six-hour legal notice served on Babu Owino on Sunday evening. The Embakasi East legislator had published posts across X and Facebook at 1:27 pm, alleging that Nairobi County records demonstrated valid approval for the multi-storey development. Owino further claimed that Ksh120 million had been paid in bribes to facilitate the clearance, accusing the county chief of misleading the public by claiming the works were unauthorized.

Sakaja’s legal counsel rejected the narrative entirely, demanding an unconditional retraction and the immediate deletion of the posts.

"Take notice therefore that we have hereby been instructed to demand that you pull down the said social media post with immediate effect due to the apparent error in the information published on your social media platforms and the already accruing defamatory effect that it is having on his professional reputation, public image and personal morale," the demand letter read.

The governor’s advocates also noted that the documentation cited by the MP erroneously attributed planning approval to Sakaja in his personal professional standing as a registered physical planner, warning of immediate civil litigation with costs should the six-hour deadline lapse without compliance.

### The Cost at Yusuf Haji Avenue

While the political and legal confrontation escalated on Sunday, the human toll of the regulatory failure hardened at the disaster site.

The National Police Service confirmed that emergency crews had pulled four survivors alive from the rubble, all of whom remained hospitalized on Sunday. The multi-agency rescue effort, coordinated on the ground by the Kenya Red Cross, continued to sift through pulverized concrete and twisted metal along 9th Street.

Official tracking metrics disclosed by Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development Principal Secretary Susan Man'geni indicated that 47 individuals were formally reported missing in the immediate aftermath of the structural failure. While Red Cross tracing teams successfully tracked nine of those individuals and reunited them with relatives away from the scene, 38 people remained unaccounted for.

With tracing desks active on-site and the Red Cross toll-free line 1199 managing distressed inquiries, the collapse has ceased to be solely an engineering disaster, shifting into a documented case study of how enforcement gaps between City Hall and the national prosecutorial machinery leave lethal urban hazards untouched until they fall.
