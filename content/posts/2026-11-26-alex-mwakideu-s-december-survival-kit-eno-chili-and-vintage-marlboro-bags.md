If you are planning to pace your stomach during the upcoming holiday season, radio personality Alex Mwakideu suggests you throw caution to the wind—and pack some Eno.

Speaking on Thursday, November 26, 2026, as the calendar turned toward the final stretch of the year, Mwakideu offered a pragmatic, if slightly chaotic, blueprint for surviving December feasts. Rather than preaching the usual gospel of austere January budgeting, he argued that after navigating a demanding 2026, holiday revellers owed it to themselves to enjoy heavy meals without reservation.

“Kesi baadaye. Kile kitu utafanya tu, anza tu kutembea tu na madawa ya tumbo. Eh, Eno na kadhalika. Hizo ni vitu za Desemba zisikosekane,” Mwakideu remarked, urging people to embrace roasted goat meat and maize while keeping relief close at hand.

His festive inventory did not stop at over-the-counter antacids. For serious food enthusiasts, the media personality added a few specialized provisions to the packing list: portable chili for spice lovers and an ample supply of toothpicks for the aftermath of heavy celebrations. Alongside his co-host, the conversation also wandered into the logistics of leftover storage, joking about the necessity of packing containers for party guests who prefer to extend the feast at home.

The lighthearted banter quickly shifted into local nostalgia as the pair recalled the heavy-duty utility of discontinued packaging. Turning back the clock to the era before the strict plastic bag bans, Mwakideu and his co-host reminisced about the legendary Marlboro-branded polythene bags. Described as virtually indestructible, the vintage carriers were celebrated for their unusual capacity to lug heavy loads, including charcoal, without tearing.

While the holiday season invariably brings a familiar chorus of warnings about January financial crunches, Mwakideu dismissed the idea that festive spending automatically ruins the opening weeks of the new year. He maintained that households would figure out their budgets when January arrived, leaving no room for anticipatory anxiety during the remaining days of 2026.
