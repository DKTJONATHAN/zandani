According to the World Meteorological Organisation (WMO), vulnerable communities across flood-prone corridors stand to receive coordinated multi-agency security and evacuation backing as an intensifying El Niño tracks toward record-breaking levels by December 2026.

The global weather agency projected on Thursday, October 8, that the ongoing phenomenon is accelerating rapidly and could rank among the strongest weather disruptions recorded worldwide since 1950. Forecasters noted a near-100 percent probability that the system will persist through February 2027, prolonging abnormal weather disruptions across East Africa and shifting typical rainfall boundaries for months.

“El Niño is firmly established and strengthening rapidly. WMO forecasts indicate a near-100 per cent likelihood that it will persist through February 2027, potentially being one of the strongest since at least 1950,” said WMO Secretary-General Celeste Saulo in the agency’s latest assessment.

### Multi-Agency Contingency Deployment

![Motorists using a flooded highway during cold and rainy weather in Nairobi](https://i.ibb.co/TxL5BFpM/a97feadd117b.webp)

The alarming global outlook has prompted the Ministry of Interior to operationalize emergency protocols under the National Contingency Plan, aiming to insulate settlements in low-lying and riparian zones from catastrophic displacement. Rather than treating the forecast as an isolated meteorological event, the state has directed administrative and security apparatuses to establish preemptive logistical corridors before the weather pattern reaches its zenith.

Under the framework, county administrators and disaster management coordinators are finalizing evacuation blueprints and identifying priority access routes. Ground-level search and rescue capabilities are being concentrated across specialized formations, bringing together the Kenya Coast Guard Service (KCGS), the Kenya Defence Forces (KDF), the National Police Service (NPS), the National Youth Service (NYS), and the Kenya Red Cross Society.

The coordinated military and civilian response is designed to provide immediate evacuation transit, logistical airlifts, and emergency shelter should waterways overflow and cut off rural transit networks.

### Uneven Onset Confounding Early Projections

![Floods along Waiyaki Way during rains experienced on June 1, 2024.](https://i.ibb.co/sp6YbND1/c13ab9103d88.webp)

The urgent mobilization comes against the backdrop of an irregular start to the short rains season. Earlier scientific projections suggested that intensified downpours would manifest noticeably by late September and early October. However, ground conditions across Kenya have remained highly uneven.

While scattered areas have seen early precipitation, vast swathes of the country have recorded subdued rainfall or sustained dry conditions, creating confusion among agricultural producers and local communities. The WMO noted that global El Niño patterns naturally distort standard weather cycles, driving severe deluge and waterlogging in specific geographies while simultaneously triggering elevated heat and drought in others.

With global atmospheric temperatures being pushed above historical baselines, emergency planners are treating the current dry pockets not as a reprieve, but as a narrow window to fortify drainage structures and relocate at-risk populations before the projected December surge takes hold.

Contingency teams remain on high alert as security agencies continue staging transport and emergency medical assets, with surveillance focused on how localized regional rainfall indices evolve ahead of the forecasted peak.
