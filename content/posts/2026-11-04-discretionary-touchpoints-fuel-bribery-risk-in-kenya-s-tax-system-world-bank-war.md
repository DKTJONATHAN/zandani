Each layer of statutory levies added to Kenya's business tax structure expands the human touchpoints where government officials exercise discretion, converting tax compliance into an arena for bribery demands.

According to the World Bank's November 2026 *Kenya Economic Update*, the accumulation of specialized charges—ranging from the Affordable Housing Levy to the Railway Development Levy and Import Declaration Fee—has multiplied administrative friction points faster than it has raised revenue.

The lender's assessment comes as total tax collections remain stuck at 12.8 per cent of gross domestic product, falling short of the government’s 13.3 per cent target.

![Kenya Revenue Authority building at Times Towers](https://i.ibb.co/7xMyFL1B/01cdfe84a5a2.webp)

Rather than simplifying fiscal administration, the proliferation of charges—which also include increased road and petroleum levies—forces private enterprises through multiple payment portals and official sign-offs. "The pile-up of charges raises the risk of bribe demands," the World Bank stated, pointing directly to the corruption risks tied to administrative processes involved in paying government fees.

While individual levies add compliance burdens to commercial operators, they have done little to bolster the state's main revenue engines. Value Added Tax (VAT) and income tax continue to carry the bulk of the national budget, accounting for 79.4 per cent of total tax revenue.

The vulnerability of this concentrated revenue base was made visible by executive tax interventions earlier in the year. The World Bank noted that the April 2026 decision to lower VAT on petroleum products from 16 per cent to 8 per cent drove VAT performance down, leaving total VAT collections 6.4 per cent below target.

![President William Ruto during a meeting with World Bank Group President Ajay Banga on the sidelines of the G7 Summit in Évian, France, on June 16, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/h5zFRQj/7230ed042da1.webp)

The resulting fiscal pressure places the Treasury in a structural bind: attempts to plug baseline revenue shortfalls through additional boutique levies increase compliance costs for businesses without replacing the receipts lost from broad-based tax adjustments.

To dismantle the environment that enables corruption, the World Bank recommended stripping individual officials of administrative discretion. The lender called for end-to-end digital systems to handle tax assessments automatically, eliminating face-to-face negotiations during routine clearance procedures.

The multilateral institution also urged the government to establish public tracking mechanisms for tax refunds and official audits, alongside structured escalation channels for resolving tax disputes when administrative impasses occur.

Improving national revenue performance, the report emphasized, depends less on creating new collection instruments and more on establishing administrative transparency that limits officer intervention during business compliance checks.
