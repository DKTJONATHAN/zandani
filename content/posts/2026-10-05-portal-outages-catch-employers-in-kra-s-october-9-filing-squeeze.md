Kenyan employers are facing a compliance high-wire act as the Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA) demands early submission of monthly payroll taxes while its own digital filing portal continues to stall user submissions.

In an advisory issued on Monday, October 5, the tax collector instructed businesses to file and remit their Pay As You Earn (PAYE) obligations well ahead of the statutory Friday, October 9 deadline. The warning aims to prevent a last-minute system rush. However, the directive comes as employers report persistent technical errors when attempting to lodge returns for August and current payroll cycles.

While KRA’s automated systems are designed to trigger immediate interest and financial penalties for late returns, the agency’s underlying digital infrastructure has been unable to process routine filings smoothly for affected users.

### Digital Friction and Statutory Fines

Under Kenyan tax law, employers act as statutory withholding agents, deducting PAYE directly from staff salaries and remitting the funds to the revenue authority by the ninth day of every month. Failure to file returns or transfer the collected revenue within this timeframe triggers automatic statutory penalties alongside compounding monthly interest.

Taxpayers attempting to clear their ledgers ahead of the October 9 cutoff have consistently reported system access failures on the portal. In response to mounting complaints from businesses unable to complete their monthly declarations, KRA publicly acknowledged the ongoing system failure, offering a brief statement advising taxpayers to remain patient while technical teams work to stabilize the platform.

The timing creates operational risk for finance departments across the country. Because return generation and payment slip processing are tied directly to the online platform, portal downtime prevents compliant firms from generating the formal filing receipts required to demonstrate timely remittance.

### Amnesty Measures versus Routine Compliance

The deadline squeeze follows a separate administrative announcement made by the tax authority on Sunday, October 4. Under that policy, KRA opened an instalment payment option for taxpayers seeking relief under the national tax amnesty, allowing principal tax liabilities to be cleared in staggered amounts through December 31, 2026.

However, revenue officials clarified that the amnesty framework operates entirely independently of monthly PAYE statutory deadlines. Current payroll taxes cannot be deferred under the amnesty scheme, leaving employers fully exposed to late-filing fines if portal errors block their submissions past Friday.

With only days remaining before the October 9 deadline, KRA has yet to issue a formal extension or statutory waiver for businesses affected by the portal outage, maintaining its call for early submission while technical teams continue work on the system.
