Kenya's bid to secure a clean bill of health against Ebola moved into its second phase on Monday as health teams began final clinical screenings to release nearly 20 quarantined contacts, marking the successful completion of the maximum 21-day incubation window without community leakage.

The discharge of the isolated individuals, all linked to the initial imported case detected on October 2, signals the conclusion of primary quarantine monitoring and establishes the baseline for the second half of the World Health Organisation’s mandatory 42-day eradication countdown.

Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale confirmed the milestone during a television address on Sunday evening, noting that the containment perimeter established around the index case has held firm without generating untraced chains in the wider population.

### The 48-Hour Clinical Gauntlet

![An image of Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale with an inset of the Ebola virus](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/duale-ebola-october.jpg.webp?itok=Ix0TJaSw)

The individuals slated for release have undergone serial screening throughout their three-week confinement. Under the Ministry of Health's isolation protocol, contacts were tested every 48 hours to track potential viral manifestation, which clinically presents between two and 21 days following initial exposure.

"The people who tested positive are not from the wider community. They are those in quarantine who had contact with the first case," Duale stated. "Every 48 hours, we test them to see whether they are positive or negative... If tomorrow they are negative, almost half of them, we will do the clinical analysis, all the protocols, and they will be safe to go back to their families."

Their departure leaves the health surveillance apparatus focused entirely on passive and active population-level screening. Over the past 48 hours alone, the ministry processed 551 suspected community alerts across the country, with all samples returning negative for the virus.

### The Mechanics of the 42-Day Clock

![cs-duale.jpg](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/cs-duale_0.jpg.webp?itok=0gq4Emro)

Under World Health Organisation guidelines, an outbreak can only be officially declared resolved after 42 consecutive days pass without a single confirmed or probable secondary infection. That standard represents exactly twice the 21-day biological incubation ceiling, a buffer designed to ensure that undocumented chains of human-to-human transmission have fully burned out.

Because all transmission to date remained confined to monitored isolation units rather than open public circulation, the ministry considers October 26 as day 21 of the national countdown. If community indicators remain flat through the subsequent three weeks, Kenya will formally petition for Ebola-free status, mirroring similar containment milestones achieved across the border in Uganda following past imported spillovers.

Across the three-week response, the ministry logged nine confirmed cases and two fatalities: the initial imported index patient and a 65-year-old woman who exhibited symptoms while already under supervised institutional quarantine. The state reported achieving 100 percent contact tracing for all known interactions stemming from the primary case.

The ministry has maintained active public reporting channels, urging citizens to log suspected symptoms through the toll-free short code 719, USSD service *719#, or via direct WhatsApp messaging on 0700 719 719 as field teams preserve heightened vigilance through the final 21-day observation period.
