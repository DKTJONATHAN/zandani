High-traffic retail centers, manufacturing entities, and boarding schools across seven counties found themselves cut off from the national grid as Kenya Power rolled out an extensive daytime maintenance schedule on Thursday, September 24, 2026.

The sweeping interruptions, slated to run for up to eight and a half hours between 8:30 a.m. and 5:00 p.m., target vital commercial corridors and institutional heavyweights at the peak of their operating hours.

In Nairobi's upscale Karen and Kitisuru neighborhoods, the cut hits directly at major commercial landmarks. The outage sweeps through the Karen Hub, the local Total and Shell petrol stations, Karen Roses, and the surrounding residential and church communities along Windy Ridge and Ngong View Estate. Retail operations, fuel pumps, and office complexes in the area face a full workday without grid power.

The disruption extends far beyond retail into industrial and agricultural processing zones. In Kiambu County, the grid shutdown targets heavy manufacturing and agricultural setups, notably pulling the plug on the Carbacid Factory alongside regional coffee estates like Karakuta and Maka, as well as commercial spots spanning Kirasha, Kinale, and Soko Mjinga.

Educational institutions are similarly caught in the crosshairs of the Thursday schedule. In Kiambu, major boarding facilities including Maryhill Girls, St. Francis Mangu Girls, and St. Anne High School fall squarely within the affected zones alongside local markets like Mangu and Igegania. Upcountry in Nyeri County, the Gatarakwa Girls community and surrounding primary schools face an identical daytime blackout window.

The massive multi-county operation spans Nairobi, Kajiado, Kericho, Narok, Nyeri, Kiambu, and the Coast region, impacting localized markets from Mulot and Ololunga down to Bamburi, Utange, and Kiembeni in Mombasa. Commercial establishments and institutions reliant on steady supply must navigate the extended hours until the scheduled restoration window closes at 5:00 p.m.
