The local aviation sector is once again under scrutiny following a tragic light aircraft crash in Kipeto, Kajiado West Constituency, which claimed the lives of a student pilot and their flight instructor. The aircraft, operated by the Kenya School of Flying, went down while conducting a routine training flight in the hilly terrain near the Kipeto Wind Power Project.

Kajiado West Sub-County Police Commander Maurine Jepkemboi confirmed the incident, noting that emergency response teams and local residents arrived at the scene shortly after the crash. Despite the rapid response, both occupants had already succumbed to their injuries. Aviation safety authorities have been notified and are expected to launch a formal investigation to determine the circumstances that led to the fatal accident.

This incident marks a grim addition to a series of aviation tragedies that have shaken the country in recent months. The regularity of these crashes has raised questions regarding safety protocols within the domestic aviation industry, which remains a critical pillar for the country’s tourism and training sectors.

In August, a separate aviation disaster occurred in northern Kenya, resulting in the deaths of all seven people on board a helicopter. That aircraft, which was transporting tourists from the Loisaba Conservancy to the Ewaso Nyiro area in Samburu County, crashed near Mount Ololokwe at approximately 9:13 am.

The Kenya Civil Aviation Authority (KCAA) confirmed that an investigation into the Samburu crash remains ongoing. The victims of that tragedy included high-profile international figures, among them Ecuador’s intelligence chief, Michele Sensi-Contugi, and his wife, Stephany Hollihan. The crash also claimed the life of Jose Alberto Suarez, a senior executive with NBCUniversal and president of Telemundo 31, 49, and Fort Myers-Naples.

Samburu Police Commander David Nkoroi confirmed at the time that there were no survivors among the six passengers and the pilot. The helicopter had reportedly picked up the clients from Suiyan, with the flight booked through End Beyond Safaris.

As the industry grapples with these back-to-back losses, stakeholders are watching closely for the findings of the investigations. For a country that relies heavily on domestic carriers to ferry visitors to remote, scenic destinations, the recurring nature of these accidents presents a significant challenge to both operational safety and public confidence.

Authorities have yet to release the identities of the two individuals involved in the Kajiado incident. For now, the focus remains on the recovery efforts and the technical assessment of the wreckage to understand what went wrong during the training exercise.
