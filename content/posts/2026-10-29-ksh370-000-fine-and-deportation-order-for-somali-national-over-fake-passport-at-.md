A Somali national caught attempting to navigate Jomo Kenyatta International Airport with falsified Kenyan credentials has been ordered to pay Ksh370,000 in fines, facing default prison terms before a mandated deportation to Somalia.

The case, handled by the JKIA Law Courts, concluded after the accused admitted to three distinct counts covering unlawful presence, falsification of identity to secure passport credentials, and presenting a fraudulent national identity card designed to bypass airport security systems.

![A section of the Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (JKIA)](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/jkia_7.jpg.webp?itok=93kRPqVe)

Frontline screening by immigration officials at the airport triggered the initial interception, handing the inquiry over to detectives from Immigration and Citizen Services Investigations. The court structured the financial penalty into three separate segments: a Ksh150,000 fine for unlawful residency without valid papers, an identical Ksh150,000 penalty for identity falsification linked to the passport issuance, and an additional Ksh70,000 fine for knowingly deploying a fraudulent identity card to deceive border control personnel.

Under the court's directives, settlement of the fines does not regularize the individual's status. Instead, the judgment triggers an immediate deportation order upon payment completion, closing the local judicial file.

![An image of a court gravel](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_300x150/public/images/media/court-gravel.jpg.webp?itok=Rks9lJk8)

Simultaneously, the Directorate of Criminal Investigations confirmed that the intercepted passport will be handed over to the Director General of Immigration Services (DGIS) specifically for physical destruction. The disposal order comes as the immigration department faces heightened public and institutional scrutiny over credential security, tracking systems, and document vetting protocols at key ports of entry.

Legal proceedings and subsequent administrative actions remain ongoing as investigators examine the wider network behind the production of the fraudulent documents.
