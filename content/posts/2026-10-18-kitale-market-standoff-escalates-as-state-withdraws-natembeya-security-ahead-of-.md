A dispute over trader allocations at Kitale’s new Masinde Muliro Market has rapidly transformed into a high-stakes institutional standoff, triggering street violence, the withdrawal of county gubernatorial security, and threats of arrest against Trans Nzoia Governor George Natembeya.

The administrative dispute over who controls the trading stalls in the newly built facility erupted into broader political warfare this week, drawing in the top brass of the National Police Service (NPS) and prompting national opposition figures to assemble a defence perimeter around the county boss.

Speaking in Mwingi on October 17 during a routine home visit, Wiper Patriotic Front leader Kalonzo Musyoka revealed that intelligence reaching the opposition indicated the police service was preparing to detain Natembeya and other allied politicians linked to the United Alternative Government. Kalonzo, who previously served as Natembeya’s legal counsel when the former Rift Valley regional commissioner was arraigned at the Milimani Law Courts, declared that the opposition would mount an immediate courtroom resistance against any fresh detention.

"We will resist any attempt to intimidate or harass elected leaders," Kalonzo stated, warning that using the criminal justice machinery to settle administrative disagreements undermines constitutional governance ahead of the 2027 general elections.

![A collage of Trans-Nzoia Governor George Natembeya (Left), and President William Ruto (Right).](https://i.ibb.co/1fnPjM1p/e71c3692078e.webp)

### The Municipal Trigger

The immediate flashpoint behind the friction remains the ownership and allocation process at Masinde Muliro Market in Kitale town. The county government constructed the facility specifically to rehouse roadside informal traders who had operated without permanent shelter for years.

However, county officials accuse the national executive of intervening directly in the tenant roll, replacing verified local roadside business owners with an alternative roster favoured by national administration figures. Natembeya publicly refused to surrender the allocation process, insisting that local Trans Nzoia smallholders must take up the spaces regardless of directives from Nairobi.

The friction spilled into the streets on October 16, when rival political groups clashed in Kitale. According to an official dispatch from the NPS, the violent confrontation left two police officers injured and two state vehicles damaged.

### Security Reductions and Executive Pressure

![Side by side images of Wiper leader Kalonzo Musyoka and President William Ruto](https://i.ibb.co/0jPjtYj8/667a06bfd9f3.webp)

Following the Kitale unrest, state enforcement against the county executive accelerated. Natembeya’s official security detail was abruptly recalled, prompting the governor to issue a formal demand for an explanation from Inspector General of Police Douglas Kanja over the statutory basis for stripping an elected county leader of protection during an active public security crisis.

Opposition leaders argue the security recall and threatened arrests are punitive measures aimed at checking Natembeya’s rising political stature in the Western and Rift Valley regions, particularly his vocal alignment with alternative political alliances.

Kalonzo also directed scrutiny toward rhetoric emerging from the presidency itself, referencing comments made by President William Ruto during a stop in Iten, Elgeyo Marakwet County, where the Head of State warned that certain political opponents would encounter accidents.

"These are careless remarks to make, especially now that we are headed to the election year," Kalonzo said, demanding that the presidency publicly clarify the statement and reassure the country regarding the physical safety of dissenting leaders.

With two police vehicles damaged, two officers recovering from injuries, and the Masinde Muliro Market stalls remaining at the centre of an unresolved jurisdictional tug-of-war, the confrontation now shifts to whether the Inspector General will restore the governor's security contingent or proceed with formal charges that would trigger a high-profile courtroom showdown in Nairobi.
