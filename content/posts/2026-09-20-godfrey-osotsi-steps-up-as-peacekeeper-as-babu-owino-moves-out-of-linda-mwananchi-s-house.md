Vihiga Senator Godfrey Osotsi took to the podium in Hamisi Sub-County with a specific task: reassuring a crowd that the high-profile political partnership powering the Linda Mwananchi movement was not falling apart. Speaking on Saturday, September 19, the movement’s treasurer insisted that Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna, Embakasi East MP Babu Owino, and Siaya Governor James Orengo remain firmly united, despite very public signs that the group's key personalities are beginning to chart separate courses.

Facing local crowds on his home turf, Osotsi attempted to brush off days of mounting speculation over backstage friction, framing the growing disagreement as routine domestic tension rather than a structural breakup. “As Linda Mwananchi, we are focused and united. If you have heard of certain problems, every house must have some misunderstandings,” Osotsi declared, promising that all internal grievances within the outfit would be sorted out shortly.

### A House With Two Front Doors

Yet Osotsi’s peacekeeper stance comes at an awkward moment for the movement. While the Vihiga senator spent his weekend rally pitching Sifuna as Linda Mwananchi’s sole presidential contender for 2027, one of the movement's biggest drawcards—Babu Owino—had already publicly announced his own separate political vehicle.

Babu recently unveiled The Mwananchi Party (TMP) as his chosen ticket for his Nairobi gubernatorial ambitions, adding that his new outfit would field candidates across the country. More tellingly, the Embakasi East lawmaker signaled that his personal political schedule would now take priority over group appearances, stating that he may not attend every Linda Mwananchi rally as he focuses on rolling out his own platform. While Babu maintains that he still backs Sifuna’s presidential bid, his decision to construct an independent political house has left movement figures scrambling to explain how a unified front can operate under separate party flags.

### Court Battles and External Blame

During the Hamisi address, Osotsi sought to redirect attention away from internal friction by targeting external opponents, accusing President William Ruto and government-aligned commentators of amplifying reports of a split. He claimed the administration had placed continuous administrative hurdles whenever Linda Mwananchi tried to formalize its registration.

“We are organised. They tried to take our name, we went to court, and on September 30, the court will decide. But Ruto has placed obstacles; whenever we try to register the party, he goes and creates hurdles,” Osotsi alleged.

Despite the legal battles surrounding their brand name, Osotsi insisted that the movement holds a contingency plan that will be unveiled before the end of the month. “We have a party. And in less than two weeks we will be announcing that party. Edwin Watenya Sifuna will stand as president of Kenya under it,” he declared.

### The Battle for Western Support

Osotsi also used the gathering to push a broader political realignment in Western Kenya, urging residents to move away from ODM. He argued that the party had long enjoyed the region's loyalty without delivering corresponding local benefits, while simultaneously dismissing ruling UDA empowerment programs as insufficient for the area's economic demands.

For Linda Mwananchi, however, managing local messaging is secondary to keeping its core leadership on the same stage. As Osotsi works to keep Western voters committed to Sifuna and promises a party unveil by early October, Babu Owino’s parallel track ensures the movement enters the final quarter of the year with two competing political vehicles and an unresolved question over who will show up to the next joint rally.
