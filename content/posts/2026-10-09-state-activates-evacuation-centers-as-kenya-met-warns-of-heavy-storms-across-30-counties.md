National emergency management teams have shifted to active standby across the country after the Kenya Meteorological Service issued an expansive seven-day forecast warning of heavy storms and severe winds cutting across more than 30 counties.

The alert, running from October 9 through October 16, 2026, prompted the government to activate national disaster response protocols. State safety agencies have readied evacuation centers, deployed contingency logistics, and positioned relief supplies as meteorologists warned that localized downpours could quickly overwhelm drainage systems and low-lying settlements.

The advisory covers nearly every major economic and agricultural corridor in the republic, spanning the coast, the western basin, the Rift Valley, and the central highlands.

### Widespread Storm Footprint

According to the forecast released by the weatherman, precipitation will blanket an extensive swath of territory over the seven-day window, with isolated violent storms projected in multiple regions.

In the coastal strip and northeastern frontier, rains are forecast across Mandera, Wajir, Marsabit, Garissa, Tana River, Lamu, Kilifi, Mombasa, and Kwale. 

Further inland, the western belt, the Lake Victoria basin, and broad stretches of the Rift Valley face sustained downpours. The counties marked for wet conditions include Kakamega, Vihiga, Busia, Bungoma, Kisumu, Kisii, Nyamira, Migori, Siaya, and Homa Bay. The alert extends directly into highland farming hubs including Kericho, Nandi, Bomet, Nakuru, Elgeyo Marakwet, West Pokot, Baringo, Uasin Gishu, and Trans Nzoia.

The central highlands, Nairobi, and eastern zones are similarly mapped for consistent rainfall. The weather department flagged Nyeri, Murang’a, Kirinyaga, Embu, Meru, Kiambu, Tharaka Nithi, Laikipia, and Nyandarua, alongside Kitui, Makueni, Kajiado, Narok, and the capital, Nairobi.

Alongside the downpours, the meteorological service issued a specific warning for intense southerly to southeasterly winds exceeding 25 knots. The strong gale conditions are projected to hit parts of Turkana, Samburu, and Marsabit counties, raising risks of roof damage, fallen trees, and dust disruptions in arid sectors before heavier clouds settle.

### El Niño Pressure Over the Horn

The week’s weather pattern reflects a broader climate shift already tracked by regional scientists. The World Meteorological Organization had previously indicated that a strengthening El Niño phenomenon would dominate the final quarter of 2026, driving wetter-than-normal conditions across the Greater Horn of Africa.

For Kenyan farmers, the October–December short rains are traditionally critical for replenishing depleted groundwater, reviving livestock pasture in pastoral zones, and supporting late-season food production. However, meteorologists cautioned that the compressed intensity of El Niño downpours introduces severe secondary threats, including flash flooding, destructive mudslides on steep escarpments, crop washouts, and transport gridlocks along major road corridors.

With national disaster protocols now live, public safety authorities have directed residents living near river basins, unstable hillsides, and recognized flood zones to monitor official advisories closely as the rain bands move inland.
