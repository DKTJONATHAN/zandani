Deputy President Kithure Kindiki announced on Wednesday that artisanal brewers who align their processing with national industrial benchmarks will soon secure official commercial licences, providing traditional drink makers an operating shield while the state turns criminal seizures into funding for public addiction clinics.

Speaking to security chiefs convened at his Karen residence in Nairobi, the Deputy President outlined a bifurcated strategy intended to draw a clear regulatory line between heritage fermentations and lethal industrial chemical syndicates.

“Traditional brews that meet the Kenya Bureau of Standards requirements will be formalised and licensed,” Kindiki stated, confirming that compliant producers of indigenous drinks will be absorbed into the formal tax and inspection regime rather than swept up in blanket enforcement.

Alongside the licensing pathway, the government has completed draft legislation designed to bankrupt illicit distillers by stripping their accumulated wealth.

![Deputy President Kithure Kindiki during the 5th Joint Cabinet Committee on discharge of Government Business on October 13, 2026 at his Karen Residence](https://i.ibb.co/sJjHPhP6/18cff59b7ab7.webp)

“The government has finalised legislative proposals that will tighten the noose on manufacturers, distributors and enablers of ethanol and drug cartels that are supplying dangerous chemical-laced drinks,” Kindiki said, adding that the proposed statutory amendments authorise the immediate confiscation of funds and physical assets linked to the trade, redirecting recovered balances to underfunded rehabilitation centres.

### Multi-Agency Operations

The policy pivot comes against a backdrop of intensified field operations over the past fortnight. Multi-agency enforcement teams have executed nine coordinated raids against bulk illicit ethanol distribution points and conducted 116 separate crackdowns on unlawful distilling dens over the last 10 days.

To prevent inter-agency friction and institutional drop-off during prosecutions, the executive has embedded a permanent operational secretariat inside Harambee House Annex in Nairobi. The hub is tasked with unifying intelligence flows between public health inspectors, standards officers, and national administration police.

![A group of men sucking busaa from a pot.](https://i.ibb.co/prPWgwyf/888826f2ae9f.webp)

Long-standing commercial ambiguity has routinely exposed traditional brewing—including Kikuyu muratina and Western Kenya’s grain-fermented busaa—to police extortion and unregulated adulteration. While cultural consumption remains widespread across rural and peri-urban hubs, recurring contamination outbreaks involving toxic chemical additives have periodically prompted indiscriminate crackdowns.

Under the impending framework, brewers seeking legal protection must submit their processes to Kenya Bureau of Standards (KEBS) verification, satisfying food hygiene, alcohol-by-volume caps, and packaging requirements before local permits are issued.

“We remain focused and make sure we complete the journey, and we are not fighting against businesses or the industry but just protecting the nation against collapse,” Kindiki noted.

The state plans to gazette an operational roadmap next week outlining how local community committees will help identify unauthorized chemical blenders while shielding vetted artisanal producers.
