Nairobi’s formal diplomatic intervention following a surge in missile and drone strikes against Saudi Arabia directly targets two critical domestic vulnerabilities: the safety of a major Kenyan migrant workforce in the Gulf state and the stability of maritime freight routes that feed East Africa's fuel and goods supply.

In a statement issued Friday, September 18, the Ministry of Foreign and Diaspora Affairs explicitly highlighted the growing danger facing thousands of Kenyan citizens employed across Saudi Arabia, alongside religious pilgrims traveling to holy sites, as cross-border military strikes spill into urban commercial centers.

### Risk to Diaspora Workforce and Holy Sites

The diplomatic push follows a Thursday military incident in Saudi Arabia's Taif governorate, located within Mecca Province, where local defense forces reported intercepting an uncrewed drone. The strike resulted in the death of a Yemeni resident and injured two others, marking a concerning geographic escalation near civilian centers and pilgrimage transit points.

Kenya’s foreign office warned that targeting civilian infrastructure, residential areas, and sacred locations violates international humanitarian standards while directly exposing migrant communities and visiting pilgrims to physical risk.

### Threat to Red Sea Energy and Cargo Arteries

Beyond the safety of citizens on the Arabian Peninsula, the foreign ministry raised alarm over recurring disruptions across the Red Sea and the Strait of Hormuz—the primary maritime corridors for crude oil imports and commercial container ships heading to East African ports.

Nairobi called for an immediate end to attacks on merchant vessels, warning that compromised navigation rights directly threaten energy security and risk driving up freight tariffs and local commodity prices.

"Kenya remains committed to working with the international community to safeguard maritime security, protect legitimate trade, and promote international peace and stability," the ministry stated, demanding unimpeded passage through key shipping channels.

### Divergent Claims Over Escalation

The statement comes amid an intensifying operational exchange between Saudi forces and Yemen's Houthi movement. Houthi leadership, including Abdil-Malik al-Houthi, publicly denied Saudi assertions that their forces directed strikes toward Mecca, characterizing the accusations as false.

Simultaneously, Houthi representatives accused Saudi-aligned coalition forces of conducting retaliatory air raids within Yemeni territories, reporting civilian casualties and infrastructure damage in contested regions.

With military operations expanding across regional airspace and shipping lanes, Kenyan authorities face the ongoing challenge of monitoring the safety of domestic workers abroad while mitigating potential supply chain shocks at home.
