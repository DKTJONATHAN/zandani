When a luxury vehicle valued at $250,000 and two separate cash envelopes land on your doorstep as a token of appreciation, the standard public expectation involves keys changing hands and social media applause. For prominent Harare advocate Lewis Uriri, the response was a firm, well-documented rejection.

Uriri formally declined a brand-new 2026 Range Rover Sport Autobiography alongside $50,000 for fuel and an additional $50,000 designated as a handbag allowance for his wife. The high-value offerings had been publicly announced by Zimbabwean businessman Wicknell Chivayo on Monday, September 21, 2026, framed as a lavish tribute to Uriri’s years of legal representation and courtroom stamina.

Yet, by Tuesday, September 22, Uriri and his wife made it clear that the garage would remain empty of the luxury sport utility vehicle. In a measured response addressing the grand gesture, the lawyer revealed that he and Chivayo had not spoken since June 2024. He clarified that no professional mandates had been undertaken since that time, and that all historical legal work referenced in Chivayo's tribute had already been performed and fully remunerated.

Rather than celebrating the windfall, Uriri centered his refusal on the optics of professional independence. Pointing to Zimbabwe’s Legal Practitioners’ Code of Conduct, principles from the International Bar Association, and writings by the International Commission of Jurists, the advocate underscored the importance of maintaining public confidence in the legal profession.

Accepting such substantial assets tied to a concluded professional relationship, Uriri argued, risked creating the perception of an ongoing personal financial obligation that stretched far beyond closed files and settled invoices. 

While explicitly thanking Chivayo for recognizing his past litigation work, Uriri maintained that the decision was final, closing the door on one of the most publicized gift refusals in regional professional circles.
