Interior Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen alongside fellow Cabinet Secretaries and Chief Justice Martha Koome has handed technocrats, professionals, and strategic figures influential state agency roles, benefiting vital sectors spanning national cybersecurity, energy transmission, and university governance.

### What we know

* Murkomen injected private tech capacity into state digital intelligence by placing the late military chief’s daughter at the National Cybersecurity Agency.
* Cross-ministry alignment across energy, water, and innovation signals a concerted effort to anchor executive policy through stable board majorities.
* Chief Justice Koome preserved regulatory oversight on aggressive commercial debt recoveries by filling a crucial Auctioneers Licensing Board seat.
* Julius Ogamba balanced regional presence and institutional memory by swiftly overhauling university councils at JKUAT, Bomet, and Kajiado.

According to the official Gazette notice dated September 11, CS Murkomen directed that Lorna Ogolla Omondi and Jerop Joy Kibor will serve on the National Cybersecurity Agency (NCA) board for three years, a mandate aimed at fortifying critical national data infrastructure.

"In exercise of the powers conferred by the enabling legislation, the appointees shall serve for a term of three years," the Gazette declaration stated, entrenching fresh administrative direction across key statutory organs.

The gazettement confirms Chief Justice Martha Koome's appointment of Mbaabu Kelvin Mutuma to the Auctioneers Licensing Board for three years, a move designed to protect commercial creditors and debtors through structured licensing oversight. Concurrently, Prime Cabinet Secretary Musalia Mudavadi deployed Avedi Evans Kituzi as chairperson of the Kenya National Innovation Agency (KeNIA), stationing Ali Nilfat Kassim, Yienga Julius, Magdaline Chepkemoi, and Abass Jihan Faisal alongside him as members to empower emerging tech enterprise ecosystems.

In higher education, Education Cabinet Secretary Julius Ogamba restructured council leadership, assigning Collins Saitaga to the Jomo Kenyatta University of Agriculture and Technology (JKUAT) council. Ogamba further appointed Prof John Shiundu to chair the Bomet University Council alongside members Emily Kwamboka Nyagarama, Rose Chepkorir, Fredrick Njoroge, Reuben C. Butaki, and Amos Okello, while designating Moses Narok as non-executive chairperson for the Kajiado National Polytechnic council.

The institutional realignment extended across productive economic sectors. Tourism Cabinet Secretary Rebecca Miano renewed the tenures of Joseph Sarisar and Dorcas Ayoma Mbalanya at the Tourism Fund Board of Trustees for another three-year term, benefiting sector marketing and revenue mobilization. Water CS Eric Mugaa revoked an earlier 2024 directive to install Khadua Jimmy Kahindi as non-executive chairperson of the Coast Water Works Development Agency, while sustaining Everlyne Nadio on the North Rift Valley Water Works Development Agency board.

At Kilimo House, Agriculture Cabinet Secretary Mutahi Kagwe tasked Chebii Kilel, Emmanuel Leina Tasur, and Rosemary Kithira Muroria with driving reforms at the Coffee Board of Kenya. Meanwhile, Energy Cabinet Secretary Opiyo Wandayi reappointed Judith Khamala Makasi Nyauncho and Kirwa Bitok to the board of the Kenya Electricity Transmission Company (KETRACO) for another three-year term, guaranteeing governance continuity for high-voltage grid infrastructure.

The synchronized changes indicate a determined consolidation of public sector leadership. By balancing fresh technocratic personnel in emerging security fields with seasoned reappointments in revenue and infrastructure units, the national executive is cementing administrative control to accelerate its structural mandates.
