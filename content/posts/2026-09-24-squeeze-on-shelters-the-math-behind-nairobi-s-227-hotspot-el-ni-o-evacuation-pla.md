Nairobi’s emergency planners face a steep logistical bottleneck as they prepare to coordinate potential evacuations from 227 newly mapped flood hotspots into just 27 designated public shelters ahead of the El Niño rains.

Speaking at a multi-agency preparedness meeting in Karen on Thursday, September 24, Deputy President Kithure Kindiki warned that residents in high-risk zones will face temporary, organized evacuations if the expected downpours threaten their safety. The heavy rainfall is projected to begin during the second and third weeks of October 2026.

![Situation along Mombasa Road following heavy rains in Nairobi, May 1, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/yFVsVty5/37547b8d20a2.webp)

While the national government and Nairobi County have spent weeks aligning rescue machinery, the math of the emergency shelter strategy reveals a significant structural strain. 

Nairobi County has flagged 227 vulnerable locations spread across all 17 sub-counties. This high-risk list spans dense informal settlements and major residential hubs, including Mukuru, Kibera, Pipeline, Imara Daima, Kware, Nyayo Highrise, Nairobi West, Madaraka, Lang’ata, and parts of Embakasi. To absorb displaced residents from these vast, populated areas, emergency planners have set aside only 27 drop-off points, which are primarily public schools and municipal facilities. This represents an average of more than eight high-risk neighborhoods relying on a single shared temporary facility.

Kindiki stated that the government is actively preparing food, medical supplies, and non-food items for those who may be forced to leave their homes, urging residents to comply with relocation orders when issued. However, executing this physical transfer of thousands of people within dense urban corridors will test a system already operating under tight fiscal limits.

![A collage of property destroyed by floods with an insert of Deputy President, Kithure Kindiki](https://i.ibb.co/VWdBj9DG/b1fa8cfb87a0.webp)

"The weather projections point to significant rainfall in Nairobi, which could increase the risk of flooding, damage to roads and drainage systems, and possible disruptions to electricity supply," Kindiki said, acknowledging that budgetary constraints will inevitably affect the ultimate scale of the state’s response.

Beyond immediate evacuations, other state departments are rushing to secure fragile utility lines before the mid-October onset. The State Department for Energy is currently inspecting power and transmission lines to minimize blackouts caused by falling trees, though officials raised alarms over ongoing energy infrastructure vandalism that could worsen disruptions. Concurrently, the Ministry of Health has rolled out an El Niño preparedness framework across 18 high-risk counties, which includes plans to distribute 10 million treated mosquito nets and a proposed monthly national cleanliness day to help prevent post-flood disease outbreaks.

With less than three weeks remaining before the projected onset of the rains, the government's ability to safely decant vulnerable families into a handful of public classrooms remains an untested logistical gamble, tethered directly to how quickly emergency funds can be unlocked.
