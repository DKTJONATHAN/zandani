The Teachers Service Commission (TSC) faces mounting pressure to safeguard the credibility of its nationwide promotional interviews following revelations that local political networks in western Kenya are actively pushing endorsement dossiers for favored candidates.

Union officials in Kisii reported that politicians have been circulating formal recommendation letters intended to sway interview panels, threatening to turn a competitive merit review into a patronage pipeline.

The alert comes as the commission concludes the second phase of a massive career-progression exercise targeting 34,016 vacancies across the country. The evaluation cycle, which ran from September 17 to September 25, opened promotional slots for secondary school educators, Teacher Training College (TTC) staff, and Curriculum Support Officers.

### Integrity Tests Across Regional Panels

![A section of Teachers Service Commission Headquarters (TSC) building in Upperhill, Nairobi. Wednesday, March 4, 2020.](https://i.ibb.co/cKNVFy99/fdebaa3ed045.webp)

Branch leaders of the Kenya Union of Post-Primary Education Teachers (KUPPET) cautioned that informal political lobbying directly compromises the constitutional independence of the employer and disenfranchises long-serving educators who lack political sponsors.

“We want TSC to act as an independent commission,” KUPPET officials stated during the Kisii briefing. “We do not want political interference in these promotions or politicians walking around with lists of a few selected teachers which they will forward to TSC to get favours.”

The union insisted that the commission must anchor the 34,016 selections strictly on verified years of service, classroom performance, and established administrative regulations, rather than regional political endorsements.

### Multi-Front Labor Friction

The promotion dispute compounds escalating tensions between post-primary teachers and state authorities over remuneration and structural reforms in the education sector.

On Thursday, September 24, KUPPET Secretary-General Akello Misori issued a formal boycott warning covering the administration and assessment of the 2026 national examinations. Misori signaled that union members would withdraw from roles as center managers, invigilators, supervisors, and examiners unless the Salaries and Remuneration Commission (SRC) revises their statutory allowances upward.

![Over 10,000 teachers attending a State House meeting with President William Ruto on Saturday, September 13, 2025.](https://i.ibb.co/bgPMpD9w/0546e9aecab5.webp)

“We want to remind the SRC to be on the frontline to safeguard the rights of the teachers or workers who undertake such projects,” Misori said. “Imagine a centre organiser, who is a professional, is being paid below what a construction worker is being paid.”

### Push for Administrative Realignment

Parallel to the examination showdown, KUPPET is leveraging public participation hearings on proposed statutory amendments to push for an overhaul of Junior Secondary School (JSS) management.

Drawing from feedback gathered across more than 40,000 JSS instructors, the union has demanded an independent governance structure that separates junior secondary oversight from primary school leadership. KUPPET argues that administrative heads lacking secondary-level certification cannot effectively direct post-primary pedagogical operations.

As the TSC compiles interview scores from the phase-two promotion panels, the commission now faces the immediate task of auditing regional evaluation sheets against local lobbying complaints before publishing the final promotion gazette.
