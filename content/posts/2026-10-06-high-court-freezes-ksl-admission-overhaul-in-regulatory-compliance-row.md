Professional training timelines for prospective Kenyan lawyers face immediate administrative turbulence after the High Court intervened to block disputed entry modifications at the Kenya School of Law (KSL).

Justice John Onyiego issued conservatory orders halting KSL and the Council of Legal Education (CLE) from enrolling applicants for the 2027/2028 Advocates Training Programme (ATP) under a newly introduced framework. The intervention targets an amended notice that attempted to restructure eligibility routes outside of formally enacted subsidiary legislation.

![The Kenya School of Law (KSL) located in Karen, Nairobi.](https://i.ibb.co/VYSCFvJY/718792497e49.webp)

At the center of the dispute is the statutory authority required to alter professional entry standards. Petitioners argued that KSL cannot bypass Parliament by using annual public notices to introduce substantive qualification criteria. Under the Legal Education Act, any regulations prescribing ATP admission requirements must receive parliamentary approval and formal gazettement following established appellate decisions.

The contested administrative notice sought to diversify entry options beyond the traditional baseline—which requires a minimum KCSE mean grade of C+ and a B plain in English or Kiswahili. The alternative pathways included provisions allowing law graduates with lower secondary scores to qualify via supplemental academic credentials, alongside a route tied to passing a Pre-Bar Examination.

While the pathways offered alternative entry for candidates who might otherwise miss out, the legal challenge targets the procedural shortcut used to establish them. Petitioners maintain that substantive modifications to professional training standards must originate strictly from the legally mandated rule-making authority through required statutory procedures, rather than institutional administrative adjustments.

![A court Gavel.](https://i.ibb.co/8pkzcKZ/15ca512210aa.webp)

The court’s intervention introduces acute uncertainty for applicants navigating active recruitment windows. KSL recently opened applications for both the 2027/2028 ATP cycle and the January 2027 Pre-Bar Examination, with strict deadlines looming on November 13 and December 11 respectively.

Respondents have been granted seven days to file their formal replies as the litigation unfolds against the backdrop of wider national proposals to decentralize advocate training beyond KSL. The High Court has scheduled the matter for an inter-partes hearing on Tuesday, October 13, to determine whether the conservatory orders will remain active pending the full determination of the petition.
