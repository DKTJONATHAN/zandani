Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna has taken the financial ledger of the Linda Mwananchi movement directly to the airwaves, placing the creative talent behind their countrywide podiums at the center of his defense against State House.

Speaking during a radio broadcast on Thursday, November 19, 2026, the vocal lawmaker pushed back against claims that the coalition’s public Paybill had turned into a private entertainment purse. Instead, Sifuna revealed that the crowd-sourced pool was financing the construction of a permanent home for Joshua, the young spoken-word artist whose stage performances have become a signature fixture at the movement’s rallies in western Kenya.

Joshua, whose fiery verses regularly warm up crowds before the political heavyweights take the microphone, had been staying with his father in precarious living quarters in Kakamega. That domestic reality prompted the movement’s ground team to redirect part of the public contributions into masonry work rather than standard podium allowances.

“There is a young boy who recites poems at our rallies. Our team in Kakamega is currently building him a house because the place where he was living with his father was not in good condition,” Sifuna explained during the broadcast.

### Bricks at Lintel Level in Kakamega

According to Sifuna, the philanthropic side project is moving quickly. Kakamega Senate aspirant Honourable Lishenga has taken direct charge of overseeing the site, transmitting progress photos to Nairobi as the walls went up.

“Honourable Lishenga, our Senate aspirant in Kakamega, has already sent me pictures of the construction. The house has reached the lintel stage, and he has assured me that by the time we return from our tour of the United States, the roofing will be complete, and we are using the contributions from the Paybill,” Sifuna said.

The disclosure marks a deliberate public pivot, connecting grassroots donations not to hotel suites, but to physical shelter for an underage rally star who has drawn applause across the movement's campaign trail.

### The Rally Payroll: Enter DJ Spider

Beyond Joshua’s residential build, Sifuna opened up on the practical production costs that keep a modern political tour moving across counties. Running large-scale public assemblies requires serious technical crew, and Sifuna confirmed that the crowd-sourced Paybill directly funds the sound desk—specifically compensating their resident deckmaster, DJ Spider.

“We use the money to pay for our DJ, Spider,” Sifuna stated plainly, demystifying where rally production funds land once crowds disperse.

The same operational pot has doubled as an emergency welfare fund for the movement's foot soldiers. Sifuna revealed that part of the Paybill collections had already gone toward settling private hospital invoices for support crew members who sustained injuries during chaotic political engagements in Homa Bay.

### Squaring Off with State House Over the Bar Tab

The itemised breakdown serves as a direct reply to President William Ruto, who publicly questioned the movement's financial integrity. In recent barbs, the Head of State alleged that the funds collected by Linda Mwananchi from struggling Kenyans were being squandered on high-end alcohol and lavish personal nightlife.

Sifuna brushed aside the accusations, terming the suggestion that he would tap public pennies to fund his leisure hours baseless.

“I promised those market women that we would never forget their acts of kindness,” Sifuna said, noting that ordinary Kenyans continue to wire micro-contributions to the line. “To everyone sending us money, we see every contribution, even the Ksh 10 donations reflecting on our Paybill.”

With the movement’s principals now preparing for their planned roadshow in the United States, Sifuna placed the completion of Joshua’s roof as the benchmark against which Lishenga's ground team will be judged upon their return.
