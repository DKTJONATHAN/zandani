Five years after a controversial radio broadcast upended his career at Homeboyz Radio, veteran disc jockey Joe Mfalme is making it clear he has zero regrets about the advice that sparked the storm.

Speaking during an interview with Tony Mwirigi, the celebrated DJ revealed that the exact philosophy he shared on-air in March 2021 is the very same guidance he intends to pass on to his daughter as she grows older. At the core of his blueprint is a single, uncompromising rule: never make yourself overly available in a relationship.

“Chenye nilisema kwa radio ndio nitamuambia… ndio nataka kumfunza. Number one, don’t be too available,” Joe Mfalme stated, defending a perspective he says is rooted in the hard realities of modern dating dynamics.

That perspective first thrust the decks-master—alongside former colleagues Shaffie Weru and Neville Musya—into a massive national controversy during the station's *Lift-Off* breakfast show. The presenters had been discussing a violent incident involving Eunice Wangari, a woman who was allegedly thrown from a 12th-floor Nairobi apartment building after rejecting a man's sexual advances. The on-air commentary drew furious public backlash, with critics accusing the team of victim-blaming and trivializing gender-based violence, leading to their immediate dismissal from the station.

Reflecting on that turbulent period during his latest sit-down, Joe Mfalme drew a direct line between the fallout of 2021 and the ongoing national conversations surrounding femicide in Kenya. Rather than distancing himself from the remarks that ended his tenure at Homeboyz, he argued that unreciprocated attraction and mismatched romantic intentions remain an everyday reality in the dating scene.

“Bro, after hiyo incident yetu, ni madame wangapi hadi saa hii unaona femicide? Mimi what I said… in the dating scene kukatiana, ule dame unataka hakutaki, na ule anakutaka hutaki story yake. Ni kitu inakuanga,” he explained.

While acknowledging that many women continue to view their historical remarks as a defense of harmful behavior, the DJ maintained his footing. He also used the platform to reiterate his loyalty to his former co-host Shaffie Weru, affirming that his stance on his embattled former colleague remains unchanged.

“Mimi bado na support Shaffie. Usiende kwa mtu hujui kwake,” Joe Mfalme added, closing the chapter on a past professional crisis by doubling down on both his personal convictions and his enduring friendships.
