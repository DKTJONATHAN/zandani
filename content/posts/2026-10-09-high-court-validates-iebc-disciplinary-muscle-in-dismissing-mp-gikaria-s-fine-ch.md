According to a determination issued by the High Court in Nairobi, the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission holds full statutory authority to levy administrative penalties against elected lawmakers, removing legal shields for politicians who breach campaign regulations.

The ruling by Justice Wilfrida Musyoka at the Milimani Law Courts directly benefits electoral oversight bodies by cementing their disciplinary autonomy. In dismissing an application filed by Nakuru Town East MP David Gikaria, the court cleared the way for the commission to enforce a Ksh2.5 million fine and related sanctions stemming from inflammatory conduct during the Ol Kalou parliamentary by-election.

Justice Musyoka ruled that the legislator failed to clear the evidential and procedural bar required for judicial review orders. The decision dissolved temporary conservatory orders that had protected the MP since July 15, immediately restoring the full determination handed down by the Electoral Code of Conduct Enforcement Committee.

### The Sanctions and the Dual-Track Mechanism

![Nakuru East MP David Gikaria speaking during a past committee meeting.](https://i.ibb.co/chjxTt9t/7310e98a011f.webp)

Following the verdict, the IEBC instructed Gikaria to comply with the enforcement committee's directives without further delay, reiterating that elective status offers no shelter from regulatory discipline.

“The Commission calls upon Hon. David Gikaria to comply with the directions and sanctions contained in the determination of the Electoral Code of Conduct Enforcement Committee,” the electoral agency said in an official statement.

The disciplinary case against the lawmaker originated on July 14, when the commission found him culpable of making inflammatory remarks during the Ol Kalou contest. The committee cited statements touching on voter bribery, cash distribution schemes, and the improper handling of national identity cards intended to depress voter participation.

Beyond the Ksh2.5 million monetary penalty, the original committee verdict required Gikaria to issue a formal public apology and retract his statements. It also triggered a dual-track legal exposure: while the commission pursued civil and regulatory compliance internally, it forwarded the file to the Office of the Director of Public Prosecutions (ODPP) to investigate potential statutory electoral offences.

### Broadening the Regulatory Perimeter

In its subsequent briefing, the commission highlighted that the judicial endorsement reinforces the enforcement committee’s broader supervisory toolkit. The High Court affirmed that the committee’s mandate to issue summons, adjudicate formal complaints, and penalise infractions runs parallel to—rather than dependent upon—criminal litigation.

Under this validated framework, the commission retains the discretion to issue reprimands, exact financial penalties, enforce apologies, restrict campaign access, or move to disqualify candidates entirely when democratic standards are threatened.

The court observed that voter manipulation schemes and voter suppression represent acute dangers to representative governance, necessitating prompt and enforceable administrative disincentives. With the High Court review extinguished, Gikaria must now settle the financial penalty and execute the required public retractions, even as the ODPP retains the statutory prerogative to determine whether to initiate formal criminal proceedings.
