Regulatory enforcement at the Teachers Service Commission remains in statutory limbo as the employer pushes to vacate orders freezing its online conduct directive before an October 15 ruling.

Appearing before Justice Francis Nyungu Kyambia at the High Court in Kiambu, commission lawyers pressed for the immediate lifting of conservatory orders that currently bar the implementation of the August circular. The commission argues that the freeze creates administrative confusion among educators over whether professional disciplinary standards still apply to their digital footprints.

![Acting Teachers Service Commission CEO Evaleen Mitei speaking at a past function.](https://i.ibb.co/N2T6S21b/92bd4ad43293.webp)

The regulatory push follows a surge in viral posts by educators and a growing number of teachers doubling as online influencers. In response, the August directive cautioned educators against cyberbullying, harassment, intimidation, and offensive digital content, warning that violations would attract disciplinary action.

However, a petition filed by an aggrieved teacher successfully halted the rollout, with the applicant arguing that the circular introduced ambiguous standards granting the commission overly broad discretion to police online expression.

Defending the directive in court, TSC lawyer Jared Gekombe maintained that the communication establishes no new disciplinary offenses or separate judicial processes. Instead, Gekombe argued, the circular merely serves as a compliance reminder drawing directly from existing statutes, including the Constitution, the TSC Act, the Code of Conduct and Ethics for Teachers, the Leadership and Integrity Act, the Computer Misuse and Cybercrimes Act, and the Data Protection Act.

![teachers state house](https://i.ibb.co/JW2bD7K0/2c104554bb9b.webp)

Lawyers opposing the application urged the court to weigh the constitutional rights of educators against the commission's supervisory mandate, emphasizing that any lifting of orders must meet stringent legal thresholds.

Justice Kyambia is scheduled to deliver his ruling on the application on October 15, determining whether the commission can resume active oversight of teachers' online conduct while the main constitutional petition proceeds.
