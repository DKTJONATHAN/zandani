A civilian blockade along the Kitale-Lodwar Highway on Friday morning has brought into sharp relief the growing friction between local communities in Turkana South and the state’s multi-agency security apparatus operating along the regional borders.

Traffic on Northern Kenya’s primary transit corridor ground to a halt early Friday, November 6, 2026, after residents of Turkana South and Aroo Sub-County erected barricades of stones and burning tyres at Kalemng’orok and Kainuk Town. The demonstration, mobilized following a public notice issued Thursday by Turkana South Member of Parliament Ariko Namoit, brought public transport and commercial haulage to a complete standstill.

Namoit arrived in Kainuk shortly after 9:00 AM as police contingents deployed to manage the growing crowds and rising local tension. While security operations in the region have focused on suppressing armed banditry and cattle rustling, demonstrator grievances in Kainuk shifted the spotlight onto the operational conduct of state forces on the ground.

### Complaints Against Specialized Operations

![A collage showing protesters blocking the Kitale–Lodwar Highway with stones and burning tyres, alongside demonstrators marching, November 6, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/twB05bLp/36a52b16abd5.webp)

Central to the local pushback are allegations directed at the Special Operations Group (SOG), a specialized unit deployed as part of ongoing multi-agency security drives. Protesters and local leaders accused officers from the unit of using excessive force during operations in Aroo, alleging instances of harassment targeting civilians, including women and schoolchildren, alongside property damage.

Beyond individual conduct, Namoit raised questions regarding the equity of the ongoing disarmament drive, arguing that the disarming exercise appears to be applied selectively, leaving the Turkana community disproportionately vulnerable to cross-border raids.

"Let us come out peacefully and stand together in demanding that the government fulfil its responsibility to protect our people, their lives and their property," Namoit had stated in his Thursday notice to constituents, urging disciplined and law-abiding demonstrations.

Local complaints also pointed to systemic operational gaps in state protection, specifically citing recurring fatal attacks along the Kitale-Lodwar Highway itself and a persistent failure by law enforcement to trace and recover stolen livestock following border raids.

![Turkana South MP Ariko Namoit arrives at the protests along the Kitale–Lodwar Highway in Kainuk, November 6, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/396cnhL8/da56910a84c3.webp)

### Border Deployment Context

The Kainuk standoff comes against the backdrop of an escalated security posture instituted by the Ministry of Interior. In September 2026, Interior Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen announced plans to post additional security personnel and tactical equipment along the volatile borders connecting Turkana, West Pokot, and Baringo counties. The deployment was aimed at dismantling armed banditry networks, executing total disarmament, and securing transit corridors.

However, Friday’s disruptions illustrate how tactical operations risk losing local co-operation when civilian populations perceive enforcement as asymmetric or aggressive. Demonstrators in Kainuk called for an immediate review of security tactics in the region, appealing directly to President William Ruto to step in and restructure enforcement protocols.

As police clear transit routes and monitor Kainuk Town, the unresolved issue remains whether the Ministry of Interior will adjust its operational engagements to rebuild community trust while maintaining security operations along the border zones.
