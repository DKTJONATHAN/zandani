President William Ruto has thrown down the gauntlet against exclusive corporate gatekeeping, declaring that the multi-billion-shilling Lamu oil refinery will not be reserved for a wealthy few.

Speaking on the structural framework of the mega-project, the Head of State insisted that ordinary citizens must secure a direct financial foothold in the country's upcoming industrial core. Rather than locking major infrastructure stakes behind closed boardroom doors, the government plans to list the refinery on the Nairobi Securities Exchange (NSE).

“The investment in the Lamu oil refinery is entirely open and transparent. The Government of Kenya will hold a stake in this refinery, and all Kenyans will have the opportunity to purchase shares in it,” Ruto stated.

The push aims to dismantle traditional investment bottlenecks that leave mega-projects in the hands of select syndicates. Rejecting the notion that strategic national enterprises belong exclusively to elite investors, the President drew a pointed parallel to existing public utilities like Kenya Power to illustrate his point about broad-based shareholding.

“Do not assume you are the only smart ones or that ownership in companies like Kenya Power belongs solely to a select few,” he remarked, pushing back against assumptions of corporate exclusivity.

Under the proposed model, the state will retain a baseline equity stake in the facility while opening the remainder to public subscription. To bridge the financial literacy gap, the administration has pledged to roll out targeted public education campaigns to guide citizens on how to navigate share acquisition on the bourse.

The Lamu refinery sits at the center of broader state ambitions to transform the coastal region into a sprawling energy and industrial corridor. Whether everyday retail investors seize the opportunity to buy into the country's next major economic frontier will depend on the forthcoming prospectus and the mechanics of the NSE listing.
