Kenya’s road transport system recorded over 9,000 fatalities in the nine months leading up to September 2026, a toll underlined by a 48-hour sequence of serious collisions spanning critical regional transit routes in Central, Coastal, and Western regions.

Data from the National Transport and Safety Authority (NTSA) confirms that 9,002 people died in road crashes between January and September 2026, reflecting persistent vulnerabilities across public service passenger vehicles, commercial heavy haulage, and arterial highway corridors.

### Ol Kalou Off-Road Plunge

In Nyandarua County, emergency responders and heavy tow trucks were dispatched on Monday, October 12, following a severe off-road crash along the Ol Kalou-Nakuru Road. A 14-seater public service matatu plunged into a roadside ditch after the driver reportedly lost control at high speed.

![Snippets of the Ol Kalou accident along the Ol Kalou-Nauru Highway involving a 14-seater matatu on Monday, October 12.](https://i.ibb.co/XxKbrY1k/23a3bc021d37.webp)

Witnesses at the scene reported that the vehicle veered sharply off the tarmac, coming to a halt with its front cabin severely crushed. Personal belongings and vehicle debris were scattered across the embankment. Several passengers sustained injuries and were evacuated for medical attention. Official traffic authorities have not released a final count of casualties or confirmed fatalities from the site.

### Commercial Arteries and Border Corridors

The Ol Kalou crash coincided with another fatal incident along the Nairobi-Mombasa Highway in Manyani, Taita Taveta County. On Monday, a heavy truck collided with a lorry on the single-carriageway section of the corridor, leaving one person dead and several others injured.

The Manyani collision renewed focus on the slow pace of capacity expansion along the country’s primary logistics artery. State planners recently abandoned the proposed Ksh468 billion greenfield Usahihi Expressway, redirecting focus toward expanding and dualling the existing 416-kilometre stretch of the Nairobi-Mombasa highway to curb head-on collisions between commercial freight hauliers.

![An accident along the Nairobi-Mombasa Highway in Manyani, Taita Taveta County, involved a truck and a lorry on October 12.](https://i.ibb.co/XrCSfHjc/6ce1ca40b6d3.webp)

Meanwhile, in Western Kenya, a cross-border feeder route in Busia County recorded a two-vehicle crash on Sunday, October 11. A Nissan matatu traveling toward Busia town collided with a private passenger car bound for Bumala at Barong’eche in Matayos. Multiple individuals, including a well-known local entertainer, sustained injuries and were hospitalized, though no fatalities were reported from the scene.

### Pedestrians and Passengers Bear the Burden

The concentration of incidents across varied terrain highlights the broad distribution of traffic risk in Kenya. According to NTSA’s statutory breakdown, pedestrians remain the single most vulnerable group on national roads, recording 836 fatalities in the first half of 2026 alone.

Motorcyclists, vehicle passengers, and commercial truck crews account for the remainder of the 9,002 registered deaths over the nine-month period, reflecting recurring systemic factors including speed management failures, narrow two-lane corridors, and inadequate non-motorized transport infrastructure.

Police traffic departments across the affected divisions in Nyandarua, Taita Taveta, and Busia have initiated technical scene inspections to establish the precise mechanical and operational causes behind each crash.
