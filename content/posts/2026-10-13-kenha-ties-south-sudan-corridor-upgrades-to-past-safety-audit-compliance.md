A new four-year road safety audit along the northern arterial to South Sudan will test whether contractors are incorporating previously issued engineering warnings into active construction zones, rather than shelving them until tarmac is laid.

The Kenya National Highways Authority (KeNHA) issued a tender notice on Tuesday inviting consulting firms to express interest in running road safety audits, public sensitization, and institutional capacity-building across the Lesseru–Kitale and Morphus–Lokichar sections of the international trunk road.

Under Phase One of the African Development Group-backed initiative, the selected consultant will not merely inspect pavement markings and signboards. A primary contractual mandate requires the firm to conduct retrospective reviews of previous road safety audits to determine whether past recommendations were incorporated into contractor work programmes and are being actively executed on the ground.

### Auditing Construction Zones and Trade Friction

![Kionyo- Kanyakine- Mutiokiama- Kithaka Naro- Mitunguu road in Imenti South Constituency.](https://i.ibb.co/wNC1RB1G/bfcca6edfdd1.webp)

The consultancy is structured to span 48 months, maintaining continuous oversight through active construction and the post-completion liability window. 

Along both the Lesseru–Kitale stretch in the North Rift and the arid Morphus–Lokichar segment in Turkana County, the assignment focuses on mitigating corridor friction. The audit firm is tasked with reviewing contractor traffic management plans and evaluating the specific physical hazards that transit trade creates for non-motorized traffic, including cyclists, pastoral pedestrians, and roadside market traders.

Beyond technical corridor evaluations, the consultant will roll out mandatory road safety awareness campaigns directed at transit drivers, schools located along the right-of-way, and settlements bordering the construction corridors.

### Breaking Inter-Agency Crash Data Silos

Alongside site-level engineering inspections, the procurement addresses a longstanding institutional breakdown in how Kenya records and acts on highway trauma.

KeNHA plans to utilize the consultant to train six separate state bodies on road crash data management under a shared four-year curriculum. The initiative brings together three road agencies—KeNHA, the Kenya Urban Roads Authority (KURA), and the Kenya Rural Roads Authority (KeRRA)—alongside the National Transport and Safety Authority (NTSA), the National Police Service (NPS), and the Ministry of Health.

![Construction workers at the Wikililye–Katulani Road site in Kitui during a KeNHA inspection, September 25, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/7xzg2cFv/a014d348e43d.webp)

Traffic crash records in Kenya have historically suffered from structural fragmentation. Crash scene investigations recorded in police dockets frequently fail to reconcile with hospital trauma registry admissions or inform immediate roadside remedial designs by road authorities. By looping public health personnel and police traffic desks into the same data management protocol as highway planners, the AfDB-financed programme seeks to establish an empirical baseline for corridor hazards.

### Expanding Regional Corridor Oversight

The Lesseru–Lokichar scrutiny follows a parallel procurement KeNHA initiated in June, when the agency sought consultants to conduct comprehensive road safety audits across critical routes under the Horn of Africa Isiolo–Mandera Corridor programme.

Financing for the consultancy on the South Sudan link is being drawn jointly from the African Development Group and the Government of Kenya.

With expressions of interest formally opened, the selection of the supervising firm will determine how aggressively KeNHA enforces design modifications on active construction sites before the 193-kilometre transit route is fully opened to heavy regional freight.
