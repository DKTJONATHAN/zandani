Opposition efforts to construct a monolithic front against President William Ruto faced a structural roadblock on Wednesday, as the Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP) rejected direct absorption into the newly minted Ukombozi Alliance.

Speaking during a public engagement on September 23, DCP Deputy Party Leader Cleophas Malala outlined a conditional, multi-tiered bargaining strategy. Rather than merging immediately into the alliance formerly known as Azimio La Umoja, DCP intends to first forge a coalition with the Linda Mwananchi Movement. 

![Former Kakamega Senator Cleophas Malala speaking during a campaign rally.](https://i.ibb.co/Cs2wqVn5/f36590cf51ee.webp)

Under the blueprint outlined by Malala, that combined faction will then sit down with Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna’s political network. Only after cementing that internal pact will the united bloc open formal negotiations with the parties comprising the Ukombozi Alliance.

"We as DCP cannot join the Ukombozi Alliance. They should join together as TUA, while we in the DCP will join Sifuna. Then we will negotiate together for one coalition," Malala stated, signaling that the party intends to enter future coalition talks from a position of consolidated collective bargaining rather than as an individual junior partner.

The announcement comes just two days after coalition leader Kalonzo Musyoka spearheaded the rebranding of Azimio La Umoja into the Ukombozi Alliance, a move intended to accommodate fresh vehicles including the Linda Mwananchi Movement and DCP. However, Malala’s remarks expose a disconnect over the hierarchy of those realignments, leaving both DCP and the Linda Mwananchi faction outside the immediate TUA framework.

![gachagua dcp](https://i.ibb.co/FqVTzsQZ/ff9366dd4cfb.webp)

While Sifuna’s movement has repeatedly maintained its intent to contest the 2027 polls under a joint opposition umbrella, the faction has yet to formally confirm or detail its involvement in Malala’s proposed pre-alliance architecture.

With opposition figures insisting that unseating President Ruto in the first round hinges entirely on fielding a single, consensus presidential candidate, the maneuvering highlights the delicate internal calculus governing 2027 pre-election pacts. As parties race to position themselves before formal campaign windows open, the friction between top-level rebranding and grassroots coalition tiering remains an unresolved hurdle for the wider anti-Ruto camp.
