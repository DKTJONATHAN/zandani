Public health officers across Nairobi have been drafted into frontline structural surveillance, receiving instructions to actively identify and report occupational hazards on active building sites following the Friday collapse of a multi-storey development on Eastleigh’s 9th Street.

The directive, issued by the Ministry of Health on Saturday, October 24, widens the traditional scope of field health inspectors by using their community-level presence as an early-warning mechanism against hazardous building developments. The policy shift comes as multi-agency search teams race against time to locate seven people confirmed missing beneath concrete slabs and twisted reinforcement metal.

### Site Isolation and Triage Control

At the scene of the collapse, disaster response teams established a 24-hour coordination desk to direct recovery efforts and stabilize incoming casualties. Rather than permitting open-access community assistance, health authorities moved quickly to enforce operational cordoning around the unstable pile.

![PS Mary Muthoni, a Kenya Red Cross official, a police officer and a KDF officer share updates at the site of the building collapse along 9th Street in Eastleigh, Nairobi, October 2](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/ps-mary-muthoni-and-other-officials-eatleigh-building-collapse-aftermath-october-2026.jpg.webp?itok=rYVz0awK)

Public Health Principal Secretary Mary Muthoni cautioned untrained residents against attempting ad-hoc rescues, warning that well-intentioned volunteers risk triggering secondary collapses or compounding injuries on site. The ministry instructed members of the public without medical training to stay behind security barriers unless assigned specific tasks by emergency coordinators.

To manage immediate clinical needs, medical personnel from national referral facilities have been moved into Eastleigh to reinforce local teams, while surrounding hospitals remain on high alert to receive survivors extracted from the debris.

### Family Tracing and Psycho-Social Desks

Beside the physical excavation zone, the Kenya Red Cross and county health personnel have organized a joint post providing field medical attention and psychological support to distressed families. An integrated tracing registry operates out of the desk to log verified identities of trapped or unaccounted-for persons and coordinate updates directly with relatives.

![Search and rescue personnel work at the site of the building collapse along 9th Street in Eastleigh, Nairobi, October 24, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/search-and-rescue-personnel-eatleigh-building-collapse-aftermath-october-2026.jpg.webp?itok=c9IpywUN)

"The ministry is working with the Nairobi City County Government to support medical teams treating victims and prepare for those who may still be found alive," Muthoni said, urging affected families to rely on official channels as heavy machinery clears unstable debris.

### Unapproved Height and Enforcement Trail

The physical failure of the structure has brought county planning records into immediate focus. Nairobi Governor Johnson Sakaja confirmed that while the 9th Street parcel was cleared for a change-of-use application in 2024, county planners never approved the developer’s formal submission to erect a 15-storey building with a basement.

County planning authorities first raised objections to the development in November 2024, citing concerns regarding commercial shops planned across the basement and ground floors. City Hall issued an enforcement order against the site earlier in 2026, though construction activity evidently continued until the fatal failure on October 23.

County authorities have transferred the property’s architectural files, planning queries, and inspection history to the Directorate of Criminal Investigations and the National Police Service. Investigators are tasked with determining how an unapproved high-rise advanced through active enforcement notices and establishing the legal liability of the developer, structural contractors, and municipal officers charged with halting work on the site.
