For motorists navigating the Westlands-CBD corridor, the Nairobi Expressway has long served as a vital pressure-relief valve. This weekend, that valve will be partially tightened, forcing a significant volume of traffic back onto the surface-level Waiyaki Way.

Beginning Saturday, September 19, at 8:00 pm, MOJA Expressway Company will close the elevated overpass deck for motorists traveling from James Gichuru Junction toward the city center. The closure, scheduled to last until midday Monday, September 21, marks a return to the surface-level congestion that has become a recurring point of friction for commuters in the area.

### The Surface-Level Bottleneck

The diversion forces motorists who would otherwise bypass the Westlands traffic to merge with local Waiyaki Way commuters. This transition is rarely seamless. Recent history suggests that when the Expressway restricts flow, the surface road-already operating near capacity-struggles to absorb the influx. As recently as September 9, a similar diversion at the ABC Place-Red Hill Road junction resulted in extended delays, with commuters reporting long, stationary queues that rippled back through key junctions.

While the operator has confirmed that the Westlands entrance to the Expressway will remain open, the closure of the primary elevated deck removes the essential bypass function for those commuting from the western suburbs. The reliance on Waiyaki Way as a fallback highlights the lack of viable alternative arteries for cross-city traffic during infrastructure maintenance windows.

### Managing the Transition

MOJA Expressway has advised drivers to build in extra time for their journeys, though for those relying on the corridor during peak hours, the options for avoiding the resulting gridlock are limited. The company has stated that traffic marshals will be stationed along the route to manage the flow, and that normal operations for those exiting the Expressway toward the overpass are expected to resume by 5:00 pm on Saturday-a detail that suggests the maintenance work is staged in phases to mitigate the total impact.

For the city’s transport planners and daily commuters, the weekend serves as another stress test for the Westlands corridor. The core question remains whether the surface infrastructure can continue to sustain the spillover from the Expressway without triggering the kind of prolonged congestion seen earlier this month.

As of Friday evening, the success of the traffic management plan rests on the ability of the ground-level network to handle the diverted volume until the elevated deck reopens on Monday.
