Geoffrey Mosiria walked into Jesus Is Alive Ministries for a Sunday service, stood on the sanctuary floor, and walked away with an audacious proposal: asking the church's own presiding bishop, Margaret Wanjiru, to shelve her own City Hall dreams and play second fiddle on his gubernatorial ticket.

The Nairobi County Chief Officer made the bold political overture public on Sunday, October 11, 2026, fresh off an appearance alongside Jubilee Party leader and United Opposition presidential candidate Dr. Fred Matiang’i at the bishop's downtown sanctuary.

Rather than treating the Sunday service merely as an exercise in quiet devotion, Mosiria seized the moment to recalibrate the 2027 pecking order, confirming he intends to run for the capital's top seat while openly courting his host for the number two slot.

### The Sanctuary Pitch

Mosiria did not dance around the overlapping ambitions between himself and the seasoned televangelist, acknowledging that Bishop Wanjiru has long eyed Nairobi’s corner office herself. Yet in a public note shared after the service, the county official framed his bid as a done deal, asking the bishop to join him as his running mate instead.

“Yesterday, I joined H.E. Dr Fred Matiang’i, the Jubilee Party leader and United Opposition presidential candidate, at a church service at Jesus Is Alive Ministries, under Bishop Margaret Wanjiru. I took this chance to inform Nairobi residents that I have heard their call to serve them as the Governor of Nairobi,” Mosiria stated.

He then laid out his offer directly to the church's founder, pairing a request for divine backing with an invitation to step into his corner.

“I know God has already chosen the leader of Nairobi, and I would love to have her as my deputy. I requested her to pray for me so that God can choose the leadership that will fix Nairobi,” Mosiria added.

### Adopting the ‘Mr Fix It’ Persona

To justify asking a political veteran of Wanjiru’s profile to take the deputy position, Mosiria leaned heavily into his newly minted, no-nonsense executive branding. Pitching himself as a hands-on manager suited for the capital's rough-and-tumble terrain, he rolled out a trio of self-styled titles.

“Nairobi is a more serious city that needs a more serious leader, and Mr Fix It, Mr More Serious, and Mr Mosiria are that leader,” he wrote, repeating the formulaic moniker to hammer home his readiness for the race.

The Chief Officer’s messaging portrayed Nairobi as an administrative breakdown waiting for decisive intervention, positioning his county-level experience as the necessary cure for City Hall’s historic headaches.

### Warning Voters Against Deep-Pocketed ‘Investors’

Beyond courting Wanjiru’s endorsement and political base, Mosiria used the platform to issue a sharp warning to Nairobi residents about the transactional nature of city campaigns. Urging locals to enlist on voter registers, he cautioned against getting swayed by cash-heavy outsiders looking to capture City Hall for private gain.

“Ni uchungu sana kama huna kura Nairobi. Register as a voter in Nairobi and vote for a leader who understands Nairobi, not an investor who will give you money to buy your vote, then leave you to suffer for another five years,” he wrote.

By framing potential opponents as predatory financiers and styling himself as the homegrown operator who grasps the city's mechanics, Mosiria is clearly intent on setting an early, aggressive pace for the 2027 contest.

Whether Bishop Wanjiru—who has mounted prominent, high-stakes campaigns of her own in past general elections—entertains the invitation to step into the backseat of Mosiria’s campaign van remains the unanswered question hanging over the pulpit.
