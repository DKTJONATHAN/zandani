In the high-stakes, big-money world of East African logistics, there is no VIP club quite as exclusive—or as lucrative—as the group of local oil companies handling Kenya’s Government-to-Government (G-to-G) petroleum imports. 

While the public focus has largely lingered on macroeconomic jargon like currency stabilization and foreign exchange liquidity, the state's latest official defense of the deal highlights the real stars of the show: a tight, hand-picked circle of local corporate giants who secured the keys to the country's energy kingdom.

### The Golden Tickets

When President William Ruto’s administration took office in September 2022, the local oil market was in chaotic survival mode. Fuel imports were devouring a massive $500 million every single month—representing 35 percent of Kenya’s entire import bill—and local dealers had to scramble for US dollars to settle those bills within a brutal five-day window.

To halt the currency tailspin, the government signed Master Framework Agreements on March 10, 2023, with Middle Eastern oil titans Aramco, ADNOC, and ENOC. But the real intrigue lay in who would actually touch the oil on the ground.

Under national licensing rules, these global International Oil Companies (IOCs) were given the power to nominate their own local counterparties. Instead of an open free-for-all, the selection became an exclusive, invitation-only affair. 

Initially, only three local heavyweights made the cut:
* **Gulf Energy**
* **Galana Energies**
* **Oryx Energies**

These three firms became the primary conduits for a system that allowed local fuel payments to be settled in Kenya Shillings, backed by letters of credit across domestic banks, while pushing payment terms out to an extended 180-day credit period.

### The Guestlist Expands

In the high-society world of corporate partnerships, success breeds access. The state has revealed that once the initial transaction was successfully "de-risked," the exclusive club of local counterparties was expanded to admit three more players:
* **One Petroleum**
* **Asharami Synergy**
* **BE Energy**

Together, these six companies have formed an elite shield around Kenya’s fuel supply chain, enjoying direct alignment with the world’s biggest state-owned oil producers. 

### High Stakes, Lower Premiums

For this chosen circle, the rewards of keeping the nation's tanks full have come with significant operational pressure to perform. The government's latest numbers show that renegotiations within this framework have steadily driven down the lucrative premiums paid to bring fuel to East African shores.

Super Petrol premiums, which started at a high of $97.50 per metric ton, were successfully shaved down to $84 by March 2025. Diesel premiums saw an even sharper drop, falling from $118 to a fixed $78 per metric ton. 

Crucially, this core group of local distributors managed to keep those rates locked in, even when geopolitical friction in the Middle East sent global market spikes screaming toward $400 per metric ton.

While critics initially eyed the hand-picked selection of these local counterparties with intense curiosity, the state’s firm defense of the arrangement solidifies their standing. Far from being a temporary emergency measure, this select group of six has officially cemented their position as the undisputed gatekeepers of Kenya’s regional energy hub.
