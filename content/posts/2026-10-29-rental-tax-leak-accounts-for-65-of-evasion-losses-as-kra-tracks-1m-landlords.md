Uncollected rental taxes represent nearly two-thirds of Kenya’s total illicit trade and tax evasion losses, making residential property the single largest revenue hole facing the Kenya Revenue Authority as it chases a Ksh2.98 trillion ordinary collection target.

In disclosures submitted on October 28 to the National Assembly Departmental Committee on Finance and National Planning, the taxman revealed that evasion in the domestic rental market accounts for roughly 65 per cent of the estimated Ksh153 billion lost each year to illicit trade, counterfeiting, and undeclared revenue. That imbalance has turned unregistered residential property owners into the central focus of the authority’s compliance push.

To bridge that multi-billion-shilling gap, KRA informed lawmakers that it has flagged over one million landlords who collect monthly rent without filing statutory returns. The identification relies on the Electronic Rental Income Tax System (eRITS), a digital monitoring platform rolled out in April 2025 to reconcile real estate data against reported earnings.

### Digital Audits and Gross Inflows

The enforcement structure targets resident property owners earning between Ksh288,000 and Ksh15 million annually. Under statutory amendments that took effect in January 2024, landlords in this threshold face a flat residential rental income tax rate of 7.5 per cent.

Unlike standard corporate income assessments, the 7.5 per cent levy operates as a final tax calculated strictly on gross receipts. The automated eRITS platform calculates tax liability directly from declared gross earnings, disallowing claims for property maintenance, management overheads, mortgage expenses, or tenant vacancy losses. Property owners are required to remit payments monthly, with compliance deadlines anchored on or before the 20th day of every succeeding month.

To identify undeclared properties, eRITS operates as a data-fusion engine linked to the state's eCitizen infrastructure. The platform matches national identity registries and property conveyance documentation against tenant payment declarations and utility connections. The authority told MPs it is currently integrating geolocation mapping into the platform, allowing audit teams to physically anchor registered titles to standing residential developments and compare neighborhood market valuations against declared rents.

### Field Audits and Revenue Pressure

The software roll-out also alters how the taxman deploys personnel on the ground. KRA is using eRITS data dashboards to supervise Revenue Service Assistants—the operational field officers tasked with door-to-door data gathering and compliance checks—by directing field inspections specifically toward properties with stark mismatches between physical occupancy and tax filings.

The urgency behind the real estate dragnet reflects escalating demands from the National Treasury. Fiscal managers have handed KRA an ordinary tax collection target of Ksh2.98 trillion for the 2026/2027 financial year, an anchor component of the national government's overall Ksh3.63 trillion projected revenue envelope.

With landlords required to settle October liabilities by November 20, the authority's ability to convert its one-million-property digital inventory into recurring monthly inflows will determine whether domestic property continues to bleed tens of billions in public revenue or finally yields its statutory share.
