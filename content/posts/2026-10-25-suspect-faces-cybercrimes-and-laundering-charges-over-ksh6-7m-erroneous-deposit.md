Criminal prosecutors are leveraging technology misuse and anti-money laundering statutes against an individual accused of treating an accidental bank deposit as a windfall rather than reporting the transaction.

Detectives from the Banking Fraud Investigation Unit (BFIU) intercepted the suspect along Lantana Road in Nairobi’s Westlands area on Saturday, October 24, 2026. The arrest stems from a financial anomaly originating on November 18, 2025, during a batch processing run of 74 electronic transactions by a commercial entity.

![A man with handcuffs](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/a%20man%20with%20handcuffs.jpg.webp?h=43b45004&itok=9raehVBf)

According to investigative findings released by the Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI), the transaction batch was meant to cover a modest Ksh72,000 shop rent obligation for premises located in Suswa. A systemic or data entry error during the transfer process instead routed an additional Ksh6,744,354 into the recipient's account.

When the paying company discovered the discrepancy and attempted to retrieve the funds, the recipient opted to draw down and spend the capital rather than facilitate its return. That decision shifted the dispute from a civil accounting error into the criminal justice pipeline, prompting a formal review by the Office of the Director of Public Prosecutions (ODPP).

Upon examining the investigative file, the ODPP directed that the suspect face specific criminal counts. These include the intentional withholding of an erroneously delivered payment under Section 35 of the Computer Misuse and Cybercrimes Act, alongside a separate money laundering charge under Section 3(b)(1) read together with Section 16(1)(a) of the Proceeds of Crime and Money Laundering Act.

![shilling-money.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/shilling-money.jpg.webp?itok=IqRFAfEG)

Despite the gravity of the charges, investigators have not publicly disclosed the exact portion of the Ksh6.7 million that was allegedly dissipated, nor have they confirmed if any fraction of the cash has been recovered through asset tracing.

The DCI reiterated its statutory warning to the public that funds credited through administrative or electronic error do not constitute legal property of the recipient, and failing to return such deposits exposes individuals to severe penal consequences.

At the time of publication, the suspect remained in police custody undergoing administrative processing ahead of his formal arraignment.
