Isaac Otesa, widely known as Goliath wa Bungoma, is facing a sharp turn in public perception as his reliance on TikTok Live financial solicitations turns into an uncomfortable debate over creator monetization and true commercial conversion.

What began as a viral story of corporate onboarding and mentorship at car dealership Car Soko has hit friction following an unexpected online intervention by his former boss, Patrick Riang’a. During a recent TikTok Live session, Patrick directly joined the stream to express visible frustration at Goliath’s ongoing habit of asking viewers for cash assistance despite having received professional guidance and marketing opportunities at the dealership.

Patrick openly questioned why Goliath consistently chose to canvas online strangers for financial help rather than quietly reaching out privately when facing personal hardship, arguing that the public requests reflect poorly on the professional backing he was given.

The confrontation quickly spilled across digital platforms, prompting observers to interrogate the actual business return generated during Goliath’s high-profile association with Car Soko. While viral figures are routinely onboarded by brands to boost digital footprint, commentary surrounding the exchange highlights growing skepticism over whether social visibility translates into vehicle transactions.

![Bungoma Goliath// Instagram](https://i.ibb.co/QF01j9vh/ab84b0f4c57e.webp)

Social media commentator Made Man voiced that exact skepticism, questioning the direct financial link between Goliath’s online presence and dealership revenue.

"I doubt that any person ever bought a car at Car Soko coz of Goliath, Patrick just chose to be charitable," Made Man noted, framing the original deal as corporate goodwill rather than a high-converting marketing partnership.

That assessment was echoed by user Muli Travels, who emphasized how much Goliath's brand presence relied on executive backing.

"Patrick is disappointed in Goliath even by listening to him. But honestly there is no Goliath without Patrick, so definitely tall guy is no longer making sales at Car Soko," the user posted.

For other observers, the exchange pointed to a breakdown in creator decorum and personal financial management. Commentator Underrared Ninja observed that Patrick appeared exhausted from repeatedly encountering his former protege soliciting funds across live feeds.

"Patrick wa Car Soko amechoka na Goliath wa Bungoma. Every time ana scroll through TikTok huwa anapata Goliath wa Bungoma na bakuli kwa live begging people for money. He mentored and left him at Car Soko but jamaa sasa ana aibisha kampuni, imebidi aingie kwa hizo live ampashe," the user stated.

Another observer, Ngazekof, recalled watching Goliath market vehicles on site while expressing concern over his long-term financial stability.

"Nimeona iyo video ya Goliath wa Bungoma akimarket pale Car Soko na nikamhurumia sana. I hope he has saved enough by now juu he is going back to Bungoma very soon," Ngazekof wrote.

While some users argue that digital creators should not face automatic public censure for seeking financial support online, the dispute underscored the tension between brief viral attention and long-term career sustainability. Goliath’s current employment standing at Car Soko has not been independently established, leaving his next commercial moves uncertain as public scrutiny around his online fundraising continues.
