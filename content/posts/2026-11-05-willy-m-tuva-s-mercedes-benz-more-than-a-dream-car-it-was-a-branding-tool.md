For most, a dream car is a private trophy of professional success. For veteran broadcaster Willy M Tuva, his Mercedes-Benz became something more: a recurring character in Kenya’s media and music landscape.

In a recent reflection shared from England, the Mseto host pulled back the curtain on the origins of the vehicle, linking its acquisition not just to personal achievement, but to a specific moment in his career where his private life and professional influence intersected. The car, which he long coveted before finally owning, became a recognizable fixture after landing a lead role in a high-profile television advertisement for Ramogi Radio.

![Willy M Tuva // Instagram](https://i.ibb.co/Fk3f8yDd/2645780161bb.webp)

Beyond the corporate screen, the vehicle served as a cross-platform asset, reappearing in the music video for Dufla Diligon’s track, *Yes You Do*. For Tuva, the alignment was symbolic of his broader career philosophy. He views the car’s dual-purpose life—moving from the set of a broadcast ad to the aesthetic requirements of a music video—as an extension of his own commitment to nurturing talent.

Tuva, who has long been a fixture in the East African music scene, used the car’s history to pivot to his relationship with the artist. He credits himself with discovering and mentoring Dufla Diligon, positioning his own success alongside that of the musicians he has platformed over the years. By weaving the story of the car into the narrative of Dufla’s ascent, Tuva underscores a recurring theme in his career: that the resources he has built are often poured back into the ecosystem of the artists he manages.

His reflection also touched on the stark contrast between his early career days and his current position. Tuva recalled a time before his media prominence when he lacked the formal resources to learn to drive. During that period, he relied on observing other motorists while working as an employee, occasionally practicing in secret using his employer’s vehicle. The transition from those surreptitious lessons to owning the Mercedes-Benz—a car that would eventually gain public recognition through his own professional channels—serves as a tangible marker of his climb.

Sharing the story from abroad, Tuva framed the vehicle’s journey as  patience. While he focused on the aspirational aspect of his story, the underlying reality remains that for a media personality with his reach, the boundary between a personal asset and a professional brand tool is thin.

As he continues to document his time driving through England, Tuva’s account of the Mercedes serves as a reminder of how, in the world of Kenyan entertainment, the personal assets of a high-profile host often do as much heavy lifting for a brand as any traditional marketing campaign.
