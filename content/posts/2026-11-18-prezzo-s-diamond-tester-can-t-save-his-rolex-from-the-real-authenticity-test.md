When internet critics whispered 'Mabati' at his wrist candy, Kenyan rapper Prezzo didn't just log off. The veteran entertainer pulled out a diamond tester to prove the stones glittering on his timepiece possessed genuine carbon properties.

The public display was a classic pushback from an artist whose brand has long traded on high-end fashion and flamboyant living. Yet while the handheld electronic probe successfully registered the physical properties of the mounted stones, it left the broader horological question entirely untouched.

In the luxury watch world, testing positive for gemstone authenticity is a far cry from proving an entire timepiece is genuine. Horology specialists point out that authenticating a high-end brand like Rolex requires examining complex internal movements, specific serial and model numbers, and brand-standard construction details that no pocket diamond checker can evaluate.

Compounding the matter is the prevalence of aftermarket modifications. Luxury timepieces frequently receive aftermarket enhancements where diamonds and decorative stones are added long after the watch leaves the manufacturer's facility. Consequently, even verified diamonds do not confirm that the watch housing them is factory-original or an authentic Swiss piece.

The incident highlights the unique pressures public figures face in the digital era, where social media commentators routinely scrutinize designer accessories. For celebrities whose public identities are anchored in visible displays of wealth, a challenge to a designer piece can quickly escalate into a public relations test.

Prezzo’s demonstration effectively shut down chatter regarding the physical composition of his jewelry stones. Whether it settles the broader debate over his luxury hardware, however, remains strictly between the rapper and the watchmakers.
