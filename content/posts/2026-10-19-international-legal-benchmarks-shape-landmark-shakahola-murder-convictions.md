By drawing explicit parallels between domestic mass fatalities and international legal frameworks, the High Court has anchored Kenya’s prosecution of doomsday cult atrocities in global jurisprudence.

Justice Diana Kavedza’s ruling on Monday, October 19, found controversial preacher Paul Mackenzie and 29 co-accused guilty on all 191 counts of murder. In securing the convictions, the court utilized criteria from the Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court (ICC) and United Nations statutes to evaluate the mass deaths linked to the Good News International (GNI) Church in Kilifi County.

![Justice Diana Kavedza presiding over the case of the controversial preacher Paul McKenzie on Monday, October 19 at the Mombasa Law Courts.](https://i.ibb.co/3901H1pb/195dbc9f53fd.webp)

“I am satisfied that the prosecution has proved the case against all the 29 accused persons for the murder of 190 children, and one adult, contrary to Section 203 of the Penal Code,” Justice Kavedza stated, finding each accused individually and collectively liable under Section 323 of the Criminal Procedure Code.

The proceedings detailed how the GNI Church operated as an apocalyptic cult characterized by isolation, psychological manipulation, and absolute obedience. Followers were persuaded to relocate to the Shakahola wilderness under the premise that fasting would bring about the appearance of Jesus. The court established that starvation caused most fatalities, compounded by physical assaults, punches, kicks, and instances of strangulation and asphyxiation.

![Pastor Paul MacKenzie during a past trial in court.](https://i.ibb.co/4rdfsBB/c3dd68217ee5.webp)

Addressing evidentiary challenges during the trial, the court ruled that exhumations remained fully valid despite the absence of a forensic archaeologist on the multidisciplinary team. According to the bench, omission of that specific specialist did not compromise any essential forensic function required to maintain the integrity of the exercise. Evidence further revealed that Mackenzie systematically discouraged followers from seeking medical treatment, rejecting vaccination and antenatal care while depicting medical institutions as instruments of evil.

The court is scheduled to receive probation reports on October 30 ahead of mitigation proceedings. The formal sentencing hearings will commence on Monday, November 2, with the final sentence expected to be delivered on Friday, November 6.
