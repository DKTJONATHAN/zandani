Engineering crews face a narrow seasonal window to overhaul drainage systems along the busy James Gichuru Road Junction–Jomo Kenyatta International Airport corridor, as authorities scramble to avert the structural devastation that crippled parts of Nairobi during previous heavy downpours.

The intervention, detailed by the Kenya National Highways Authority (KeNHA) following a stakeholder forum with local leaders and residents from Imara Daima, Mukuru kwa Njenga, and Kware wards, centers on high-risk sectors prone to severe runoff accumulation. Embakasi South Member of Parliament Julius Mawathe joined local administrators at the briefing, where the agency outlined its immediate construction blueprint.

![A collage of sections of KeNHA-maintained road and a section of the Kisumu Busia Highway during maintenance works](https://i.ibb.co/LXGBzCZr/4649a49a1fd3.webp)

At the core of the project is the physical lining of the Imara Daima Canal alongside the installation of six box culverts positioned at strategic crossing points. These structures are designed to channel unmanaged stormwater safely toward the Ngong River crossing, addressing long-standing structural vulnerabilities along the A8 highway that have historically left adjacent settlements submerged.

Beyond flood mitigation, highway planners intend the civil works to protect the integrity of the transport corridor from pavement degradation caused by standing water, while introducing beautification and landscaping elements. However, executing heavy construction in densely populated residential and commercial zones requires tight coordination to prevent major gridlock and community friction, particularly with past flood seasons having left a legacy of displacement, property damage, and fatalities in the city's eastlands.

![An aerial view of Nairobi CBD and the Nairobi Expressway](https://i.ibb.co/jN3jQSF/657600ab78bc.webp)

The highway drainage overhaul runs parallel to broader municipal preparations, with Nairobi County establishing multi-agency teams spanning emergency response, shelter, and traffic management. 

Construction schedules remain tightly bound to the approaching October–December short rains, leaving little margin for project delays as engineers attempt to secure the vulnerable corridor before precipitation levels spike.
