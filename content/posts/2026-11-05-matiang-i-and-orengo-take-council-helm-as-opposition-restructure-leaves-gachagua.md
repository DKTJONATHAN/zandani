A decisive restructuring within the opposition's top leadership has consolidated power around Jubilee Party Leader Fred Matiang'i and Siaya Governor James Orengo, while pointedly locking Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP) leader Rigathi Gachagua out of executive council roles.

The leadership shift was announced on Thursday morning by Wiper Patriotic Front leader Kalonzo Musyoka upon his arrival at Kisumu International Airport, and later formalized in a statement by Executive Director Phillip Kisia. Under the new arrangement, Matiang'i assumes the chairmanship of the Ukombozi Alliance Council, with Orengo serving as co-chair.

![Side by side images of Jubilee Party Leader Fred Matiang'i and Siaya Governor James Orengo](https://i.ibb.co/gLsv0QQj/2160bfa088c7.webp)

The dual appointment resolves a prolonged leadership vacuum created when former President Uhuru Kenyatta stepped down from the council post. Kenyatta's resignation followed a court order enforcing statutory limits that bar retired presidents from holding political party leadership positions six months after leaving office.

While coalition insiders had previously floated Gachagua alongside Orengo as candidate co-chairs to replace Kenyatta, the final lineup bypasses the former Deputy President entirely. The move reflects deepening operational strain among key opposition figures as tensions escalate over the selection of a single presidential candidate for 2027.

Principals within the coalition, including Matiang'i and former Attorney General Justin Muturi, have publicly faulted Gachagua in recent weeks, accusing him of aggressive tactics and driving internal division. Gachagua has pushed back against the criticism, insisting that each principal must independently build regional support rather than expect a guaranteed nomination.

![Azimio Chairperson and former President Uhuru Kenyatta with Wiper party leader Kalonzo Musyoka during a past event](https://i.ibb.co/vvgfdc88/821b39539d39.webp)

By elevating Matiang'i and Orengo, the opposition leadership is attempting to stabilize its core decision-making body while steering the outfit toward a broader platform. Kalonzo signaled that the current realignment forms part of a wider effort to construct a collective front, termed the Super Alliance, designed to challenge President William Ruto's administration in the next general election.

The immediate test for the expanded council lies in navigating the competing presidential ambitions of its principals. With Matiang'i and Orengo now controlling the council's administrative machinery, the opposition faces the task of managing factional rivalries while attempting to formalize rules for its eventual ticket.
