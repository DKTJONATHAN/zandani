Kenyan businesses struggling with delayed client payments face a deeper liquidity pinch after the Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA) confirmed an extended waiting period for value-added tax refunds on qualifying bad debts.

Under regulatory updates tied to the Finance Act 2026, the statutory timeframe required before a business can apply for a VAT refund on an unpaid debt has been pushed from two years to three years. The shift forces firms that have already accounted for and remitted VAT on taxable supplies to shoulder the tax burden for an extra twelve months when customers default.

![Customers being attended to at KRA's offices](https://i.ibb.co/mFFv4DCM/8d46beb73c0f.webp)

To successfully secure a refund once the three-year window lapses, the tax authority is requiring rigid documentary proof. Businesses must maintain comprehensive records, including original invoices and verifiable evidence of active, documented recovery efforts made to collect the outstanding sums.

The extended bad debt window is part of a wider slate of VAT adjustments taking effect across multiple sectors. The new rules also target businesses whose taxable supplies transition to exempt status. Firms that previously deducted input VAT on unsold stock before a classification change must now account for that input tax directly in the return period when the exemption takes effect.

At the same time, the taxman has introduced narrow carve-outs in other categories. Employee-related overheads—such as salaries, wages, and statutory deductions incurred by a supplier—are now explicitly excluded when calculating the taxable value of qualifying outsourcing services. Similarly, hire-purchase finance charges are left out of the taxable value of goods provided the supplier holds a valid license under the Hire Purchase Act, while returning passengers enjoy an expanded VAT-free allowance raised from Ksh38,850 to Ksh259,000.

![An undated photo of business stalls selling clothes and jewellery along a busy road in Nairobi.](https://i.ibb.co/NgkVdsd1/c20a10f3018c.webp)

Digital payment platforms face tighter oversight as well, with transaction fees and commissions for aggregation, payment processing, gateways, and merchant acquiring subjected to standard-rate VAT.

With different statutory requirements now active across various commercial sectors, the tax authority has directed enterprises to audit their contracts, invoicing systems, and ledger classifications to prevent misreporting under the updated framework.
