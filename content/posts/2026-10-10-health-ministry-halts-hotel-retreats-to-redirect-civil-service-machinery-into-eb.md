Health Principal Secretary Mary Muthoni has placed an immediate freeze on all government-funded seminars, workshops, and conferences hosted in commercial hotels, moving to divert departmental bandwidth and containment logistics toward active Ebola surveillance.

The restriction, communicated through an internal circular to all directorates, divisions, and programme managers, benefits frontline biosecurity teams by halting non-essential inter-agency congregations while health authorities track movements linked to the country's first confirmed Bundibugyo virus infection.

Under the directive dated Wednesday, October 7, 2026, the State Department for Public Health revoked standard discretionary scheduling for offsite civil service workshops, establishing a centralised clearance threshold that requires accounting officers to petition the Principal Secretary’s office directly for any exceptional assembly.

![Interior Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen sanitising his hands ahead of cabinet meeting at State House in Nairobi on Friday, October 9, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/8DD7vtnF/1de26f6eddec.webp)

"Following confirmation of an imported case of Bundibugyo virus disease, the State Department is prioritising emergency preparedness, contact tracing and response measures," the internal instruction stated. "Accordingly, all meetings, seminars and conferences scheduled to be held in hotels are suspended with immediate effect from Wednesday, 7th October 2026, until further notice."

Speaking on Saturday, October 10, Muthoni indicated that the operational pause aligns with intensified physical observations across administrative hubs as technical teams expand containment protocols.

### Containment Over Routine Logistics

![Interior Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen sanitising his hands ahead of cabinet meeting at State House in Nairobi on Friday, October 9, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/hJbMCk6n/2637378b5671.webp)

The blanket suspension curtails a major channel of routine state expenditure—the hiring of hotel conference infrastructure for departmental reviews—reallocating managerial attention to tracing networks across the capital. Ministry units have been instructed to limit external interfaces and comply strictly with emergency biosecurity guidelines.

The administrative clampdown follows the October 6 confirmation of Kenya’s initial imported case of Bundibugyo Ebola virus disease, which culminated in the death of a patient at Nairobi Hospital.

The patient, a Kenyan national based in the Democratic Republic of Congo, had manifested symptoms roughly four weeks before travelling overland through Uganda and arriving in Nairobi on Saturday, October 3. Upon worsening clinical distress, the individual was transferred to Nairobi Hospital, placed in specialized isolation, and subsequently succumbed to the infection.

With the circular taking effect across all health divisions, the ministry's immediate operational focus rests on sustaining contact monitoring around the deceased patient's transit footprint and enforcing strict clearance conditions before any state personnel can reconvene in public hospitality venues.
