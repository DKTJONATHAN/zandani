Despite multi-billion-shilling national allocations flowing into Homa Bay County's infrastructure, local implementation in Karachuonyo constituency is hitting execution bottlenecks on key feeder roads, raising questions about government project supervision.

Karachuonyo parliamentary aspirant Steve Okute has raised specific concerns over delayed and defective road works, warning that poor craftsmanship by contractors threatens to undermine broader state investments across the constituency.

Speaking on the state of local infrastructure, Okute pointed directly to slow progress on the Adiedo–Karabok and Ojijo–Oteko roads as primary points of failure in the local transport network. On the Adiedo–Karabok stretch, he noted that incomplete drainage structures are already creating hazards for motorists and residents.

“Adiedo–Karabok is not yet completed. The contractor is doing a shoddy job, and even culverts are missing. The contractor should get to work. The same case applies to Ojijo–Oteko Road, which is taking too long to be completed,” Okute said, urging state enforcement agencies to step up field supervision.

The friction over rural feeder roads comes alongside high-profile state project handovers in the constituency. President William Ruto recently commissioned the Kendu Bay Modern Market, a facility designed to transition small-scale traders and local vendors out of informal, open-air setups into built commercial spaces.

In addition to the market facility, state infrastructure agencies have deployed resources toward transport and water reticulation in the constituency. The Kenya Rural Roads Authority (KeRRA) is managing the construction of the 10-kilometre Njeri-Oriwo-Border-Pala Road, intended to link agricultural producers directly to regional markets.

Concurrently, the Lake Victoria South Water Works Development Agency (LVSWWDA) is implementing the West Karachuonyo Last Mile Connectivity Water Project. The project forms part of a broader KSh2 billion water and sanitation expenditure across Homa Bay County, encompassing the conversion of water systems to solar power, the rehabilitation of concrete storage tanks, and line extensions to rural households.

Beyond monitoring active construction contracts, Okute outlined proposals aimed at reviving dormant local industries and organizing primary producers. Central to his local economic push is the revival of the Kendu Bay Cotton Ginnery, a legacy industrial asset designed to anchor the region's agricultural processing.

“Reviving the Kendu Bay Cotton Ginnery will empower Karachuonyo cotton farmers and restore our constituency’s pride as a hub for quality cotton,” Okute added.

For the constituency's fishing sector, Okute proposed forming a dedicated fishermen’s cooperative society. The structure is intended to give beach management units stronger commercial bargaining power, streamline payment settlements, and establish a unified platform to address recurring cross-border security disputes with Ugandan authorities on Lake Victoria.

As water agency teams and market administrators wrap up recent project deliveries, the timeline for the Adiedo–Karabok and Ojijo–Oteko roads remains anchored on whether state oversight bodies compel contractors to fulfill their engineering specifications.
