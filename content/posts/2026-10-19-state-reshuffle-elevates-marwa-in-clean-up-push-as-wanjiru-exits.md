A late-week batch of executive appointments has reconfigured leadership across several critical state agencies, headlined by the removal of Bishop Margaret Wanjiru from the Nairobi Rivers Commission in favour of career administrator Nelson Marwa.

The shake-up, published in a special Kenya Gazette notice dated Friday, October 16, signals an operational shift in how the administration intends to manage high-friction urban recovery and statutory oversight portfolios over the next three years.

By substituting Wanjiru with Marwa, President William Ruto replaces a prominent political figure with an administrator renowned for an uncompromising, direct enforcement style honed during tenures as Coast Regional Coordinator and Devolution Principal Secretary. The Nairobi Rivers Commission has faced mounting scrutiny over the pace of riparian reclaim work, waste interception, and relocation dynamics along the capital’s central water basins, turning the chairperson's seat into an intense operational hot seat.

Marwa takes over the commission on a fixed three-year term under the executive order revoking Wanjiru’s tenure, stepping immediately into inter-agency coordination with city authorities, environment regulators, and security apparatus.

### Curriculum and Standards Continuity

![President William Ruto chairing a cabinet meeting on Friday, October 9, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/8Dc8xD71/a318056a3dd7.webp)

While the river rehabilitation docket saw a leadership purge, other critical regulatory agencies were handed stability through renewals and substantive confirmations.

President Ruto reappointed Mount Kenya University founder Dr Simon Gicharu as Non-Executive Chairperson of the Kenya Institute of Curriculum Development (KICD) for an additional three-year term. Exercising powers under section 5 (2) (a) of the KICD Act, the Head of State anchored Gicharu at the helm of the curriculum agency with effect from October 16, preserving executive continuity as basic education navigates the advanced phases of the Competency-Based Curriculum rollout.

In the Ministry of Trade, Cabinet Secretary Lee Kinyanjui formalised the leadership of the Kenya Bureau of Standards (KEBS) by appointing Esther Njeri Ngari as Chief Executive Officer. The appointment solidifies operational oversight at the national quality regulator, which has spent recent years balancing anti-counterfeit crackdowns at point-of-entry ports with trade facilitation demands.

Across the Interior docket, Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen handed Nelson Dzuya a three-year extension as a member of the Public Benefit Organisations Regulatory Authority, reinforcing regulatory oversight of non-governmental groups under the operationalised PBO framework.

### University Councils Reordered

![Cabinet Secretaries attend a meeting chaired by President William Ruto at the State House, Nairobi, June 30, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/dFFVGk6/3ce7f101ca95.webp)

The ripple of Gazette notices extended deeply into higher education governance, where Education Cabinet Secretary Julius Ogamba restructured council composition at two established public universities.

Ogamba appointed governance scholar Professor Peter Wanyande as the Non-Executive Chairperson of the Council of the Jaramogi Oginga Odinga University of Science and Technology (JOOUST) for three years. Concurrently, the CS named Geoffrey Nafukho Omulayi to the Council of Moi University for an identical three-year period, inserting fresh oversight into an institution currently navigating protracted operational and financial consolidation challenges.

### AFCON Secretariat Mobilisation

The largest single institutional deployment in the notice came out of the Ministry of Sports and Youth Affairs, where Cabinet Secretary Salim Mvurya gazetted a 40-member Local Organizing Committee (LOC) to steer Kenya’s preparations for co-hosting the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations alongside Uganda and Tanzania.

The expansive committee will be steered by Antony Emmanuel Inzahuli Lung’aho as Head of Secretariat.

Rather than assembling an untested team, the sports docket drew the bulk of its 40 appointees from the advisory group that delivered the 2024 African Nations Championship (CHAN) regional joint-hosting mechanics. The committee now takes custody of infrastructure timelines, facility inspections, transport logistics, and Confederation of African Football liaison work, carrying statutory mandates that bind Kenya to the tripartite regional hosting calendar.
