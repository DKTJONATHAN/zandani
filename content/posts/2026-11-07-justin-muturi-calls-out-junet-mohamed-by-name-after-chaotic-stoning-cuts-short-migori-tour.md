Justin Muturi did not bother with diplomatic political codes when stones began bouncing off his motorcade in Migori. 

Rather than issuing a broad grievance against anonymous hecklers, the Democratic Party leader leveled a direct, personal accusation at Suna East MP Junet Mohamed, claiming the local lawmaker orchestrated the disruption that abruptly ended the opposition’s tour of the county on Thursday, November 5.

Muturi was traveling alongside Wiper boss Kalonzo Musyoka and DAP-Kenya leader Eugene Wamalwa as part of an opposition delegation testing their ground game across the Nyanza region. While multiple whistle-stops had gone off without disruption earlier in the day, the entry into Migori town dissolved into pandemonium when armed assailants pelted their convoy with rocks, triggering a rapid police intervention that dispersed the crowd with tear gas.

### The Direct Finger-Point at Junet Mohamed

For Muturi, the disruption in Suna East was not an organic flare-up of grassroots tension. In an unapologetic post-chaos assessment, the former Cabinet Secretary named the constituency’s ranking legislator as the author of the hostile reception.

“The attack on us in Migori Town by goons sent by Junet Mohammed is a blatant act of political intolerance and an assault on our democratic freedoms,” Muturi declared. “It is deeply disturbing that, instead of engaging in democratic competition, some political actors resort to violence and intimidation to silence opposing voices.”

Framing the ambush as “the politics of cowardice and desperation,” Muturi insisted the opposition team would not retreat from asserting their presence in rival strongholds.

“You may send goons to attack us, but you cannot intimidate the people of Kenya into surrendering their democratic freedom,” Muturi said. “We shall stand firm, speak boldly and continue fighting for a democratic Kenya.”

Muturi further pushed for accountability against those behind the chaos, arguing that organized violence must not be allowed to cement itself as an acceptable tool of political mobilization.

### Kalonzo Questions the Tear Gas ‘Script’

While Muturi zoomed in on the local backyard dynamics and named Junet directly, Kalonzo Musyoka aimed his frustration at the national security apparatus and President William Ruto’s administration.

Kalonzo challenged the rationale of the officers who unleashed tear gas into what he maintained was an otherwise orderly assembly of supporters welcoming the principals to the town center.

“This administration has lost it. Clearly the whole country has gone one down. We’ve seen it here, and everywhere people want to do away with this government,” Kalonzo said. 

The Wiper leader took direct aim at the dual onslaught of rocks from the streets and canisters from the police line, suggesting the chaos followed a pre-arranged sequence.

“The police used tear gas. Why? We had a very peaceful meeting. Why throw the tear gas? I think the script is to throw the tear gas and stones, right? It’s very shameful,” Kalonzo remarked.

The dramatic cutoff in Migori marked an abrupt end to an otherwise smooth multi-stop day across the Nyanza circuit for the trio. With Muturi lodging an explicit public charge against Junet and Kalonzo taking aim at State House, the fallout now shifts squarely to whether the named Suna East lawmaker and county authorities will address the accusations head-on.
