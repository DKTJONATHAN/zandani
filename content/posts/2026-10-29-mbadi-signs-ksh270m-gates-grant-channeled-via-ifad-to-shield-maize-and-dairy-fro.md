Treasury Cabinet Secretary John Mbadi announced on Thursday that Kenya has concluded a USD 2.08 million (Ksh270 million) financing pact with the Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation, routing the funds through multilateral channels to directly insulate smallholder food producers from recurring weather extremes.

The intervention is calibrated to support farmers anchored in vulnerable food supply chains—chiefly maize growers and dairy producers—whose output has contracted amid irregular rainfall, sudden dry spells, and broad climate volatility across Kenya’s primary agricultural zones.

According to the National Treasury, the grant is structured under the Climate Smart Agribusiness Partnerships for Resilience (CSAPR) framework and will be administered through the International Fund for Agricultural Development (IFAD), a long-term development financier in Kenya’s rural economy.

“CS Hon FCPA John Mbadi has signed a US$2.08 million Climate Smart Agribusiness Partnerships for Resilience (CSAPR) Grant Agreement funded by the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation, strengthening Kenya’s efforts to build climate resilience in agriculture,” the Treasury said in a statement.

### Channelling Data to Field Operations

Rather than serving as general budgetary support, the capital injection is earmarked to expand the state-backed Integrated Natural Resources Management Programme (INReMP). The programme pairs government counterpart funding with external resources to install Climate Information Systems across regional supply corridors.

The core objective is operational: equipping grassroots agribusinesses and farmers with localized, advance weather metrics. By incorporating real-time hydrological and meteorological data into daily farm management, producers can calibrate planting schedules, manage input applications, and hedge against post-harvest losses before severe weather events materialize.

The technical deployment addresses an acute vulnerability across the Greater Horn of Africa, where alternating El Niño and La Niña episodes have repeatedly upended seasonal projections. Unpredictable oscillations between severe heatwaves, prolonged drought, and flash flooding have routinely suppressed national grain reserves and constrained commercial milk yields.

### Expanding Philanthropic Co-Financing

The agricultural agreement deepens the Gates Foundation's active co-financing footprint across state departments and statutory research agencies in Kenya.

In January 2026, the philanthropic vehicle committed Ksh516 million to the Kenya Medical Research Institute (KEMRI) to anchor a multi-country African women’s health research programme, alongside ongoing maternal, infant, and immunization monitoring projects.

With the grant framework now executed at the Treasury, implementation responsibility shifts to the joint IFAD and INReMP technical committees, which are tasked with rolling out the digitized climate warning platforms across targeted county farming clusters.
