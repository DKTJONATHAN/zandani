Edwin Sifuna is not letting a tribunal challenge slow down his presidential rollout, taking to social media to brush off a petition targeting his political outfit and boldly claiming the legal bid to derail his launch has already collapsed.

Yet inside the corridors of the Political Parties Disputes Tribunal, advocate Danstan Omari has secured a December 1, 2026 hearing date, aiming to freeze the entire structural foundation of Sifuna’s newly unveiled Linda Mwananchi Movement (LMM).

At the centre of the clash is an aggrieved founder who claims the vehicle Sifuna intends to drive into State House was hijacked through a breakneck corporate overhaul conducted behind locked doors.

### The 26-Day Party Overhaul

Court papers filed by Emmanuel Willyngtone Khamala, a founding member of The Equitable Party (TEP), chart a dizzying paper trail that transformed an obscure political formation into Sifuna’s frontline banner in less than a month.

According to the filings, TEP was officially registered on October 4, 2026. Just four days later, on October 8, an application was lodged with regulators seeking to change the party's official identity.

By October 30, the entity re-emerged publicly as the Linda Mwananchi Movement during a high-profile rollout. That public launch came two full weeks before the party convened the November 13 National Delegates Conference (NDC) that was supposed to legally ratify the rebrand and install a fresh leadership slate.

Khamala, represented by Omari, argues that the sequence flipped standard party procedures upside down. He flagged formal objections through letters dated October 28 and November 6, but contends his warnings were met with silence as the rebranding train sped forward.

### Secret Venues and Locked Doors

In his urgent application, Khamala alleges that the November 13 conference was orchestrated to keep ordinary members in the dark until the ink was dry.

He claims delegates and registered members were not informed of the conference venue until proceedings had concluded, effectively barring any internal vetting of the new party officials or the name change.

Omari argues that executing major structural realignments without broad member participation violates constitutional baselines on fair administrative action and political rights, specifically citing Articles 10, 47, 50, and 90 of the Constitution alongside Sections 39, 40, and 41(2) of the Political Parties Act.

Khamala is asking the tribunal to certify the matter urgent and issue interim orders suspending all resolutions passed during the November 13 NDC, a move that would temporarily paralyse LMM’s official operations pending a full hearing.

### Sifuna Points the Finger at State House

Sifuna, whose political stock has surged as he pitches himself as Kenya’s sixth president, is treating the court battle as deliberate political interference rather than an internal administrative glitch.

Dismissing the legal challenge as baseless, the Nairobi Senator accused his detractors of acting as proxies for President William Ruto’s administration to sabotage his momentum ahead of the 2027 General Election.

In his online remarks, Sifuna projected complete unconcern, informing his followers that the petition seeking to overturn the NDC decisions was already dead in the water. He maintained that his roadmap to the ballot remains fully intact regardless of courtroom manoeuvres.

Whether Sifuna’s public confidence matches the tribunal’s assessment will be tested on December 1, when Omari formally steps before the bench to demand that the Linda Mwananchi machinery be halted at the starting line.
