A mechanical brake failure on a heavy commercial truck turned a midnight religious pilgrimage into a mass-casualty catastrophe on Saturday, claiming 17 lives on the spot at Salama in Makueni County along the Nairobi-Mombasa Highway.

The crash, which occurred shortly after midnight, underscores the persistent danger heavy freight vehicles pose to night-time passenger transit along Kenya's primary transport corridor.

According to preliminary accounts from the scene, the lorry lost braking control on an approach section of the highway, hurtling into multiple vehicles in its path. Among the impacted transport was a 14-seater matatu ferrying faithful travelling across counties to the Subukia National Marian Shrine in Nakuru County for scheduled weekend prayers.

![The mangled wreck of a 14-seater matatu involved in an accident at the Salama area along Nairobi-Momba highway on Saturday, October 3, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/8LJk79St/c75474e92b67.webp)

The force of the multi-vehicle collision tore through the commuter vehicle, shattering its superstructure and leaving seventeen occupants dead before emergency services could intervene. Seven surviving victims sustaining varied injuries were rescued from the wreckage and transferred to the Sultan Hamud Sub-County Hospital for emergency medical treatment.

The devastation at Salama represents the second critical freight-related disruption on the A109 within 48 hours. On Friday, an opposing-lane collision between two heavy haulage trailers across the Kapiti Plains in neighbouring Machakos County blocked both carriage lanes, locking transit traffic in a ten-hour standstill.

Saturday's fatal pileup triggered immediate gridlock across the Makueni stretch, forcing long-distance hauliers and morning passenger buses to divert onto unpaved feeder routes while emergency crews worked to clear the wreckage.

![A road sign accident](https://i.ibb.co/4ZRLBtPw/3832a2ad5153.webp)

The fatal collision arrives amid a steep deterioration in national road safety metrics. Fresh data from the National Transport and Safety Authority (NTSA) reveals that more than 2,100 people were killed on Kenyan roads during the first six months of 2026—an 11 per cent surge compared to the same period in 2025.

While the safety regulator has continually cited behavioral violations such as dangerous overtaking and speeding, mechanical unroadworthiness—particularly brake system degradation among commercial hauliers operating on steep arterial descents—remains a critical contributor to multi-vehicle pileups.

Police teams and disaster management personnel remained at the Salama site through Saturday morning to conclude physical accident investigations and restore normal traffic flow along the highway corridor.
