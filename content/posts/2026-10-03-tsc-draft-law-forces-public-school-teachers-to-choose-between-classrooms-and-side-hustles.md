Public school educators who balance classroom duties with external business ventures are facing a direct ultimatum under sweeping statutory proposals now moving through Parliament.

The Teachers Service Commission (Amendment) Bill, 2026, currently under consideration by the National Assembly, explicitly classifies secondary employment as professional misconduct. If enacted, the legislation will force teachers serving under the Teachers Service Commission (TSC) to choose between their state employment and outside commercial pursuits, effectively outlawing secondary income streams commonly known as side hustles.

Under the proposed legal framework, any educator found undertaking outside employment or running secondary business ventures will face formal disciplinary proceedings. The sanctions for non-compliance are severe, with the commission empowered to institute disciplinary action that can culminate in outright dismissal or permanent deregistration from the official teaching roll.

The push to eliminate dual employment forms part of a broader statutory overhaul aimed at tightening performance management, enforcing full dedication to the classroom, and safeguarding learner welfare. Beside the prohibition on secondary gainful employment, the bill significantly expands the statutory definition of teacher misconduct to address evolving digital and institutional infractions.

Under the expanded disciplinary list, educators can be cited for digital misconduct such as cyberbullying, alongside traditional administrative offences including chronic absenteeism, negligence, and the misuse of institutional resources. The bill also codifies strict sanctions for conduct directly affecting students, making it an actionable offence to expose learners to drugs or adult content, or to engage in sexual exploitation and inappropriate relationships with students.

The draft law also introduces strict financial and administrative measures during disciplinary investigations. Teachers charged with serious professional offences will be suspended on half-basic salary pending the final determination of their cases, creating immediate financial pressure during legal and administrative proceedings.

To balance the commission’s expanded enforcement authority, the proposed legislation includes a statutory safeguards mechanism. The bill mandates the establishment of a Teachers Service Review Committee, providing aggrieved educators with a formal legal avenue to appeal disciplinary decisions and contest administrative rulings.

If passed by the National Assembly, the amendments will alter working conditions for hundreds of thousands of public school teachers across Kenya, establishing a statutory standard that requires complete commercial exclusivity to the classroom.
