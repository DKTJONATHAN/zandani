Nascent alliance negotiations orchestrated by former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua have run into immediate friction over Nairobi County, where Embakasi East MP Babu Owino has drawn a hard line against proposals to surrender his gubernatorial ambitions in exchange for a coordinated ticket.

The pushback follows consultations at Gachagua’s Wamunyoro residence in Nyeri County on Tuesday, October 20, held shortly after the Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP) leader returned from the United States. While the session brought together Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna and figures linked to the Linda Mwananchi movement to sketch broader 2027 electoral cooperation, it surfaced early attempts to zone the capital’s high-stakes executive office.

Under the floating proposals, Babu was urged to shelve his City Hall campaign and instead contest the Nairobi Senate seat, freeing the gubernatorial contest to accommodate alternative alliance partners. Babu publicly dismissed the arrangement on Wednesday, declaring that his run for the capital’s top job remains non-negotiable.

“My unworthy opponents have given up and are now banking on fake news,” Babu said in a statement. “Nairobi has already been decided. The gubernatorial race is between Babu Owino and Paul Ongili.”

### The Wamunyoro Arithmetic

The tension highlights the logistical difficulty of assembling a unified opposition front across a metropolis with 2,415,310 registered voters. DCP Deputy Party Leader Cleophas Malala had earlier publicly urged Sifuna to build working ties with Gachagua, signalling an effort to merge Mt. Kenya and western-leaning voting blocs ahead of the next general election.

Yet translating national-level dialogue into county-level consensus requires painful seat-brokering. Gachagua’s camp already houses Embakasi North MP James Gakuya, a close confidant who has mounted his own campaign for the governorship. Accommodating both Gakuya’s base and Babu’s bid within a single coalition umbrella creates an immediate structural conflict that Senate or legislative swaps fail to resolve.

Babu pre-empted coalition-level vetoes on September 18, 2026, when he unveiled the Mwananchi Party as his independent vehicle for the 2027 election. That move insulated his candidacy from mainline party machinery and candidate-selection committees, granting him autonomy to resist zoning pacts drawn up behind closed doors.

### City Hall’s Crowded Field

Ticket management is not an isolated problem for the emergent Gachagua-allied formation. Incumbent Governor Johnson Sakaja is recalibrating his own defensive posture, amid persistent reports that he could defect from the ruling United Democratic Alliance to ODM as political ground shifts under the county executive.

At the same time, the field of declared aspirants continues to fragment the electorate across diverse constituencies. Former Nairobi County Chief Officer Geoffrey Mosiria has joined the fray, pulling figures like former Governor Mike Sonko and former Interior Cabinet Secretary Fred Matiang'i into informal strategy talks. Other contenders actively testing voter appetite include Kasarani MP Ronald Karauri, businesswoman Agnes Kagure, former Starehe MP Margaret Wanjiru, Makadara MP George Aladwa, and commentator Tony Gachoka.

This is the second time in 2026 that Babu has had to extinguish rumors of an impending strategic retreat. In January, the Embakasi East legislator moved to quash claims that he planned to exit the Nairobi race to seek a parliamentary seat in Kisumu County.

With voter registration and transfers altering the baseline numbers across Nairobi’s 17 constituencies and 85 wards, the viability of any regional alliance will hinge on whether coalition brokers can enforce discipline on ambitious principals—or watch competing allied tickets divide the capital's ballot.
