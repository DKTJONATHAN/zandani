Health Director General Patrick Amoth confirmed Kenya’s second Ebola Bundibugyo fatality on Friday evening, pointing to early institutional isolation as the firewall that has protected Nairobi from uncontrolled community spread.

The deceased, a 65-year-old woman admitted to an infectious disease unit in Nairobi, was the third confirmed patient in the country. Her death brings Kenya’s fatal outcomes to two out of nine documented infections, with the public health system benefiting from an uninterrupted epidemiological chain: every confirmed patient belongs to a single cluster of primary contacts rather than an unmapped community outbreak.

“According to the Health DG, all nine cases remain linked to a single cluster of known contacts, with no evidence of community transmission,” Amoth stated, confirming that containment rings established in early October have prevented the virus from jumping into public transit, residential blocks, or commercial centers.

### Quarantine Timeline and In-Ward Deterioration

![Health Director General Patrick Amoth addressing the press on Thursday, October 22, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/patrick-amoth-1.jpg.webp?itok=SdiZE6z2)

The patient was placed under monitored quarantine following the death of the index patient—a close relative who died on October 5, 2026. Surveillance teams recorded her initial symptoms eight days later, on October 13, transferring her immediately to an infectious disease unit for specialized treatment.

Her clinical deterioration over the following ten days underscores the volatile trajectory of the Bundibugyo strain, which can destabilize suddenly even under close medical observation. Amoth noted that the Ministry has opened direct consultations with the family to conduct a dignified burial that complies fully with specialized bio-hazard safety mandates.

“Trained teams will conduct the burial with respect for the family's wishes and beliefs while protecting the family and community from further possible infection,” Amoth said, noting that handling remains without specialized barrier equipment presents one of the highest transmission risks during filovirus outbreaks.

### Dual-Facility Isolation for Surviving Caseload

![Red Cross Society workers wearing personal protective equipment gathered after handling the coffin of a man who died in Ebola.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/ebola-1.jpg.webp?itok=bmOek4sI)

Kenya’s surviving Ebola caseload now stands at seven patients, all of whom are receiving supportive therapy divided between two designated isolation facilities in Nairobi. Distributing the cluster across two centres allows infectious disease clinicians to balance barrier nursing ratios, maintain negative-pressure unit standards, and prevent operational bottlenecks in individual wards.

Public health teams continue to enforce bio-security measures to ensure that medical workers within both facilities avoid secondary exposure during continuous fluid management and patient monitoring.

### Surveillance and Public Reporting Channels

To preserve the barrier that has held transmission to zero outside the initial household ring, the Ministry of Health has cautioned the public against handling bodily fluids or preparing bodies of individuals who succumb to unexplained illnesses without medical clearance.

Surveillance teams have advised anyone experiencing fever, weakness, muscle aches, vomiting, diarrhoea, or unexplained bleeding to seek immediate triage. Emergency reporting has been directed through national hotlines, with citizens asked to report suspected symptoms via the toll-free line 719, USSD code \*719\#, or the Ministry’s designated WhatsApp service on 0700 719 719 as observation of the remaining quarantined contacts continues.
