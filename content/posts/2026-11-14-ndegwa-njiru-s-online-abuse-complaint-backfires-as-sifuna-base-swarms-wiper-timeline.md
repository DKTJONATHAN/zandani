Ndegwa Njiru tried to blow the whistle on coordinated political trolling, but the timeline had other plans.

When the Wiper Patriotic Front spokesperson took to X on Friday, November 13, to accuse an unnamed presidential aspirant of unleashing digital operatives to insult rival candidates, he intended to frame his complaint around political discourse ahead of the 2027 General Election. Instead, the anonymous subtweet served as an invitation for supporters of Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna to step into the targeted slot, claim the online heat, and turn the replies into a critique of Kalonzo Musyoka’s political posture.

Njiru’s statement warned that systematic online abuse was degrading discourse and heightening early campaign tensions across the country. Yet by withholding the specific name of the leader he was targeting, he left a vacuum that Sifuna’s vocal online ecosystem quickly filled.

Within hours of the post, Sifuna loyalists flooded Njiru’s timeline, rejecting the suggestion that their candidate was pulling strings behind the scenes while openly defending their right to challenge Wiper.

### The Digital Counter-Offensive

Commentator Benji Ndolo pushed back directly against Njiru's insinuation of central command, arguing that Sifuna was not orchestrating his online supporters.

“Sifuna has not directed anyone to do anything,” Ndolo wrote. “In fact he is busy doing his job and his own things and it’s self-propelling teams that make decisions often that he may not agree with but we move.” Ndolo pointed back to recent public appearances to argue Sifuna’s role in managing crowd energy rather than inciting it, referencing his effort to restore calm at a recent political gathering.

Other supporters took a sharper line, leaning directly into the 2027 rivalry between Sifuna and Kalonzo. A user operating under the handle Mtu wa Sifuna framed the senator as an unstoppable political force, writing: “Wakili, sema wafuasi wa Rais wa awamu ya sita, H.E Sifuna. We are very happy and proud of our presidential candidate. No one has stopped you to praise, abuse or indeed whatever you want with your preferred presidential candidate.”

### Mocking the Runway

The most pointed responses targeted Wiper’s persistent frustration with online criticism, questioning why Kalonzo’s camp was appealing for decorum instead of matching the digital energy.

User Bestylionaire challenged Wiper to hit back on the same terms: “Why not ask your Kalonzo to unleash the same energy on us? Or is online political aggression only a concern when your side is on the receiving end?”

Another commenter, Carlmaury, traced the online friction to the broader shift in opposition politics following Raila Odinga's exit from everyday coalition dynamics. “When Raila left the opposition lane open, Kalonzo had a whole runway and refused to take off,” Carlmaury posted. “Now Sifuna has done the hard work and gained traction; suddenly you’re demanding he hand over the steering wheel.”

### Brewing Opposition Friction

The exchange reflects deepening fault lines between the camps of two leaders jockeying for dominance within the opposition ahead of 2027. Friction between Kalonzo and Sifuna previously boiled over into the public domain on October 31 during the secretariat launch of the Linda Mwananchi Movement.

At that event, Kalonzo made a point of publicly clarifying that while Sifuna held a leadership role within the party, he was not its presidential candidate—a remark that immediately sparked angry reactions from Sifuna’s supporters online.

That tension comes as Sifuna’s party, formerly The Equitable Party (TEP), recently concluded a National Delegates Council meeting to formalize its rebrand into the Linda Mwananchi Movement. With other heavyweights like former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua of the Democratic Change Party, former Interior Cabinet Secretary Fred Matiang’i of Jubilee, and People’s Liberation Party leader Martha Karua also positioning themselves in the opposition space, Wiper's attempt to police online etiquette highlights how social media timelines have become the primary battleground for 2027 supremacy.
