Babu Owino has pulled back the curtain on the quiet power struggle that ruptured his high-profile political partnership with Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna, revealing that an initial pact to split State House ambitions and party machinery unraveled the moment they walked out of the Orange Democratic Movement.

Speaking in an interview on Friday, November 13, 2026, the Embakasi East legislator described an unwritten understanding forged when the two vocal opposition figures mapped out their political survival outside ODM. Under that original roadmap, Sifuna was designated to run as the presidential candidate, while Babu was positioned to hold the reins as party leader.

According to Babu, that balance was designed to let both ambitious politicians expand their personal influence without getting in each other's way. But the arrangement reportedly evaporated as soon as their departure from the Orange party became a reality.

“Initially, we had agreed with Sifuna and other members that the moment we get the party out, Sifuna was to be the presidential candidate and I was to be the party leader, but things changed. I also don’t know how things changed,” Babu said.

### The Battle for the Driver’s Seat

The friction, as Babu framed it, stemmed from an internal push by allies who argued that a presidential contender could not afford to outsource the party leadership to someone else. For the Embakasi East lawmaker, that sudden shift threatened to turn a mutual partnership into a single-man show.

“Some were saying that a presidential candidate cannot fail to be a party leader,” Babu explained during the Friday broadcast. “But we said that we needed to both of us grow. Both of us had to grow.”

The standoff effectively ended any prospect of the two operating under a single political roof. Rather than yielding control of the internal party machinery, Babu walked away from the nascent Linda Mwananchi Movement, an exit that set off months of speculation about bad blood between Nairobi's two most recognizable young opposition voices.

Following that quiet exit, Babu made his move on the Muungano Party, rapidly rebranding it into the Mwananchi Party and installing himself as its undisputed leader.

### The Parallel Scramble for Party Vehicles

While Babu secured his own outfit, Sifuna spent recent months attempting to build his own platform from scratch. The Nairobi Senator initially attempted to register the Linda Mwananchi Movement as an independent political party through the Office of the Registrar of Political Parties (ORPP), but ran into bureaucratic bottlenecks.

Sifuna subsequently pivoted, acquiring The Equitable Party (TEP) as an alternative vessel. That operational maneuver crossed the finish line on Friday, November 13, when TEP officially completed its rebranding into the Linda Mwananchi Movement party, giving the senator his own formalized base heading into the next general election.

Both lawmakers now find themselves heading entirely distinct political machines after failing to make a dual-leadership model hold together.

### The Gachagua Precedent

Defending his refusal to play second fiddle or lean on an outfit he does not directly control, Babu pointed to the bitter lesson provided by former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua's impeachment.

He argued that Gachagua's political downfall served as an unmistakable warning to ambitious politicians against operating inside parties where they do not hold institutional ownership. The former deputy president, Babu noted, had openly conceded that holding the deed to his own political formation would have shielded him from being isolated and ousted by shifting parliamentary coalitions.

For Babu, carving out the Mwananchi Party was less about abandoning a friend and more about political self-defense—ensuring that when alliances splinter in Nairobi, he holds the keys to his own door.
