Nairobi’s transport network absorbed another severe blow on Friday morning as a double-lorry crash near the Kibra Bridge crippled movement along the Southern Bypass, exposing the fragile reliability of the capital's heavy-transit corridors.

The collision, reported around 6:15 am, left both commercial vehicles overturned and completely obstructing the main carriageway. Transported payloads—bags of maize on one vehicle, and sand and soil on the other—spilled across the lanes as traffic quickly backed up behind the wreckage. A third stuck truck compounded the bottleneck, sealing off access points for motorists attempting to navigate the highway.

![Traffic builds up along Nairobi’s Southern Bypass following a crash involving two lorries, October 16, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/8LCxgzkD/b17fd42927f0.webp)

The morning disruption was part of a punishing week for the city's road network. It followed a separate traffic paralysis on Thursday, October 15, which had already choked outbound lanes heading toward the Central Business District along the same bypass. That incident preceded a fatal crash near Jomo Kenyatta International Airport involving an overturned City Hoppa bus that left three dead and several injured.

Emergency responders converged on the Kibra Bridge scene as vehicular queues stretched backward, prompting urgent diversion advisories from traffic monitors. Motorists travelling from Langata Road toward Mombasa Road were redirected past Nyayo Stadium, while drivers inbound from Mai Mahiu found their routes entirely blocked.

![Screengrabs showing two lorries that overturned on Nairobi’s Southern Bypass, one carrying bags of maize and the other transporting sand and soil, October 16, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/7tt91R5d/095cb54dfe4c.webp)

By midday, authorities had not established the root cause of the collision, nor was immediate data released concerning injuries or the medical status of the drivers involved. The sequence of successive disruptions leaves the corridor's commercial transporters facing recurring bottlenecks with limited alternative diversion capacity.
