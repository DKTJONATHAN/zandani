Kenya Railways has initiated the procurement of specialized technical oversight to anchor the planned electrification and overhaul of Nairobi’s Ksh65 billion commuter rail network, moving the capital’s mass transit transition from high-level policy declarations toward project-level engineering.

The state rail corporation confirmed Tuesday, October 6, that it has applied for financing from the World Bank under the Kenya Urban Mobility Improvement Project (KUMIP), earmarking a portion of the institutional credit to enlist four key external specialists for a two-year tour of duty beginning December 2026.

The deployment seeks to bridge specialized technical gaps in traction power and automated network control as the agency prepares to retire its heavy reliance on diesel-powered locomotives across the Nairobi Metropolitan Area.

### The Four-Pillar Engineering Roster

![An image of railway workers conducting maintenance on a railway line.](https://i.ibb.co/Z6vq6kcD/304422b89669.webp)

The procurement call isolates four distinct technical disciplines required to reconfigure the metropolitan line for high-capacity electric rolling stock:

* **Railway Electrical Systems Expert:** Tasked with the core electrical engineering architecture, focusing on the overhead or track-level power transmission systems, substations, and energy delivery networks needed to energize the passenger fleet.
* **Signalling and Telecommunication Engineering Expert:** Assigned to design and integrate automated train protection, trackside telecommunications, and signaling interfaces required to handle higher train frequencies safely.
* **Rail Civil Engineering Expert:** Responsible for track geometry overhauls, structural integrity assessments of bridges, track realignment, and civil works capable of supporting faster, electric rolling stock.
* **Transit-Oriented Development (TOD) Expert:** Focused on the urban-planning perimeter, guiding the commercial and physical design around designated stations to integrate railway facilities with local foot traffic, commerce, and feeding transit lines.

Applicants have been directed to provide detailed technical profiles, verified academic credentials, and track records from comparable railway engineering assignments, with contracts structured to run for approximately 24 months.

### Re-Engineering the Metropolitan Commuter Belt

![An artistic impression of a complete Nairobi Railway City project.](https://i.ibb.co/Nd1qVVnq/1722c9725c8f.webp)

The consultancy procurement arrives three months after the government formalized its intent under KUMIP to bring electric trains to Nairobi's commuter corridors. Shifting the network to electric traction is targeted at reducing commuter transit times, expanding hourly passenger intake, and curbing urban carbon emissions generated by older diesel engines.

Beyond technical track hardware, the scheme establishes a wide geographical footprint designed to decongest vehicular bottlenecks across the capital's commuter shed. The project footprint cuts across four peri-urban counties: Kiambu, Machakos, Kajiado, and Murang’a.

Target passenger nodes along the electrified corridors include Ruiru, Thika, and Kikuyu to the north and west; Ngong, Kitengela, and Syokimau along the southern commuter arc; and dense internal city hubs spanning Embakasi, Karen, and Dagoretti.

To prevent modernized stations from operating as isolated islands, the scope of works under KUMIP pairs trackside electrification with last-mile feeder links. This includes the engineering of designated station access feeder roads and dedicated multimodal interchange hubs where commuters can transfer directly between rail coaches and road-based public transport.

With candidate applications now open, Kenya Railways faces a narrow two-month administrative window to evaluate, award, and clear the consultancy contracts before the December 2026 service start date.
