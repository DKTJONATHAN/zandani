To unlock centuries of unrecorded communal ownership, the Ministry of Lands has structured a Ksh 6.37 million unit expenditure model for each community land block as part of a proposed Ksh 2.22 billion five-year master plan.

The *Draft Community Land Mass Registration Master Plan 2026–2031*, jointly developed with the National Land Commission (NLC), aims to register 348 community lands. The ambitious schedule directly addresses a decade-long institutional delay that has seen only 64 community titles processed since the enactment of the Community Land Act in 2016.

### Budget Distribution and Annual Rollout Targets

Under the draft framework, the government has mapped out a phased progression to systematically expand registered acreage. The ministry intends to register 50 community lands in 2026, scaling up operational output annually until reaching 83 registrations in 2030.

![A photo of the Ministry of Lands headquarters in Nairobi](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/a_photo_of_the_ministry_of_lands_headquarters_in_nairobi.jpg.webp?itok=-1yiBtVj)

The proposed unit spending of Ksh 6.37 million per community is structured to cover the entire administrative chain: community sensitization, structural governance setup, boundary delimitation, ground surveying, and final title deed issuance.

### Digital Tools and Alternative Dispute Mechanisms

To bypass protracted court battles over boundaries and community membership—a primary catalyst for past delays—the strategy relies on non-judicial dispute resolution. The Ministry and NLC plan to deploy alternative dispute resolution (ADR) panels during early boundary identification rounds.

On the technical front, surveying efficiency will depend on satellite imagery and digital mapping tools rather than manual boundary sweeps. The Ministry plans to train local residents to assist field surveyors, reducing administrative costs and improving local verification accuracy.

### Public Amenity Excision and ArdhiSasa Integration

Before any community title is registered, the draft master plan enforces strict boundary separation for public infrastructure inside communal tracts. Schools, health centers, public transit corridors, and municipal markets located within targeted community lands will be identified and registered under separate public titles.

![A sample of title deeds at a real estate firm](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/a%20sample%20of%20title%20deeds%20at%20a%20real%20estate%20firm.jpg.webp?itok=IamkESyN)

Once boundaries are verified and public assets carved out, surveyed plots will be logged into the national digital land registry, ArdhiSasa, which serves as the final gateway before community title deeds are generated and issued.

### A Decade of Slow Progress

The master plan highlights how slow community land formalization has been over the past decade. Out of the 64 titles registered so far, 48 were converted from former group ranches while 16 were transitioned from former trust lands.

These initial titles remain concentrated in nine counties: Samburu, Laikipia, West Pokot, Turkana, Taita Taveta, Baringo, Kajiado, Garissa, and Tana River.

The Ministry of Lands has opened the draft master plan to public participation, setting an October 8, 2026 deadline for stakeholders and affected communities to submit feedback before the operational guidelines are finalized for implementation.
