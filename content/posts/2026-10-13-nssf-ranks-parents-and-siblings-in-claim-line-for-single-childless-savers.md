The National Social Security Fund (NSSF) has issued formal guidelines establishing that surviving parents and siblings serve as the primary legal beneficiaries of retirement savings when a contributing worker dies single and without children.

The clarification shields accrued workplace deductions from lapsing into institutional limbo, assuring contributors that statutory savings remain collectible even in the absence of a conventional nuclear household. Under standard fund operations, a deceased member’s balance is released directly to a surviving spouse or to adult children aged 18 and older. Where neither exists, the fund routes the entitlement directly to the next tiers in the domestic lineage.

"The husband or the wife of the member qualifies first, then all the children, regardless of their age or gender, where the spouse of the deceased is also late," the Fund stated. "The parents of the deceased qualify if they had no spouse or children. Brothers or sisters, where the deceased was single, had no children and both parents are deceased."

### The Administrative Succession Ladder

![NSSF building](https://i.ibb.co/cKGsccg4/bedec74b79d0.webp)

Under the tiered mechanism outlined by the agency, surviving parents take first priority once it is established that the deceased contributor left neither a matrimonial partner nor biological or adopted offspring. Both parents are entitled to access and claim the balances held in the member's account.

Should both parents also be deceased, the transmission line shifts downward horizontally to the member’s biological brothers and sisters, who become the automatic lawful claimants. 

Where dependants are minors without adult representation, the fund permits legally recognized guardians to file claims on their behalf. Beyond the immediate bloodline, the fund provides an institutional mechanism for non-dependant relatives or executors through the courts: an applicant holding formally extracted Letters of Administration can claim the savings once all statutory dependant tiers have been fully exhausted.

### Evidentiary Gates at County Hall

![NSSF](https://i.ibb.co/CKShwkMw/cdd7fbb86dea.webp)

Access to the funds remains strictly conditional on surviving kin meeting several evidentiary checks designed to weed out contested or fraudulent claims. 

Claimants are required to lodge an original death certificate alongside a national identity card photocopy of the late member, accompanied by their own official identification documents—either a national ID card or a valid passport. Applicants must also present the contributor's registered NSSF membership number to locate the account.

Beyond civil registration papers, the state fund has placed an administrative verification checkpoint at the grassroots level: claimants must secure a formal introductory letter from the County Administration confirming the deceased member’s genuine family tree and listing all known dependants.

Once claimants produce the county letter and primary identity papers, NSSF issues official claim dockets to guide the remaining clearance steps. The fund stated that it maintains a 15-working-day processing timeline to disburse the benefits once an application packet satisfies all verification tests.
