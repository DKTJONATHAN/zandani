A scheduled convoy of 51 commercial vessels began berthing at Kenya’s coastal terminals this week, delivering critical energy reserves and industrial manufacturing materials through a tightly compressed 14-day maritime pipeline.

The Kenya Ports Authority (KPA) shipping manifest, covering October 19 through October 31, commits berths across Mombasa and Lamu to six specialized energy carriers alongside bulk carriers hauling cement clinker, steel coils, and food staples. The arrivals arrive at a sensitive juncture for national supply chains, following warnings from the Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority (EPRA) regarding tight domestic product buffers after regional maritime security incidents.

### Hydrocarbon Offloading Sequence

The petroleum component of the schedule focuses exclusively on the Port of Mombasa, where specialized berths are managing a sequential discharge of transport fuels, domestic gas components, and heavy lubricants.

![A collage of the MV Baltimore Express, a massive 369-meter container ship operated by German shipping line Hapag-Lloyd, pulled into Lamu port on May 10 from Oman's Salalah Port.](https://i.ibb.co/3yYxcNdr/28ca26c48d50.webp)

According to KPA’s operational stowage plan, the rotation opened on October 19 with a tanker carrying butane—a primary blending feedstock required for domestic Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG) cylinder refilling and portable stove fuel. A carrier hauling V-Power premium motor fuel was scheduled to dock on October 20.

Midweek operations center on productive sector fuels. On October 21, the port scheduled simultaneous handling for a tanker delivering base oil, the foundational compound used by domestic blending plants to manufacture automotive and machinery lubricants, alongside a vessel hauling gasoil destined for heavy agricultural machinery and transport fleets. The energy sequence concludes on October 29 with an inbound carrier carrying industrial palm oil.

### Industrial Feedstocks and Grain Traffic

Beyond refined petroleum products, the coastal shipping manifest reveals heavy resource allocation toward key construction and manufacturing inputs intended to maintain manufacturing continuity.

Mombasa is slated to receive two consignments of clinker, the essential raw material for Portland cement grinding, on October 22, with one shipment arriving as bulk cargo to supply coastal and upcountry processing plants. Bulk steel coils, intended for domestic roofing and structural fabrication lines, are scheduled for discharge on October 26.

Food security reserves form another segment of the arrivals, with a bulk wheat carrier booked to enter Mombasa harbor on October 25. The port will also handle automotive logistics at month-end, clearing two dedicated roll-on/roll-off car carriers on October 29 and October 31.

### Split Berthing Between Mombasa and Lamu

The logistical workload remains heavily weighted toward Mombasa, which is designated to process 41 of the 51 inbound vessels. The island port's breakdown includes 26 container ships, seven conventional general cargo vessels, the six energy tankers, and the two motor vehicle carriers.

The Port of Lamu will absorb the remaining 10 ships, reflecting its ongoing positioning as an alternative transshipment and deep-water handling node. Lamu's projected calls consist of nine container liners and a single general cargo vessel over the two-week window.

The scheduled offloading operations provide immediate logistical relief to the domestic supply chain following heightened anxiety in the energy sector. EPRA had cautioned the market last week regarding national stock positions just before reports surfaced that two Kenya-bound petroleum tankers faced drone attacks in the Strait of Hormuz.

While ongoing diplomatic discussions between Washington and Tehran over maritime corridors in the Persian Gulf have eased market volatility, the timely docking and clearance of the October manifest at Mombasa remains the primary operational buffer ensuring domestic filling plants, milling facilities, and transport networks maintain uninterrupted inventory.
