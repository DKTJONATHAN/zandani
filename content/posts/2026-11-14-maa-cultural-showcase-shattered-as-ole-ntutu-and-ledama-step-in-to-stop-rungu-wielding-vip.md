Governor Patrick Ole Ntutu and Narok Senator Ledama Olekina found themselves forced into an emergency peace-making role on Saturday, November 14, 2026, when the Maa Cultural and Tourism Festival at Sekenani Gate descended into a chaotic altercation directly in front of the VIP dais.

What was billed as a premier showcase of heritage and tourism quickly broke down as rival groups of supporters clashed near the main arena. The violence erupted just as President William Ruto was arriving at the venue, transforming the front of the main platform into a scene of scramble and disarray.

### Flying Chairs and Fleeing Media Crews

The confrontation stemmed from tensions between factions reportedly aligned with Narok West Member of Parliament Gabriel Tongoyo and Governor Ole Ntutu. As arguments escalated, plastic chairs were weaponized and hurled across the grounds, leaving broken seating scattered across the grass near the front rows.

Visuals from Sekenani Gate showed several attendees brandishing traditional Maasai rungus amidst the commotion. The sudden surge of violence forced journalists and broadcast crews stationed near the VIP podium to rapidly pick up their cameras and transmission equipment to avoid the flying debris and rising crowd pressure.

### Leaders Take the Microphone

With the event brought to a complete standstill, Governor Ole Ntutu and Senator Ledama Olekina stepped forward to restore order. The two leaders took to the stage to publicly appeal for calm, urging the aggressive factions to cease throwing chairs and stand down.

Their joint intervention managed to cool tensions across the venue, allowing the rival groups to separate, clear the walkways, and allow organizers to reset the main program so the festival could proceed.

### Security Cordon Strengthened

The disruption occurred precisely as President Ruto approached the Sekenani Gate arena. While it remained unconfirmed whether the Head of State had already taken his seat on the dais when the chairs began flying, presidential security detail acted immediately.

Officers from the Presidential Escort Unit (PEU) and the General Service Unit (GSU) moved in swiftly to ring the main platform. Heavily armed GSU personnel formed an all-round protective cordon around the President, maintaining a close watch over the crowd for the remainder of the cultural proceedings.

### Heightened Scrutiny Following Past Breaches

The swift tactical response at Sekenani Gate comes against the backdrop of heightened security protocol around presidential functions. In May 2026, a high-profile breach occurred in Ganze, Kilifi County, when an intruder bypassed security lines and climbed onto the dais near President Ruto.

That Kilifi episode was characterized by Inspector General of Police Douglas Kanja as a major security failure, prompting a structural overhaul of the Presidential Escort Unit. The command structure saw Recce Sub-Unit commander William Sawe replace long-serving commandant Noah Kirwa Maiyo, alongside a reshuffle of the unit's deputy leadership.

As the festival resumed its program under dense security surveillance, the National Police Service had not issued an official report regarding injuries or arrests stemming from the Sekenani Gate disturbance.
