When Deborah Chepkisich Lotodo walked across the graduation stage at the Catholic University of East Africa (CUEA) on October 23, 2026, to receive her doctorate at age 78, she capped an educational trail that spanned nearly seven decades, seven academic institutions, and multiple career shifts across North Rift and Central Kenya.

Her academic trajectory illustrates the non-linear path many adult learners navigate in Kenya, where financial constraints and family responsibilities routinely interrupt formal schooling—requiring repeated re-entry points through vocational colleges, distance examinations, and mid-life degree programs.

### Early Interruption and High School Disruption

Born fourth in a family of 14 in the Mt Elgon region, Lotodo spent her early childhood herding livestock and assisting with domestic responsibilities. She did not enter a classroom until age 11, enrolling after a local administrative chief conducted a community sweep directing families to send children to primary school.

![An image of Deborah Chepkisich Lotodo PhD Graduate at the Catholic University of East Africa, class of 2026](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/lotodo-october.jpg.webp?itok=KuFi2kPq)

After completing her primary education, she earned a placement at Kapsabet Girls High School. Financial shortfalls forced her to drop out before finishing secondary school, after which she married and started a family.

Her secondary education remained stalled until after the birth of her third child, when she registered independently for her Form Four examinations to secure her exit credentials.

### Vocational Re-Entry and West Pokot School Founding

Lotodo’s technical education began with an admission to Ortum Nursing School, though that program was also cut short. She pivoted to teacher training, securing a slot at Mosoriot Teacher College, where she completed her certificate and joined the public teaching workforce.

Posted to West Pokot, Lotodo taught in local classrooms before establishing a primary school in the area. What began as a modest community learning centre expanded into a full primary institution serving children across the rural locality.

Alongside her teaching duties, she expanded into practical technical fields, acquiring an A+ certification in information technology.

### The Post-Bereavement Ladder

Following the death of her husband, Lotodo returned to formal higher education to specialize in mental health. She selected psychological counselling, structuring her qualifications sequentially:

* **Certificate Level:** Initial foundation studies in counselling protocols.
* **Diploma Level:** Advanced practical coursework in psychological support.
* **Bachelor’s Degree:** Degree conferment at Tangaza University College.
* **Master’s Degree:** Postgraduate studies at Mount Kenya University.

That multi-stage progression positioned her for doctoral admissions at CUEA, culminating in her October 23 doctoral graduation.

Lotodo's completed PhD stands as a detailed record of lifelong learning in Kenya's education system, proving that foundational disruptions in early youth can be systematically resolved through adult re-entry frameworks.
