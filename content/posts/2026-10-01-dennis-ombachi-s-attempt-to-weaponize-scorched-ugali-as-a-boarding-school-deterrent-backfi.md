Dennis Ombachi has built a massive digital following on the back of his ultra-precise, high-energy culinary creations. But when the content creator and former rugby star tried to apply those same kitchen skills to a highly specific parenting intervention, the results did not go according to his recipe.

Faced with his young son's persistent requests to attend boarding school, Ombachi decided to mount a preventative strike using the stove. The plan was simple: recreate the famously dreaded, scorched, and bitter boarding school meals of the millennial generation to convince the boy that the boarding experience is a culinary prison.

Instead, the experiment backfired. After tasting the deliberately ruined meal of burnt ugali and bitter, overcooked sukuma wiki, Ombachi's son unexpectedly declared the food "good"—neutralizing the chef's attempt at culinary reverse-psychology.

"Transferring generational trauma, hoping fear succeeds where parenting failed," Ombachi noted of his failed strategy.

The comedic experiment was captured in an Instagram reel filmed on his signature high-rise balcony overlooking Nairobi. Stepping away from his usual gourmet repertoire, Ombachi approached the preparation of the sub-par meal with the mock seriousness of a Michelin-starred chef.

To replicate the bitter greens that defined many a Kenyan high school student's teenage years, Ombachi roughly chopped sukuma wiki, threw it into a hot pot, and purposefully allowed the bottom layer to scorch. Rather than throwing out the ruined batch, he deadpanned that the resulting char offered an "authentic smoky vegan barbecue flavor."

He went on to sum up the survivalist cooking philosophy of boarding school kitchens: "Wash, cook and clean at the same time, in the same pot, using the same water."

The ugali received similarly deliberate mistreatment. Ombachi dumped maize meal into boiling water and stirred it with aggressive speed, explaining that the ultimate goal of high school cooking was to simultaneously overcook and undercook the staple. He described the final product as "a culinary contradiction perfected by high school chefs."

While the food failed to scare his son, the parody struck a deep chord with Ombachi's peers. Online followers immediately chimed in to relive their own high school dining hall experiences. Lifestyle creator @just_ivy_africa_ admitted that the description of the ugali left her "triggered," while @i_amkabugi joked that the scorched bottom layer of the greens was actually "still the deal" for many who grew up on it.

Another follower, @sir_homes, suggested that Ombachi should expand the mini-series to tackle school-style githeri, recommending he include "a pinch of weevils for that extra protein" to complete the authentic nostalgic nightmare.

Recognizing that his warning had fallen flat on the next generation, Ombachi turned his satirical sights back onto his own peers who often boast about how well they have aged. 

"You millennials, you don’t look young because of the food you ate," he joked. "You are just malnourished and stopped growing."

The culinary deterrent might have failed to keep his son from dreaming of boarding school dorms, but it cemented a familiar truth for Kenyan parents: sometimes, the next generation is entirely immune to the warnings of the past.
