The arrival of 2,930 metric tonnes of heavy machinery at the Port of Lamu this week has accelerated the development timeline for the Ksh2.2 trillion Dangote East Africa Refinery, but the project is now confronting a significant legal hurdle. A group of 133 residents has filed a petition in the Malindi High Court, seeking to freeze all site activities until the government addresses long-standing compensation and resettlement grievances.

At the heart of the dispute is land parcel LR No. 13061 in Chandavai. Residents claim that their families have held ancestral ties to this territory for generations, utilizing the space for agriculture and livestock. The petition asserts that the land is not merely an industrial asset but a social nexus containing residential homes, mosques, and family burial grounds.

![Residents of Lamu protesting on September, 28, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/XxKv7t9v/84620ee1fa97.webp)

While the project aims to process 700,000 barrels of crude oil daily—drawing from both the Lokichar oilfields and regional supply chains—the current infrastructure push has left the local community in an administrative vacuum. According to the plaintiffs, they were given no formal notice or compensation when bulldozers first arrived on August 7, 2024, to clear crops and vegetation. Subsequent claims from local administrators linked the land acquisition to both the LAPSSET Corridor Development Authority and the Ministry of Defence’s operations around Manda Bay, leaving residents confused about which entity is ultimately responsible for their displacement.

The court filing names the National Land Commission, LAPSSET, Dangote Industries, and two construction contractors as respondents. For the residents, the urgency of the legal action is underscored by recent activities on the ground; they allege that soil testing, which began in July, was followed by a more aggressive clearing operation led by police and local officials on September 10 to prepare for next week’s planned groundbreaking ceremony.

![resident William Ruto (left) talking to Billionaire Aliko Dangote during their visit to the Dangote Petroleum Refinery and Petrochemical Company in Lekki, Nigeria, on Friday, Septe](https://i.ibb.co/Q3TntNjW/cdf89da4d7fa.webp)

As the state leans into the massive scale of the petrochemical complex, the Malindi High Court is now tasked with determining whether the project’s advancement can proceed without first clarifying the resettlement status of the Chandavai community. The court’s decision on whether to grant a temporary injunction will likely dictate the pace of the construction phase, as the community demands a transparent framework for their future before the ground is broken for the new facility.
