Citizen TV news anchor Trevor Ombija has carried his boardroom and studio battles onto the political stump, telling Nyakach residents that former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua attempted to have him fired from Royal Media Services following a heated on-air confrontation earlier this year.

Addressing a gathering in Nyakach on Friday, September 18, Ombija framed the fallout from the March 2026 interview not as an occupational hazard of prime-time journalism, but as personal evidence of standing up to executive intimidation.

"As I stand here to address you, the former Deputy President Gachagua once called for my dismissal from work because we confronted each other," Ombija told the crowd, adding that his ethnic heritage was raised during the push to push him out of the broadcast house.

The disclosure marks an aggressive transition for the 38-year-old broadcaster, who is seeking to turn eight years of prime-time visibility at RMS into grassroots traction for the Nyakach parliamentary seat in the 2027 General Election. Alongside the allegations against Gachagua, Ombija used the platform to formally align himself with President William Ruto’s broad-based administration, arguing the current executive has a viable development agenda.

### The Anatomy of the Studio Clash

![A photo collage of Citizen TV news anchor Trevor Ombija (left) and former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua.](https://i.ibb.co/MyVn18TS/9b22e106fc9b.webp)

The roots of Ombija’s campaign-trail claim trace back to March 2026, when Gachagua appeared for an interview co-anchored with presenters from Ramogi TV. The exchange deteriorated sharply in its final segments, with the then-deputy president visibly frustrated by lines of questioning regarding national cohesion and regional politics.

Gachagua openly accused Ombija of staging an ambush, branding the segment "unprofessional" and questioning why he was brought on what he termed a "tribal show."

"Trevor, you have been very biased in this show, which has been very unprofessional," Gachagua charged as the broadcast concluded, alleging that the moderator was framing queries unfairly and putting words into his mouth. Ombija pushed back on air, stating that Gachagua himself had initiated the request for the television appearance.

While the testy exchange played out before hundreds of thousands of viewers, Ombija’s Friday remarks represent the first time the anchor has asserted that Gachagua took the dispute off-screen to RMS executive suites.

### Silence From RMS and Gachagua

Ombija’s claim that Gachagua actively pressured RMS management to terminate his contract remains uncorroborated by independent institutional records. 

![Citizen TV studio and an insert of former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua.](https://i.ibb.co/YF5cKyVz/1e74c6678e48.webp)

Neither the former Deputy President nor the executive leadership at Royal Media Services had publicly responded to the campaign allegations by Saturday. Media managers at top Kenyan broadcast networks frequently absorb pushback from aggrieved senior politicians, though formal dismissal demands rarely surface outside private boardrooms unless made public by the journalists themselves.

By elevating the dispute in Nyakach, Ombija is effectively establishing his political biography around executive resistance—a calculated move in an opposition stronghold where challenging national figures carries significant electoral weight.

### From the Anchor Desk to the Trail

Ombija joined RMS in 2018, rising to anchor flagship programmes including *Monday Report* and morning current-affairs show *Day Break*. His formal entry into constituency politics mirrors a well-trodden path in Kenyan public life, where broadcast fame provides an immediate advantage in name recognition over career local operatives.

However, pairing his campaign rollout with public backing for President Ruto's broad-based framework introduces a delicate balancing act in Nyanza politics. By invoking a high-stakes standoff with Gachagua—whose own political standing fractured ahead of his eventual departure from office—Ombija is attempting to shore up his regional credentials while simultaneously defending his ties to the national executive.

With the 2027 election cycle drawing earlier political declarations across Kisumu County, Ombija's opening salvo signals that his media career will serve as both his central credential and his primary campaign shield.
