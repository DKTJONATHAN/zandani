For the first time in the history of national holidays, this year’s Mashujaa Day celebrations will scatter their footprint across three distinct counties in the Rift Valley, testing the logistical coordination of regional infrastructure.

Internal Security and National Administration Principal Secretary Dr. Raymond Omollo confirmed that the upcoming national fete will span Uasin Gishu, Nandi, and Elgeyo Marakwet counties, with a dedicated Thematic Week running from October 13 to October 18 centered on the theme “Security, Sports and the Finance Sector (The Economy).”

At the heart of the multi-county layout is Kipchoge Keino Stadium, designated to host the main celebrations. The facility, which holds a seating capacity of 15,043, currently stands at 68 per cent completion. Neighboring venues are racing against the calendar to meet readiness targets: Kapsabet Stadium in Nandi County, with a 10,000-seat capacity, is marked at 45.4 per cent completion, while Kamariny Stadium in Elgeyo Marakwet, also built for 10,000 spectators, reaches 58 per cent completion.

Beyond the primary sporting arenas, the preparatory push includes coordinated civil works to upgrade municipal roads within Eldoret City, Kapsabet, and Iten, alongside parallel investments in local water connectivity. The heavy lifting at the stadium sites is being driven by the Ministry of Sports, operating under the direct support and supervision of the Ministry of Defence.

Dr. Omollo, who chairs the steering committee, noted that technical teams have maintained late-night shifts to push the projects toward completion. The execution relies heavily on cross-county institutional collaboration, bringing together governors, members of parliament, and local ward representatives from all three jurisdictions.

With visitors and dignitaries expected to accompany the President for the national address, security agencies have maintained an active operational posture across the wider region. Addressing lingering security concerns involving banditry and cattle rustling in parts of Samburu, Isiolo, and Meru, Dr. Omollo pointed to ongoing multi-agency interventions and direct engagement with local leadership to stabilize vulnerable corridors ahead of the October events.
