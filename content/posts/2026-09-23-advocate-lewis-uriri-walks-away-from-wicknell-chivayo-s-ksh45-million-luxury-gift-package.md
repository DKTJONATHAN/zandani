Advocate Dr. Lewis Uriri has drawn a sharp line under high-profile benefactor culture by turning down a luxury windfall valued at approximately KSh45 million from Zimbabwean businessman Wicknell Chivayo.

The Harare-based Senior Counsel and head of chambers at The Temple Bar declined a brand-new 2026 Range Rover Sport Autobiography valued at roughly KSh32 million, alongside twin cash gifts totalling KSh13 million intended for fuel and his wife.

The rejected package, announced on Monday, September 21, 2026, placed the veteran litigator at the centre of regional attention, forcing a spotlight onto a legal career spanning more than two decades across commercial arbitration, constitutional litigation, and transnational disputes.

Uriri indicated that he and his wife carefully weighed the proposal before turning it down, noting that the gesture caught him off guard given that he and Chivayo had not spoken since June 2024. He explicitly clarified that any historical legal work referenced by the businessman had been executed in his professional capacity and settled in full.

“The historical work to which your tribute refers was performed in my professional capacity and paid for in full,” Uriri maintained.

The refusal underscores a distinct boundary between professional advocacy and personal patronage in a legal career marked by heavy institutional responsibility. Uriri is one of Zimbabwe’s four designated representatives on the International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes (ICSID), the World Bank’s dispute resolution arm, and maintains chambers at Harare’s Commercial Arbitration Centre.

His courtroom footprint spans some of the country’s most delicate constitutional battles. In 2018, Uriri formed part of then-President Emmerson Mnangagwa’s legal defense team during the presidential election challenge brought by opposition leader Nelson Chamisa at the Constitutional Court. He also represented Sengezo Tshabangu in contentious legal proceedings concerning the recall of Citizens Coalition for Change parliamentarians and councillors.

Beyond active litigation, Uriri’s background includes academic stints at the University of Zimbabwe’s departments of private and procedural law, alongside leadership roles within the Zimbabwe Inns of Court and the Law Society of Zimbabwe’s advocacy training faculties.

With the luxury vehicle and cash payments officially returned to sender, the prominent barrister remains anchored to his independent referral practice without any active professional engagement linking him to the Harare businessman since their last contact over two years ago.
