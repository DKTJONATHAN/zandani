Paul Mackenzie will spend an extra weekend waiting in detention before discovering his fate in Kenya’s most scrutinized murder trial, following an administrative directive shifting the landmark ruling across county borders.

The controversial preacher, alongside 30 co-accused followers, had been scheduled to receive judgment on Friday, October 16, 2026. Instead, presiding judge Lady Justice Diana Kavedza-Mochache issued a formal court notice setting a revised date for Monday, October 19, 2026, at 9:00 AM, while transferring the venue from Malindi to the Mombasa Law Courts.

“Take Notice that under the direction of Hon. Lady Justice Diana Kavedza-Mochache, the Judgement date for Case no. E003 of 2024 has been moved to 19th October 2026 at 9:00am at Mombasa Law Courts,” the official directive dated October 9 confirmed.

The three-day calendar adjustment brings Mackenzie to the edge of an exhaustive legal chapter that has gripped the nation since mass graves were first unearthed in Kilifi County. The October 19 decision will determine whether the Good News International Church leader and his inner circle are criminally culpable for the deaths of scores of minors who perished under his watch.

### The Weight of 191 Counts

Registered under Case Number E003 of 2024, the trial represents one of the largest single murder indictments in Kenyan judicial history. Mackenzie and his co-accused stand charged with 191 counts of murder stemming from the discovery of shallow graves inside the Shakahola Forest expanse.

According to state filings prepared by the Office of the Director of Public Prosecutions (ODPP), the deaths took place over a two-and-a-half-year period running from January 2021 through September 2023. Of the 191 young victims cited in the consolidated charge sheet, forensic teams and prosecutors formally identified 11 children by name, while 180 others remain listed as unidentified minors.

Throughout the proceedings, Mackenzie has maintained an impassive posture inside court holding cells, watching as state prosecutors systematically assembled evidence linking his doctrinal teachings to the mass starvation uncovered in the remote coastal woodland.

### An Exhaustive Evidentiary Record

The road to this month’s verdict involved an extensive evidentiary campaign that saw the state call 121 witnesses to the stand. Prosecutors paired eyewitness testimony from survivors and responders with detailed postmortem reports, expert clinical evaluations, and technical documentation prepared by government analysts.

That foundation proved decisive on April 15, 2026, when Justice Kavedza ruled that the state had met the threshold for a prima facie case. Applying Section 211 of the Criminal Procedure Code, the judge placed all 31 defendants on their defense, formally rejecting early efforts to secure an acquittal before the defense presented its case.

The proceedings had previously weathered significant turbulence over the structural scale of the indictment itself. Defense lawyers staged a sustained challenge against the state’s decision to prosecute 191 separate murder counts in a single trial, arguing that such a massive docket compromised the accused persons' constitutional guarantee to a fair and manageable hearing.

That legal roadblock was settled in April 2025, when the Court of Appeal overturned a High Court directive that had sought to whittle down the charge sheet. The appellate bench ruled in favor of the ODPP, ordering that the entire 191-count indictment remain intact throughout the trial.

### The Final Dock in Mombasa

The venue switch to the Mombasa Law Courts places Mackenzie and his followers inside the region’s principal judicial complex for their final reckoning. With the defense phase closed and the prosecution’s 121-witness record complete, Justice Kavedza-Mochache’s scheduled reading on October 19 will close out years of forensic exhumations, procedural clashes, and courtroom debate.

For Mackenzie, whose descent from a controversial broadcast preacher to the central figure of the Shakahola tragedy redefined public scrutiny of fringe religious movements, the upcoming morning session in Mombasa leaves no remaining procedural buffer between his defense and the court's final ruling.
