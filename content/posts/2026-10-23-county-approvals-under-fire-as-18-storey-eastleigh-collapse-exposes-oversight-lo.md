Nairobi’s building regulation framework faces acute scrutiny following disclosures that the 18-storey structure that collapsed in Eastleigh on Friday held initial county construction permits, yet operated without an occupation certificate or certified site engineers.

The structural failure along Yusuf Haji Avenue, adjacent to Day to Day Supermarket on 9th Street, occurred while commercial businesses were actively operating on lower levels despite heavy structural work continuing on upper floors. As multi-agency emergency teams clear concrete blocks to reach victims trapped beneath the rubble, the disaster has exposed a critical gap between City Hall’s approval process and physical site enforcement.

### Paper Approvals Without Site Oversight

Nairobi Governor Johnson Sakaja confirmed that while the project had secured formal development approval from county planning desks, it lacked an occupation certificate authorizing public or commercial use. Sakaja revealed a widespread practice where developers present credentials of licensed architects and engineers to pass initial vetting, only to dismiss the professionals once approval certificates are issued.

![of a building that collapsed along Yusuf Haji Avenue, next to Day to Day Supermarket in Eastleigh, Nairobi, on October 23, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/building-collapse-eastleigh_1.jpg.webp?itok=M51qRx2i)

"We are appealing to all professionals that once you are given an approval and later fired, you must report to the regulators as well as the county government, because from the look here it appears like there was no professional on site," Sakaja stated at the scene.

Because professional regulatory bodies and county planning inspectors lack automated reporting mechanisms when lead engineers are terminated, unvetted teams frequently manage complex high-rise construction. Video recordings captured shortly before the collapse showed the building reached up to 18 storeys, with ground-floor businesses open to the public while heavy construction remained ongoing above.

### Active Search and Criminal Liability

Rescue operations remain underway along 9th Street, coordinated by personnel from the Kenya Defence Forces (KDF), General Service Unit (GSU), Anti-Terrorism Police Unit, Bomb Disposal Unit, National Youth Service, and the Kenya Red Cross. First responders are utilizing heavy excavators, concrete breakers, and sniffer dogs to penetrate the compacted debris.

![eastleigh-building-collapse-2.jpg](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/eastleigh-building-collapse-2.jpg.webp?itok=2ncUKuv0)

Emergency workers successfully rescued one woman from the rubble on Friday afternoon and cleared debris channels to supply oxygen after hearing voices from survivors trapped inside the structure. A casual site labourer reported that over 70 people—including construction workers, food vendors, and service providers—may have been in the basement area when the structure caved in during a lunch break. Authorities noted that the estimate remains unverified due to the absence of a verified site log.

Nairobi Regional Police Commander Issa Mohamed announced that law enforcement agencies have launched a manhunt for the building’s owner, who went into hiding after the incident. Mohamed warned that police will pursue criminal negligence charges against the owner and any public officers who authorized or overlooked the ongoing construction.

### Calls for Independent Vetting

The collapse has intensified political and public pressure over Nairobi's urban planning administration. Embakasi East MP Babu Owino called for an immediate independent investigation into City Hall’s building approval system, alleging that corruption, administrative negligence, and weak enforcement continue to allow unauthorized construction across the capital.

Police have urged local residents to clear the surrounding perimeter on Yusuf Haji Avenue to allow heavy rescue machinery unhindered access, as search teams work continuously to establish contact with remaining survivors under the rubble.
