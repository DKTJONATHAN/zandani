Bien has drawn a definitive line under his long-running differences with Otile Brown, publicly dismissing their historical friction as an unnecessary distraction for two established Kenyan acts.

Speaking during a recent interview, the Sauti Sol vocalist indicated that he holds no lingering animosity toward Otile, making it clear that he prefers to channel his energy into his career rather than sustain a public back-and-forth. Rather than trading fresh counter-remarks or keeping the door open for media drama, Bien offered explicit goodwill to his fellow artist, signaling a desire to move forward.

![Bien // Instagram](https://i.ibb.co/chcTYr19/e85b277f0494.webp)

The public relationship between the two musicians has long been a fixture of local entertainment chatter, marked by occasional social media cross-talk and interview jabs. In an industry where personal disagreements frequently threaten to eclipse musical output, Bien’s latest posture points to a deliberate recalibration. His comments bypass the usual cycle of retaliation, treating the rivalry not as a permanent artistic battle line, but as professional dead weight that no longer serves either performer.

Both artists occupy distinct spaces within the local scene. Bien rose to mainstream prominence as a core member of Sauti Sol before carving out a prolific solo identity, while Otile Brown built a dedicated following through his consistent delivery of romantic ballads and contemporary R&B releases.
