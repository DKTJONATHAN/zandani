President William Ruto has elevated the response to Kenya’s single confirmed Ebola fatality from routine public health monitoring straight to the top of executive security apparatus, directing the National Security Council Committee (NSCC) to convene and formulate containment measures following the patient's death.

The directive shifts the management of the imported case into an active biosecurity operation, with state surveillance teams isolating and monitoring an immediate contact ring of 56 individuals across three distinct exposure groups.

Following the NSCC assessment, health surveillance officers placed eight family members of the deceased into mandatory quarantine under daily symptom evaluation. Alongside the immediate household, 21 healthcare workers who provided direct care to the patient before death have been isolated under clinical monitoring.

The third arm of the containment perimeter extends to flight tracing, where health and port authorities identified and reached 27 passengers who shared the incoming flight with the deceased. State teams have initiated public health protocols for all identified flight contacts to prevent secondary transmission.

The patient, who entered the country with an imported infection before succumbing, has been interred under established safe-burial protocols designed for viral hemorrhagic fevers. State officials confirmed that no secondary positive cases have been detected among quarantined contacts or the wider population.

### Border Screening and Diagnostic Reach

The activation of the National Security Council Committee builds on a national surveillance framework deployed when the broader regional outbreak emerged in May 2026. Official data shows that point-of-entry controls screened 652,584 inbound travellers as of October 6, 2026.

Diagnostic verification relies on a network of five specialized laboratories that have evaluated 267 suspected biological samples across the country. Testing facilities include the National Virology Reference Laboratory (NVRL), Kenya Medical Research Institute (KEMRI) centers in Nairobi and Kisumu, and two mobile laboratories positioned at border points in Busia and Lwakhakha in Bungoma County.

Out of the 267 samples processed nationwide, the single sample drawn from the deceased patient remains the only positive result recorded.

### Clinical Capacity and Public Safeguards

To manage potential clinical exposure, state agencies have completed specialized training for 4,971 healthcare workers at national and subnational levels. Training modules focused on symptom detection, isolation mechanics, and personal protection protocols.

Select medical facilities have been designated for specialized Ebola management, with health authorities confirming plans to expand treatment centers if the situational assessment shifts.

While monitoring the 56 quarantined contacts, public health officials reiterated key symptoms requiring immediate facility reporting, including high fever, severe headache, muscle pains, physical weakness, vomiting, diarrhoea, and unexplained bleeding. The Ministry of Health is expected to issue formal operational guidelines detailing institutional and individual preventive mandates.
