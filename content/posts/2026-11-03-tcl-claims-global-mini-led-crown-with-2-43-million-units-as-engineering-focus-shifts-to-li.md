TCL is sitting at the top of the global display market, moving 2.43 million Mini LED television units in the first half of 2026 alone. According to market data compiled by Omdia, that performance translates to a 77.1% surge compared to the same period last year, cementing a commercial reach that follows a 31.1% global shipment share recorded through 2025.

Yet moving millions of units is only half the battle in a consumer electronics landscape where hardware terminology can easily blur together. As Mini LED moves past its early adopter phase—accounting for 13% of global TV shipments by the second quarter of 2026—market leadership increasingly hinges on explaining what happens behind the glass rather than just celebrating shipment ledgers.

The technical conversation underpinning this growth centers squarely on light control. As backlights scale down into denser arrays of microscopic LEDs, display performance relies heavily on how hardware manages independent zones across the panel. Full Array Local Dimming architecture replaces perimeter lighting with a grid of controllable zones positioned directly behind the screen, allowing brightness to be directed precisely where an image demands it.

Industry analysts note that as these panels appear across a wider spectrum of price points, raw zone counts and dimming mechanics become the primary metrics for evaluation. For a manufacturer holding the top global shipment spot, the operational challenge shifts from securing factory scale to demystifying these specifications for everyday buyers.
