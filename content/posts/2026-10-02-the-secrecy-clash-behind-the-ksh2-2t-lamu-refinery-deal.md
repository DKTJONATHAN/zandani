A sharp dispute has emerged over the newly launched Ksh2.2 trillion Dangote oil refinery in Lamu, exposing a deep rift between President William Ruto’s defense of foreign investment and demands from Kenyan motorists for legally binding fuel price reductions and full contract disclosure.

The Motorists Association of Kenya has formally rejected the current administrative and pricing structure of the mega-refinery. The lobby is refusing to endorse the project unless the state provides written, legally binding assurances that the domestic refining capacity will translate directly to lower prices at the pump.

### The Battle Over Transparency

This consumer demand directly collides with the executive posture. President Ruto has publicly shielded the investment by Nigerian billionaire Aliko Dangote, characterizing public demands to inspect the state's agreement with the Dangote Group as attempts to sabotage the deal.

![President William Ruto and Nigerian businessman Aliko Dangote talk during the refinery groundbreaking ceremony in Lamu, September 30, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/1fXRXMQj/d90af067bd61.webp)

"Imagine people are opposing that refinery in Lamu. Just imagine, yesterday I heard some people asking for the agreement of the Dangote refinery," President Ruto said, framing the calls for transparency as an effort to extort and undermine the investor.

But the motoring public argues that its demands are a rational response to a pattern of unfulfilled government promises on energy and transport costs. The association contends that while regional trade merchants and political elites celebrate the Ksh2.2 trillion investment, the primary stakeholders—the motorists who fund the road network and consume the bulk of the fuel—have been systematically shut out of the discussions.

### A Legacy of Broken Guarantees

To justify their skepticism, motorists are pointing to a trail of failed policy assurances. Chief among these is the state's Government-to-Government (G2G) fuel supply arrangement. When introduced, the government promised a six-month price stabilization guarantee. Instead, motorists faced subsequent pump price increases, which officials attributed to geopolitical tensions in the Strait of Hormuz.

The lobby also cited the Turkana oil project, noting that domestic consumers never benefited from the crude extracted from the northern oilfields, despite continuing to shoulder heavy financial burdens for road development through the Road Maintenance Levy.

![A sign at the refinery site during the groundbreaking ceremony in Lamu on September 30, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/W4mLXzD6/c127cceac9d7.webp)

This distrust is compounded by recent aggressive fiscal shifts that have steadily increased the cost of driving in Kenya. These include:
* The elimination of the Ksh5 per litre fuel subsidy.
* The doubling of Value Added Tax (VAT) on fuel from 8 percent to 16 percent.
* A recent Ksh7 per litre increase in the Road Maintenance Levy.

### Where Do the Savings Go?

Aside from demanding the publication of the full contracts signed between the government and the Dangote Group, motorists are questioning the government's promotional focus on high-seas insurance premium savings.

While state officials have highlighted these logistical savings as a major win for the project, the association points out there is no mechanism or clear commitment ensuring these corporate savings will ever be passed down to retail buyers at the station.

As the dust settles on the groundbreaking ceremony in Lamu, the core conflict remains unresolved. The state is proceeding with a highly guarded investment framework, while the consumer lobby maintains that without a statutory covenant on pricing, the Ksh2.2 trillion refinery risk remains entirely one-sided.
