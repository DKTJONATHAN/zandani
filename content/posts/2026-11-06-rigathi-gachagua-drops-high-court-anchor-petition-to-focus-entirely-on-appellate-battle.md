Rigathi Gachagua has formally pulled the plug on his anchor High Court suit challenging his October 2024 impeachment, shifting his entire defense campaign to the Court of Appeal as he seeks to safeguard his political future.

His legal team officially notified the court of the withdrawal of Constitutional Petition No. E522 of 2024—the primary lawsuit originally launched to halt his removal from office by the National Assembly and the Senate.

Speaking on the decision, Gachagua’s advocates reiterated that petitioners retain the full right to withdraw legal actions at any stage, confirming that the former Deputy President chose to exercise that discretion to avoid wasting judicial resources.

### A Strategic Exit From Petition E522

When the legal fight first erupted in late 2024, Petition E522 formed the cornerstone of Gachagua’s defense strategy. High Court Judge Lawrence Mugambi designated the suit as the lead anchor case, consolidating it alongside five related petitions challenging the ouster.

Given the weight of the constitutional issues raised—ranging from procedural fairness and public participation to alleged political bias—Chief Justice Martha Koome empaneled a specialized three-judge bench to hear the consolidated matters.

However, the legal team's initial effort to freeze the parliamentary process stalled when the High Court declined to issue emergency stay orders, allowing the Senate trial to proceed and culminating in Gachagua's removal.

### Why the High Court Suit Was Dropped

According to Gachagua’s lawyers, continuing to litigate the suit at the High Court level had become redundant. Core constitutional questions regarding the threshold for public participation and procedural fairness were progressively addressed in subsequent judicial determinations, rendering the initial pre-emptive suit functionally overtaken by events.

Rather than spending time fighting multi-layered procedural battles in the lower constitutional court, Gachagua’s legal counsel confirmed that their focus and resources have been 100 percent redirected toward their pending appeal at the Court of Appeal.

### The November 19 Appellate Stand-Off

The appellate court has scheduled November 19, 2026, to issue key directions on how Gachagua's challenge against the High Court’s rulings will proceed.

The shift in venue comes at a critical juncture for Gachagua, who continues to position himself alongside opposition figures aiming to contest the presidency in the 2027 General Election.

His political viability rests almost entirely on the outcome of the appellate process. Under Article 75(3) of the Constitution of Kenya, any public officer removed from office for violating Chapter Six leadership and integrity standards faces a permanent disqualification from holding any state or public office.

By dropping the High Court anchor suit, Gachagua’s legal team is betting that a direct appellate strategy offers the cleanest path to overturning the legal bar standing between him and the ballot box.
