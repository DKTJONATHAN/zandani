Oburu Oginga has drawn a strict operational line for Orange Democratic Movement members questioning the party's alignment with President William Ruto, revealing that a two-tier committee system has been put in place to manage the arrangement.

Speaking virtually to supporters during the closing rally of the month-long Mwezi wa Baba celebrations at Nairobi’s Jacaranda Grounds on Saturday, October 31, 2026, the ODM Party Leader rejected assertions that the political accommodation was entered into without forethought.

“We are in this broad-based government, and we are not there blindly,” Oburu told the gathering. “Raila did not go there blindly, and we are not ourselves there in this broad-based government blindly. We are there consciously, and we are there knowing what we are doing.”

To handle ongoing policy and constitutional frameworks within statutory boundaries, Oburu pointed to an already functioning five-member technical team. Beyond that immediate layer, he disclosed that a dedicated coalition negotiation team—appointed jointly by ODM and President Ruto’s camp—will take shape before February 2027 to map out the formal political pathway.

“Immediately after that, we had promised that before February next year we are going to have a coalition team, which will be completely different from the technical team,” he explained.

The push for structured engagement comes amid visible public friction among party figures over whether to back President Ruto’s reelection conditionally or unconditionally. Oburu used the platform to demand strict adherence to official positions, warning members against airing conflicting personal views.

“If you want to express your own view, don’t express it against the policy of the party. If you are a member of the party, please be disciplined and follow the policies of your party as we move towards the 2027 General Election,” he said.

The Saturday event marked the end of weeks of commemorative activities honoring the legacy of the late former Prime Minister Raila Odinga, with Oburu praising his brother’s historic political struggles.

A heavy turnout of senior political figures flanked the event, including Treasury Cabinet Secretary John Mbadi, Nairobi Governor Johnson Sakaja, Homa Bay Governor Gladys Wanga, Kisii Governor Simba Arati, and National Assembly Minority Leader Junet Mohamed.
