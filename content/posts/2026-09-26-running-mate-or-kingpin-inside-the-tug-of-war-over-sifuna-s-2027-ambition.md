Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna finds himself standing between two radically different political scripts for 2027, with both camps moving aggressively to define his destination before he can announce it himself.

On one side stands Wiper leader Kalonzo Musyoka’s camp, which has spent recent weeks quietly drawing up a joint opposition ticket that casts Sifuna as the junior partner and prospective running mate, targeting an official unveiling as early as October. On the other stands Trans Nzoia Governor George Natembeya, who walked straight to the podium on Thursday, September 24, to announce that Sifuna has no intention of playing second fiddle to anyone.

Instead, Natembeya declared that Sifuna will contest the presidency on the United Democratic Party (UDP)—a political vehicle formerly associated with the late Lugari politician Cyrus Jirongo—under their burgeoning Linda Mwananchi banner.

“We have a political party. It is the United Democratic Party (UDP), Sifuna’s party, which is the one we intend to use in the 2027 general election,” Natembeya said, setting off immediate ripples across the wider opposition alliance.

Natembeya argued that opposition players would eventually have to sit at the negotiating table as party principals rather than junior supplicants. By assigning Sifuna the UDP vehicle, the Linda Mwananchi group effectively pushed back against backroom efforts to box the vocal Nairobi senator into an early secondary slot.

Yet just 24 hours after Natembeya made his public pitch, new polling numbers arrived to test whether the electorate sees Sifuna as an independent contender or a supplementary ticket-booster.

A Mizani Africa survey released on Friday, September 25, asked respondents which opposition leader was best placed to take on President William Ruto. Kalonzo emerged at the top of the preference list with 37.2 per cent, leaving Sifuna in second place at 19.7 per cent—a 17.5-point gap.

The poll placed former Interior Cabinet Secretary Fred Matiang’i third at 12.9 per cent, followed by former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua at 6.4 per cent and Kiharu MP Ndindi Nyoro at 5.1 per cent. Former Chief Justice David Maraga managed 2.2 per cent, while Martha Karua and Busia Senator Okiya Omtatah registered 0.7 per cent and 0.4 per cent respectively. A sizable 15.4 per cent of respondents said they remained undecided.

### The Math of a Joint Ticket

While the individual numbers establish Kalonzo as the pollster's early frontrunner among opposition hopefuls, Mizani's comparative testing revealed why Kalonzo’s strategists are so keen on locking Sifuna into their fold.

When Mizani tested a joint Kalonzo-Sifuna ticket, the pairing commanded 40.8 per cent support among respondents evaluating prospective opposition slates. That unified total suggests Sifuna’s urban appeal and Western Kenya backing provide the exact geographic and generational bridge Kalonzo needs to construct a competitive national challenge.

Without that unity, the fractured opposition picture presents a steeper climb. In a separate three-way presidential preference question included in the survey, President Ruto led the field at 38.7 per cent, with Kalonzo trailing at 27.5 per cent and Sifuna carving out 13.2 per cent on his own.

Mizani Africa did not provide its sample size, methodology, or margin of error alongside the findings, leaving the figures as an unweighted snapshot of stated political preferences rather than a predictive forecast. But within opposition backrooms, the perception created by the numbers carries its own weight.

For Sifuna, the dilemma is becoming immediate. Accepting the running mate slot in Kalonzo’s planned October rollout guarantees him second billing on a tested opposition ticket, but it risks cooling the momentum Natembeya and the Linda Mwananchi faction are trying to build around an independent Western-led vehicle. Choosing the UDP path preserves his autonomy as a presidential contender, but risks leaving him fighting for third place in an already crowded race.

With Kalonzo’s team pushing to settle the ticket within weeks and Natembeya already planting the UDP flag, Sifuna will soon have to decide which script he is actually performing.
