Suba North Member of Parliament Millie Odhiambo has dismantled a long-standing public myth surrounding her high-voltage political persona, revealing that her fiery parliamentary interventions and energetic public appearances are powered by nothing stronger than Fanta Orange.

Speaking during a television broadcast on Thursday, November 19, 2026, the veteran lawmaker addressed widespread assumptions that her uninhibited, confrontational public style is fueled by heavy alcohol consumption or narcotics. Instead, Odhiambo confirmed she has lived her entire life as a strict teetotaler, choosing to make peace with persistent rumors rather than tone down her natural exuberance.

"I have never touched alcohol in my entire life, never touched drugs. I was born a natural high," Odhiambo stated during the broadcast. "I actually get high on Fanta Orange."

For years, Odhiambo’s dramatic floor interventions in the National Assembly, vocal political rallies, and unapologetic clashes with opponents have fueled public assumptions about her off-duty lifestyle. Observers routinely assumed that her intense energy required chemical fuel or late-night liquid courage. By publicly setting the record straight, the Suba North legislator redirected the conversation toward the daily friction of operating as an unfiltered politician in a deeply calculated system.

Beyond clearing the air regarding her personal habits, Odhiambo offered a sobering assessment of the environment she has navigated across successive parliamentary terms. According to the veteran lawmaker, the primary challenge of public office has not been handling media scrutiny or aggressive campaigns, but managing the pervasive culture of dishonesty inside Kenya's political ecosystem.

Odhiambo estimated that roughly three-quarters of politics revolves around deceit—a reality that repeatedly clashes with her personal preference for immediate, unvarnished directness. She explained that refusing to engage in political doublespeak often leaves her isolated on legislative matters, describing her day-to-day work in governance as a process of "swimming upstream" against systemic insincerity.

Her candid audit underscores a distinct paradox in her public life. While her booming voice and confrontational style are frequently misread by the public as substance-induced aggression, Odhiambo attributes her survival to an innate, unmedicated drive that stands in sharp opposition to the polished dishonesty she encounters in committee rooms and political negotiations.

Rather than softening her posture or adopting the diplomatic smoke screens common among her peers, Odhiambo maintained that she has learned to live with public misjudgments. Her statement places her unyielding floor presence in a clear light: a veteran politician drawing her energy from a soft drink and her frustration from a political landscape she estimates to be 75 percent dishonest.
