The national government has set aside Ksh 7.5 billion to construct 3,000 housing units in Lamu County, directly underwriting accommodation infrastructure for the anticipated workforce arriving for the Dangote petrochemical refinery.

President William Ruto announced the funding on Saturday, October 3, during a land title issuance ceremony in Mokowe, framing the development as a prerequisite for managing the human settlement pressure that the Ksh 2.2 trillion industrial complex will trigger over its 40-month construction cycle.

"We are allocating Ksh 7.5 billion to build an additional 3,000 housing units to support housing as we develop the refinery in Lamu County. So that those who are coming to live here will have decent dwelling," President Ruto told residents in Mokowe.

### Preparing for a 60,000-Worker Migration

![Heavy machinery on site ahead of the commissioning of the Dangote East Africa Oil Refinery in Lamu](https://i.ibb.co/7JrXyVBq/e4fa235c4d94.webp)

The targeted accommodation drive responds to projections that roughly 60,000 personnel will be required across engineering, construction, and operational phases. Groundbreaking on the regional refining facility took place on Wednesday, initiating site preparations for a plant designed to process 700,000 barrels of crude oil per day.

Project management and technical execution are already being mobilized following the award of a Ksh 58 billion procurement, engineering, and construction management contract to an Indian state-owned engineering firm. Alongside foreign technical staff, the Dangote Group plans to train 1,000 local Lamu residents for operational placements, while broader logistical, marine, and manufacturing satellite businesses establish support hubs.

Without an immediate ramp-up in public dwelling infrastructure, the impending labor migration risks overwhelming Lamu's local rental market and municipal capacity.

### Broader Housing Portfolio and Execution Hurdles

![President William Ruto interacting with workers at the Affordbale Housing Programme in Garissa, Thursday, February 6, 2025.](https://i.ibb.co/1fz0vQ5d/623f9931c882.webp)

The worker-targeted scheme expands the government's wider urban portfolio in the county, where an additional Ksh 6 billion has been budgeted for civic markets, student hostels, and standard affordable housing blocks. 

However, the rapid scaling of state builds in the coastal county faces existing execution bottlenecks. President Ruto acknowledged ongoing delays affecting the planned housing site in Faza, though he maintained the stalled island project would proceed.

To prevent similar lags on mainland projects, the President directed Lands Cabinet Secretary Alice Wahome to accelerate works in Mpeketoni, where a formal contract for 900 residential units has already been signed.

With ground cleared for both the industrial zone and supporting residential blocks, line ministries face a rigid operational window to deliver habitable units before the bulk of the construction labor force arrives on site.
