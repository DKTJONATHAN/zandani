A five-month operational breach inside a financial institution's core software platform has come to light at the Milimani Law Courts, detailing how internal privilege settings were allegedly manipulated to prepare for system theft.

Appearing before Resident Magistrate Irene Thamara on Friday, October 23, a bank employee denied charges of unauthorised computer access, attempted theft by servant, and conspiracy to commit a felony. The court granted the accused a cash bail of Ksh200,000, with an alternative bond term set at Ksh600,000.

![Entrance to Milimani Law Courts, Nairobi.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/milimani_law_courts_1_0.jpg.webp?itok=tBfvTdMv)

Charge sheet details show that the alleged system tampering occurred repeatedly between May 2026 and October 21, 2026. Central to the state's case is the targeted manipulation of Finacle—the primary software platform utilized by commercial lenders to process account transactions, manage customer balances, and enforce operational permissions across branch and back-office networks.

According to prosecutors, the accused secured improper entry into the financial institution's network by creating duplicate copies of two specific Finacle user profiles: `VM53601` and `BC 23924`. In core-banking architecture, user profiles establish strict administrative boundaries, dictating which staff members can approve ledger entries, modify account parameters, or initiate fund transfers.

By cloning existing profiles, the prosecution contends that internal access controls were bypassed, establishing secondary authorization routes intended to facilitate a planned theft under Section 389 of the Penal Code.

![ODPP offices in Nairobi.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/odpp.jpg.webp?itok=vmww54oB)

"You secured unauthorised access to a computer system belonging to the bank by duplicating Finacle user profiles VM53601 and BC 23924 with intent to commit a further offence, namely theft," the formal charge reading states.

The court documents further indicate that the accused did not act in isolation. The charge sheet explicitly frames the activity as a joint venture with other individuals who have yet to be apprehended or brought to court.

The case will return to court as state prosecutors finalize system forensic logs detailing the history of profiles `VM53601` and `BC 23924`, while law enforcement tracks the remaining accomplices named in the charge sheet.
