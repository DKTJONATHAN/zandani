Detectives from the Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission (EACC) have arrested two Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) officers attached to Kipkelion Sub-County after they were caught red-handed receiving a bribe to terminate an active murder investigation.

### What we know
* **Officers Arrested:** Two DCI sleuths stationed at the Kipkelion Sub-County offices in Kericho County were taken into custody on Thursday, September 10, 2026.
* **Extortion Claim:** The officers allegedly demanded a sum of Ksh500,000 from a complainant in exchange for dropping a murder charge against him.
* **The Trap:** EACC officers mounted a sting operation and caught the duo receiving an initial sum of Ksh120,000.
* **Bail & Processing:** The suspects were processed at the EACC South Rift Regional Office in Nakuru and subsequently released on a Ksh30,000 cash bail pending further legal action.

The operation was executed early Thursday morning following a tip-off from a member of the public who reported that the two officers had put a price on his freedom. According to anti-graft investigators, the Kipkelion-based officers insisted on receiving Ksh500,000 to alter official records and ensure the homicide file against the complainant was closed.

EACC sleuths laid a trap, moving in on the officers at the exact moment they received a partial payment of Ksh120,000 in cash. Following their arrest, the duo was escorted to the EACC South Rift Regional Office for formal processing. They were later granted a cash bail of Ksh30,000 each as investigators finalize the file for submission to the Director of Public Prosecutions (DPP).

The Kipkelion bust comes barely 24 hours after a similar crackdown exposed corrupt dealings within the Judiciary. Just a day earlier, EACC officers arrested a Court Assistant attached to the Magistrate’s Court in Eldoret for soliciting a Ksh20,000 bribe, alongside a Court Process Server at the Nakuru Law Courts accused of demanding money from litigants just to secure favorable case mention dates.

For wananchi on the ground, the reality that an offense as grave as murder can be reduced to a cash transaction inside a sub-county DCI office is deeply chilling. Across local towns like Kipkelion and the broader South Rift, police stations are expected to be sanctuaries of law and order. When murder dockets are treated like commercial merchandise, public confidence in law enforcement collapses entirely-leaving ordinary citizens to feel that justice in Kenya isn't delivered by the law, but bought by the highest bidder.
