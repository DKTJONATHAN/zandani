A sharp acceleration in merchant price expectations across Kenya's food supply chains threatens higher retail shelf prices for household staples, driven by the delayed impact of poor rainfall earlier in the year and expanding haulage costs.

Data from the Central Bank of Kenya’s (CBK) September 2026 Agriculture Sector Survey reveals a pronounced upward shift in how agricultural producers, wholesalers, and retailers view short-term pricing. Out of 466 market actors surveyed nationwide, 74 percent expect fresh packeted milk prices to climb in the coming weeks.

![Central Bank of Kenya Governor Kamau Thugge appears before the National Assembly’s Committee on Delegated Legislation, July 30, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/WpKjngQz/b4ecad9a6707.webp)

The shift in market sentiment is equally steep across commercial grain products. Expectations of price increases for white wheat flour rose from 29 percent in August to 48 percent in September, while fortified maize flour saw expected increases jump from 31 percent to 47 percent. Anticipated price hikes for sifted maize flour more than doubled over the same period, rising from 19 percent in August to 40 percent in September, alongside a jump in sugar price expectations from 15 percent to 28 percent. Garden peas also emerged among non-core commodities facing imminent upward adjustments.

The central bank's survey points to a dual squeeze on food supply chains: lingering environmental deficits and immediate logistics overheads. An overwhelming 97 percent of respondents cited weather conditions as a primary factor influencing retail prices, while 92 percent cited transport expenses and 80 percent pointed to the cost of farm inputs.

The upward pressure on dairy stems directly from a delayed supply bottleneck created during the depressed March-May 2026 long rains season. Poor rainfall severely degraded pasture across key livestock regions, depressing raw milk production and creating a structural shortage that is now working its way into processor and retailer pricing.

![A photo of a Kenyan shopping at a local supermarket in Nairobi on March 27, 2019](https://i.ibb.co/rGZdQwGx/313f4b252add.webp)

Beyond farmgate shortages, structural friction within distribution networks continues to pad end-consumer costs. CBK findings show that 62 percent of respondents identified brokers and middlemen as major influencers of final retail shelf costs, while 58 percent pointed to disruptions in global supply chains. Elevated fuel prices have increased the cost of moving food from agricultural production zones to urban retail markets, prompting traders to adjust their forward pricing to preserve margins.

The survey's Balance of Opinion index confirms that price expectations are leaning heavily toward upward revisions across almost all sampled core food commodities. How those expectations translate onto supermarket shelves through October and November will depend heavily on the onset of seasonal rainfall and the trajectory of transit fuel costs along main freight routes.
