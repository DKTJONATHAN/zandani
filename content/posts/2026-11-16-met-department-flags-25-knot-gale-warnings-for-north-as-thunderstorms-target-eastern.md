The Kenya Meteorological Department has put travelers, motorists, and outdoor operators on notice across the country, outlining a sharply divided 24-hour weather cycle marked by high-velocity wind warnings in the far north and abrupt thunderstorm intervals sweeping through the eastern interior and coastal belt.

The national forecaster’s daily advisory, covering the window running through 9pm on Monday, November 16, 2026, highlights turbulent atmospheric shifts rather than steady seasonal weather. While baseline daytime heat will persist across northern borderlands, localized storm cells and sudden pressure drops are set to interrupt typical daytime routines in over a dozen counties.

### Gale-Force Gusts and Northern Heat Extremes

The most pressing operational hazard identified by the department centers on Marsabit and Turkana counties, where forecasters warned of sustained, powerful wind currents capable of complicating road transport and open-air activities.

“Strong southerly to southeasterly winds of above 25 knots (12.5m/s) expected over parts of Marsabit and Turkana Counties,” the department stated in its alert.

Alongside the 25-knot gusts, Marsabit faces an unpredictable thermal swing. Forecasters project nighttime lows dipping to 17°C alongside isolated showers and overnight thunderstorms, before daytime conditions recover to an afternoon high of 25°C under broken cloud cover.

Further east along the border corridor, Mandera is projected to record the country’s highest daytime peak, with thermometers climbing to 36°C. The border county will clear from nighttime clouds into sustained sunny intervals during the day, maintaining intense dry heat while surrounding regions contend with localized precipitation.

### Coastal Belt Navigates Lingering Storm Cells

Along the Indian Ocean coastline, holidaymakers and logistics networks across Mombasa, Kwale, and Kilifi are managing humid, wet conditions that started with overnight showers on Sunday. 

The Met Department projects that all three coastal counties will experience intermittent morning and afternoon rainfall punctuated by sunny breaks. In Mombasa and Kwale, daytime highs will touch 31°C, offset by muggy overnight minimums of 23°C and 24°C respectively.

The coastal hinterland will see sharper instability. Tana River county faces moderate showers and thunderstorms that built up overnight, rolling into Monday morning dampness before regenerating into heavier afternoon electrical storms. In Lamu, cloud cover is expected to thin out earlier, leaving the archipelago with lighter daytime showers framed by broad sunny intervals.

### Interior Thunderstorm Arc Spans Eastern Counties

In the lower eastern and central zones, the meteorological pattern shifts decisively toward afternoon thunderstorm activity. Commuters and agricultural hubs across Kitui, Machakos, and Makueni are navigating a synchronized weather pattern characterized by cloudy overnight conditions giving way to humid sunny spells, which then culminate in afternoon electrical downpours.

Kitui and Machakos both recorded overnight showers heading into Monday morning, with forecasters anticipating that early clearing will yield to renewed afternoon thunder in scattered locations. Makueni follows an identical curve, transitioning from morning light rain into unstable afternoon skies.

Further south, Taita Taveta remains under one of the wettest tracks in the current forecast cycle. After overnight downpours, residents face moderate morning showers followed by a second, broader wave of light-to-moderate rainfall and localized thunderstorms later in the day.

To the north of Mount Kenya, Meru and Isiolo share a matching trajectory. Both counties recorded partly cloudy conditions with light showers overnight, with the Met Department indicating that morning sunshine will be cut short by mid-afternoon rain and thunder across elevated and low-lying zones alike.

With high-wind thresholds active through Monday evening in the north and unstable cloud formations tracking across the coastal and eastern corridors, the department expects conditions to remain dynamic as regional pressure systems continue to fluctuate.
