NAIROBI - Kenya will become the first African nation to host the World Athletics Championships after Nairobi defeated London, Rome, and Munich to secure the hosting rights for the 2029 global showpiece.

### What we know

* World Athletics selected Nairobi over heavy European favorites, ending Africa's decades-long wait to stage track and field's marquee global event.
* A lessons-learned pivot from Kenya's failed 2025 bid forced officials to secure solid state funding commitments and clear stadium upgrade blueprints.
* Massive public interest and elite athletic prowess gave Nairobi's pitch a cultural edge that European commercial centers failed to replicate.
* State promises surrounding Kasarani, Nyayo, and Talanta stadiums now transition from diplomatic pitch deck talking points to non-negotiable construction deadlines.

The governing body’s decision was officially announced on Tuesday, September 15, 2026, during the 242nd World Athletics Council Meeting in Budapest, Hungary.

The council awarded the 2029 edition to the Kenyan capital while granting the 2031 hosting rights to Munich, concluding a highly competitive, multi-city bidding cycle that evaluated operational readiness, financial guarantees, and long-term athletic heritage.

"We were fortunate to have four outstanding bids for 2029 and 2031, and this was not an easy decision for the Council," World Athletics President Sebastian Coe said in Budapest. "Nairobi and Munich ultimately presented two compelling propositions. They offer very different but equally exciting opportunities for our sport, and I am delighted that we can now begin working with both cities to stage two world-class championships."

Nairobi’s breakthrough comes after a disappointing loss in its campaign for the 2025 championships. Rather than stepping back, local sports administrators used the setback to assemble a far more rigorous submission built on established hosting experience from the 2017 World Under-18 Championships, the 2021 World Under-20 Championships, and the annual Kip Keino Classic.

The selection committee’s evaluation noted Kenya’s unmatched fan engagement, citing internal metrics where 68 per cent of surveyed Kenyans expressed high interest in track and field, compared to a global baseline of 39 per cent. On the track, Kenya remains the second-most successful nation in World Athletics Championships history, coming off a strong 2025 campaign where national athletes brought home 11 medals, including seven golds.

The successful application was contingent on strict government guarantees to modernize the city's sports infrastructure. Capital expenditures are already directed toward the rehabilitation of the historic Kasarani and Nyayo stadiums, alongside the ongoing construction of the multi-billion-shilling Talanta Stadium.

Winning the vote completes the diplomatic leg of Kenya's athletics ambitions, but the hard reality of delivering international-standard infrastructure now rests entirely on local project management. Having beaten global powers in the boardroom, Nairobi must now ensure that stadium deadlines and logistics plans hold firm before the world arrives in 2029.
