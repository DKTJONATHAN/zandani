Farmers and residents in Nyandarua are facing a significant cold snap this week, with the Kenya Meteorological Service Authority (KMSA) forecasting nighttime temperatures to plummet to 5°C by Monday.

This sharp decline-the most extreme in the five-day outlook released for the period ending Thursday, September 24-poses a particular challenge for the region’s agricultural sector. While much of the country is contending with the arrival of light and moderate rainfall, the sustained cold in the Central highlands, where temperatures are expected to linger between 5°C and 6°C, threatens to impact livestock health and sensitive outdoor crops.

### A Divided Forecast
While Nyandarua faces the most severe thermal stress, the broader forecast describes a country split between damp conditions and cooling nights.

In Nairobi and Kiambu, nighttime temperatures are expected to hover between 13°C and 15°C. Similar conditions are anticipated across Nandi, Kericho, and Bomet, where thermometers will likely read between 12°C and 13°C. In Laikipia, residents should prepare for a range of 11°C to 13°C, while Nyeri will see nights settling between 13°C and 15°C. The KMSA has also flagged Wajir and Garissa for intermittent cold, a notable deviation for the typically warmer arid regions.

### Rainfall Distribution
Beyond the temperature concerns, the KMSA has mapped out a week of uneven precipitation.

Coastal counties, including Mombasa, Kwale, Kilifi, and Lamu, are set for moderate showers, particularly on Sunday. Kilifi is expected to experience the most consistent activity, with showers projected to persist through the end of the five-day window on Thursday.

In the Eastern lowlands, Kitui and Machakos will see light morning rains during the first half of the week, while Embu is slated for more persistent, daily rainfall. Central Kenya, specifically Nyeri and Murang’a, is expected to receive rainfall throughout the entire five-day period, marking a wetter stretch compared to the three-day light rain window forecasted for Nairobi and Kiambu.

Westward, the forecast includes a wide swath of the Rift Valley and Western Kenya, with Trans Nzoia, Uasin Gishu, Nandi, Nakuru, Narok, Kajiado, Kericho, Bomet, Kakamega, Vihiga, Bungoma, Homa Bay, Kisii, and Nyamira all expected to record rain.

As the week progresses, the primary concern for agricultural stakeholders remains the duration of the cold in the highlands, where the 5°C floor in Nyandarua serves as a reminder of the volatility in seasonal transitions.
