Advocate Danstan Omari took his trademark directness to the couch of *Oga Obinna Live*, puncturing growing online assumptions that a multi-million-shilling compensation plea from Sharon Otieno’s family could quietly settle the murder case against former Migori Governor Okoth Obado.

Addressing the buzz that followed reports of Sharon’s parents asking the court for leniency, Omari laid out the sharp boundary separating private reconciliation from the state's criminal justice system.

“Obado can be ordered to compensate for the death of Sharon and still go to jail for a life-or-death sentence. Sharon’s mother cannot withdraw the case!” Omari told host Oga Obinna.

### The KSh 4.7 Million Leniency Plea

The discussion on Obinna’s set was sparked by submissions from Sharon’s parents during the ongoing sentencing proceedings. The family told the court they had forgiven the former county boss, urging the judge to weigh reconciliation alongside the future welfare of Sharon’s surviving children and dependants.

To highlight the financial toll of the eight-year saga, the family presented an estimated KSh 4.7 million in expenses. That figure captured the heavy cost of attending years of court sessions in Nairobi, handling funeral arrangements, and supporting dependants left behind after the Rongo University student was murdered in 2018.

While the family's openness to compensation fueled social media speculation about a possible out-of-court resolution, Omari pointed out that such private arrangements have no power to erase a conviction.

### Why Criminal Law Rejects Private Buyouts

Speaking on the show, Omari broke down why capital offences cannot be treated like private civil disputes or customary settlements once the court has entered a guilty verdict.

In serious criminal cases, the state is the complainant prosecuting an offence against the public order. While victim impact statements and family pleas for mercy form part of the mitigation record, they do not dictate the bench's final decision.

“Once a person has been convicted of a serious criminal offence such as murder, the victim’s family does not have the power to withdraw the case and automatically terminate the proceedings,” Omari explained, stressing that the trial judge retains sole discretion over custodial terms.

Even if the court directs Obado to shoulder financial restitution for the KSh 4.7 million burden borne by the family, that monetary order would run alongside, rather than replace, any jail term the court sees fit to hand down.

### Awaiting the Final Sentence

Obado was convicted in July 2026 alongside his former personal assistant Michael Oyamo and former Migori County clerk Caspal Obiero over Sharon’s murder, closing a high-profile trial that drew national scrutiny.

With the trial now in its final phase, the High Court is weighing the prosecution’s closing arguments against the defence’s formal mitigation and the family's stated stance.

As Omari made clear under the studio lights, while Sharon's family is free to forgive and tally their financial losses, the gavel remains entirely in the hands of the sentencing judge.
