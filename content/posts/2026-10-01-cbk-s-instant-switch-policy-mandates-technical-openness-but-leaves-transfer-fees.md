Kenya’s monetary authorities are preparing to compel commercial banks, mobile network operators, and independent fintechs onto a shared digital highway, enforcing technical integration across the country's fragmented payment channels.

Under the newly released Draft National Payment System Policy dated August 2026, the National Treasury and the Central Bank of Kenya (CBK) propose building a national instant payment switch. The central infrastructure is designed to route transactions instantly between competing platforms, breaking down operational walls that currently slow or complicate cross-system transfers.

To power the switch, regulators plan to mandate open Application Programming Interface (API) standards across all licensed financial entities. This technical mandate will force legacy software systems at commercial banks and mobile wallet operators to exchange data seamlessly using uniform national or global messaging protocols.

### Opening the Rails to Non-Banks

![A photo collage of Central Bank of Kenya governor Kamau Thugge and shilling notes and coins](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/collage%20of%20cbk%20and%20shilling.jpg.webp?itok=enOZnWRy)

Beyond technical links, the draft policy redefines who can access Kenya's core clearing and settlement channels. Non-bank payment providers, long restricted to working through partner commercial banks, are slated to receive direct access to key payment infrastructure.

The regulatory framework introduces a dual mechanism of carrots and sticks to enforce compliance:

* **Compliance Audits:** Regular technical audits will check if financial firms meet operational uptime and message formatting standards.
* **Institutional Incentives:** Financial institutions that achieve seamless interoperability will be granted regulatory relief and operational incentives.
* **Testing Sandboxes:** A structured testing regime will evaluate emerging financial technologies before commercial rollout.
* **Legal Revisions:** Amendments to primary payment legislation will cement non-bank access rights and technical standards into law.

### The Pricing Discretion Gap

![Mobile money](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/mobile%20money.jpg.webp?itok=z9S5s3-G)

While the policy frames shared infrastructure as a path toward lower transaction expenses, it stops short of capping what service providers charge end users.

The policy document explicitly omits statutory tariff ceilings or fixed transaction fee brackets. As a result, any pass-through savings from reduced infrastructure costs will depend on commercial pricing decisions by individual banks and mobile operators rather than state price controls.

Without capped charges, financial institutions retain total discretion over cross-platform processing fees, leaving consumer cost reductions tied to market competition rather than regulatory decree.

### Next Operational Steps

Implementation now hinges on legislative amendments required to update Kenya's existing payment legal framework. The draft policy will undergo stakeholder review before CBK and Treasury gazette operational guidelines, establish technical API documentation, and define audit schedules for participating institutions.
