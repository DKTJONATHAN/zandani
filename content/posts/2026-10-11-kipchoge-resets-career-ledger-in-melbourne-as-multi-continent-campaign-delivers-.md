Race organizers in Australia confirmed Sunday that Eliud Kipchoge clocked two hours, nine minutes, and 10 seconds to win the Nike Melbourne Marathon, a victory that validates the veteran runner's tactical shift away from elite commercial majors into an independent global touring circuit.

The 41-year-old Kenyan distance runner trimmed two seconds off the previous Melbourne course record on October 11, ending a 37-month dry spell without a top-podium finish. Kipchoge’s previous marathon victory had come at the Berlin Marathon in September 2023.

The result in Victoria delivered a clear competitive dividend for Kipchoge, who pulled away from local runners Thomas Do Canto, who crossed second in 2:10:35, and Reece Edwards, who took third in 2:14:33.

### Stepping Off the World Marathon Majors Treadmill

![Kenyan Marathoner Eliud Kipchoge wins the Nike Melbourne Marathon on October 11, 2026, in Australia](https://i.ibb.co/QjfLNhhm/fec02895984f.webp)

The Melbourne race marks the third leg of a self-directed, two-year world campaign launched in May 2026, through which Kipchoge plans to compete across six continents, explicitly excluding Antarctica. The move has effectively redirected the two-time Olympic champion away from the unforgiving fields of the Abbott World Marathon Majors, where age and rising competition had blunted his dominance.

Between 2024 and 2025, Kipchoge experienced unprecedented competitive setbacks at the pinnacle of international road racing. He finished 10th at the 2024 Tokyo Marathon with a time of 2:06:50 before recording a DNF at the Paris Olympic Games later that year.

His 2025 campaign across London, Sydney, and New York yielded sixth, ninth, and 17th-place finishes, respectively. Those results signaled a closing window against top-tier fields setting sub-2:03 paces.

### Calibration on Regional Roads

![Kenyan Marathoner Eliud Kipchoge wins the Nike Melbourne Marathon on October 11, 2026, in Australia](https://i.ibb.co/8D9pY8yQ/baf9de23b68f.webp)

The pivot to an intercontinental tour initially exposed continued fitness and transition hurdles across varied topographies and conditions.

Kipchoge opened the campaign in May 2026 at the Cape Town Marathon in South Africa, where he crossed the line in 16th place with a time of 2:13:29. Two months later, his South American stop at Brazil’s Porto Alegre Marathon in July resulted in a 12th-place finish.

In Melbourne, however, Kipchoge found the tactical rhythm that eluded him in earlier legs. While his 2:09:10 mark sits more than eight minutes outside his peak historical capabilities, it proved fast enough to dictate terms against a regional field and establish a new benchmark for the Australian course.

With Africa, South America, and Oceania now logged within a five-month span, Kipchoge's two-year timeline leaves stops across North America, Europe, and Asia to complete the six-continent circuit before mid-2028.
