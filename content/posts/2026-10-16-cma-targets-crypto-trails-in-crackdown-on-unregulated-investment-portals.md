Regulatory enforcement against digital investment schemes is pivoting from blanket public cautions to the forensic collection of blockchain data, as investigators target anonymous wallet transfers moving through rogue online platforms.

In a joint directive with the Capital Markets Fraud Investigations Unit (CMFIU), the Capital Markets Authority (CMA) has trained its sights on Global Investment Group and QVSE. Rather than relying solely on compliance checks, investigators are actively hunting for the digital breadcrumbs left behind by suspicious transfers.

![CMA Chief Executive FCPA Wyckliffe Shamiah](https://i.ibb.co/Sz9JL6j/ce98d60bf6a0.webp)

The regulator has issued a specific appeal for cryptocurrency wallet addresses and transaction identification hashes linked to the two entities. These technical identifiers are critical for mapping the flow of funds across decentralized networks where traditional banking controls do not apply.

“Any person who has information relating to the cryptocurrency wallet addresses associated with suspected fraudulent activities and transaction hashes/IDs linked to suspicious transfers by these entities is requested to share any information to support ongoing investigations,” the authority stated via its whistleblower portal and official channels.

This digital-first forensic push underscores the shifting nature of financial policing in Kenya. Online platforms have lowered the barrier for citizens seeking alternative savings vehicles and high-yield returns, but they have also created opaque conduits for capital flight and unregistered deposit-taking.

![A person counting money in Kenyan currency.](https://i.ibb.co/Q79SyNsz/c424e1a464bf.webp)

The current investigation builds on an aggressive expansion of regulatory oversight. Last month, on September 12, the regulator flagged 15 separate platforms accused of operating without authorization, soliciting capital across forex trading, cryptocurrency, and money market schemes. Those cases have since drawn in the Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI).

Despite successive warnings, retail investors continue to be drawn to online portals promising outsized returns. The regulator maintains that verifying a firm's licensing status through its official register remains the primary safeguard against capital loss, though the proliferation of anonymous digital assets complicates recovery once funds are transferred.

Investigators have not yet detailed the total volume of funds tied to Global Investment Group or QVSE, nor the specific mechanisms used to solicit capital from retail participants.
