Prince Indah traded the familiar lakeside stages of Western Kenya for the humid ocean breeze of Nyali, anchoring the latest regional deployment of Johnnie Walker’s AfroExchange tour at Kingston Beach Resort.

The performance represented a deliberate geographic and sonic leap for the popular Ohangla and Afro Rhumba artist—locally known as Janabi—transplanting the heavy percussive rhythms of Nyanza directly into a Swahili coastal setting near Nyali City Mall. Rather than staging a standard urban gig in Nairobi, the production used the beachside venue to force a cultural meeting point between two distinct Kenyan identities: the Lake Region and the Coast.

![Prince Indah Takes Ohangla to Mombasa as Johnnie Walker's AfroExchange Celebrates Kenyan Culture](https://i.ibb.co/33TbVPB/7b849fa5f247.webp)

Behind the cross-country routing is a calculated brand-activation strategy. Speaking on the mechanics of the initiative, Johnnie Walker Brand Manager Elizabeth Otieno explained that the platform is engineered to expose regional audiences to unfamiliar African music cultures while forging commercial and communal connections through shared live experiences.

"At Johnnie Walker, we believe that African culture moves forward when we open ourselves to new experiences, embrace different perspectives and bring people together," Otieno stated at the Mombasa showcase, framing the event as a deliberate bridge to introduce Lake Region cultural depth to coastal consumers.

![What to Know About Goalkeeper Jesús Owono After Fatal Spain Collision](https://i.ibb.co/ymkqLLz4/5d8961a731fd.webp)

The Mombasa stop is part of a broader, sustained itinerary across the country. The AfroExchange platform has previously planted its footprint in Kisumu, Nanyuki, and Nairobi, systematically rotating different musical expressions, fashion aesthetics, and regional identities through corporate-backed events rooted in the brand's 'Keep Walking: One Step Closer' philosophy.

For Prince Indah, the high-profile coastal deployment underscores the widening commercial reach of Ohangla outside its traditional stronghold, testing whether regional vernacular sounds can successfully anchor national experiential marketing campaigns on neutral territory.
