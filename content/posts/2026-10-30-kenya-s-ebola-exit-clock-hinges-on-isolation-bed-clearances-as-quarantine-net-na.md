Kenya's bid to initiate the internationally mandated 42-day countdown to be declared free of Ebola remains tethered to laboratory confirmations in isolation wards, even as authorities prepare to discharge dozens of monitored contacts after a 10-day lull in new infections.

While the epidemiological curve has flattened across the country following nine confirmed cases and two deaths, the formal timeline to close out the emergency cannot legally begin while active viral shedding remains under clinical management.

Addressing the ninth Ebola response task force meeting on Friday, Public Health Principal Secretary Mary Muthoni outlined a phased exit plan for the individuals still held under institutional observation. Under the schedule, 46 quarantined contacts who have shown no symptoms are slated to complete their monitoring protocol and return home on Tuesday, November 3.

The remaining individuals in containment are set to be released in tranches over the subsequent eight days, with authorities targeting November 11 to conclude institutional quarantine for the current cohort.

### The Clinical Logjam in Isolation

Yet the decisive hurdle for health managers lies inside treatment centers rather than quarantine facilities. Before the World Health Organization protocol allows Kenya to start the 42-day clock—equivalent to two full incubation cycles of the virus—every confirmed patient must test negative twice, separated by a mandatory clinical interval.

Health Director General Dr. Patrick Amoth disclosed that two of the seven patients currently receiving supportive treatment in isolation facilities have registered their first negative tests for the virus. Both individuals are undergoing a mandatory 72-hour observation window before repeat laboratory tests determine whether they are cleared for discharge.

The remaining five hospitalized patients are listed in stable condition, with medical teams conducting continuous clinical evaluations. Until those five achieve double-negative lab clearances, the national countdown remains at zero regardless of how many days pass without new community admissions.

### Unwinding the 135-Person Tracing Web

![Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale, during a meeting at Afya House, with an inset of an Ebola Virus Bundibugyo Strain under a microscope.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/aden-duale-ebola-virus.jpg.webp?itok=0ojPUx92)

The pending release of the 46 contacts represents the second major cohort to clear containment. An initial group of 20 people completed their 21-day quarantine cycle without developing symptoms and returned to their communities on October 26.

Disease surveillance teams have successfully tracked down all 135 primary and secondary contacts flagged since the index case was identified. That tracing operation extended across borders, encompassing nine contacts who had traveled outside the country and were placed under cross-jurisdictional surveillance protocols.

Despite the progressive clearance of the contact register, public health officials are resisting calls to lower operational vigilance. Muthoni noted that disease surveillance at formal land border points, maritime terminals, and international airports will remain on heightened alert to insulate the health system against potential cross-border reinfection.

County health departments have also been instructed to maintain supplies of personal protective equipment for frontline clinical workers handling routine triage, while the national emergency center continues monitoring community alerts through the 719 emergency platform.

For public health administrators, the coming week presents an operational balance: returning cleared citizens to their homes while awaiting the secondary lab results that determine whether Kenya can finally begin counting down the six weeks needed to declare the outbreak over.
