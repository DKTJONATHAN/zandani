A persistent contraction in formal dairy intake that has driven urban retail milk prices up to Ksh80 per half-litre packet has forced an unconventional intervention from the state: importing dairy buffaloes and superior camel breeds to break Kenya’s reliance on exotic cow herds.

Speaking at the Agriculture and Food Security Summit at Jamhuri Park in Nairobi on Friday, October 9, President William Ruto instructed Agriculture Principal Secretary Jonathan Mueke to initiate procurement frameworks for high-yielding camel genetics and commercial water buffaloes.

The directive comes as formal processors struggle to secure raw volumes against a backdrop of prolonged dry spells in high-yield dairy belts. Deliveries received by commercial dairies fell from 84.4 million litres in June to 81.3 million litres in July 2026, with the downward trend extending through August and September.

### The Squeeze on Urban Shelves

The supply deficit has translated rapidly to retail counters in Nairobi, Mombasa, and Kisumu. Standard 500ml fresh milk pouches, which retailed between Ksh60 and Ksh70 earlier in the year, now command between Ksh75 and Ksh80.

That margin pressure has begun splintering the country's distribution machinery. Facing rising feed and overhead costs, a growing segment of smallholder farmers has bypassed formal cooperative collection centres and cooling stations entirely, diverting raw milk directly to neighbourhood vendors and unregulated brokers who offer faster cash settlements.

![President William Ruto attending the Agriculture and Food Security Transformation Summit at Jamhuri ASK Showground, Nairobi, on Thursday, October 8, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/bR7QYkyt/18dcdd6adc0c.webp)

Addressing the summit, Ruto argued that diversifying the national dairy herd beyond traditional cattle breeds would provide a biological buffer against recurring climatic shocks.

"Currently, we have a slight shortage of milk due to drought," Ruto said. "Camels have more milk than cows. We will ensure we bring in a better breed."

### Eyeing Asian Commercial Models

To justify introducing water buffaloes—an animal virtually absent from commercial livestock farming in East Africa—the President pointed to the Asian subcontinent, where the species underpins vast commercial dairy operations.

“In India there are buffaloes. Buffaloes also produce a lot of milk,” Ruto told delegates. “There is no problem in that. So PS Mueke, do as we had agreed. Bring in buffalo and also bring in a better breed of camel because we need to diversify our milk sources.”

The President also pointed to the commercial disparity already evident in Nairobi’s retail market, where camel milk commands a premium well above conventional cow milk.

![water bufallo](https://i.ibb.co/1Jbj9hn7/1eb4e03c7159.webp)

“Here in Nairobi, the camel's milk is about Ksh190 and is selling at almost Ksh200, beating cow's milk three times,” he noted. “We need to start thinking.”

While camel milk has historically remained confined to arid northern counties and specialized urban retail niches, shifting consumer habits and higher retail returns have elevated its commercial standing.

### Operational Hurdles Ahead

Translating the executive order into measurable litres on retail shelves will require the Ministry of Agriculture to navigate significant logistical and regulatory terrain.

Introducing commercial dairy buffaloes requires establishing dedicated breeding protocols, disease management guidelines, and feed strategies adapted to local agro-ecological zones. Unlike cows, water buffaloes require specific wallowing habitats and feed formulations to sustain high lactation yields.

Similarly, scaling camel milk production to relieve pressure on urban centres depends heavily on cold-chain infrastructure along arid transport corridors, where remote production centres remain detached from central processing plants.

With formal milk intakes continuing to slide into the final quarter of 2026, the immediate test rests with PS Mueke’s desk to outline import timelines, quarantine clearances, and the breed distribution strategy for smallholder farmers currently navigating high operational costs and shrinking farmgate margins.
