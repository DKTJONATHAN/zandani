Digital content creator Kelvin Kinuthia met a major global health advisory with characteristic humor this week, responding to a United States Food and Drug Administration warning regarding popular weight-loss injections after spending years publicly documenting his own clinical body transformation.

Reacting to an online post outlining the regulatory alert—which links medications such as Ozempic and Mounjaro to a rare eye condition that can cause permanent vision loss—Kinuthia left a brief comment that quickly drew widespread attention from his audience.

“Eeiiyy nionge kwa kusimama ama kwa kuketi,” Kinuthia wrote, lightheartedly poking fun at the medical advisory.

The creator’s reaction resonated across online platforms precisely because Kinuthia has maintained one of the most transparent public records of medical weight management among Kenyan public figures. Rather than treating weight loss as a secret routine, he has openly shared dates, specific clinic names, procedures, and step-by-step scale milestones.

On October 20, 2026, Kinuthia shared side-by-side comparison photographs contrasting his physique in October 2023 with his progress in October 2026. Over that three-year window, his total weight dropped from 148kg down to 112kg, representing a overall reduction of 36kg.

That milestone followed a series of distinct medical interventions. Kinuthia first underwent a gastric balloon procedure in 2022 to kickstart his weight management. By May 2025, he introduced injectable GLP-1 treatments, documenting a 5kg drop during his initial week on Ozempic shots.

More recently, Kinuthia transitioned to Mounjaro injections. On October 5, 2026, he publicly credited local facility Nure Aesthetics for guiding his recent progress, revealing that Mounjaro helped him shed 10kg within a single month, bringing his weight down from 125kg to 115kg before he subsequently hit the 112kg mark later that month.

Kinuthia’s humorous remark came as the FDA formally instructed drug manufacturers to update official safety labeling for the broad class of GLP-1 receptor agonists, which includes Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, and Zepbound.

The updated warnings center on nonarteritic anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy, commonly known as NAION. The medical condition occurs when blood flow to the optic nerve is sudden and severely reduced, potentially leading to sudden, painless vision impairment or loss, typically restricted to one eye.

According to regulatory data, clinical review panels identified a slightly higher frequency of reported NAION cases among patients using GLP-1 medications compared to unexposed control groups. However, health authorities stressed that the condition remains statistically rare and does not affect the vast majority of patients undergoing treatment.

Under the new guidelines, regulators advise patients using GLP-1 therapies to seek immediate medical evaluation if they experience sudden vision changes, blurred eyesight, or persistent blind spots. The agency also emphasized that individuals should consult their prescribing physicians before making any unilateral decisions to stop or adjust their medication schedules.

For Kinuthia, whose public brand has integrated candid health updates alongside lifestyle content, the global medical directive adds a sobering layer of context to an ongoing transformation that thousands of followers continue to watch in real time.
