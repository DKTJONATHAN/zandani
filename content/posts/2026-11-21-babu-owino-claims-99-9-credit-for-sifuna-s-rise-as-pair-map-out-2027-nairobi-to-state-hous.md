Babu Owino is not just partnering with Edwin Sifuna for 2027—he is auditing the Nairobi Senator's political resume with decimal-point precision.

Speaking on his political alignments within the Linda Mwananchi movement, the Embakasi East MP made a striking assertion regarding his role in his colleague's political trajectory, claiming he is personally responsible for 99.9 percent of Sifuna’s growth. Despite the outsized claim, Owino maintained that the bond between the two remains unbreakable as they finalize distinct ballots for the upcoming General Election.

Under the blueprint laid out by the legislators, Sifuna is positioned as the presidential flagbearer for the Linda Mwananchi movement, while Owino sets his sights on capturing the Nairobi gubernatorial seat. Far from anticipating a collision course between two dominant personalities operating in the same political ecosystem, Owino insisted that their joint ascent is entirely frictionless.

“Edwin Sifuna is my brother. I have contributed 99.9 per cent in his growth, and I still want him to grow,” Owino stated during a media interview. “Within Linda Mwananchi movement, he’s our presidential candidate. Babu is the next governor of Nairobi. There’s no problem if we both grow.”

The positioning comes alongside maneuvers to integrate the Mwananchi Party as a core pillar of the broader movement. Rather than viewing the outfit as a splinter threat, Owino framed the party as an essential structural tool designed to capture fragmented urban loyalties that might not otherwise transfer cleanly down a single ballot.

Addressing the distinct voter sentiments across the capital, the lawmaker pointed out a pragmatic electoral reality: certain urban constituencies lean heavily toward Sifuna while holding reservations about Owino, and vice versa. By utilizing a separate political vehicle tethered to the broader coalition, the two figures aim to pool overlapping supporters while catching voters who might alienate themselves from one camp but remain willing to back the other.

“There are people who like Sifuna and don’t like Babu and vice versa. Some like both of us. People who don’t like him, won’t they go to him through Babu Owino? And they need a vehicle,” Owino explained.

The framework leaves the coalition's legislative machinery intact as both leaders prepare to test their independent electoral weight against rival camps ahead of 2027.
