For Kenyan comedian DJ Shiti, a routine journey to the countryside turned into a reflective pilgrimage through his family's past. Passing through the Posta estate in Nakuru, the entertainer paused to look back at the neighborhood where he grew up during a time when his family was still complete.

The reflections emerged on Tuesday, October 6, 2026, as the comedian continues to navigate the mourning period following the death of his father. Sharing a snapshot of the familiar residential grounds online, he noted how quickly years slip away.

“My Childhood Estate Posta Nakuru.. I passed by nikija ocha… moments don’t last but memories do… days when everyone was alive,” he wrote, capturing the stark contrast between childhood stability and adult loss.

Yet the solemn mood did not last long before the comedian pivoted to familiar village grievances. Alongside his reflections on mortality and memory, DJ Shiti turned his attention to rural folklore and local nuisances, calling out nighttime disturbances in his usual theatrical style.

“Jamani night runner tunaomboleza ni nini kutusumbua unakata kamba za nguo na kugwara mabati?…Mamangu ameninunulia nyahunyo,” he posted, taking issue with someone tampering with clotheslines and scratching iron sheets while the family was trying to mourn in peace.

To drive the point home, the comedian issued a mock-serious warning anchored in his family lineage, invoking his late mother’s name to ward off the midnight trespasser.

“Utajua mimi ni lastborn wa dada Mary Goreti Ndede ntakutandika!” he added.

The juxtaposition highlights how public personalities often navigate personal grief by blending raw vulnerability with the distinct brand of local humor that built their careers, moving seamlessly from estate nostalgia to village gatekeeping as he completed his journey to the countryside.
