Strict statutory limits governing commercial timber harvesting—specifically a mandatory 6:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. working window—have become the main line of defense for state foresters facing an unprecedented wave of citizen surveillance.

The Kenya Forest Service (KFS) issued a formal clarification on Sunday, October 25, pushing back against viral video reports that alleged illegal nocturnal logging inside the Ngong Hills Forest reserve. According to KFS, the tree felling visible in circulating footage is part of a fully vetted, 56-hectare commercial timber concession situated within the Kibiku Forest block.

The agency stated that the timber rights were awarded through a competitive bidding process under the Public Procurement and Asset Disposal Act and the Forest Conservation and Management Act. Responding directly to social media posts claiming that contractors were cutting trees under the cover of darkness, KFS attributed the late-night presence of timber vehicles to a mechanical breakdown rather than unauthorized harvesting.

"The forest harvesting operations are required to take place between 6:00 am and 6:00 pm, as stipulated under the Forest Conservation and Management Act," KFS said, adding that photographic evidence circulated online failed to establish active night-time felling. The service noted that the stalled vehicle was undergoing repairs on site when it was recorded.

### Concession Boundaries and Community Replanting

Under standard commercial plantation guidelines, cleared blocks are slated for immediate reforestation using local labor networks. KFS confirmed that the local Community Forest Association has been mobilized to participate in replanting the 56-hectare Kibiku site through the Plantation Enterprise and Livelihood Improvement Scheme (PELIS). The scheme allows surrounding communities to cultivate short-term food crops alongside newly planted tree seedlings until the forest canopy closes.

However, the speed with which the Kibiku footage spread highlights a sharp contraction in public trust regarding state-managed canopy loss. Citizen watchfulness has intensified following a series of unexplained or controversial tree-clearing incidents across both rural ecosystems and urban corridors.

In August, KFS launched an internal investigation at the Saboti Forest Station within Trans Nzoia County's Mount Elgon ecosystem after 350 young trees in the Makunga forest block were cut down overnight and abandoned on site.

### Urban Expansion Drives Broader Pushback

The friction in Ngong Hills mirrors broader public resistance to tree removal in built-up areas. Earlier in October, Nairobi residents raised alarms over the felling of mature roadside trees along State House Road, where local reports indicated clearing was underway to accommodate a proposed dual carriageway expansion.

Similarly, in Nakuru, Governor Susan Kihika issued a public warning to the Kenya Urban Roads Authority (KURA), demanding that the infrastructure agency cease cutting down historic Jacaranda trees without explicit county government authorization and environmental clearance.

In response to the public outcry, KFS is urging citizens to verify logging claims directly with local forest stations before publishing footage online, while directing reports of genuine illegal activity to the police. As timber harvesting continues across the 56-hectare Kibiku block within legal daylight hours, the agency faces ongoing pressure to maintain public transparency over commercial logging schedules and post-harvest replanting timelines.
