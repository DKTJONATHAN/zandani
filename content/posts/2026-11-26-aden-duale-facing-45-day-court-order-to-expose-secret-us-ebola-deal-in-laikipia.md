Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale is facing a strict 45-day judicial clock to expose all classified documentation surrounding a controversial US-backed Ebola quarantine facility at the Laikipia Air Base. 

On Thursday, November 26, 2026, High Court Justice Patricia Nyaundi brought all ongoing works and operationalization at the installation to a full stop, ruling that Duale and the state violated constitutional standards by establishing the center in secret and without public involvement.

Delivering judgment on consolidated petitions filed by the Katiba Institute and the Law Society of Kenya (LSK) against Duale and Attorney General Dorcas Oduor, the court established that the Health Cabinet Secretary personally breached national values and governance principles set out in Article 10 of the Constitution. Justice Nyaundi further declared that the government violated citizens' rights under Article 35(3) by concealing vital project details from the public.

The facility was specifically designed to isolate and treat American nationals exposed to the Ebola virus. Under the High Court's directives, the state has been given 45 days to publish the entire bilateral agreement entered into with the United States government.

The mandatory disclosure order encompasses:
- All parliamentary, regulatory, and Laikipia County government approvals.
- Comprehensive biosafety, public health, environmental, and security impact assessments.
- Standard operating protocols detailing how Ebola-exposed individuals would be admitted, handled, isolated, and treated at the military site.

Justice Nyaundi issued an injunction prohibiting Duale from conducting any further construction or opening the facility until the High Court personally reviews and verifies total compliance with the disclosure mandate.

Katiba Institute Executive Director Nora Mbagathi welcomed the ruling as a crucial victory against executive overreach. Mbagathi stressed that decisions carrying direct consequences for public health and safety cannot be hidden from citizens, confirming that the Laikipia site will remain completely frozen until the judicial conditions are satisfied.

The legal showdown began on May 28, 2026, when the Katiba Institute filed an urgent petition hours before the Ministry of Health's planned launch, responding to mounting anxiety among Laikipia residents over safety protocols. The case escalated in June 2026 when Duale was cited for contempt of court after construction work quietly continued at the air base despite an active judicial stay.

With the 45-day timeline now active, the Health Ministry and the Office of the Attorney General must make the complete bilateral file public or face a permanent judicial block on the Laikipia facility.
