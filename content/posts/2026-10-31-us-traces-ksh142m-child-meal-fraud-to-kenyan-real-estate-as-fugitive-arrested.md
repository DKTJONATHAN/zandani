United States federal prosecutors say proceeds from a Ksh142 million child nutrition scheme in Minnesota were directly routed into Kenyan real estate, vehicle purchases, and family bank accounts before a 10-month cross-border fugitive run ended in arrest.

The Department of Justice disclosed the financial trail on Friday, October 30, naming a 54-year-old Kenyan national among 13 individuals indicted across wider schemes targeting Medicaid and Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) coffers. The suspect was taken into custody on September 30 after spending nearly a year hiding in Kenya.

According to charging documents, the primary beneficiaries of the siphoned welfare funds were the suspect, his son, and asset holdings in Kenya, where diverted taxpayer money was converted into physical property.

### The Anatomy of the 6,000-Meal Claim

Investigators established that the defendant integrated into the fraudulent network through Feeding Our Future, an organisation that acted as a sponsor for federal child nutrition disbursements during the pandemic. 

![The United States Department of Justice addressing the press regarding the ghost student fraud scheme involving Kenyans on Thursday, October 29, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/u.s-department-of-justice-1.jpg.webp?itok=wUQTPIU8)

Federal filings allege the suspect and his associates claimed to operate two large-scale distribution sites serving a combined 6,000 meals to needy children every day across a four-month window:

* **Bilaal Mosque in Pelican Rapids:** The group filed documentation claiming to serve more than 3,000 children daily.
* **Multicultural Resource Centre in Moorhead:** Suspects reported an identical tally of another 3,000 daily rations.

Federal agents concluded the attendance registers and daily child tallies were manufactured wholesale. The fabricated documentation allowed the syndicate to collect more than Ksh142 million in federal reimbursements for food that was never prepared or distributed.

### Offshore Routing and Asset Purchases

Rather than procuring pantry staples and funding kitchen operations, court filings indicate the suspect rapidly moved the public reimbursements into private conduits. Prosecutors detailed that the 54-year-old issued large check payments to himself and his son, acquired a personal vehicle, and wired capital abroad to finance property purchases within Kenya.

“He laundered his share of the proceeds through large check payments to himself and his son, as well as the purchase of a property in Kenya and a car,” the prosecution noted in its filings.

![The Federal Bureau of Investigations (FBI) during a past operation in the United States.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/fbi-during-an-operation.jpg.webp?itok=rN3uWrxR)

Following the collapse of the Feeding Our Future network under federal scrutiny, the defendant fled the United States to Kenya, remaining out of reach of American law enforcement for nearly 10 months before being apprehended at the end of September.

### Widening Cross-Border Scrutiny

The unsealing of the nutrition charges coincides with an aggressive clampdown by US authorities on financial crime networks operating between East Africa and North America. Just a day prior, on Thursday, October 29, the Justice Department indicted multiple Kenyan nationals in a separate Ksh17 billion student aid fraud syndicate.

That multi-agency offensive, designated Operation Ghostbusters, exposed synthetic identity rings using stolen American credentials to siphon college loans and educational grants. During that probe, federal investigators froze approximately Ksh97 million held in a cryptocurrency wallet linked directly to a Kenyan operator.

Addressing the coordinated court actions, Assistant Attorney General Colin McDonald stated that federal enforcement agencies are structuring investigations to penetrate foreign sanctuaries and seize offshored assets.

McDonald affirmed that authorities will track illicit welfare outflows into secondary overseas accounts, indicating that federal recovery efforts will continue targeting real estate and liquid holdings tied to the Minnesota indictments.
