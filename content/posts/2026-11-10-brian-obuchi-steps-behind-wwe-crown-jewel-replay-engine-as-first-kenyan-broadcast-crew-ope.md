Brian Obuchi sat behind an EVS broadcast console on Saturday, November 7, controlling the exact instant replays and slow-motion sequences that delivered WWE Crown Jewel 2026 to global television audiences. The Multimedia University alumnus stepped directly into the technical nerve center of World Wrestling Entertainment’s live production, marking the first time a Kenyan broadcast specialist has operated inside WWE’s live ringside television crew.

In a video walkthrough published from the event's production area, Obuchi offered a direct look at the technical hardware responsible for turning live wrestling maneuvers into immediate visual replays. Operating the EVS server—the industry-standard digital replay machine used across premier live sports and entertainment broadcasts—Obuchi demonstrated how live multi-angle camera feeds are selected, clipped, and piped back into the international broadcast feed in real time as matches unfold.

"Hii inaitwa EVS. Hii ni machine ya kufanya replays and slow motion," Obuchi explained while guiding followers through his workstation inside the venue. "Today is a very special day to me because I am part of the crew that is doing WWE wrestling live."

The milestone represents a complete shift in role for the Kenyan media professional, who built his early interest in wrestling television during childhood broadcasts in Kenya featuring classic WWE performers such as The Rock, The Undertaker, and John Cena. Rather than remaining a passive television viewer, Obuchi mapped his professional path through media training at Multimedia University before securing position inside international broadcast ringside teams.

Reflecting on his progression in social media updates following the event, Obuchi highlighted the transition from watching wrestling programming on local feeds to controlling the technical delivery behind major global spectacles.

"Growing up in Kenya, I watched WWE Wrestling on television, fascinated by legends like The Rock, The Undertaker and John Cena," Obuchi stated. "Little did I know that one day I would go from watching these productions on TV to working behind the scenes of a major WWE event."

In live television production—particularly fast-moving combat entertainment like WWE Crown Jewel where major physical spots require instant visual emphasis—the EVS operator holds immediate responsibility for capturing multi-angle action and feeding pristine replay clips to the broadcast director. A missed cue or delayed slow-motion cut directly impacts the viewing experience for millions watching live worldwide.

By taking his seat inside the technical engine room for the November 7 Crown Jewel broadcast, Obuchi demonstrated how Kenyan media graduates are establishing operational footprints inside international live entertainment systems long dominated by foreign production crews.

"From a WWE fan in Kenya to working on a global WWE production, what a journey! Proud to raise the Kenyan flag on the international stage," he added.

The successful live execution of Crown Jewel 2026 adds a major international credential to Obuchi's broadcast portfolio, establishing a concrete practical reference for East African technical crews pursuing roles in global media franchises.
