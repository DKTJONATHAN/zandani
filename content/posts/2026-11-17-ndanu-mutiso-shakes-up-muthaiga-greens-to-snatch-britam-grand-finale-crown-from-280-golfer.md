Ndanu Mutiso stepped onto the first tee at Muthaiga Golf Club expecting nothing more than a relaxed Friday morning stroll, a decent flight of golf, and a spot of fairway camaraderie. By dusk, the handicap-22 player was hoisting the season’s biggest corporate golf silverware, having outplayed more than 280 competitors to claim the Britam Golf Series Grand Finale title.

Her route to the podium was an exercise in calm, low-stakes execution that caught the seasoned field off guard at Nairobi’s venerable "Home of Golf." Teaming up in a high-energy four-ball alongside Benjamin Mukhisa—who arrived fresh off his own triumph at the series’ Kigali leg—Mutiso turned what was meant to be an easy day out into a clinical points haul.

She carded 40 Stableford points, matching the clubhouse leaders and sealing the overall crown on countback.

"I just came out to have fun and was not expecting this victory," Mutiso said after the prize-giving ceremony. "I had a pleasant flight and was fortunate to play alongside inspiring golfers, including the Kigali leg winner. The motivation within our group pushed us to be very clinical on the course."

Mutiso pointed to steady, low-risk course management on a day when chilly weather and deliberate greens tested the patience of more aggressive hitters. She kept stray shots off the scorecard and let her flatstick do the heavy lifting when it mattered most, noting that while the greens were slightly on the slow side, the course layout rewarded disciplined patience.

Her surprise triumph capped a high-stakes finale that drew a packed roster of business executives, seasoned club regulars, and regional visitors to Muthaiga on November 13.

Behind Mutiso, the leaderboard turned into a tight scramble across the day's major categories. Ndiga Githae captured the men’s division title after carding 40 points, mirroring Mutiso's tally, while Esther Waithera matched the 40-point benchmark to secure the women’s crown.

The sharpest individual card of the day, however, came from Britam’s internal ranks. Financial adviser Anne Wanjama posted 42 Stableford points to finish as the top-performing staff player. In the age-grade categories, Mumbi Gatu commanded the junior contest with a round of 38 points, while Jonathan Siano collected the senior title on 36 points.

The Muthaiga finale marked the end of a multi-month circuit that Britam Group Managing Director and CEO Tom Gitogo framed around networking and cross-border community building rather than purely boardroom interaction.

"What we value most about this series is the opportunity it gives us to engage with our customers, partners and friends beyond business," Gitogo said during the clubhouse address, hinting at plans to retain the regional footprint for future editions.

The 2026 circuit had traversed three countries after teeing off at Sigona Golf Club on February 7, stopping at Great Rift Valley Lodge before crossing into Uganda. The third leg at Uganda Golf Club on February 28 pulled in more than 200 players through morning downpours, complemented by an on-site tree-planting drive.

From Kampala, the tour made rounds at Ruiru Sports Club, checked into Rwanda for the Kigali Golf Club leg on May 16, and rolled through Kenya's regional fairways: Thika, Karen, Eldoret, Nyali, Limuru, Nanyuki, Nakuru, Kericho, Kitale, and Nyahururu.

For Mutiso, the broader corporate itinerary ended where her personal breakthrough began. While the tour left behind thousands of newly planted trees across regional clubs, her countback victory at Muthaiga proved that on the fairways, walking up with modest expectations and a steady putter can still take down the most competitive field in town.
