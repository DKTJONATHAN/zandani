The Kenya Ports Authority (KPA) is facing renewed scrutiny over its terminal safety protocols after a heavy-machinery accident at the Port of Mombasa toppled shipping containers and injured a gantry crane operator.

The incident, which occurred at approximately 3:40 p.m. on Thursday, September 17, 2026, involved a Rubber-Tyred Gantry (RTG) crane—the massive specialized vehicles used to stack and organize shipping containers in the port’s high-density yards. While a truck tractor operator involved in the mishap escaped uninjured, the RTG crane operator sustained soft-tissue injuries to his legs. He was rushed to a nearby hospital for emergency assessment and was later discharged after being cleared by medical personnel.

The accident came to public attention after mobile phone footage began circulating on social media, showing a chaotic scene of stacked containers that had crashed onto the terminal tarmac. KPA emergency and safety response teams were seen cordoning off the area around the damaged crane and debris.

In a statement addressing the incident, KPA did not disclose the precise mechanical failure or operational error that caused the crane to destabilize. Instead, the port management focused on managing the public fallout, urging Kenyans to refrain from sharing unverified footage of terminal disruptions.

"We urge the public to rely on official KPA communication channels for accurate and verified information and to refrain from sharing unverified reports that may cause unnecessary alarm or misinformation," the authority stated.

Despite the call for calm, the mishap has refocused attention on the hazardous nature of heavy logistics operations at East Africa's busiest maritime gateway. This is the second high-profile industrial accident at the harbor within months.

Earlier this year, a fatal accident occurred at Berth 14 of the Port of Mombasa, where a cargo rigger was crushed and killed by heavy pipes that rolled over him during offloading operations from the vessel *MV Hin Hong Bao Shi*.

KPA has launched a formal investigation to determine the root cause of the crane failure on Thursday. The port management indicated that the probe will be conducted strictly under its established safety and incident management guidelines.

While KPA has committed to implementing "corrective measures" once the investigation concludes, the recurring equipment and handling failures raise ongoing questions about how the port balances its round-the-clock commercial pressure with safety protections for its frontline workforce.
