Suba North MP Millie Odhiambo has shut down speculation that she is plotting a run for the Homa Bay gubernatorial seat, adopting the cheeky moniker of a political "fence-leaper" to describe her position inside an increasingly divided ODM.

Appearing on Citizen TV, the National Assembly Minority Whip dismissed rumors that county leadership featured anywhere in her political ambitions for 2027. The clarification comes as Odhiambo navigates persistent friction within ODM following the October 2025 death of party leader Raila Odinga and the subsequent creation of a broad-based government arrangement with the ruling United Democratic Alliance (UDA).

"I am in ODM. I am in broad-based. But if there’s something wrong there, I will mention it for what it is," Odhiambo said, pointing to her leadership post in the National Assembly as proof of her standing. "If I was not a member of ODM, I wouldn’t be a minority whip."

The Suba North legislator has found herself under heavy fire from internal party rivals who labeled her a "fence-sitter" after she publicly questioned the legal structure of the broad-based government in February 2025. At the time, Odhiambo argued that ODM was cooperating with the State without formal legal integration, a distinction that drew sharp rebukes from party colleagues.

When ODM chairperson and Homa Bay Governor Gladys Wanga dissolved the internal Linda Ground faction in May 2026, Odhiambo doubled down on her independence, refusing to pledge allegiance to any side while asking sarcastically whether her stance had upgraded her status to a "fence-leaper."

That political tightrope walk recently boiled over into public hostility when Odhiambo was heckled during an appearance in Kisumu—a moment that highlighted mounting voter frustration in Nyanza over politicians operating across the boundary between opposition politics and the broad-based administration.

Odhiambo pushed back against claims of personal animosity between herself and Governor Wanga, though the two remain at odds over critical party strategy ahead of the 2027 General Election. Wanga has insisted that ODM must enforce strict political zoning before signing any binding coalition agreement with UDA. Odhiambo and a bloc of parliamentary allies have warned that pushing for early zoning risks blowing up the broad-based coalition before it matures.

The Suba North MP has also broken ranks over the high-profile exit of former ODM Secretary General Edwin Sifuna, who departed his post after eight years to map out a separate political trajectory following internal leadership disputes.

Despite the vocal clashes, Odhiambo claimed that her periods of public silence are strategic rather than calculated retreat.

"There are things that are good, that I can tell you that I’m seeing, and I can share them," she explained. "But sometimes, because you want to move along, you keep quiet so that you move in unity."

With gubernatorial ambitions officially off the table, Odhiambo’s focus remains anchored on her parliamentary role and defending her brand of blunt internal criticism—promising to speak her mind even as the party’s coalition strategy continues to strain local allegiances.
