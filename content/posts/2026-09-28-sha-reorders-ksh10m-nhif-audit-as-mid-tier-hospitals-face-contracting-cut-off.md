According to the Social Health Authority, selected Level 4 hospitals holding at least Ksh10 million in defunct National Health Insurance Fund (NHIF) debts must now submit to an altered on-site surveillance timetable—a reshuffle that gives state auditors structured leeway over high-exposure balance sheets while hospital administrators navigate an overlapping licensing cut-off.

The regulatory agency instructed all affected facilities to pull the revised timetables directly from its digital portal, assemble paper trails, and prepare clinical and accounting records for incoming field inspection squads.

“Affected facilities are requested to check the revised schedule, note their new dates, and have the required claim supporting documents ready for verification,” SHA stated in its advisory to healthcare providers.

### The Ksh10 Million Audit Threshold

![SHA CEO Mercy Mwangangi](https://i.ibb.co/whwgmwTR/1f16304bc32b.webp)

The altered calendar modifies a baseline directive issued on September 18, 2026, which established an audit perimeter around mid-tier institutions carrying eight-figure legacy balances. Under the updated regime, the state insurer has conditioned final settlement of historical debt on physical, in-person validation of every individual claim file at facility premises.

Level 4 institutions—comprising primary referral centers, sub-county hospitals, and private nursing homes that shoulder substantial inpatient volumes—have been directed to keep complete supporting dossiers on hand prior to the arrival of SHA surveillance personnel.

“The revised list and schedule are available on the website,” the authority noted, directing facility managers to confirm their designated inspection slots to prevent disruptions during physical visits.

The inspection drive forms a critical component of SHA’s effort to clean up liabilities inherited from the defunct NHIF, preventing unverified or inflated claims from transferring onto the new scheme's active balance sheets.

### 72-Hour Contracting Squeeze

![Social Health Authority building in Nairobi.](https://i.ibb.co/6RvthkGs/17ef742b657a.webp)

The rescheduling arrives alongside a separate operational ultimatum that threatens to sideline institutions that fail to formalize their broader operational status.

In a simultaneous regulatory notice, SHA Chief Executive Officer Mercy Mwangangi cautioned that all existing institutional contracts lapse on September 30, 2026, at precisely 11:59 p.m.

“Facilities without an executed contract for the 2026–2029 cycle will not be permitted to offer services to beneficiaries after the deadline,” the agency warned, urging operators seeking continuous empanelment to complete paperwork before October 1.

For hospital administrators, the confluence of directives creates an administrative test. Level 4 operators must pull administrative personnel to retrieve, catalog, and defend disputed inpatient records from defunct insurance schemes, even as compliance desks rush to clear legal and financial onboarding hurdles for the upcoming three-year regulatory cycle.

With the September 30 deadline locking down service authorisations at midnight, healthcare facilities must clear both compliance tracks—validating historical balances with state inspectors while securing triennial operating pacts—to retain operational access to state-backed patient admissions next month.
