Security agencies have shifted their primary examination monitoring toward parents and educators, issuing a direct warning that individuals soliciting cash for advance access to national test papers are operating commercial extortion rackets.

Hours before the 2026 Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education (KCSE) examinations commence on Monday, November 2, the Ministry of Interior confirmed that law enforcement teams are tracking individuals peddling purported copies of upcoming tests to anxious families and school managers.

Interior Principal Secretary Raymond Omollo stated on Sunday that modern custody protocols prevent examination materials from entering public circulation before their scheduled administration, meaning any papers traded in advance are fraudulent copies designed to exploit desperation.

"As we do the national exams, I want to appeal to the parents and teachers not to fall into the trap of those who want to make money by lying to you that they will steal for you examinations," Omollo said. "When you do that, you are not only ruining the life of our students but also yours as a parent or a professional teacher."

![ps-omollo](https://i.ibb.co/RGPCcZJK/bf4e5002b134.webp)

### The Commercial Trap

The warning highlights an operational recalibration by state security agencies. Rather than focusing solely on classroom invigilation, investigators are targeting the informal financial pipelines where syndicates solicit substantial sums from adults seeking an unfair advantage.

Omollo noted that law enforcement officers have deployed active surveillance to intercept fraudsters soliciting money, emphasizing that the state will pursue criminal penalties against adult buyers alongside the peddlers.

"Anyone who is deluded that they would want to take advantage, our law enforcement agencies are on your trail," Omollo added, citing the government's substantial capital investment in the education sector and the need to insulate national assessment standards from commercial tampering.

### Coordinated Ministerial Warning

The Interior Ministry's intervention follows a matching caution delivered days earlier by Education Cabinet Secretary Migos Ogamba. Speaking in Makueni during the distribution of the Kenya Junior School Education Assessment (KJSEA) and Kenya Primary School Education Assessment (KPSEA) papers, Ogamba firmly rejected claims that national papers could leak ahead of time.

![Interior CS Kipchumba Murkomen speaking with Inspector General of Police Douglos Kanja before a public baraza in Keringet, Kuresoi South, Nakuru County, on Saturday, July 25.](https://i.ibb.co/sJNP5wHj/e6fef0d8906d.webp)

Ogamba cautioned candidates, guardians, and school administrators against syndicates that demand money for purported early access, directing members of the public to forward details of anyone trading test materials directly to ministry and police channels for immediate prosecution.

"Such claims are fraudulent, and nobody can have advance access to the exams to facilitate cheating or malpractice," Ogamba said.

### High Stakes at the 8-4-4 Sunset

The intense administrative focus on adult intermediaries arrives as the 2026 KCSE marks the second-to-last sitting of the secondary examination under the 8-4-4 curriculum. As the country completes its transition toward the Competency-Based Curriculum (CBC), shrinking re-sit windows and heightened institutional competition have intensified pressures on candidates and schools.

With distribution logistics locked down across regional storage containers ahead of Monday morning's official seal openings, police units and inter-agency monitoring teams remain on notice to arrest illicit sellers and trace any transaction trails linked to fraudulent advance papers.
