Rigathi Gachagua did not cross the Atlantic to shower polite diplomatic praise on his fellow opposition heavyweights. Speaking before a packed gathering of the Kenyan diaspora in the United States on Sunday, September 27, 2026, the Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP) leader decided it was time to hold a public mirror up to the alliance’s fragile egos, warning that behind closed doors, several key players are proving entirely too slippery to trust.

The former Deputy President cut straight through the carefully curated displays of solidarity, demanding that every principal angling for the 2027 presidential ticket look into the camera and publicly vow to support whoever eventually wins the nomination.

“From where I sit, I don’t want to talk about other people because they can disappoint you,” Gachagua said, laying out his personal terms before laying down the gauntlet. “I will support anyone who is chosen, if not me. And I challenge my brothers and sisters also to give a similar commitment publicly.”

### Targeting the Fanbase Ultimatums

What appeared to irritate Gachagua most was not just the behind-the-scenes posturing among party bosses, but the bitter, scorched-earth commentary being churned out by their digital followers back home. Over recent weeks, competing factions within the opposition have flirted with boycotts or spiteful defections whenever their particular candidate’s chances look challenged.

Gachagua pushed back hard against supporters claiming that if their darling politician is not handed the coalition crown, they would rather cross the floor or sit out the contest entirely.

“Some are a little bit slippery, and that is why I have a little doubt,” Gachagua told the diaspora audience. “I want them to tell their supporters to stop issuing some statements I hear in Kenya that if it is not so and so, then we’d better vote for Ruto, or so and so is the sixth, or Ruto remains the president.”

For Gachagua, those slogans are symptoms of an alliance whose principals are failing to discipline their inner circles. He argued that everyday citizens have already made up their minds about wanting an alternative to President William Ruto, leaving party leaders with no excuse to hold the ticket hostage to personal entitlement.

“Kenyans are telling us we know who we want, but we know who we don’t want,” he added. “They are waiting for us to give them a candidate, and they don’t care who it is.”

### A Crowded Stage and a Ticking Clock

The tension Gachagua aired across the ocean lands right in the middle of a crowded, high-stakes backstage rivalry. The line of declared and potential flagbearers is growing longer by the week, with Wiper leader Kalonzo Musyoka, Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna, and former Cabinet Secretary Fred Matiang’i all commanding significant, vocal loyalists across different regions.

Each camp has brought its own political arithmetic to the table. Kalonzo’s loyalists argue his seniority and past electoral runs make him the natural consensus figure, while younger, assertive factions backing Sifuna push for an energetic generational shift. Matiang’i’s allies, meanwhile, continue to position him as an executive-ready technocrat capable of appealing to undecided voters.

Yet beneath the personality clashes sits an even sharper institutional dispute: the calendar.

The coalition remains split down the middle on when to actually reveal the candidate tasked with challenging Ruto. One hardline faction is pressing for an immediate announcement, insisting that only early clarity will allow the alliance to run an organized, nationwide ground campaign. 

Another camp is urging a cautious delay, advocating to push the flagbearer unveiling all the way back to May 2027—a mere three months before voters head to the ballot boxes in August. With distrust already spilling into diaspora hall speeches, whether these rival figures can hold together until May without the alliance fraying into rival camps remains the unanswered test.
