Candidates sitting the 2026 Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education (KCSE) face an unyielding security and desk-level compliance regime, with the Kenya National Examinations Council (KNEC) ordering mandatory outer body searches, a 15-minute pre-session seating lock-in, and an outright ban on wristwatches and personal bags across all test centres.

Under instructions circulated to guide conduct before, during, and after each paper, the national examiner has tied candidate entry to rigid operational timelines. Sittings will run in two daily blocks—morning papers commencing at 8:00 am and afternoon assessments at 2:00 pm. Candidates must submit to physical security searches outside the exam room and be settled at their assigned desks at least 15 minutes before the starting bell.

Centre supervisors have been directed to shut out chronic delays. Any candidate arriving after a paper starts must provide an admissible explanation, while those arriving more than 30 minutes late will be barred from sitting the test, with entry permitted only under verified exceptional circumstances.

### Stationery and Script Mechanics

![Secondary School students sitting for their Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education (KCSE) examinations.](https://i.ibb.co/DH65F47Q/9236315a632f.webp)

Once inside, students will operate under exacting technical standards governing answer booklets. Candidates are required to inspect question papers immediately upon distribution to verify subject codes and flag missing materials before writing begins. Every answer script must bear the candidate's verified name, index number, signature, and examination date.

KNEC has barred writing within script margins, although candidates are permitted to write on both sides of the page. The guidelines strictly prohibit students from copying questions into answer booklets and mandate leaving a blank line after every finished answer.

Writing instruments are equally restricted: candidates must use blue or black ink for text answers, with pencils permitted solely for drawing diagrams.

### Prohibited Gear and Approved Equipment

The examiner has tightened the perimeter against outside aids by barring personal bags—including backpacks, briefcases, and handbags—from test centres entirely. 

Inside the examination hall, mobile phones, electronic communication gadgets, programmable calculators, wristwatches, and unauthorized papers or books are prohibited within a candidate’s reach. 

![The Kenya National Examinations Council (KNEC) offices in Nairobi.](https://i.ibb.co/TDK6nNMt/c88406841b50.webp)

Where computational aids are necessary, KNEC permits only non-programmable calculators stripped of their protective covers, and restricts their use to 15 specific subjects:

* Mathematics
* Biology, Physics, and Chemistry
* Agriculture and Geography
* Computer Studies and Business Studies
* Technical tracks: Woodwork, Metalwork, Building Construction, Power Mechanics, Electricity, Drawing and Design, and Aviation Technology

### Custody and Departure Protocols

The council’s instructions also restrict movement at the end of each session. Candidates must drop their pens the moment supervisors give the order, pausing to cross-check index numbers, signatures, and dates before invigilators collect the scripts.

To prevent the early circulation of assessment materials, no student will be allowed to leave the examination room prior to the scheduled conclusion without explicit, documented permission from the supervisor. Even when emergency exits are granted, leaving candidates are barred from carrying question papers or answer booklets past the door, ensuring all examination materials remain locked within official custody.
