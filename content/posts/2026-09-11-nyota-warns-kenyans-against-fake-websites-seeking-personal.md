NAIROBI - Administrators of the National Youth Opportunities Towards Advancement (NYOTA) project have issued an urgent fraud alert cautioning Kenyan youth against sophisticated online scams designed to steal personal data under the guise of offering government business grants.

### What we know
* **No web or app portal:** NYOTA does not operate any mobile application or website for registering beneficiaries or disbursing grant money.
* **Official USSD channel:** Enrollment is conducted exclusively through the `*254#` USSD code, requiring no web links or external portals.
* **Direct payments:** All legitimate funds are transferred directly to the mobile phone numbers provided by applicants during original registration.
* **Targeting Phase II:** Cyber scammers are capitalizing on the recent rollout of Phase II funding, which saw 122,000 young entrepreneurs receive a second tranche of Ksh25,000.

The alert follows a surge in fake social media pages and fraudulent websites circulating links that promise to fast-track youth enrollment for the project's second phase. These rogue platforms frequently display forged testimonials and fake payout receipts to dupe unsuspecting jobseekers into handing over sensitive personal information, including national identity numbers and financial details.

Project managers emphasized that the government agency does not distribute third-party web links or require digital application fees to process financial assistance.

“The NYOTA Project does not have an app or website for disbursing funds to beneficiaries. Beneficiaries receive payments directly to the phone numbers they used during application for the respective NYOTA interventions,” the project management clarified in an official statement.

Warning the public against sharing unverified links, the statement added: “NYOTA Project does not use links to enrol beneficiaries into the project. Do not click on or share links claiming to register you for NYOTA or facilitate payments.”

Official communications regarding grant disbursements are transmitted strictly via direct SMS and verified state channels. Members of the public who encounter fraudulent domains or suspicious mobile applications have been urged to report them immediately to cybersecurity authorities.

The resurgence of phishing schemes targeting state welfare programs underscores the deep vulnerabilities facing Kenya's unemployed youth. Following President William Ruto’s July launch of the Phase II business support component-which raised total individual disbursements to Ksh50,000 for tens of thousands of young traders-cyber criminals have seized on widespread economic anxiety to target those desperate for capital. While public advisories offer immediate guidance, the proliferation of these deceptive portals highlights an urgent need for law enforcement agencies to actively dismantle cyber-syndicates rather than leaving desperate jobseekers to navigate an increasingly hostile digital landscape alone.
