Two Miami-Dade Sheriff's Office sergeants filed a defamation lawsuit against the production company founded by actors Ben Affleck and Matt Damon, arguing that the Netflix crime film *The Rip* drew too closely on a real police operation and damaged their professional reputations.

Jason Smith and Jonathan Santana filed the case in federal court in South Florida in May 2026. The defendants named in reporting on the complaint include Artists Equity, the company founded by Affleck and Damon, and production entity Falco Pictures.

### Why the officers say the film is identifiable

The film follows South Florida officers who discover a large amount of cash during an investigation. Parts of its story were inspired by a 2016 case in which police found more than $21 million in a Miami Lakes home linked to a suspected marijuana trafficker.

Smith and Santana say they held roles in the real investigation and that the film's setting, narcotics-team details and other similarities could lead viewers to associate them with fictional characters. The officers allege that the characters' depiction suggests serious misconduct, including theft of seized money and other criminal behaviour. They say the portrayal has affected how colleagues, friends and relatives view them.

The officers were not named in the film. Their lawsuit sought compensatory and punitive damages, legal fees, and a public retraction or correction, according to the Associated Press account of the filing.

### Production company contests the allegations

Artists Equity's lawyer had rejected the officers' concerns in a response before the lawsuit. The company's position was that the film did not claim to tell the true story of the 2016 incident or portray real people, and that a disclaimer identified the story as fictionalised.

The dispute therefore centres on whether the similarities alleged by the officers are enough to identify them with characters in a fictional production. The claims in the lawsuit are allegations, not findings by a court. The case was in its early stages when the Associated Press reported on it in May 2026.
