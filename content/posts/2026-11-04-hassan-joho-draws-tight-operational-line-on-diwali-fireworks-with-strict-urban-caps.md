Cabinet Secretary for Mining Hassan Joho has drawn a hard operational line on event organizers and commercial dealers planning public displays for the upcoming three-day Diwali celebrations.

In a directive issued from Nairobi, the Ministry restricted all public pyrotechnic displays to a tight four-hour window each evening, running strictly between 7:00 PM and 11:00 PM across the festival period from Friday, November 6, to Sunday, November 8, 2026.

Organizers intending to light up the night sky must clear an administrative hurdle before setting off any pyrotechnics. Official permits must be secured directly from the State Department for Mining at Madini House in Nairobi or through regional county mining offices prior to any event. Under the updated guidelines, authorized public pyrotechnics are confined strictly to registered clubs, hotels, and sports grounds. Any deviation requires explicit written clearance from an Inspector of Explosives.

The Ministry also laid down technical boundaries for urban areas to curb safety hazards in populated neighborhoods. Aerial display shells are capped at a maximum of four-inch calibre within built-up zones unless a special exemption is granted by an Inspector of Explosives. , operational rules mandate that only qualified, experienced, and sober individuals are permitted to ignite high-altitude aerial shells.

Commercial dealers face heavy oversight under the Explosives Act Cap 115 Laws of Kenya. The Ministry strictly prohibited the sale of any fireworks to children under the age of 13, classifying violations as severe offenses carrying heavy financial penalties. Dealers are also barred from offloading specialized aerial stock—such as shells, rockets, Roman candles, and cakes—to buyers who fail to produce valid permits signed by an Inspector of Explosives.

“The Hindu community is requested to ensure that fireworks displays are carried out within the requirements of the Explosives Act Cap 115 Laws of Kenya. Fireworks displays should be carried out in an orderly manner so that the general public are not inconvenienced,” CS Joho stated.

The regulatory crackdown coincides with the Festival of Lights, a major fixture on the Hindu calendar celebrating the triumph of light over darkness through family gatherings, traditional oil lamps, and community celebrations. While President William Ruto previously pledged in 2024 to formally elevate Diwali to a national public holiday observed on November 1 annually, the legislative proposal remains un-enacted, keeping the celebration designated exclusively for members of the Hindu faith under the Public Holidays Act.
