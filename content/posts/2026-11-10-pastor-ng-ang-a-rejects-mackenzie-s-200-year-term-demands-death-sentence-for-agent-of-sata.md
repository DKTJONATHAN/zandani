Outspoken preacher Pastor James Ng’ang’a has rejected the High Court’s 200-year prison sentence handed to doomsday cult leader Paul Nthenge Mackenzie, arguing that the penalty falls far short of justice for the hundreds who perished in the Shakahola Forest massacre.

Addressing the judgment, the 73-year-old preacher insisted that the court should have imposed the death penalty instead of a custodial term. Ng’ang’a targeted the fundamental mechanics of Mackenzie’s indoctrination, pointing out the contradiction of a religious leader instructing followers to fast to death while remaining alive and well himself.

“Jaji hapo hata ungemhukumu kunyongwa tu aende mtu anaambia watu wafunge yeye hakufi, agent wa shetani anatumia jina la Yesu,” Ng’ang’a said, openly questioning the theological basis that persuaded hundreds to starve themselves under the pretext of divine passage.

“Yesu gani huyo? Eh Yesu gani huyo? Watu wafunge wakufe waende mbinguni,” he added.

Ng’ang’a extended his critique into a broader warning aimed at contemporary religious figures who rely on staged revelations and manipulative doctrines to exploit worshipers. Distancing his own ministry from what he labeled as pulpit theatricality, he asserted that fraudulent leaders would eventually face inevitable consequences.

“Utavuna kule ulikopanda. Waongo muache uongo. Me I don’t support dramas. Manabii wa uongo,” he remarked.

Turning to his own public standing and frequent brushes with controversy, the veteran preacher struck a defiant posture regarding state surveillance or legal threats against his own operations. Pointing to his age, Ng’ang’a declared himself unbothered by potential imprisonment or regulatory pressure.

“Me, I’m 73. Hata ukanifunga hiyo mimi nitakuwa tu niko nyumbani tu,” he stated.

Ng’ang’a’s comments follow the landmark ruling delivered by High Court Judge Diana Kavedza Mochache at the Mombasa Law Courts. The court found Mackenzie and 28 co-accused guilty on charges linked to 191 murder counts—comprising 190 children and one adult. The prosecution called 120 witnesses to secure the conviction, which split the 200-year sentence into 100 years for murder by starvation and another 100 years for direct homicide.

The judicial outcome follows exhaustive exhumations across Mackenzie’s compound in Shakahola Forest, Kilifi County, where investigative teams recovered a total of 429 bodies from shallow mass graves.

While residents in Furunzi village welcomed the severe prison term, Mackenzie’s extended family in Machakos County rejected the conviction and confirmed plans to file an appeal. Meanwhile, Mackenzie’s son, Dan Mackenzie, revealed that his decision to resist his father’s teachings as a child saved him from being drawn into the deadly coastal encampment.

“Escaping this cult was just rebellious. I didn’t want to join or going to church when I was a kid. So, he just gave up on me and it didn’t involve me to go to Shakahola,” Dan explained.

With Mackenzie’s legal team preparing to lodge an appeal against Judge Mochache’s sentencing, the trial outcome continues to trigger debate across Kenya’s religious and legal sectors over pulpit accountability and state oversight.
