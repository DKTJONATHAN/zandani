The High Court has locked public spending under the intergovernmental deal between President William Ruto's administration and Nairobi Governor Johnson Sakaja's county executive, ruling that no public funds can be released or spent without explicit legislative approval.

Under the directive issued following a constitutional challenge by the Katiba Institute, national and county treasuries cannot commit resources to joint capital projects unless the expenditure is directly authorized by Parliament or the Nairobi County Assembly. The court ruled that any financial transfers from the national government to Nairobi under the arrangement must strictly adhere to Article 202(2) of the Constitution as conditional or unconditional additional allocations.

The order, issued on September 28, 2026, places tight operational boundaries on an executive agreement that spans several key urban sectors, including waste management, road networks, street lighting, public markets, housing infrastructure, water distribution, and sanitation systems.

### Executive Under Disclosure Orders

![President William Ruto speaking during the signing of the national government-Nairobi County Cooperation Pact, State House, February 17, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/3mvPqTnG/898cf941bfa9.webp)

While declining to freeze the intergovernmental agreement entirely, the bench issued specific disclosure orders targeting both the national government and City Hall leadership.

Prime Cabinet Secretary Musalia Mudavadi and Governor Sakaja have been ordered to produce and file the complete, unredacted text of the cooperation agreement currently being executed. The two officials must also submit comprehensive schedules detailing all ongoing projects, their exact sources of funding, and the formal minutes of all joint steering and technical committees established to run the initiatives.

Governor Sakaja faces additional reporting mandates. The court directed City Hall to file an ad hoc report alongside complete records of public participation, detailing the county executive's legal considerations and the formal assembly approvals obtained prior to executing the deal.

### Constitutional Guardrails on County Functions

The court emphasized that political cooperation cannot serve as a shortcut to bypass constitutional procedures governing county autonomy. 

![An undated aerial view of Nairobi's Upper Hill side.](https://i.ibb.co/XkL0MRwB/7231085a9444.webp)

In its ruling, the court stipulated that no county powers, functions, municipal assets, staff, or local revenue streams may be transferred to or exercised by national agencies unless every statutory step under Article 187 of the Constitution is met. The provision requires formal agreements, clear resource transfers, and explicit structural handovers before any devolved responsibility can alter hands.

By keeping the underlying framework active while freezing unappropriated funding and demanding paper trails, the court shifted the burden onto state officers to prove that joint urban programs comply with statutory budget laws.

### Hearing Set for February 2027

The Katiba Institute had petitioned the court questioning whether the bilateral executive deal impermissibly stripped devolved responsibilities from Nairobi County without constitutional safeguards or public oversight.

Finding that the petition raises substantial questions of constitutional law regarding national-county relations and fiscal oversight, the High Court scheduled the full hearing for February 18, 2027, when the bench will determine the ultimate validity of the cooperation framework.
