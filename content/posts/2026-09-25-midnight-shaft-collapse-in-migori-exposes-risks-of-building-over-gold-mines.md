A midnight ground failure in Migori’s Nyatike Sub-County has brought the dangers of artisanal gold mining directly to surface housing, after several residential and commercial buildings caved into underground mining shafts, trapping at least two residents beneath the debris.

The incident occurred shortly before midnight at the Osiri-Matanda gold-mining village in Macalder. Unlike typical mine cave-ins that trap workers deep inside production tunnels, the ground collapse tore open the surface layer directly underneath settled structures, swallowing houses and business premises into subterranean voids.

Two women living in the affected structures are currently suspected to be trapped in the rubble and underlying shafts as emergency crews work to locate them.

![A collage showing the scene where a gold mine collapsed at Osiri-Matanda gold mining village on September 25, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/99BzqmWv/06de9936c219.webp)

Migori County Executive Committee (CEC) Member for Environment and Natural Resources, Edwin Omondi, confirmed that the county's Disaster Management team was dispatched to the scene in Macalder during the night.

“Two residents are suspected to be trapped. Rescue teams are working tirelessly,” Omondi said, noting that responders are attempting to stabilize the perimeter while digging through collapsed material.

County authorities have ordered civilians to stay clear of the affected perimeter in Osiri, warning that unstable surface conditions could lead to secondary cave-ins while heavy rescue gear and response teams operate on site.

![mine-collapse-migori-nyatike.jpg](https://i.ibb.co/nNmWgyVQ/604829f4dcfa.webp)

The collapse highlights a growing spatial conflict in Western Kenya’s gold belts, where informal commercial centers and housing settlements expand directly over historical and active underground shafts. While traditional mining risks are concentrated on underground laborers, surface construction over unmapped or poorly backfilled shafts places non-mining residents directly above structural hazards.

Fatal mining incidents have recurred across the region in recent months. In July, an underground section at the Kilimapesa mine in Transmara, Narok County, caved in and killed five miners. Earlier in February, two miners died after being trapped inside an abandoned underground tunnel in the same region.

While previous fatalities involved workers actively operating inside production tunnels, the Osiri-Matanda event demonstrates how subterranean instability can compromise structural foundations at the surface without warning.

The exact trigger of the midnight collapse, the total number of damaged properties, and the condition of any additional injured persons remain unconfirmed as rescue teams clear debris and county geologists begin evaluating the physical stability of adjacent ground.
