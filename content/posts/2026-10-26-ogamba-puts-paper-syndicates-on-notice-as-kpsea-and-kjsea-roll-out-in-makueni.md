Education Cabinet Secretary Julius Migos Ogamba has placed fraudulent examination racketeers under direct state scrutiny, moving to protect parents and candidates from financial extortion as the 2026 national assessments got underway on Monday morning.

Speaking in Makueni during the physical distribution of the opening assessment papers on October 26, Ogamba issued a stern advisory against individuals claiming to possess early access to Kenya Junior School Education Assessment (KJSEA) and Kenya Primary School Education Assessment (KPSEA) materials.

The Cabinet Secretary stressed that national distribution protocols have completely closed technical vulnerabilities, rendering any commercial sale of purported question papers an outright confidence trick designed to fleece nervous households.

### The Warning Over Predatory Syndicates

Ogamba stated that the Ministry of Education had implemented robust administrative safeguards to ensure that test papers remain inaccessible prior to scheduled dispatch times at examination centres.

![Education CS Migos Ogamba overseeing the distribution of 2026 KJSEA exam papers to candidates in Makueni on October 26, 2026](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/ogamba-kjsea.jpg.webp?itok=8ATB8HIh)

"I particularly caution parents, candidates and members of the public against individuals who claim to have access to examination papers and demand money in exchange for purported advanced access. Such claims are fraudulent," Ogamba stated.

He reiterated that advance leaks are impossible under current distribution protocols, advising families to preserve their funds and alert security organs whenever solicitations occur.

"I urge anyone with information concerning these activities to report the matter promptly to relevant authorities," he added, noting that investigative agencies are actively tracking leads on commercial peddlers of counterfeit tests.

### Dual Timetables for Grade Six and Grade Nine

The ministerial alert coincides with the simultaneous rollout of transitional assessments across primary and junior schools nationwide, placing thousands of learners under test conditions across staggered morning and midday papers.

According to timelines established by the Kenya National Examinations Council (KNEC), Grade Six candidates sitting KPSEA opened their assessment cycle with Mathematics during the morning session. After a scheduled 30-minute break, the candidates returned to their desks to tackle the English language test in the mid-morning.

![Education CS Migos Ogamba speaking to the press after overseeing the distribution of 2026 KJSEA exam papers to candidates at Kiambani Comprehensive School in Makueni on October 26,](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/ogamba-kpsea.jpg.webp?itok=QTqAjrvR)

Concurrently, Grade Nine candidates undergoing the inaugural KJSEA administration followed an inverse linguistic sequence. The learners sat for their English language paper during the morning session before reconvening after a 30-minute recess to write the English composition paper.

### Criminal Sanctions for School Administrators

Beyond warning the public against extortion rackets, Ogamba redirected administrative accountability squarely toward the field personnel responsible for managing examination rooms.

The Cabinet Secretary cautioned centre managers, supervisors, and invigilators that any compromise of testing integrity would lead to severe criminal prosecution alongside immediate administrative dismissal.

"Let me reiterate that the government will not tolerate any examination malpractice in any form or manner," Ogamba said. "Centre managers, supervisors, invigilators and all officials entrusted with the administration of examinations and assessments must discharge their responsibilities with the highest standards of integrity and professionalism."

To cushion the exercise against external disruptions, the Ministry of Education has integrated field operations with the Ministry of Interior, KNEC, and allied security formations. The inter-agency team has established logistics response mechanisms to address flash flooding, heavy rainfall, insecure corridors, and difficult road terrain across remote testing centres as the testing week progresses.
