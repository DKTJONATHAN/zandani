The National Employment Authority (NEA) has issued a strict two-step verification directive for Kenyans pursuing overseas jobs, ordering prospective migrant workers to confirm both recruiter accreditation and specific job orders before parting with any money.

### What we know
* State regulators are pushing verification onto the digital NEAIMS portal to insulate job seekers from predatory middlemen.
* Multi-agency detectives are actively pursuing nearly 400 rogue recruiters, signaling an aggressive push against transnational labour trafficking networks.
* Official paper accreditation alone is no longer adequate; every foreign job order must reflect live on state databases before payments occur.

Under the new compliance advisory, job seekers must first confirm that any recruitment outfit pitching foreign employment is fully registered and carries an active operational licence on the National Employment Authority Integrated Management System (NEAIMS). The second check requires prospective workers to cross-examine the specific overseas vacancy against approved listings on the same government database to ensure the post actually exists and is cleared by state regulators.

The Authority emphasized that no candidate should pay agency fees, medical processing charges, or visa deposits without securing independent verification on the portal.

The heightened vigilance follows a sweeping criminal investigation into overseas labour cartels. Earlier this year, Labour and Social Protection Cabinet Secretary Alfred Mutua disclosed that a multi-agency task force-comprising detectives from the Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI), prosecutors from the Office of the Director of Public Prosecutions (DPP), state lawyers from the Attorney General’s Office, and ministry inspectors-was probing 390 cases of recruitment and travel fraud.

Of the flagged entities under active investigation, 116 were operating as rogue travel agencies without registration from either the NEA or the Business Registration Service (BRS). Investigators have completed 15 detailed files and submitted them to the DPP with recommendations to charge the masterminds behind the phantom placement rings.

On the ground across Nairobi, Eldoret, and Mombasa, backstreet agencies have routinely exploited the desperation of unemployed youth, collecting millions of shillings in non-refundable "processing fees" for non-existent agricultural, security, and hospitality postings in the Gulf and Europe. By shifting the burden of validation directly onto candidates via NEAIMS, the state is attempting to close loopholes exploited by briefcase recruiters who operate outside formal bilateral labour agreements.
