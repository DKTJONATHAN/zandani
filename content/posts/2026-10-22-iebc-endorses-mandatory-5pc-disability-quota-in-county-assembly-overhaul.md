Political parties will no longer have discretionary leeway to sidestep persons with disabilities in devolved legislatures if amendments backed by electoral authorities clear Parliament.

Under proposed statutory changes presented to the National Assembly’s Justice and Legal Affairs Committee (JLAC) on Thursday, the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) endorsed a binding framework that ties county assembly nominations directly to total ward counts, replacing a rigid list system that has routinely locked out marginalized groups.

The submissions, delivered by IEBC Director of Legal Services Chrispine Owiye, gave institutional support to the County Governments Election Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2024. Sponsored by Nominated Senator Crystal Asige, the legislation has already sailed through the Senate and now sits before MPs for deliberation.

### Closing the Section 36 Loophole

At the center of the legislative push is the failure of the existing legal framework to enforce Article 54(2) of the Constitution, which requires the state to progressively ensure that at least five percent of members in elective and appointive public bodies are persons with disabilities.

![Nominated Senator Crystal Asige speaking during a convention on March 2, 2024](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/nominated%20senator%20crystal%20asige%20speaking%20during%20a%20convention%20on%20march%202%2C%202024.jpg.webp?h=46e11913&itok=G84El9D8)

Currently, Section 36 of the Elections Act compels political parties to submit an eight-member party list for county special seats, partitioned to include two candidates with disabilities, two youth representatives, and two nominees from marginalized groups.

In practice, that formula only regulated the slate submitted to the commission, without guaranteeing that parties winning multiple nominated slots actually allocated winning seats to candidates with disabilities once electoral calculations were completed. As a result, several county assemblies have operated with negligible or zero disability representation.

Asige’s bill dismantles that static roster. The amendment mandates the IEBC to draw nominees directly from prioritized party lists to guarantee that persons with disabilities secure at least five per cent of total county assembly seats, or a statutory baseline of two seats—whichever figure is greater.

### Impairment Categories and Devolved Structure

The proposed law also introduces specific qualitative controls on how parties compile their nomination dockets. Rather than treating disability as a uniform bracket, county lists will be legally required to account for distinct impairment categories, including physical, visual, and hearing impairments.

![Press IEBC](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/press%20iebc.jpeg.webp?itok=RwiQl7Dc)

The size of party submission slates will also expand to mirror the ward density of each county, preventing political party headquarters from submitting uniform national lists that ignore local administrative realities.

Alongside the threshold for persons with disabilities, the restructuring rebalances other special seats within the County Governments Act. The revised allocation carves out two nominated positions explicitly for youth and two for minority groups, setting these aside before factoring in the mandatory disability ratio.

### Wider Electoral Code Revisions

The electoral commission noted that the Asige-sponsored amendments dovetail with its own proposed legislative interventions under the draft Elections (Amendment) Bill, 2026, which similarly seeks to enforce prioritization rules on political party slates before elections.

By endorsing the bill before JLAC, the IEBC removes a significant regulatory obstacle to the proposals, signaling administrative readiness to manage the expanded quota system across all 47 counties ahead of future devolved assembly determinations.

The bill now awaits committee review and subsequent floor readings in the National Assembly, where lawmakers will determine whether to adopt the Senate amendments in full or subject the text to a mediation committee.
