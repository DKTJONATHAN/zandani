Consulting firms bidding to retool Kenya’s Competency-Based Curriculum (CBC) must demonstrate prior experience in authoring artificial intelligence coursework, establishing a stringent technical threshold for a nationwide digital literacy project funded by the World Bank.

The requirement, published Tuesday by the Information and Communications Technology Authority (ICTA), effectively shuts out generalist IT vendors and non-academic technical consultancies from managing the assignment. Under the terms of the tender, bidding entities must verify that education has formed their core commercial business for a minimum of five years.

The selected transaction adviser will be contracted for 40 calendar weeks to embed digital skills and gender-inclusive instructional models into the national school curriculum. The procurement is being financed under the Kenya Digital Economy Acceleration Project (KDEAP), a multi-billion-shilling facility backed by the World Bank to anchor digital skilling across public institutions.

### Scope Left Undefined Despite Prior Rollout

![A teacher and students during a learning lesson in Kenya.](https://i.ibb.co/BHBGF7vK/0c9e86e0e28e.webp)

While the tender documents demand specific technical profiles—including proof of managerial capability and audited project sheets displaying assignment scopes, contractual values, and client addresses—the state has left the actual classroom footprint of the reform ambiguous.

ICTA's notice does not specify which learning areas or academic tiers within the CBC structure will absorb the revised coursework. The silence leaves parents and teachers uncertain whether the modifications will land in upper primary learning areas, Junior School tracks, or upcoming Senior School pathways.

The curriculum overhaul follows an earlier push by the Ministry of Education in July, when the government began introducing introductory AI instructional frameworks across more than 10,000 public junior schools. During that rollout, Deputy President Kithure Kindiki framed early exposure to emerging technologies as an economic imperative, arguing that foundational exposure was vital to preparing Kenyan learners for an automated labour market.

Yet, the new 40-week consulting framework indicates that the institutional scaffolding for digital competencies remains an evolving draft, with the state now turning to external subject matter experts to design gender-responsive teaching strategies.

### Tough Bidding Hurdles for Vendors

Beyond demonstrating familiarity with generative and applied artificial intelligence concepts, applicants must produce past client records that prove their models were successfully integrated into formal schooling systems.

ICTA’s procurement guidelines mandate that firms submit detailed institutional balance sheets, technical managerial rosters, and comprehensive portfolios showing prior curriculum reform deliveries. The Authority noted that expressions of interest will be filtered strictly on whether a firm's operational structure has remained centered on educational design since at least 2021.

The tender window remains narrow. Consulting consortia and standalone educational advisory firms have until 10:00 a.m. on Wednesday, October 28, 2026, to file their technical submissions with the Authority ahead of formal evaluation and contract awards.
