Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna finds himself at the center of a fresh political debate as his online popularity collides directly with party insider skepticism. While the Linda Mwananchi Movement leader surged to the top of a November 2026 presidential opinion poll, his own Orange Democratic Movement (ODM) colleague Samuel Atandi has wasted no time pouring cold water on Sifuna's State House ambitions.

The survey, conducted by research firm Mwelekeo Insights between November 11 and November 20, 2026, placed Sifuna at 25.1% among 25,672 online participants. That score gave the vocal Nairobi legislator an 11.7 percentage point cushion over President William Ruto, who finished second with 13.4%.

However, the headline-grabbing figures failed to impress ODM MP Samuel Atandi. Dismissing a separate TIFA poll that placed Sifuna on equal footing with President Ruto, Atandi openly questioned the senator's actual political clout, arguing that Sifuna lacks the national weight required to steer the opposition as its presidential flag-bearer.

According to Atandi, Sifuna's presidential posture faces a hard ceiling inside opposition ranks. The lawmaker claimed that veteran political figures, including impeached Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua and Wiper Party leader Kalonzo Musyoka, would flatly refuse to accept Sifuna as their top candidate in the run-up to the 2027 General Election.

Atandi's reality check comes even as the Mwelekeo Insights poll array revealed sharp divides across the national lineup. Behind Sifuna and Ruto, former Interior Cabinet Secretary Fred Matiang’i secured third place with 12.6%, edging out Kalonzo Musyoka (12.5%) by a mere 0.1 percentage points.

Further down the numerical breakdown, Kiharu MP Ndindi Nyoro and Macharia Njeru tied in fifth place with 10.1% each. Roots Party leader George Wajackoyah recorded 5.5%, while former Chief Justice David Maraga secured 3.4%. Busia Senator Okiyah Omtatah registered 2.3%, followed by Narc-Kenya leader Martha Karua at 1.5%.

At the lower tail of the rankings, Gachagua registered 1.2%, with gospel musician Reuben Kigame receiving 0.7%. Activist Boniface Mwangi and businessman Jimi Wanjigi tied at 0.5% each, while nominated MP Eliud Owalo posted 0.4%. Undecided respondents and other candidates accounted for the remaining 0.2%.

Mwelekeo Insights appended explicit disclaimers to the numerical outcome. The firm cautioned that because the survey relied on voluntary online participation, the results do not constitute a statistically representative sample of the Kenyan electorate, carry no conventional margin of error, and should not be treated as a forecast for 2027.

For Sifuna, the contrast highlights an escalating divide between digital popularity and formal party hierarchy. As polling firms continue tracking shifts in voter sentiment across national and county levels, Sifuna's primary challenge will be convincing internal skeptics like Atandi that his numbers can survive beyond online click-throughs.
