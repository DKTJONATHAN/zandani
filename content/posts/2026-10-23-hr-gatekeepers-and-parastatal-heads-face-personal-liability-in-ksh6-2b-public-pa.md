Public Service Cabinet Secretary Geoffrey Ruku has placed government human resource administrators and parastatal heads at the center of a multi-billion-shilling payroll fraud investigation, giving system controllers 30 days before law enforcement agencies move to make formal arrests.

Speaking at a certified human resource professional ceremony in Nairobi on Friday, Ruku signaled an operational shift in how the state addresses public wage bill losses. Rather than focusing solely on fake workers on government payrolls, the Ministry of Public Service and the Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) are targeting the administrative gatekeepers who create, approve, or maintain fraudulent entries across state agencies.

The heightened enforcement follows a forensic audit across 53 state departments that uncovered Ksh6.2 billion in unauthorized or suspicious transactions across 12 audited units. DCI detectives have focused on key public entities, including the Ministry of Health, the Kenya Police Service, and the Kenya Prisons Service, where personal staff identification numbers and payroll entry points are undergoing forensic verification.

At the core of the state's action plan is a dual track of criminal prosecutions and immediate administrative sanctions against personnel managing public wage distribution.

![cs-ruku-nys.jpg](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/cs-ruku-nys.jpg.webp?itok=GI2hsskA)

### HR Controllers Face Direct Court Action

Under the enforcement directives, human resource managers found to have facilitated ghost worker entries or altered payroll records face immediate dismissal and criminal charges in court.

"On matters of payroll, as the Minister in charge, I want to assure Kenyans that I will be extremely firm," Ruku stated. "Several human resources professionals will be taken to court, and they will lose their jobs based on the investigations."

The Public Service Ministry confirmed that law enforcement agencies have gathered digital logs and institutional records establishing organized networks involved in payroll manipulation. Specific enforcement actions are set to begin at the Kenya Prisons Service, which has been cited as a primary area of focus before detectives expand operations to additional state departments.

The move marks the second major administrative action within a fortnight. On October 16, the government revoked payroll system access for 84 human resource officers, placing them on mandatory 30-day leave to allow forensic teams unhindered access to institutional databases.

### Parastatal CEOs Given One-Week HRIS Ultimatum

![Officers from the Kenya Prison Service during a routine parade.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/kenya-prison-service.jpg.webp?itok=DoCcecfK)

Alongside criminal inquiries into HR managers, the ministry has targeted chief executives of state corporations who operate outside the centralized Human Resource Information System (HRIS).

Parastatal accounting officers who continue to process salaries through standalone or non-designated systems face public exposure and potential administrative termination. Ruku confirmed that a public list identifying non-compliant state bodies will be published next week.

While chief executives within the Public Service Ministry face direct administrative dismissal for failing to onboard their organizations onto the digital portal, non-compliant heads of institutions under other portfolios will be formally reported to the Cabinet and named publicly.

Centralizing state wage processing through the HRIS platform is designed to block off-budget recruitments, duplicate personal numbers, and unverified salary disbursements. By establishing strict executive responsibility, the ministry aims to force parastatal leadership to eliminate non-standard payroll channels that enable public fund leakage.

The crackdown follows explicit Cabinet directives instructing the DCI to trace every unverified personal number used in public salary processing and hold accountable all officials responsible for authorizing illegal payments.
