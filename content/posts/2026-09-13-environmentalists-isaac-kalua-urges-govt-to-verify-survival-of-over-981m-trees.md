Green Africa Foundation Chief Steward Dr Isaac Kalua Green has challenged the Kenyan government and conservation agencies to overhaul national afforestation metrics, a shift that promises taxpayers, communities, and climate financiers genuine returns on ecological investments by measuring seedling survival rather than ceremonial planting tallies.

### What we know

* Kalua's critique strikes at state vanity metrics, forcing environmental policymakers to trade easy planting photo-ops for audited, long-term canopy survival data.
* By acknowledging that his own 981-million-seedling campaign lacks verified standing figures, the conservationist disarms critics and raises the evidentiary bar across the board.
* The proposed verification framework borrows proven post-planting audit mechanisms from Niger and South Korea to insulate public afforestation funds against drought and neglect.
* Unveiling the doctrine in Monaco leverages high-level international backing just as Kenya seeks deeper external financing for its ambitious national forest restoration targets.

According to Dr Green, who unveiled his new book *Come Back and Check* during an audience with Prince Albert II of Monaco on Saturday, environmental initiatives must institute post-planting audits to confirm that seedlings mature into full canopies.

"The success of tree-growing campaigns should ultimately be measured not only by how many trees are planted, but by how many survive and continue growing," Dr Green stated, proposing the adoption of a formal "Count What Survives Standard" backed by a "Living Tree Account" and an "Evidence Ladder" to systematically track forestry investments over time.

The call comes as Kenya intensifies nationwide greening programmes aimed at aggressively expanding forest cover through coordinated public and private mobilisations. While state agencies and non-governmental entities routinely announce the distribution of millions of seedlings, harsh climatic shocks, pest infestations, livestock encroachment, and poor post-planting maintenance regularly decimate young saplings before maturity.

Dr Green turned this scrutiny directly onto his own flagship initiative, the Plant Your Age campaign, which has logged an estimated 981 million tree-growing contributions since its launch in 2011. He cautioned that unverified planting figures must not be mistaken for standing forest cover, citing successful verification regimes such as community-led regeneration systems in Niger, government forestry auditing in the Philippines, and South Korea’s structured post-war reforestation tracking as workable templates for Kenya.

The Monaco engagement further marked over two decades of partnership between the Green Africa Foundation and the Prince Albert II of Monaco Foundation, building on joint conservation efforts dating back to the Prince's 2010 working visit to environmental projects in Kitui. Dr Green, who also serves as the Board Chair of the Green Nairobi Company, highlighted that pairing urban climate infrastructure with transparent rural restoration models will remain essential for securing sustained donor confidence and meeting Kenya's long-term conservation obligations.

By forcing a structural pivot from input-based reporting to verifiable survival accounting, environmental managers and local communities stand to gain far more durable microclimates and resilient water catchment areas. Moving away from output public relations toward audited survival rates ensures that public funds allocated to tree-growing drives translate into authentic, permanent forest cover across the country.
