Internal Security Principal Secretary Dr Raymond Omollo has thrown down a direct challenge to political handlers, sponsors, and recruiters attempting to bankroll youth violence ahead of the 2027 General Election. 

Speaking as the government officially flagged 109 organized criminal gangs operating across the country, Omollo warned that security agencies are shifting focus from street-level youth operatives to the wealthy financiers directing hired goon squads for political intimidation and disruption.

### Western Kenya Leads Gang Count as Trans Nzoia Tops Arrests

The government's newly published inventory marks a sharp rise from the 89 illegal groups outlawed under Gazette Notice No. 10427 of 2016. Western Kenya emerged as the primary hotspot for gang infrastructure, accounting for 38 of the 109 identified outfits.

However, law enforcement figures compiled between January 1 and August 31, 2026, reveal that Trans Nzoia County became the operational epicenter for police crackdowns. Trans Nzoia recorded 491 arrests out of the 1,827 total suspects detained and arraigned nationwide for gang activity and goonism during the eight-month window.

Court proceedings over the same period yielded 729 convictions, with Rift Valley tribunals securing 36.5 percent of all successful guilty verdicts. A further 716 cases remain active before the courts as state prosecutors push to convert police dragnets into long-term sentences.

### Following Bank Trails and Targeting Political Sponsors

According to Dr Omollo, police leadership has instructed intelligence units to target the underlying networks that allow criminal outfits to regenerate after routine arrests. Investigators are now tracking bank accounts and paper trails to link money flows from extortion, illegal trades, and political patronage directly to gang organizers.

Omollo emphasized that individuals who recruit, finance, or give operational directives to violent groups will face prosecution alongside the youth executing commands on the ground. The Inspector-General of Police has issued directives to Regional Police Commanders to suppress gang operations, while Omollo warned that police officers found aiding criminal groups or turning a blind eye will face administrative and disciplinary action.

### Economic Pipelines and Reporting Channels

Beyond criminal prosecutions, the government acknowledged that economic hardship leaves young people vulnerable to exploitation by moneyed political figures offering quick cash for violence. 

To prevent released offenders from returning to criminal networks, the state is pairing enforcement with rehabilitation initiatives alongside the Probation and Aftercare Service, Kenya Prisons Service, county administrations, and community organizations. 

Youth are being urged to tap into formal credit and business channels such as the Hustler Fund and the NYOTA Programme as alternatives to illegal employment. 

As political activity picks up momentum, security authorities have urged members of the public to report suspicious group mobilization or extortion rings through the Directorate of Criminal Investigations toll-free line, *Fichua kwa DCI* (0800 722 203), or standard emergency police contacts.
