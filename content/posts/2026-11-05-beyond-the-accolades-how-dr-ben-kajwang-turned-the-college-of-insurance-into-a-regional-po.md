For years, the College of Insurance occupied a traditional space in Kenya’s professional development landscape. Under the leadership of Dr. Ben Kajwang, however, the institution has undergone a transformation that moves beyond classroom instruction, focusing instead on commercial viability and regional influence across East Africa.

This week’s receipt of the 7th Global Change Makers Award serves as an international marker of that pivot. The award, presented by Project 100, places the focus on leadership and social impact—but for those in the insurance sector, the achievement is inseparable from the physical and administrative architecture Kajwang has put in place since 2014.

![Dr Ben Kajwang Wins Global Change Makers Award After Being Named National Hero](https://i.ibb.co/mFHwgZGq/150d1366b56f.webp)

Central to this strategy was the conceptualization and development of the Edge Hotel and Convention Centre. By constructing a five-star facility, Kajwang effectively bridged the gap between professional training and the MICE (Meetings, Incentives, Conferences, and Exhibitions) industry, a move that culminated in the venue hosting the 48th African Insurance Organisation Conference. This diversification moved the institution’s model away from a singular reliance on tuition fees, creating a commercial ecosystem that supports the academic mission.

Kajwang’s tenure has been marked by a clear drive to regionalize the college's footprint. His work has expanded training modules into Uganda, Rwanda, Burundi, and Malawi, catering to the growing needs of the wider COMESA region. The raw numbers behind this expansion are significant: his leadership has overseen the training of over 8,500 pension trustees and 5,500 insurance professionals, effectively creating a standardized baseline for risk management across multiple borders.

This regional approach explains the trajectory of his recent accolades. The national hero recognition he received on October 20, 2026, where he was listed as Hero No. 186, was specifically framed under the category of ‘Scholarship, Professionalism and Research.’ That recognition, coming from the State, mirrors the institutional work he has led at the Policyholders Compensation Fund, where he serves as the Chairman of the Technical Committee.

Kajwang’s influence relies on a blend of industry credentials—spanning the UK’s Institute of Risk Management to the Chartered Insurance Institute—and a persistent publishing schedule. With nine books and over 70 academic papers to his name, he has maintained a high volume of output that keeps the College of Insurance at the center of the industry's intellectual discourse.

The Global Change Makers Award adds a layer of international validation to his local efforts, but it essentially acts as an audit of the last two years. Between his time as President of the African Insurance Organisation (2022–2023) and his ongoing roles at the Africa Re Foundation and the AIO Foundation, Kajwang has been consolidating influence in the regional regulatory space.

As the College of Insurance looks toward the end of 2026, the challenge remains shifting that influence into long-term institutional sustainability, ensuring that the infrastructure and regional networks Kajwang has built survive the leadership cycles that follow.
