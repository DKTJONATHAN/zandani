The United Democratic Alliance has instituted a strict 24-hour window for election petitions as it rolls out ward-level grassroots contests across 10 counties, racing to lock down its grassroots delegates by the end of October.

According to an official notice issued by the party’s Electoral and Nomination Dispute Resolution Committee (ENDRC), any aspirant aggrieved by the outcome of the ward elections must lodge their complaint within a day of the vote. The tight contest window is designed to prevent protracted local petitions from stalling the formation of ward congresses as the ruling party accelerates its nationwide organizational calendar.

The exercise forms the administrative bridge between polling-centre committee members and the party’s broader county machinery. Under the UDA constitution, registered members at every polling station elect a 20-person committee representing specific interest groups, including farmers, traders, professionals, religious representatives, youth, and persons with disabilities. Those committee members then serve as an electoral college to pick the ward congress officials who direct the party across Kenya’s 1,450 civic units.

### Staggered Voting Calendar

![Deputy President Kithure Kindiki leads a meeting of UDA polling centre officials at Nembure Stadium in Manyatta Constituency, Embu County, September 12, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/XfLvFCRT/922461c271ae.webp)

The National Elections Board scheduled the latest phase across three distinct dates, distributing the vote among pastoral frontier counties, western corridors, and strongholds in the Rift Valley and Mount Kenya.

Samburu County opened the sequence on October 16, followed on October 17 by balloting across five counties: Tana River, Vihiga, Nakuru, Marsabit, and Isiolo.

The final group, slated to cast ballots on October 31, will cover Meru, Embu, Tharaka Nithi, and Bomet. The choice of clusters moves the process through expansive logistical zones—from sparsely populated northern pastoral wards to high-density farming constituencies in upper Eastern and the South Rift.

### The Delegate Numbers Game

The push to conclude ward elections follows a massive turnout during the party's foundational polling-centre phase. In its initial launch across 20 counties, UDA drew 310,651 candidates competing for roughly 237,940 committee seats spread over 12,000 polling stations.

Subsequent phases and re-runs pushed the cumulative candidate tally past 450,000, with party internal estimates reaching upwards of 500,000 as interest expanded. Consolidating that bottom tier into formal ward congresses represents the most decisive shift in internal authority since the party swept into power, determining which local factions will control constituency caucuses and party operations heading into election preparations.

With hundreds of thousands of aspirants vying for a limited slate of civic executive posts, the ENDRC faces immediate strain. The 24-hour filing deadline signals an effort to centralize and expedite rulings before disputed delegations can disrupt the next level of the pyramid.

### The 2027 Regulatory Clock

Beyond internal party management, the speed of the ward-level rollout is driven by mandatory timelines enforced by the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) and the Registrar of Political Parties.

Political parties face a statutory deadline of March 16, 2027, to present fully certified membership registers and submit verified lists of candidates intending to seek nomination. The commission's broader calendar further mandates that all party primaries and dispute adjudications conclude by May 9, 2027.

By completing the ward congress elections before the end of the year, UDA aims to establish a fully constituted delegate network capable of managing candidate vetting, membership drives, and branch structures long before the statutory submission deadlines take effect.
