Commercial suppliers waiting on approximately Ksh175 billion in stalled government payments now face automated tax recovery before receiving their money, under a fresh revenue enforcement strategy submitted to Parliament.

The Kenya Revenue Authority told the National Assembly’s Finance and National Planning Committee that it plans to intercept payments flowing through government pending bills to recover outstanding tax liabilities from large and medium corporate contractors.

The proposed interception arrives as the tax collector works to recover from a Ksh132.1 billion shortfall in the financial year ending June 30, when it collected Ksh2.844 trillion against an assigned target of Ksh2.977 trillion. That deficit has deepened pressure on the National Treasury to bridge budget financing through public debt.

### The IFMIS Interception Net

![An undated image of the KRA entrance](https://i.ibb.co/SwcKbKQK/4741848a6af7.webp)

Under the framework presented to lawmakers, KRA will match state procurement payouts against taxpayer debt profiles in real time. Technical units are being deployed to cross-reference data from the Integrated Financial Management Information System (IFMIS) directly with domestic tax records.

Suppliers flagged with unpaid obligations will have their settlements seized or adjusted at the source, converting discrepancies directly into revenue inflows. Alongside the pending bills mechanism, the authority is conducting withholding tax reconciliations on goods and services delivered to ministries, state departments, and corporate entities.

The enforcement package also incorporates an internal offset rule, allowing the agency to apply verified and approved tax refunds against an enterprise’s unpaid liabilities instead of disbursing cash back into the private sector.

### The Arrears on the Other Side of the Ledger

While the plan tightens enforcement on private businesses that have waited years for state settlements, the authority's own parliamentary filing reveals significant arrears within its own operations.

KRA reported an accumulated pending bill portfolio of Ksh5.58 billion as of June 30, 2026. The vast majority of that debt—Ksh4.34 billion—stems from unpaid contracts for Excise Goods Management System stamps used to track excisable goods across the country.

![Commissioner General of the Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA), Mr. Adan Mohamed](https://i.ibb.co/WNypYk4x/fcf2728c9007.webp)

The taxman’s internal debt book also contains a Ksh829.59 million obligation arising from a court settlement involving Habimana Sued, as well as Ksh416.36 million owed to the Administration Police Service for facility and operational security.

### The Practical Squeeze on Commercial Contractors

For commercial contractors, the enforcement proposal risks compounding an already acute liquidity freeze. Business lobbies have repeatedly warned that delayed national and county government disbursements have impaired operational capital, forced commercial loan defaults, and suppressed formal employment.

By converting pending bill disbursements into instant tax recoveries, the state effectively ensures its fiscal claims take priority over commercial supply-chain debts and accumulated bank interest.

The implementation schedule for the IFMIS data matching now rests before the Finance Committee, where lawmakers are assessing the legality and operational viability of garnishing overdue commercial claims before contractors receive their principal disbursements.
