Hassan Joho has stepped into the digital spotlight with another rare piece of Maranello engineering, flanking a factory-finished Rosso red Ferrari Purosangue estimated to cost upwards of KSh 105 million to put on Kenyan tarmac.

Footage circulating across social media platforms over the past 24 hours captured the Mining and Blue Economy Cabinet Secretary alongside the high-performance four-door machine, signaling the latest high-octane escalation in a garage portfolio that has long set the pace for political and celebrity tastemakers in Nairobi and Mombasa.

The acquisition represents a distinct operational upgrade within Joho's personal stable. While the former Mombasa governor has historically leaned into low-slung performance and heavy-duty luxury cruisers, the Purosangue introduces a different category altogether: Ferrari’s aggressively protected entry into utility performance.

### The Landed Economics of Maranello’s Utility Flagship

Bringing the Italian machine through the port and onto domestic logbooks comes with heavy fiscal friction. Automotive market valuation estimates place the landed cost of a Ferrari Purosangue in Kenya between KSh 100 million and KSh 115 million once initial acquisition, international freight, customs clearance, and local import tariffs are fully cleared.

That figure makes the vehicle one of the most expensive utility-badged automobiles legally cleared for Kenyan roads, easily surpassing the landing figures of standard showroom luxury crossovers.

Underneath the sculpted Rosso red bodywork sits an engineering package designed to out-muscle traditional super-SUVs. The vehicle runs on a 6.5-liter naturally aspirated V12 engine capable of pushing out 725 horsepower without turbocharging assistance.

According to factory technical data, the unit pulls the machine from a standstill to 100 km/h in 3.3 seconds. For open stretches, the engine's mechanical ceiling stretches beyond a top speed of 310 km/h, delivering track-tier velocity wrapped in high-ground-clearance sheet metal.

### Curating a Multi-Million Horsepower Stable

Joho’s selection of the Purosangue deepens a well-documented relationship with elite motoring marques. Rather than abandoning his established automotive anchors, the four-door machine slots directly into an expansive private lineup that spans British grand touring and German utility.

His known vehicle footprint already features the Bentley Mulsanne, a classic symbol of ultra-luxury executive travel, alongside the open-top Ferrari California that established his Maranello loyalty early in his national political profile. Rounding out his heavy-security transport roster is the Mercedes-Benz G-Wagon, the standard off-road benchmark among regional power brokers.

By adding the Purosangue, Joho balances raw sports performance with four-seater practicality, securing a model deliberately framed by the Italian automaker as an exclusive crossover rather than a mass-production SUV.

### The Mechanics of the ‘Ferrari Utility Vehicle’

Maranello has gone to unusual lengths to distance the Purosangue from typical utility offerings, marketing the platform strictly as a “Ferrari Utility Vehicle.” The chassis marks the company's first production model built with an authentic four-door, four-seat layout, allowing rear-seat passenger access without abandoning the sports car architecture found on its mid-engine siblings.

The circulating visual footage confirms the arrival of a bespoke vehicle whose local presence remains exceedingly scarce across the regional transport register.

With valuation tallies pushing toward the KSh 115 million threshold under Kenya's tax regime, the vehicle stands as the latest high-profile verification of Joho's uncompromising automotive taste, adding a 725-horsepower V12 flagship to an already formidable domestic collection.
