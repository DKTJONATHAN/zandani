Geoffrey Mosiria found himself wrestling with a very modern indignity hours after losing his executive job at City Hall: nobody had remembered to remove him from the bosses’ WhatsApp group.

Taking to his social channels on Thursday, October 22, 2026, the outspoken former Chief Officer turned the awkward administrative oversight into public comedy, comparing his discomfort to the prolonged miseries of Manchester United supporters.

“Man -U na katibu mkuu tuko same Whatsapp group bi uchungu sana,” Mosiria wrote, laying bare the silent tension of sitting inside the exact digital forum where county leadership coordinates daily business just a day after being pushed out the door.

Rather than slipping into quiet grievance, Mosiria used the sudden online spotlight to execute an immediate political flip. He informed his followers that his exit from county employment had cleared the runway for a bigger assignment, claiming that ordinary Nairobians had demanded he trade his administrative badge for the county’s executive office.

“Wananchi Mnasema Hamtaki politician mnataka a worker, a servant not a master. Wananchi wa Nairobi mmeniita niwe Gavana wenu, nimukubali wito wenu,” he declared.

From there, the former chief officer launched into full campaign theater, rolling out an unpunctuated blitz of rhyming slogans aimed squarely at unseating his former boss, Governor Johnson Sakaja: “NAIROBI NI MORE SERIOUS NA GEOFFREY MOSIRIA! SIRI NI MOSIRIA! NAIROBI NI MOSIRIA! MOSIRIA NI NAIROBI!”

The public theatrics follow an abrupt administrative purge formalised on Wednesday, October 21, 2026. Nairobi County Secretary and Head of Public Service Machel Waikenda issued an official notice revoking Mosiria’s appointment as Chief Officer for Public Participation, Citizen Engagement, and Customer Service.

To keep the department running, City Hall assigned Zipporah Njeri Mwangi, the substantive Chief Officer for City Culture, Arts, and Tourism, to take charge of Mosiria’s public participation desk in an acting capacity.

The rupture at City Hall did not come without warning. Mosiria had spent recent weeks openly telegraphing his intention to challenge Sakaja for the Nairobi governorship in the 2027 General Election. While his Wednesday termination letter represented a definitive institutional break, Mosiria insisted the tension had been simmering behind closed doors long before his campaign declarations.

According to Mosiria, the rift trace back nearly three years to December 2023, when he was abruptly stripped of his responsibilities in the county’s health sector. He claimed that prior to his reshuffle away from medical facilities, his operational war chest suffered a devastating blow, alleging his department’s budget was slashed from Ksh 1.2 billion down to Ksh 100 million.

That fiscal squeeze, Mosiria argued, deliberately choked his operational capacity across city health centers and set off a pattern of lateral shuffles that eventually parked him in the citizen engagement docket.

By packaging his formal dismissal between football punchlines and high-decibel campaign chants, Mosiria has ensured that his departure from City Hall reads less like a quiet bureaucratic termination and more like the opening act of an unruly race toward 2027. Whether the administrators have finally tapped the button to eject him from the county leadership group chat remains, for now, an open question.
