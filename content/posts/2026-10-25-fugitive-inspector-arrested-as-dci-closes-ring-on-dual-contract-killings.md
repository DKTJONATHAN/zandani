The arrest of Police Inspector Kenneth Kipkemboi Sang on the streets of Eldoret has closed the manhunt for the suspected tactical coordinator behind the fatal ambush of two Nairobi professionals killed ten months apart.

Detectives from the Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) headquarters intercepted Sang along Uganda Road in the Eldoret central business district on Saturday, October 24, as he drove a silver Toyota Fielder. According to homicide investigators, the fugitive officer had spent months off the radar and was attempting to liquidate the vehicle to finance his continued evasion.

Sang’s detention removes the final fugitive from a multi-agency wanted list that connects the September 9, 2025 shooting of city advocate Mathew Kyalo Mbobu along Magadi Road to the July 29, 2026 execution of mental health researcher Dr. Victoria Nthunya Mutiso in Upper Hill.

### The Operational Coordination Inside the Service

Investigators have cast Sang not merely as an accessory, but as the central logistical linchpin who allegedly ran coordination between civilian plotters and boots on the ground. 

![An undated photo of clinical psychologist and mental health researcher Dr Victoria Nthunya Mutiso, who served as the Head of Research at the Africa Mental Health Research and Train](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/_dr-victoria-nthunya-mutiso.jpg.webp?itok=cjEVNd7W)

DCI findings indicate the inspector liaised directly with key suspects already in custody to organize surveillance, route logistics, and operational planning for Mbobu’s killing, while maintaining ties to the network that struck Dr. Mutiso ten months later.

Sang’s arrest directly expands the institutional footprint of the investigation within the National Police Service. He becomes the third serving officer detained in connection with the double homicide, joining two police constables who have been held in remand.

The Director of Public Prosecutions (ODPP) has already cleared formal murder indictments against the two constables, alongside three civilian members of a single family who detectives say held overlapping commercial and personal motives targeting both Mbobu and Mutiso.

### Connecting Magadi Road to Upper Hill

For nearly a year, the two homicides were handled as separate urban ambushes. Mbobu was intercepted and fatally shot inside his vehicle on an evening commute along Magadi Road in what police initially categorized as an isolated contract hit. In July 2026, Dr. Mutiso was similarly cornered and shot dead in Upper Hill.

![DCI Headquarters, Nairobi](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/dci-gate-1.jpeg.webp?itok=S6cELRJp)

The breakthrough arrived several weeks ago when ballistic matching and forensic phone data established that the civilian sponsors funding the surveillance on Dr. Mutiso shared direct financial and legal conflicts handled by Mbobu before his death. 

That forensic cross-match pointed detectives toward a shared enforcement cell operating inside uniform lines, with Inspector Sang identified as the operational bridge between the civilian financiers and the tactical units executing the ambushes.

### Arraignment and Expanding Evidentiary Leads

With Sang in custody, the State faces the task of consolidating two sprawling capital files into a synchronized prosecution. 

Detectives are examining his call records, digital transactions, and movements between Nairobi, the Rift Valley, and western transit corridors during his period on the run to establish whether additional junior officers provided safe houses or logistical assistance during the manhunt.

Sang is expected to be processed through the DCI headquarters before being produced before the High Court in Nairobi to take a plea alongside his co-accused constables and the civilian family members facing murder charges.
