Rather than relying on parliamentary committees or domestic investigative agencies, the political opposition has moved to apply external diplomatic pressure on the Ministry of Health, directly asking the United States to track the utilisation of billions allocated for emergency biosecurity.

Wiper Patriotic Front leader Kalonzo Musyoka confirmed that he has formally engaged officials at the U.S. Embassy in Nairobi, urging Washington to activate its bilateral oversight mechanisms to monitor how the state is deploying Ebola preparedness grants.

Speaking during a public address in Mombasa on Saturday, October 10, Kalonzo claimed that administrative negligence and procurement risks within government threaten to siphon emergency resources away from active surveillance.

"Kenyans must not lose their lives because of negligence and corruption," Kalonzo told the gathering, asserting that bilateral partners must demand line-item verification for all health interventions directed through the exchequer.

### The Ksh2.1 Billion Preparedness Portfolio

![Wiper Leader Kalonzo Musyoka speaking at a rally in Mombasa on October 10, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/XfyNhhhL/6d4cfcc3d549.webp)

The funding portfolio under dispute stems from external emergency allocations committed ahead of and immediately following Kenya's confirmation of its first imported Ebola case on October 2, 2026, which resulted in a fatality supervised by state burial teams.

In July 2026, the United States directed $14.5 million—equivalent to Ksh1.9 billion—under the Trump administration to fortify Kenya's technical defences against the virus. That grant was designated for specific, auditable front-line pillars:

* Real-time disease surveillance across high-risk counties and transit corridors.
* Laboratory diagnostic capacity and rapid-testing reagent supply chains.
* Standardised training for primary healthcare personnel managing suspect admissions.
* Points of entry screening infrastructure, including international airports and border outposts.
* Emergency coordination hubs and targeted community risk communication.

Complementing the American allocation, China remitted an additional Ksh259 million grant, earmarked for joint Ebola containment activities alongside drought mitigation measures.

Together, the packages created an emergency funding pool exceeding Ksh2.1 billion, intended to prevent community transmission before an outbreak could take root in major urban centres.

### Reopening the 2017 Precedent

Kalonzo’s strategic decision to petition the embassy directly exploits Washington's rigid foreign-aid compliance framework—a pressure point that carries historical consequences for Kenya's public health administration.

![President William Ruto and U.S President Donald Trump at the G7 summit in Évian, France, June 17, 2026 PCS](https://i.ibb.co/w9Ym73m/040180379d55.webp)

In 2017, the U.S. government took the rare step of suspending direct financial assistance to the Ministry of Health after internal audits revealed systemic accounting discrepancies and procurement irregularities. That freeze forced Afya House into a protracted overhaul of its grant-management protocols before normal direct disbursements resumed.

By framing the current Ebola expenditure as vulnerable to similar mismanagement, the opposition is attempting to force donor compliance officers to demand real-time verification from the Ministry of Health, rather than waiting for post-expenditure reviews by the Auditor-General, which often take years to reach Parliament.

While Kalonzo asserted that officials have put public safety at risk through weak preparedness, he did not publish primary documentation of diversion during the Mombasa rally, leaving the burden of verification on donor audit desks.

### The Operational Stakes at Afya House

The opposition's demand introduces a delicate governance challenge for the Ministry of Health at a moment when epidemiological vigilance remains active. With contact tracing teams already deployed and isolation facilities on standby following the October 2 fatality, any donor hesitation or administrative compliance deadlock would directly threaten field operations.

The U.S. Embassy in Nairobi has not issued a formal public statement on Kalonzo's representations or indicated whether bilateral review missions have flagged procedural anomalies in the drawdown of the Ksh1.9 billion fund.
