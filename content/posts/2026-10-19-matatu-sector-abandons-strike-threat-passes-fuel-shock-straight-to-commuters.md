Kenya’s commercial transport lobby has abandoned the picket line in favor of an outright balance-sheet transfer, declaring that matatu operators will absorb no operational losses from the October pump shock and will instead pass the entire burden directly to passengers.

The decision by the Matatu Owners Association (MOA) closes the door on another paralyzing national transport withdrawal, even as diesel climbed to KSh 227.76 per litre under the Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority’s (EPRA) October–November pricing cycle. But rather than using fleet shutdowns to force statutory concessions as they did five months ago, operators have decided their commercial survival lies in immediate fare recalibrations at the stage.

Speaking on Sunday, MOA President Albert Karakacha confirmed that operators have no appetite for service withdrawals, framing the hike as a routine cost factor that will simply be invoiced downstream to everyday travelers.

"We will continue running our business. We are business people and we will do what we have to do," Karakacha stated. "The one who will feel the brunt are our customers, and I feel sorry for them because the effects will trickle down to them."

### The Broken Mombasa Accord

Behind the refusal to strike lies an unraveling executive pact. The transport lobby’s decision to pass costs straight to commuters comes alongside sharp accusations that the Kenya Kwanza administration reneged on structural promises hammered out during high-level negotiations earlier this year.

![Albert Karakacha](https://i.ibb.co/FbdgbWHg/d529ae20c7de.webp)

Following a crippling nationwide strike in May 2026, transport leaders met President William Ruto at State House in Mombasa to broker an exit deal. According to Karakacha, the executive made two core commitments: to maintain near-term diesel stability despite Middle Eastern crude volatility, and to facilitate the duty-free or state-backed importation of more than 100,000 electric public service vehicles (PSVs).

The proposed electric fleet was designed as a permanent hedge to insulate route operators from global crude spikes. Yet five months later, the transition mechanism remains frozen in administrative limbo.

"We are still discussing the electric buses," Karakacha said. "If the president is able to give a clear direction on how we will acquire these vehicles, the issue of fuel will be over."

Instead of receiving clean-energy fleet financing, operators entered October confronting an across-the-board pump increase. Diesel rose by KSh 9 per litre, super petrol jumped by KSh 12, and illuminating kerosene surged by KSh 28 per litre, blindsiding transport businesses that had anchored their cash flows to the lower margins achieved during the mid-year price reprieve.

### The Memory of May 2026

Operational calculations within the matatu sector are heavily informed by the high cost of the May shutdown. That five-day crisis, which kicked off on May 18, grounded logistics nationwide, stranded school children, and shuttered major commercial arterial corridors.

![OTC Bus Station](https://i.ibb.co/4wv0fZCW/0556684979ce.webp)

The strike also escalated into street unrest that left four people dead and more than 30 injured. While the State House Mombasa intervention yielded an immediate KSh 10 cut that brought diesel down to KSh 222.86 for the June–July cycle, the relief proved short-lived. With pump prices now higher than they were prior to the Mombasa accord, operators view industrial confrontation as an expensive political trap where business owners bear vehicle damage and lost revenue while policy reversals quietly lapse.

By treating fuel hikes as a routine passthrough rather than an industrial grievance, the MOA has effectively insulated its balance sheet from the regulatory regulator while leaving passengers defenceless against predatory fare adjustments at bus termini.

### Legal and Street Resistance Gathers

While matatu owners step aside from confrontation, institutional and civic backlash against EPRA’s pricing schedule is escalating on other fronts.

The Counties Commuters Welfare Association (COCOWA) issued a formal 14-day ultimatum on Sunday demanding that EPRA revoke the October price increments immediately or face litigation in the High Court. COCOWA contends that the pricing cycle was conducted without mandatory public participation or structured consultations with consumer bodies, leaving travelers exposed to arbitrary fare increases without regulatory oversight.

Concurrently, civil society organizations and human rights activists have called for national demonstrations on Tuesday, October 20, to protest the mounting cost of living and unbuffered fuel price reviews.

With matatu owners declining to shut down stages and commuters left without institutional shielding, the pressure shifts directly to Tuesday's street mobilization and EPRA's looming court deadline.
