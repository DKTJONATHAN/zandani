For years, the public saw the finished product: impeccably pressed bespoke suits, sharp tailoring, and deliberate cut choices that came to define former Prime Minister Raila Odinga’s statesman silhouette on national and international podiums. 

For Nairobi fashion designer Bolo Bespoke, however, those iconic public appearances always began with a quiet domestic ritual—a cup of tea, bolt swatches laid out on a table, and a routine phone consultation to pick patterns. 

Marking nearly a full calendar year since Odinga’s passing, the couturier pulled back the curtain on that intimate artisan bond on Friday, October 2, sharing an emotional retrospective on the personal void left behind when those tailoring appointments abruptly ceased.

### The Lost Tea and Fabric Sessions

Writing to his followers on Friday, the designer who crafted scores of Odinga’s signature pieces spoke directly to the everyday mechanics of their relationship, framing his grief around the sudden quiet that settled over his workshop.

“Ohh BABA… It has been one whole year without shaking your hand, sharing a cup of tea with you, calling you to confirm the fabric and design for your next outfit, hearing your voice face to face,” Bolo wrote. “One year later, the silence still hurts. All we have now are the memories, the history we shared. Since you left us, Baba, there has been a silence that words cannot explain.”

Bolo’s tribute highlighted a side of the veteran opposition leader rarely captured on the campaign trail: a client who took personal interest in the tailoring craft, engaging in regular one-on-one sessions to review textures, cuts, and seasonal choices.

“Your absence is deeply felt,” the designer added. “Your voice is dearly missed, and your memory lives in the hearts of all who loved and admired you. You left behind a legacy that time can never erase. Continue resting peacefully, Baba Raila.”

### Vigils and City Projections

The designer’s private reflection arrived just as the capital transitioned into a massive public staging of remembrance. On Friday evening, thousands of citizens converged on Uhuru Park to inaugurate “Mwezi wa Baba,” a month-long commemorative program designed to honor Odinga’s political and social imprint.

Attendees carried floral wreaths, framed portraits, and handheld wax candles, transforming the city park into an illuminated sea of remembrance. The gathering brought together an eclectic mix of clerics, vocalists, and performers reflecting Odinga’s sprawling cultural crossover appeal.

Pastor Robert Burale led interfaith prayers on the main stage, guiding the crowd through an evening of solemn invocation before the tempo shifted to music. Gospel minister Solomon Mkubwa performed alongside celebrated Ohangla heavyweights Prince Indah and Elisha Toto, while Emmanuel Musili added his voice to the commemorative roster.

As darkness fell over the central business district, the commemorative imagery expanded onto Nairobi's skyline. High-powered visual projections lit up the exterior facade of the Kenyatta International Convention Centre (KICC) tower, casting historical reels and snapshots of Odinga’s five-decade political journey across the concrete landmark.

### Nationwide Remembrance Leading to Bondo

The gathering at Uhuru Park was mirrored across several regional hubs on Friday night. Simultaneous candle-lighting ceremonies took place in Kisumu, Mombasa, and Eldoret, drawing urban crowds who held parallel vigils in town squares and assembly grounds.

The weekend events formally launched a continuous schedule of anniversary observances leading up to Thursday, October 15, which will mark precisely 12 months since Odinga died at the age of 80 while undergoing specialized medical treatment in India.

The memorial circuit is slated to culminate in Siaya County at his ancestral home in Kang’o ka Jaramogi in Bondo. There, state and family officials are scheduled to officially unveil an architectural mausoleum constructed over his resting place, concluding the anniversary month with permanent physical honors for the late statesman.
