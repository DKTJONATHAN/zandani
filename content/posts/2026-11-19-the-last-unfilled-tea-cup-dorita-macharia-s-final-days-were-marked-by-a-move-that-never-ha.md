For 86-year-old Dorita Lasinder Macharia, the end of October 2026 was meant to be defined by a change of scenery. She had spent the weeks leading up to her death finalizing a quiet, personal milestone: the relocation from her long-time Loresho residence to a new home in Nyari Estate.

That transition, intended to represent a fresh chapter, remained incomplete when she was killed at her Loresho home on the night of October 30. During her funeral service held on Wednesday, November 18, the details of that unfinished move became a poignant focus for those who knew her.

Her friend, Henry Macharia, provided a stark view of just how close she had come to closing the door on her Loresho life for good. He recounted a conversation from just seven days before her death, where the logistics of the move were already settled. According to Henry, Dorita had already transferred her belongings to the new property in Nyari. She was, quite literally, just waiting for the final step of moving herself into the space.

“A week before your demise, you promised me a cup of tea in your new house at Nyari Estate,” Henry recalled during the service. “You told me that you had moved everything to the house; the only thing remaining was yourself.”

That promise of a casual, domestic gathering—a cup of tea—now stands as the final marker of her intentions, a detail that sharply contrasts with the reality of the investigation that followed. 

As family and friends gathered to celebrate her life, the service also highlighted the fractured state of her immediate family. Her son, Ian Macharia, who delivered the eulogy, noted that the gathering served as a final tribute to a woman he described as a devoted mother to himself, Regina, and Alton (also referred to as James in segments of the service). However, the service was marked by a notable absence; Ian was the only member of her immediate family present to witness the final rites.

The funeral occurred only a day after the legal proceedings surrounding her death took a definitive turn. On Tuesday, November 17, the Director of Public Prosecutions (DPP) appeared before the Kibera Law Courts to confirm that 86-year-old Dorita’s granddaughter, Thandie Malaika Wanjiku Gitao, will face charges for the murder. Thandie is the daughter of former television actress Regina Wambui Gitao, widely known by her stage name, Regina Re.

With the criminal investigation now moving toward the courts, the items Dorita had already packed and moved to the Nyari residence remain as a silent, unresolved collection of a life that was meant to continue elsewhere.
