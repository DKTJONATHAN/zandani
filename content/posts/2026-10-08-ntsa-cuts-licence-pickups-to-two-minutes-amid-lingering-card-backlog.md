The National Transport and Safety Authority (NTSA) announced Thursday that motorists with processed driving licences can now pick them up at service centres in under two minutes, an operational change targeted at ending persistent hall congestion during Customer Service Week.

According to an official directive issued on October 8, the expedited dispatch applies to applicants whose physical documents have already been manufactured and dispatched to branch desks nationwide. Motorists must still appear in person at their designated regional NTSA station to complete the handover.

“Happy Customer Week, at NTSA we have transformed service delivery,” the regulator stated. “Visit and collect your driving licence in under two minutes. No more queues at NTSA.”

### The Collection Counter vs The Printing Queue

![Kenyans lining up inside the National Transport and Safety Authority (NTSA) office in Nairobi in 2024.](https://i.ibb.co/j9YmfsDT/7f92b29c2fc1.webp)

While the two-minute window addresses the final handover at customer counters—historically marked by multi-hour queues—the accelerated pickup does not alter the upstream waiting period required to get cards manufactured.

Frustrations among transport operators and private motorists have mounted over substantial gaps between biometric capture and final card issuance. Numerous applicants report waiting between two and three months after completing enrollment and settling processing fees without receiving notification that their smart driving licence cards are ready for pickup.

Internal administrative estimates indicate that hundreds of thousands of applications remain pending across the system. The sheer volume of incoming requests has routinely outpaced the state agency's in-house machinery, constraining daily output for both driver credentials and motor vehicle number plates.

### Push Toward Private-Sector Printing

![NTSA DG Nashon Kondiwa during a past meeting with MPs](https://i.ibb.co/pjfHY9kb/5b867cbde138.webp)

To bridge the gap between application volumes and industrial capacity, transport administrators have weighed unbundling manufacturing from state desks entirely.

NTSA Director General Nashon Kondiwa previously confirmed that the agency has actively explored onboarding private commercial fabricators to absorb the heavy document production load and dismantle chronic backlogs.

Speaking on Citizen TV, Kondiwa argued that civil registries in comparable jurisdictions rely on certified industrial partners to press plates and print secured cards, allowing regulators to focus strictly on compliance, testing, and data verification.

“I do not think a government, or any government institution, should be trading in number plates, which is what has been happening in Kenya since independence,” Kondiwa said, referencing systemic equipment bottlenecks that have slowed both driver credentials and registration plates.

For applicants visiting regional halls this week, the immediate gain remains confined to the counter: those holding active collection alerts can walk in and out in minutes, while tens of thousands still waiting on factory lines must await long-term structural reforms to printing infrastructure.
