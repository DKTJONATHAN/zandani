Media personality Alex Mwakideu has shifted the public conversation on automated traffic enforcement, questioning why digital speed traps operate with a selective memory for vehicles missing registration plates.

The debate follows the National Transport and Safety Authority's hardline stance on outstanding traffic penalties. The regulatory body announced that it had placed the driving licences of motorists with unpaid speeding fines on hold, making it clear that offenders captured by road cameras would receive no leniency.

Mwakideu acknowledged that he fell into the enforcement net himself, admitting he was caught speeding, received a fine, and cleared the balance. But his compliance triggered a broader observation about parity on Kenyan roads: while registered vehicles are easily traced through digital databases, motorists driving around without number plates appear entirely immune to the same electronic dragnet.

“NTSA wakasema nayo nayo. Hatutabembeleza madereva ambao hawalipi fine,” Mwakideu remarked on Friday, November 27, 2026, addressing the authority's zero-tolerance directive.

At the core of his critique is the mechanics of automated surveillance. Modern speed cameras rely entirely on clear captures of vehicle registration details to log offenses and dispatch electronic notifications to registered owners. When a vehicle carries legitimate plates, tracking is straightforward. Licence details link directly to the owner's file, making the freezing of a driving licence a swift administrative process.

However, Mwakideu questioned how traffic authorities reconcile this digital precision with the growing presence of vehicles operating openly without number plates. He pointed out the apparent contradiction in a system that penalizes compliant motorists while offering a natural loophole to those dodging identification entirely.

“Kwa nini mimi niko na number plate, na huyu ambaye hana, anaendesha mbio camera haiwezi mshika sababu hana number plate?” he asked, highlighting the equity gap in automated policing.

The radio host maintained that accountability should apply across the board, noting that his own decision to settle his fine stemmed from the fact that his registration details made him traceable. As NTSA tightens the screws on digital compliance and locks out defaulters from their portal services, the underlying question remains how roadside technology plans to bridge the gap left by unnumbered vehicles slipping past the lens.
