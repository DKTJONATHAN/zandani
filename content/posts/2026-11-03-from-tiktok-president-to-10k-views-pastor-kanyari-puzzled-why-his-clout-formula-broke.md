Pastor Victor Kanyari has traded his self-declared throne as the "president of TikTok" for a quiet radio confession, admitting that the frantic chase for midnight views and virtual gifts left his public reputation deeply scorched.

Speaking during an interview on Radio Maisha, the head of Salvation Healing Ministry looked back at the peak of his late-night digital broadcasts. Operating around midnight, those sessions routinely pulled massive crowds and substantial financial returns from online audiences.

"Kuna wakati nilikuwa the president of TikTok. Iyo wakati nilikuwa naongea vitu, ndo maana nilitoka TikTok," Kanyari recounted, acknowledging that the pressure to keep late-night viewers hooked pushed him into content that clashed sharply with his standing as a member of the clergy.

That trade-off, however, came with a heavy toll on both his standing and his numbers. While his live streams once commanded immense digital real estate, his current sessions average roughly 10,000 viewers—a steep descent from his peak influence.

"Nilikuwa naingia saa sita usiku lakini sasa niko na views kama elfu kumi, lakini at the same time nimechoma kuchoma," he admitted, pointing directly to the combination of reputational damage and audience erosion.

Yet, beyond the regret over chasing digital validation, the preacher revealed a lingering frustration with how unpredictable social media algorithms operate. Despite his willingness to court controversy for engagement, Kanyari confessed he is completely baffled as to why his previous formula no longer yields traction.

"Mimi nashangaa, I don’t know hata saa hizi nitatrend tu mimi. Sijuangi ni nini nimesema na mbona mambo yangu hayatrend kama mambo yangu funny," he said, reflecting on his current struggle to recreate the viral momentum that once defined his late-night broadcasts.
