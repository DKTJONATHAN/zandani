Tanzanian entrepreneur Isaya Yunge is using high-impact motivational storytelling to shape his public narrative just weeks after his high-profile wedding to President William Ruto’s daughter, Charlene Ruto.

Rather than offering routine post-wedding updates, Yunge turned to the story of Malawian innovator William Kamkwamba on Thursday, November 19, publishing a social media video that paired Kamkwamba’s famous journey from rural farmland to international forums with a message on faith and ambition.

In the post, Yunge warned his followers against permitting external circumstances to constrain their personal growth or define their output.

“Do not accept to be a caged bird, and if you find yourself caged, sing until the heavens ascend to your vindication tune,” Yunge wrote.

He urged his audience to pursue tangible achievements rather than drifting quietly through life. “Don’t just pass like a shadow,” he added, before concluding with a direct spiritual charge: “Be encouraged today, never give up, keep going and fix your eyes to the KING OF KINGS, JESUS CHRIST, not mere mortals.”

The choice of accompanying media—focusing on Kamkwamba, who gained global prominence after constructing a functioning electricity-generating windmill out of scrap materials to assist his village—serves as an explicit anchor for Yunge's emphasis on overcoming limited opportunities and financial hardship. Kamkwamba’s achievement later formed the basis of the book and film *The Boy Who Harnessed the Wind*.

The motivational broadcast marks a continuation of Yunge’s deliberate social media presence following his November 6 white wedding ceremony in Mwanza, Tanzania. That nuptial followed a traditional Kalenjin engagement ceremony held in August, cementing a cross-border union that immediately placed the Tanzanian businessman under intense regional spotlight.

Since the ceremony, both Yunge and Charlene have shared selective updates regarding their married life. Charlene has publicly expressed her satisfaction with the union, while Yunge has previously described his wife as a blessing alongside earlier posts offering prayers for individuals undergoing personal hardships.

By relying on faith-based declarations and stories of self-made resilience, Yunge appears intent on maintaining a distinct professional and inspirational brand, balancing his private entry into Kenya’s first family with an ongoing public emphasis on personal drive and religious devotion.
