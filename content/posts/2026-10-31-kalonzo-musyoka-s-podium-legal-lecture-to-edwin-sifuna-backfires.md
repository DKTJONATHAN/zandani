Kalonzo Musyoka took the microphone at the launch of the Linda Mwananchi Movement with a lawyer’s urge to set the record straight. Instead of riding the rally enthusiasm, the Wiper Patriotic Front leader paused to give Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna a technical lesson on political protocol—a move that immediately turned the crowd against him.

The friction began when Democratic Action Party of Kenya (DAP-K) leader Eugene Wamalwa spoke ahead of Kalonzo and referred to Sifuna as a presidential candidate. When Kalonzo stepped up to address the gathering, which brought together key opposition political figures, he chose to address Wamalwa's introduction directly, treating Sifuna to a legal reality check from the podium.

“Eugene has declared you a presidential candidate. But let me tell you legally because you are a lawyer. You have to wait for the 13th of November. And I hope you will invite us again. And from then is where we will talk with a fellow presidential candidate. For now he is still a party leader,” Kalonzo told Sifuna.

That unsolicited technical correction instantly shifted the temperature of the event. Rather than taking the advice quietly, sections of the audience responded with sustained boos and vocal protests. As the Wiper leader attempted to regain control of his speech, attendees began chanting Sifuna’s name over his remarks.

Visibly frustrated by the growing noise and lost momentum, Kalonzo stopped his address entirely and put a direct ultimatum to the crowd.

“Do you want me to speak? Or should I leave?” he asked.

With the crowd refusing to settle, Sifuna was forced to step in as an unexpected referee. The Nairobi Senator moved to calm his supporters, urging the crowd to allow the former Vice President to complete his remarks.

The intervention did not immediately restore the stage dynamic. Kalonzo exited the podium short of his planned sequence, leaving the remaining platform management to Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP) leader Rigathi Gachagua. Kalonzo was only ushered back to the stage after Gachagua concluded his address to wrap up the opposition appearance.
