According to the Kenya Meteorological Service Authority (KMSA), urban transport networks and emergency services across Nairobi, Kiambu, and Nakuru face a compound operational challenge this November as above-average rainfall threatens fragile drainage systems alongside sustained unseasonal heat.

The monthly forecast, issued following an assessment of regional atmospheric systems on Friday, October 30, outlines a dual-hazard scenario for the central highlands and urban hubs: daytime temperatures will peak between 20°C and 30°C, even as the ongoing October-to-December short rains deliver heavier-than-normal precipitation.

### The ITCZ Mechanism Behind the Anomaly

Meteorologists attributed the combination of high temperatures and intense downpours to the southward passage of the Intertropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ) across the East African region. The movement is feeding substantial cloud cover and ambient humidity into the lower atmosphere while trapping elevated surface heat.

![Motorists using a flooded highway during cold and rainy weather in Nairobi](https://i.ibb.co/5hL5Vx96/6147359a2edf.webp)

“The coolest conditions are generally expected over the highlands, where mean temperatures range between 15°C and 20°C, while the arid and semi-arid lowlands are likely to experience warmer conditions, with mean temperatures exceeding 28°C,” the meteorological authority stated in its advisory.

The agency cautioned that the resulting atmospheric profile will generate volatile microclimates across the central rift and central highlands. While daytime conditions will remain warm and muggy, nighttime minimums are projected to drop as low as 4°C in specific high-altitude zones and reach up to 20°C elsewhere, creating wide diurnal shifts.

### Infrastructure Exposure in Metropolitan Hubs

The central hazard highlighted by forecasters rests on urban surface infrastructure. In Nairobi and municipal centers across Kiambu and Nakuru, existing drainage channels frequently clog during heavy downpours, transforming trunk roads into bottlenecks.

KMSA explicitly warned that enhanced rainfall over the coming weeks risks triggering flash floods, riverine overflows, and slope failures in fragile topographies. For commuters in the capital, saturated ground conditions and overwhelmed culverts are expected to cause sudden transit disruptions and road gridlock during evening peak hours.

![Kenyans crossing a road in Nairobi County during a rainy season in March 2023.](https://i.ibb.co/j9pS8J9W/2d886592f0d9.webp)

Counties adjoining Nairobi—including Murang'a, Nyeri, Kirinyaga, Nyandarua, Laikipia, Meru, Embu, and Tharaka-Nithi—share identical rainfall and temperature projections, leaving agricultural belts and road corridors similarly exposed to runoff damage.

### High Heat Extends to Arid and Coastal Belts

Beyond the central urban belt, higher temperature extremes are projected for low-lying and coastal counties. Kajiado, Machakos, Kitui, Makueni, Taita Taveta, and inland sections of Tana River County are expected to record daytime maximums reaching up to 35°C.

Along the Indian Ocean littoral, Mombasa, Kilifi, and Lamu will operate under narrower temperature bands, with night minimums holding between 20°C and 25°C alongside midday spikes that match the dry interior.

With both soil saturation and daytime thermal stress elevated through the month, county disaster units and municipal engineers face the immediate task of clearing stormwater corridors before high-volume precipitation peaks over the next four weeks.
