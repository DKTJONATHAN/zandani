The Social Health Authority (SHA) has moved to untangle the heaviest portion of the defunct National Hospital Insurance Fund’s (NHIF) Ksh33 billion legacy debt, subjecting 451 healthcare facilities to on-site evidentiary inspections before releasing claims of Ksh10 million and above.

While the national health scheme has settled accounts for approximately 91 percent of previously contracted providers by clearing claims capped at or below the Ksh10 million mark, the capital-intensive balance remains frozen behind physical verification. 

In a circular issued Sunday, SHA announced that field surveillance and forensic vetting of high-value pending claims will resume on Monday, October 26, zeroing in on Level 2 dispensaries, Level 3 health centres, and Level 4 primary hospitals.

### Evidentiary Thresholds for Outstanding Invoices

![SHA, NHIF collage](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/nhif%20sha%20collage.jpg.webp?itok=82Nv87AQ)

The targeted vetting exercise focuses strictly on high-value arrears inherited when the NHIF was formally dissolved on October 1, 2024, to make way for the Taifa Care framework. 

Unlike smaller claims settled through digital clearing, providers owed eight-figure sums must furnish physical treatment proof before receiving disbursement approvals. 

“The Social Health Authority informs Level 2, Level 3 and Level 4 healthcare facilities that the surveillance and verification exercise for pending claims of KSh 10 million and above relating to the defunct National Health Insurance Fund will resume on 26 October 2026,” the authority stated.

Under the audit terms, affected hospital administrators are required to match individual billing records against physical admission books, pharmacy invoices, and diagnostic paper trails. SHA has directed administrators to consult an inspection timetable uploaded to its portal to align with incoming surveillance teams.

### Operational Strain on Primary Providers

The prolonged settlement pipeline has tightened liquidity for mid-tier and peripheral facilities, many of which accrued large arrears providing inpatient, surgical, and emergency coverage under the former insurer's terms. 

![SHA CEO Mercy Mwangangi](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/mercy%20mwangangi.jpg.webp?itok=Oi5-7hip)

Because Level 2 through Level 4 facilities operate with smaller cash reserves than national referral centres, carrying unpaid NHIF invoices for up to two years has squeezed day-to-day operations, including staff payroll and drug replenishment.

The administrative backlog has been compounded by transitional hurdles across the new health architecture, where initial rollouts were slowed by patient record verification snags and digital integration hitches.

To plug systemic verification gaps, the Ministry of Health has integrated the Health Management Information System (HMIS), embedding biometric patient authentication and unified digital records across facilities to avoid duplicate billing.

### Verification Milestone Ahead

The ongoing verification exercise represents the final administrative hurdle before SHA can formally close the NHIF’s liabilities. Whether the state can clear the 451 pending portfolios depends on how quickly inspection teams validate paper archives against claims data published on the authority’s visitation registry.
