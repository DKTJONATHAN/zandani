Embakasi East Member of Parliament Babu Owino is leaning on strict statutory timelines to anchor his political vehicle, choosing official state registries over endless court battles to settle a heated branding clash.

Faced with a brewing legal fight at the Political Parties Disputes Tribunal over the rights to the word "Linda," Owino and the leadership of his movement have put out official documentation from the Office of the Registrar of Political Parties (ORPP). The move formally marks the death of "Linda Mwananchi" as a registered identity and cements the birth of "The Mwananchi Party."

The dispute stems from an aggressive challenge mounted by officials of the Liberty National Democratic Alliance (LINDA). The alliance insisted it held exclusive rights to the "Linda" moniker, symbols, and overall branding, having kicked off its own registration process back in November 2024. When LINDA took the matter to the tribunal, filing an urgent petition to freeze the reservation of the name, Owino's team chose a clean regulatory path out of the conflict rather than getting bogged down in procedural litigation.

According to an official statement released by the party on Thursday, September 24, 2026, the leadership had already moved to insulate the outfit by writing to the ORPP on August 4, seeking formal guidelines on how to transition the brand cleanly.

The regulatory paper trail shows that the ORPP published a Gazette Notice on August 28, inviting members of the public to submit objections to the proposed name change. That notice triggered a quick challenge on August 30, when a third-party petition was lodged objecting to the transition from the Muungano Party to The Mwananchi Party.

Rather than letting the petition stall the entire vehicle, party officials resolved the underlying grievances directly with the petitioner, leading to the formal withdrawal of the objection on September 8.

With no remaining hurdles on the ORPP desk, the Registrar issued a final determination citing Section 20(5) of the Political Parties Act, Cap. 7D. Official correspondence confirmed that the transition to The Mwananchi Party formally took effect on September 10, 2026.

By releasing the explicit dates and state gazette citations, Owino is attempting to draw a firm line under the ownership debate, insisting that political legitimacy rests on what is stamped in the official gazette rather than public claims by rival outfits.

With the name shift legally locked in under Cap. 7D, The Mwananchi Party enters the political arena with a fully cleared statutory slate, leaving the contested "Linda" identity behind in the tribunal files.
