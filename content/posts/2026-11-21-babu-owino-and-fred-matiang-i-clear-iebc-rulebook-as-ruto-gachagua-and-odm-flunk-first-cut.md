Embakasi East MP Babu Owino has pulled off an unexpected administrative flex, steering his political vehicle past the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission's strict nomination audit while Kenya’s most entrenched political machines stumbled.

When the electoral agency released its report card on Friday, November 20, 2026, the outspoken legislator found himself in an exclusive circle. Out of 105 political formations that submitted their certified nomination rules ahead of the November 6 deadline, only 11 met all legal standards. Babu Owino’s party and the Jubilee Party—now under the leadership of former Interior Cabinet Secretary Fred Matiang’i—were among the few outfits to secure unconditional passage.

In contrast, the commission sent 94 parties back to the drawing board, serving notice to President William Ruto’s United Democratic Alliance (UDA), its broad-based government ally the Orange Democratic Movement (ODM), and former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua’s newly minted Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP).

### The Heavyweights Sent Back to Class

The IEBC’s audit created an awkward reality check for political titans accustomed to setting the national agenda. Despite their vast legal secretariats and resource machinery, UDA and ODM were lumped into the non-compliant basket alongside Gachagua’s outfit and retired Chief Justice David Maraga’s United Green Movement.

The list of outfits failing the statutory threshold reads like a roll call of established political brands. Gideon Moi’s KANU, Moses Kuria’s Chama Cha Kazi, Alfred Mutua’s Maendeleo Chap Chap, Ekuru Aukot’s Third Way Alliance, Mwangi Kiunjuri’s Tujibebe Wakenya Party, and the Equitable Party were all flagged for structural defects in their internal nomination frameworks.

While Gachagua and the broad-based government leaders battle for supremacy on the ground, their administrative secretariats tripped over basic compliance benchmarks set out for the August 10, 2027 General Election.

### The Eleven That Made the Cut

Beside Babu Owino’s outfit and Matiang’i’s Jubilee, the green light went to a disparate mix of legacy names and smaller outfits that managed to satisfy the commission’s statutory review on the first attempt.

Wiper Patriotic Front secured its clearance alongside the Safina Party, Liberal Democratic Party, Kenya Moja Movement Party, Grand Dream Development Party, Democratic National Alliance Party, Vision for Development Alliance, Forward Kenya Party, and Mwananchi Party.

For Matiang’i, securing approval for Jubilee establishes an early operational foothold as he maneuvers his political comeback. For Babu Owino, who has spent months positioning his political brand independently, beating seasoned party secretariats at rulebook compliance offers tangible bragging rights heading into early campaign positioning.

### Where the Others Fumbled

The commission’s audit was far from a routine rubber-stamping exercise. Electoral officials placed certified internal party manuals under microscopic review, zeroing in on how candidates will be vetted, chosen, and contested.

Key areas scrutinized included the composition and statutory mandate of internal party election boards, clear eligibility criteria for aspirants, candidate nomination procedures, and the formulas used to compile party lists. The IEBC also evaluated internal dispute resolution mechanisms, adherence to the Electoral Code of Conduct, and safeguards designed to curb electoral malpractices.

IEBC Chairperson Erastus Ethekon underscored that frameworks must demonstrate meaningful, measurable inclusion. The commission specifically took issue with gaps regarding representation guarantees for women, youth, persons with disabilities, and marginalized groups, demanding that parties eliminate ambiguous language that allows leadership cabals to manipulate internal polls.

### Reprieve Before the Resubmission Wire

Failing the opening round does not lock UDA, ODM, or Gachagua’s DCP out of the 2027 ballot just yet. The electoral agency confirmed that failing the initial audit does not carry an automatic ban, as statutory regulations grant parties a cure period to correct internal deficiencies.

Ethekon confirmed that the specific shortcomings have been formally dispatched to each of the 94 affected secretariats. The non-compliant outfits now face a ticking clock to revise their clauses, reconvene their internal organs, and resubmit their frameworks before the window closes.
