Kamene Goro has drawn a clear geographical line across her marriage, confirming that she and DJ Bonez run their household across a 500-kilometer divide between Nairobi and Mombasa without compromising their union.

Taking an on-air phone call from Milele FM presenter Ankali Ray, the veteran radio host dismantled circulating claims of a domestic breakdown by laying out the logistical reality of their working lives. Rather than managing a shared daily address, the pair operate out of separate urban bases dictated by their respective employment.

"Hatuna ubaya, there is no problem between Bonez and me, but we are not staying together," Kamene explained during the broadcast. "Bonez is in Mombasa, and I am in Nairobi. Everyone has their work. We don’t hate each other. Yeah, we respect each other."

### The Geography of a Working Union

The admission pulls back the curtain on how media personalities navigate commercial obligations that do not always align under one roof. While DJ Bonez remains anchored at the Coast managing deejaying residencies and event circuits, Kamene’s commercial and broadcasting interests remain tightly tethered to the capital.

In Kenya's digital entertainment space, physical separation between high-profile partners is routinely interpreted as code for separation. Kamene noted that the public expectation of unbroken joint domesticity ignores the routine compromises required by demanding careers.

For the couple, maintaining distinct addresses is simply an operational accommodation that allows both individuals to pursue professional contracts without demanding unilateral relocation.

### Digital Breadcrumbs and Public Conclusions

The scrutiny that prompted the radio exchange had simmered for days across social media platforms. It began when Kamene published an early-morning update referencing waking up by herself, set against a soundtrack by Bien.

Commentators rapidly translated the solitary morning routine and the contemplative music into evidence that the marriage had quietly dissolved. In an environment where personal relationship timelines are aggressively tracked through cryptic captions, the brief social post was treated as a declaration of singlehood.

Kamene used the radio platform to address the reach of that online assumption directly, stressing that personal contentment in an empty room does not translate to an open relationship status.

"People like to misinterpret things," she said. "I put Bien’s song because I love that song, and just because I woke up alone is not that I am complaining, and it also does not mean that I am single; people need to get that right."

She rejected the presumption that any unattached moment signals an active return to dating: "I have said I’m not single; that is it… everyone now thinks that I am available. I am not available at all. Just because I woke up alone does not mean I am single. I’m just saying how I woke up feeling great on my own."

### An Enduring Six-Year Anchor

The cross-county arrangement is underpinned by an established history that predates their formal vows. DJ Bonez has previously gone on record to dismiss third-party speculation about their stability, noting the longevity that anchors their relationship behind the scenes.

"I am married, and I love my wife so much; my marriage is six years," Bonez noted in an earlier public intervention, pushing back against the recurring chatter that follows their private choices.

For Kamene, the core issue remains the public's reluctance to accept that a marriage can thrive outside standard domestic blueprints. By confirming the Nairobi-Mombasa commuter dynamic, she has turned what was framed as a clandestine split into a straightforward professional decision, leaving her followers with a clear directive to stop reading crisis into ordinary logistical independence.
