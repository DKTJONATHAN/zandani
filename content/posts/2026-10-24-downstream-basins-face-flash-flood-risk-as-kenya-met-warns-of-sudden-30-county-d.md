Dry riverbeds and low-lying drainage basins face sudden flash surges over the coming week, as an abrupt shift from unseasonably dry heat sends intense downpours across parched soil in more than 30 counties.

The alert, issued Friday by the Kenya Meteorological Service Authority (KMSA), warns that floodwaters will not be confined to areas under direct cloudbursts. With parched ground unable to absorb high-volume deluges quickly, runoff will travel into low-lying and downstream communities, creating severe flash hazards where skies may still appear clear.

According to KMSA's seven-day advisory running through October 30, moderate to heavy rainfall exceeding 20 millimetres will strike across the Coast, Northeastern, Central Highlands, Southeastern Lowlands, and Highlands both east and west of the Rift Valley, alongside Nairobi.

### Gale Gusts and Urban Choke Points

![Motorists using a flooded highway during cold and rainy weather in Nairobi](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/rains_1.jpg.webp?itok=gejNPgyy)

The storm systems will carry localized surface disruptions beyond standing water. Kenya Met projects wind gusts exceeding 46 kilometres per hour across coastal, eastern, and northern sectors. The combination of intense wind and sudden downpours increases risks of fallen branches, damaged roofs, and compromised visibility along primary transport corridors.

In urban centres like Nairobi, Kiambu, and Nakuru, the forecasted rainfall volumes are set to test storm drainage channels clogged by weeks of dry-season debris. The meteorological agency advised residents to steer clear of perimeter grilled windows and avoid taking shelter under trees during severe thunderstorm activity.

The warning spans a vast geographical arc, encompassing maritime and coastal zones such as Mombasa, Kwale, Kilifi, Lamu, and Tana River; pastoral northern counties including Garissa, Wajir, Mandera, Marsabit, and Isiolo; and agricultural belts across Trans Nzoia, Uasin Gishu, Nandi, Kericho, Bomet, Kakamega, Bungoma, and Kisii.

South-Rift and eastern administrative zones—including Narok, Kajiado, Machakos, Makueni, Kitui, Meru, Tharaka-Nithi, and Embu—are likewise within the primary precipitation path.

### Maritime Swells and Seasonal Disruption

Along the Indian Ocean coastline, atmospheric instability will translate into hazardous marine conditions. The weatherman projected ocean wave heights climbing past two metres by Sunday, October 25, prompting an explicit advisory for artisanal fishers, commercial mariners, and passenger boat operators to restrict offshore operations until swells moderate.

The aggressive seven-day forecast follows weeks of anomalous weather patterns across East Africa. The October-November-December (OND) rains—initially projected to settle in by the second or third week of the month with enhanced intensity—suffered prolonged regional delays. Instead of steady seasonal moisture, extensive swaths of the country experienced persistent heatwaves and severe moisture deficits.

This rapid snap from protracted dry conditions to violent, concentrated bursts of more than 20 millimetres sharpens the danger for pastoral and farming communities. With riverbanks brittle from extended dry spells, water authorities and local emergency response units in downstream valleys face their first critical containment test of the season through October 30.
