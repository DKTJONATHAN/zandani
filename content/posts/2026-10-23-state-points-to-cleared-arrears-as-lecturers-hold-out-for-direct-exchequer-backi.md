The Ministry of Education is leaning on settled historical payrolls to coax public university staff back into lecture halls, but academic unions remain entrenched over how their next multi-year contract will be funded.

Speaking on Friday, October 23, during the 10th graduation ceremony at Thika Technical Training Institute in Kiambu County, Education Cabinet Secretary Julius Ogamba declared that the government has wiped out all outstanding liabilities tied to the 2017–2021 and 2021–2025 collective bargaining agreements (CBAs).

With no legacy arrears remaining on state books from prior cycles, Ogamba argued that academic staff have no practical justification to keep campuses shut while talks on future terms progress.

![Education Cabinet Secretary Julius Ogamba speaking at the 10th Graduation Ceremony of the Thika Technical Training Institute in Kiambu County on Friday, October 23, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/julius-ogamba.jpg.webp?h=f4287aec&itok=ZmHN-a4u)

"We are committed to the timely negotiation and conclusion of the current CBA," Ogamba said. "This is why I reiterate my invitation to our university staff unions and individual lecturers to return to work and allow room for negotiations to resolve the pending issues."

Yet the standoff that shuttered public universities on October 2 is less about settling past balances than securing a structural shift in how future pay packages are financed.

At the bargaining table, the Universities Academic Staff Union (UASU) and the Kenya Universities Staff Union (KUSU) have drawn a firm redline: the 2025–2029 agreement must be legally ring-fenced by direct National Exchequer disbursements, rather than funded through the internally generated revenues and tuition fees of individual universities.

Years of delayed settlements across public institutions have made staff wary of accords backed solely by council budgets. The nationwide walkout was triggered after negotiations collapsed between the staff unions and the Inter-Public Universities Councils Consultative Forum, which failed to offer the structural guarantees staff demanded.

![Lecturers singing solidarity songs after holding a press conference in October, 2025.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/lecturers-1.jpg.webp?itok=WFWHOHvL)

Beyond the funding architecture, the economic gulf between the two sides remains wide. Lecturers rejected the state's opening compensation adjustment, with UASU seeking basic salary increases ranging between 36 and 68 per cent across different job grades.

The union's 2025–2029 demands also encompass enhanced medical coverage, car loans, mortgage benefits, restructured pensions, and accelerated hiring to relieve severe teaching deficits across understaffed faculties.

While Ogamba highlighted progress in other segments of tertiary education—pointing to the recruitment of additional trainers to balance teaching loads in technical and vocational institutions—public university campuses enter a fourth week of paralysis.

With the state offering goodwill talks based on cleared past accounts and the unions insisting on written Treasury guarantees before resuming duties, the stalemate now hinges on whether the exchequer will formally underwrite the next phase of university payrolls.
