According to an appointment directive issued by Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention Director General Dr Jean Kaseya, African Union member states have secured a dedicated continental coordinator to ensure external donor funding aligns directly with national health priorities rather than partner-driven vaccine agendas.

Dr Kaseya confirmed the selection of Kenya’s Principal Secretary for Public Health and Professional Standards, Mary Muthoni, to chair Task Force 4 under the newly instituted Continental Immunisation Forum. The assignment, formalised in an October 15 appointment letter delivered at Afya House in Nairobi on Friday, positions the Kenyan administrator at the helm of continental efforts to accelerate vaccine rollouts under the Continental Immunisation Strategy 2026–2030.

“We would be honoured by your chairing in Task Force 4 during this inaugural Continental Immunisation Forum,” Dr Kaseya stated, noting that the portfolio places Muthoni at the centre of intergovernmental coordination and cross-border delivery logistics.

### Protecting Sovereign Priorities in Vaccine Delivery

The creation of Task Force 4 responds to long-standing operational disconnects across Africa's public health sector, where domestic immunization budgets frequently clash with fragmented donor requirements. Under the terms of the Africa CDC mandate, Muthoni’s team must coordinate directly with both national ministries and international development financiers to guarantee that immunization investments reflect each country's specific disease burden.

The task force charter explicitly protects national ownership, binding the leadership to existing African Union governance frameworks while extracting practical field data from member states to resolve vaccine transport and cold-chain bottlenecks.

For frontline health systems, the structural mechanism provides direct leverage: AU capitals will funnel operational implementation challenges to Muthoni’s desk, establishing an institutional channel to demand partner accountability on commitments pledged under the five-year continental strategy.

### Expanding Regional Health Portfolio

The Africa CDC post broadens Muthoni’s institutional footprint across pan-African public health administration. It builds on her election on May 5, 2026, as Chairperson of the African Permanent and Principal Secretaries for Health, effectively consolidating administrative oversight over civil service health leadership with technical vaccine delivery coordination.

Her first official assignment under the new portfolio will take place at the inaugural Continental Immunisation Forum meeting in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, running from November 23 to 26, 2026.

That forum, convened on the sidelines of the International Conference on Public Health in Africa, will require Muthoni to table the operational framework reconciling national vaccine procurement timelines with partner-financed distribution commitments before AU health ministers.
