Police cells in Siaya hold twelve young men this Wednesday morning after detectives rounded them up for pelting stones at First Lady Rachel Ruto’s motorcade on Tuesday afternoon, September 8, 2026, right outside the Kenya Medical Training College grounds in Siaya town.

* Directorate of Criminal Investigations officers detained 12 suspects on Tuesday evening following violent confrontations at KMTC Siaya.
* The First Lady had concluded a women’s economic empowerment forum at the venue before her convoy headed toward the helicopter pad.
* Youths attempted to breach the security barrier at the main gate and hurled stones at escort vehicles around 4:30 pm EAT.
* Security teams deployed tear gas along the Siaya-Nyadorera road to clear the path, ensuring the VIP delegation departed safely.

The trouble started just as the official motorcade prepared to leave the Kenya Medical Training College venue for a waiting helicopter. According to the Directorate of Criminal Investigations, a crowd of youths gathered near the exit gate, shouting and blocking the tarmac before trying to push through a cordon formed by local administration police and officers attached to the VIP protection unit.

When security officers held the line, rocks flew toward the convoy. Escort drivers accelerated through the melee while anti-riot squads fired tear gas canisters into the crowd to carve out an exit path toward the chopper landing zone. Field officers combed nearby estates, including Ahindi Gardens and the market periphery, rounding up twelve suspects within two hours of the confrontation.

The suspects were booked at Siaya Police Station, where county detectives spent Tuesday night interrogating them over charges of unlawful assembly, obstruction, and incitement. Investigators are reviewing local intelligence to track specific political fixers suspected of mobilizing the crowd with cash handouts outside the college gate.

Mrs. Ruto had spent the morning in the county distributing seed capital and poultry farming kits to local women groups through the Joyful Women Organization. Her entourage left the lakeside county without further incident, landing safely in Kisumu shortly after 5:45 pm EAT before returning to Nairobi.

### The take

Siaya is familiar with political friction, but throwing rocks at a convoy carrying poultry cages and table-banking cheques is bad arithmetic for local organizers. The immediate beneficiaries here are security hawks in Nairobi. Every thrown stone gives the state apparatus a tidy justification to flood opposition backyards with anti-riot units and surveillance teams.

For the youth caught in the sweep, the payout is always bitter. The local mobilizers who handed out five hundred shillings for noise will disappear into Kisumu hotel lounges, leaving twelve families to scramble for cash bail in front of a strict magistrate.

If you are stuck in evening traffic on Waiyaki Way looking at this on your phone, do not misread it as simple regional hostility. It is raw economic anxiety weaponized by middlemen. When the dust settles on the tarmac in Siaya, the political principals fly out, and the foot soldiers stay behind in the cells.
