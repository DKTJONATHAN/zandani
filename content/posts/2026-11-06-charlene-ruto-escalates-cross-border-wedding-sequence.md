Charlene Ruto has revealed the final visual details of her wedding attire in Nairobi, bringing First Lady Rachel Ruto and her younger sister Stephanie into the frame to inspect a cathedral-length floral veil ahead of her upcoming white wedding to Tanzanian fiancé Isaya Yunge.

The gown fitting captures the transition of the wedding timeline from private Nairobi preparations to high-profile ceremonial events across the border. During the fitting, Rachel and Stephanie Ruto assisted in adjusting an elaborate white bridal gown constructed with three-dimensional floral embellishments across the bodice and skirt, paired with a sweeping cathedral veil carrying matching embroidery.

### The Nairobi Bridal Shower

The fitting followed a sequence of celebratory milestones anchored in Nairobi. On Tuesday, November 3, 2026, Charlene hosted a garden-themed bridal shower where she appeared in a white gown topped with a gold tiara, while guests, including her sister Stephanie, adhered to a pink dress code.

The gathering featured custom cakes displaying inscriptions such as “Miss Babe to Mrs Babe” and “Hey Mrs Yunge.” Reflecting on the event, Charlene acknowledged the support of her close circle, stating, “Grateful that the amazing women I have done life with over the years chose to celebrate me in my new season,” while emphasizing that faith and spiritual grounding would serve as the cornerstone of the marriage.

### Transition to Mwanza

The logistical center of gravity for the celebration shifted to Tanzania on Wednesday, November 4, 2026. Visuals shared by Charlene showed her arrival at Mwanza Airport, where her entourage was received with a formal protocol footprint that included a private aircraft, a procession of luxury vehicles, and performances by traditional drummers and dancers.

The upcoming white wedding in Tanzania forms the second major public phase of the couple's marital itinerary. On August 8, 2026, the two families convened at Intonna Heritage Farm in Kilgoris, Narok County, for a traditional Kalenjin Koito ceremony. That gathering brought together relatives, state officials, and dignitaries from both Kenya and Tanzania to formalize customary rites ahead of the final church and reception ceremonies.
