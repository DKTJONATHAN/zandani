In the middle of a high-risk delivery ward, gospel disc jockey DJ Mo found himself saddled with an unusual medical duty: cracking continuous jokes to keep his wife from drifting off to sleep.

His wife, gospel singer Linet Munyali, popularly known as Size 8, was in critical condition while giving birth to their second-born son, Junior. Doctors monitoring her failing vitals issued a blunt warning to the decks master—if she fell asleep, she might not wake up.

Size 8 revisited the harrowing ordeal during a candid sit-down alongside her husband and fellow gospel musician Daddy Owen, breaking down the physical and domestic toll wrought by three consecutive high-risk pregnancies.

“I remember it was traumatising,” Size 8 said. “In the labour ward, DJ Mo was told to keep me company and ensure I didn't sleep, because if I slept, I would die. He was keeping me company while completely panicked.”

For DJ Mo, managing his own terror while trying to animate a fading patient proved an exhausting, unnatural assignment.

“You are panicked, yet she is not supposed to sleep,” DJ Mo recounted. “And to keep her awake, you cannot sing to her. You have to crack jokes and do something dynamic so that she stays awake.”

### The 28-Week Tightrope

The delivery of their son Junior was not an isolated scare but the culmination of a pregnancy that had threatened the singer’s life for months. Size 8 revealed that medical staff had to nurse the pregnancy under fragile observation just to push it to the 28-week threshold.

During the gestation, her body began shutting down under extreme strain. She suffered paralysis along the entire left side of her body, lost the ability to speak, and experienced severe nerve damage.

When labour finally commenced, both mother and child had to be placed on magnesium infusions to manage acute complications. Junior was delivered prematurely, weighing a frail 1.7 kilogrammes, and was immediately rushed into specialized care to fight for survival.

“When I gave birth to Junior, I was put on magnesium, and even Junior was placed on it because he was on the verge of death,” Size 8 explained. “I gave birth to him preterm at 1.7 kilograms.”

### Underlying Blood Pressure Mechanics

The root of the singer's repeated delivery room emergencies lies in a chronic blood disorder that severely restricts how her body processes oxygen.

Size 8 explained that she has long managed a haemoglobin deficiency. When pregnancy elevated her circulatory demands, her heart was forced to pump at extreme pressure to compensate for inadequate oxygen distribution, triggering dangerous hypertensive episodes.

“My body for a very long time has had a problem with haemoglobin, which affects oxygen,” she said. “When the heart pumps with more pressure, the blood pressure spikes. Pregnancy was simply adding pressure to an issue that was already there. My body could not handle that load; it was rejecting the pregnancy. Every single time I carried a pregnancy, it was life-threatening.”

### A History of Delivery Room Trauma

The birth of Junior came on the heels of repeated reproductive trauma for the couple. Size 8’s first pregnancy ended in an emergency Caesarean section, following which her newborn baby was taken directly into the Intensive Care Unit (ICU).

A subsequent second pregnancy ended in a miscarriage, heightening the emotional stakes when she conceived Junior.

While public attention often centers on the glamorous milestones of celebrity parenthood, the singer acknowledged that enduring three critical reproductive medical emergencies had left deep psychological scars on their household.

“It has been a bad journey,” Size 8 admitted, noting the lingering toll on her spouse. “It deeply traumatised DJ Mo.”
