The Higher Education Loans Board (HELB) has issued a nationwide compliance directive ordering all employers to remit payroll loan deductions by the 15th of every month, stepping in to protect formally employed graduates whose individual credit standings are being damaged by unremitted workplace deductions.

In an official circular dated October 13, the state student lender instructed human resource and payroll desks across the public and private sectors to reconcile employee withholdings through its digital portal and clear all payments within the mid-month cutoff.

“Employer Reminder! HELB deductions for your employees are due for remittance by the 15th of every month,” the board said. “Ensure timely declaration and remittance to keep your employees’ loan accounts in good standing. Beat the deadline. Remit on time. Stay compliant!”

The directive aims to eliminate a growing administrative bottleneck where companies deduct loan repayments from workers' monthly salaries but fail to wire the funds to the agency, leaving employees with delinquent balances despite having paid at source.

### Statutory Fines and Legal Exposure

To enforce compliance, the board is invoking punitive statutory measures under the HELB Act, which exposes non-compliant firms to steep recurring financial liabilities. Employers who fail to declare and remit deductions face a mandatory penalty of Ksh3,000 per affected worker for each month of default. Crucially, that liability can be backdated to the specific date the staff member joined the organization.

Beyond the flat monthly charge, the Act levies an additional interest penalty of approximately five per cent on any outstanding deduction balance for each month the remittance remains in arrears.

Corporate executives who persist in ignoring the mid-month transfer window face direct judicial action. Under Section 17 of the HELB Act, failing to comply with employer obligations without reasonable cause exposes company officials to criminal prosecution, alongside civil recovery suits initiated by the board to seize unremitted payroll balances.

To facilitate compliance, the board requires payroll accountants to log into the HELB Employer Portal, verify each staff member's targeted deduction bracket, and submit verified documentation alongside bank settlements before the 15th.

### A Tightening Recovery Environment

The compliance crackdown comes as HELB struggles with sharp contractions in its operational revolving fund, which relies heavily on matured loan recoveries to finance new cohorts of tertiary students.

Official data from the Kenya National Bureau of Statistics (KNBS) 2026 Economic Survey indicates that HELB’s total loan recoveries fell by 21 per cent to Ksh4.1 billion over the past year. Cumulative defaults have expanded to Ksh40 billion, driven by more than 281,000 former university and college students who have failed to service matured debts, pushing the fund’s non-performing loan portfolio to an elevated 31.4 per cent.

Compounding this deficit is Ksh34 million in statutory deductions that employers have already withheld from salaried workers but have yet to submit to the agency. The board has also recorded a severe downturn in diaspora repayments, which shrank from an earlier peak of Ksh100 million down to Ksh20 million.

The board's aggressive recovery posture also unfolds against broader uncertainty surrounding state tertiary funding. While President William Ruto has proposed structural reforms aimed at transitioning admitted university and college students into full state funding through amendments to the HELB Act, Parliament is yet to enact the necessary statutory changes or resolve unresolved questions regarding the long-term financing mechanism of the state model.
