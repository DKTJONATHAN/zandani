Dr. Gideon Muriuki is sitting on a water financing ledger that completely rewrote its own ceiling long before the ink on a three-year pact had even time to dry.

When the Group Managing Director and CEO locked down a partnership with Water.org back in 2023, the baseline paper target looked straightforward enough: disburse upwards of Ksh 3 billion over three years to lift access to water and sanitation for roughly 110,000 people. By the time auditors and sector players gathered at Nairobi’s Radisson Blu for the Africa WaterCredits Forum 2026 on October 16, those conservative projections had been blown wide open.

Instead of the promised Ksh 3 billion, Co-op Bank’s books logged an actual disbursement of Ksh 4.39 billion by August 2026—crossing the finish line months ahead of schedule and exposing a massive, hungry commercial market for grassroots utility loans.

The engine behind the surplus is the bank’s specialized utility vehicle, the Co-op A Maji product. Rather than focusing solely on monolithic national dams, the lending mechanism slices straight down to the ground level, giving individuals, MSMEs, and local institutions direct access to credit for water tanks, solar-powered pumps, pipeline connections, exhausters, bowsers, and smart meters.

For Dr. Muriuki, the award handed over at the Nairobi forum—the Leadership in Water Infrastructure and Finance trophy—is less of a traditional plaque and more of a receipt for a commercial gamble that paid off in peri-urban and rural distribution networks. The bank has effectively slid into position as one of Kenya’s heavy-hitting institutional financiers for water service providers, packaging infrastructure loans that bridge the gap between municipal deficits and community taps.

As sector stakeholders dissected the mechanics of commercial water investment during the one-day gathering, the numbers showed that specialized micro-infrastructure—tanks, meters, and solar rigs—holds far more immediate liquidity and demand than traditional corporate utility financing usually accounts for.

The partnership timeline officially wraps up its initial cycle, leaving Co-op Bank sitting atop a verified portfolio of nearly 4.4 billion shillings deployed into a sector that traditionally relied entirely on donor funding and state exchequer allocations.
