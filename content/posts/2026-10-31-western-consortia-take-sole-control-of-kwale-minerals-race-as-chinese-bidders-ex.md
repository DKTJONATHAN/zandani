Mining Cabinet Secretary Hassan Joho confirmed on Friday that Kenya will pick a developer for the Mrima Hill rare earth concession within three weeks, leaving Western and allied mining consortia as the exclusive beneficiaries of the Kwale County deposit after two Chinese contenders abruptly dropped their bids.

The unannounced withdrawal of Hunan Research Institute for Nonferrous Metals and Jinan Yuxiao Group Co. Ltd. leaves five corporate syndicates competing for the site, which sits roughly 65 kilometres south of Mombasa and holds deposits estimated in the tens of billions of dollars.

“Within the next two to three weeks we should be able to award Mrima Hills to the successful bidder,” Joho stated, noting that proposals officially closed on October 21.

### The Final Five Contenders

![Several trucks at a mining site in Kenya](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/several_trucks_at_a_mining_site_in_kenya.jpg.webp?itok=MwCvVoYm)

The exit of the Chinese state and commercial outfits consolidates the bidding field into three American-led groupings, one Australian partnership, and a Kenyan-British joint venture. 

Washington’s commercial footprint anchors three separate bids. Meridian Mineral Holdings has entered the race backed by engineering giants Fluor Corp and Tetra Tech. A second American group, Frontier Minerals LLC, features ReElement Technologies, while a third entity, Mrima Earth, operates under Critical Metals Corp. 

They face competition from an Australian joint venture pairing RareX with mineral sands miner Iluka Resources, alongside Gem of Dreams, a joint venture combining Kenyan and British capital.

While Joho declined to explain why Hunan Research Institute and Jinan Yuxiao stepped away from Mrima Hill, he confirmed that Chinese interests have not abandoned the wider market. At least one of the withdrawing firms has submitted an alternative bid for a different, unspecified Kenyan mining asset.

### Viability Hurdle Against a Fast-Track Clock

The three-week procurement sprint shifts heavy technical scrutiny onto the winning consortium. Mrima Hill is rich in niobium—used to strengthen industrial steel and manufacture aerospace alloys—and rare earth elements essential for electric vehicle batteries and electronics. 

![A collage of US President Donald Trump (left) and President Willian Ruto (right), with a hand holding rare-earth minerals in the middle.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/ruto-trump-minerals.jpg.webp?itok=8Bko0hmB)

However, the Ministry of Mining has yet to complete conclusive studies establishing the deposit's commercial extraction viability. Any developer selected by mid-November must finance and execute comprehensive feasibility drilling to verify that the mineral concentrations justify the capital costs of commercial refining.

That technical uncertainty has played out against intense international positioning. In June, President William Ruto announced that Nairobi was finalising a bilateral critical minerals framework with the United States, followed by American commitments to support domestic value addition and refining.

Joho maintained that the state is evaluating bids purely on commercial terms, dismissing suggestions of external pressure from foreign capitals.

“Kenya faces no pressure from either Washington or Beijing regarding the selection,” Joho said, insisting the final evaluation would follow statutory criteria and national economic interest.

The ministry is scheduled to conclude technical and financial evaluations before mid-November, when the tender committee will formally announce the concessionaire tasked with proving and monetising Kwale’s underground reserves.
