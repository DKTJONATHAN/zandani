Teacher training colleges face a strict administrative and financial timeline to clear thousands of referred and absent diploma candidates after the Kenya National Examinations Council linked upcoming supplementary assessments directly to rigid institutional compliance rules.

Under directives issued to college heads, candidate registration opens on November 9, 2026, and closes on December 15, 2026, setting up a five-week operational window for institutions to verify candidate records and collect required fees ahead of the assessment period scheduled from January 18 to February 12, 2027.

### Mandatory Payments and Eligible Categories

The assessment model enforces a uniform candidate fee structure processed exclusively through state digital channels. Each registering trainee must pay a base fee of Ksh7,200 via eCitizen to attempt outstanding papers. Candidates sitting referred practical assessments face an additional Ksh5,000 charge.

![Entrance to KNEC offices along Dennis Pritt Road in Nairobi](https://i.ibb.co/FqXZRN37/b6791a2e03a4.webp)

Eligibility covers two primary groups of diploma trainees:

* **Referred candidates:** Trainees who failed previous examinations under the Diploma in Primary Education (DPTE), Diploma in Secondary Teacher Education (DSTE), and Diploma in Early Childhood Teacher Education (DECTE).
* **Missed assessment candidates:** Trainees who failed to sit their Year One and Year Two College Based Assessments (CLBAs) in July 2026 under the DSTE and Diploma in Teacher Education for Pre-Primary and Primary (DTE PP&P) programs.

Candidates in both groups must complete both College Based and Summative Assessments according to individualized timetables released following registration.

### Institutional Consolidation and Administrative Cut-offs

To manage examination logistics, KNEC has restricted independent assessment status based on institutional enrolment figures. Colleges with fewer than five registered candidates for the session will not be allowed to operate as standalone assessment centres. Principals of affected institutions must coordinate with KNEC to cluster and register their trainees through approved colleges carrying higher candidate numbers.

![The entrance of the Kenya Technical Trainers College (KTTC) in Gigiri, Nairobi County.](https://i.ibb.co/FqJs1J45/aba5abe1de45.webp)

Beyond candidate registration, college principals retain direct accountability for regulatory verification. Institutions must compile and submit signed nominal rolls, proof of eCitizen payment, and formal medical reports for trainees living with disabilities to KNEC headquarters by December 30, 2026.

### Clearance for March 2027 Graduation

The examination council scheduled the early 2027 assessment window following a formal petition by the Kenya Teachers Colleges Principals Association. College heads sought the supplementary window to enable referred students to fulfill academic requirements in time for scheduled graduation ceremonies in March 2027.

Without the January assessment slot, referred trainees would have faced delayed entry into the professional teaching register and deferred deployment opportunities by the Teachers Service Commission.

The supplementary timetable comes as KNEC concludes its ongoing 2026 DSTE summative assessments, which began on October 26 across participating colleges using the council's online Teacher Education Assessment (TECBA) platform, with administration concluding on November 6.
