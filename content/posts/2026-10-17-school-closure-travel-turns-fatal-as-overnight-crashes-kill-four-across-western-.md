The end-of-term rush along western transit routes turned catastrophic on Friday night after a head-on collision between two public service vehicles in Homa Bay County claimed four lives and left 21 passengers injured, including students travelling home following school closures.

The crash, which occurred at Maembe Mbili in the Nyakwere area along the Katito-Kendu Bay road, involved two 18-seater matatus loaded with passengers heading in opposite directions. The force of the impact crushed both vehicle fronts, immediately trapping commuters inside the passenger compartments before local residents and police commenced rescue efforts.

First responders initially rushed the injured to Pap Onditi Sub-County Hospital in neighboring Kisumu County. However, the volume and severity of trauma cases forced facility personnel to stabilize and transfer victims onward to Russia Hospital—the Jaramogi Oginga Odinga Teaching and Referral Hospital in Kisumu—where at least one survivor remained in critical condition on Saturday morning.

Among those caught in the Homa Bay wreckage were grassroots civil society leaders. Awasi Bunge La Wananchi confirmed that two of its members sustained severe injuries during the crash.

![Wreckage of two PSV matatus after a head-on collision at the Maembe Mbili area in Nyakweree on the Katito-Kendu Bay road on Friday, October 16.](https://i.ibb.co/TqBHYjzr/b94d5cea97c5.webp)

“We wish to send our heartfelt message of quick recovery to our two members who were involved in a tragic road accident today at Maembe Mbili, Homa Bay County,” said Jimpery Omollo, the chapter’s president, verifying that the civic officials were receiving medical care alongside surviving student passengers.

### Multi-Vehicle Collisions on the Kisii-Keroka Ridge

While emergency wards in Kisumu managed the influx from the Katito-Kendu Bay road, another severe transit failure unfolded along the Kisii-Keroka road at Nyosia.

At that scene, an Easy Coach passenger bus and a 14-seater Simba matatu collided, causing the bus to overturn and come to rest on its side across the roadway, while the matatu ended up on the opposing shoulder with heavy structural damage across its entire cabin frame. Passersby and emergency workers broke into the wreckage to pull trapped travelers out of both vehicles, transferring multiple injured passengers to regional treatment centers.

![Images showing the aftermath of three accidents in Homa Bay, Kisii, and Kiambu counties on Friday, October 16, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/nMHKXXBp/b7af03472285.webp)

The vulnerability of that exact stretch deepened minutes later. Just 200 meters from the overturned Easy Coach, near Ronya Hospital, a secondary three-vehicle pile-up occurred when two Probox commercial vans and a motorcycle collided in the operational congestion. Several occupants and the rider suffered injuries and were admitted to nearby medical facilities.

### Rift Valley Freight and Passenger Corridors

The overnight travel distress extended back along the primary trunk corridor feeding Western Kenya. On the Nairobi-Nakuru Highway, a long-distance passenger bus en route to Siaya collided with a commercial lorry ferrying sand.

Physical inspections at the scene revealed catastrophic front-end damage to the sand truck, including an entirely crushed bumper assembly, while the Siaya-bound bus absorbed heavy damage along its passenger side, fracturing the windscreen and tearing through side paneling. Despite the severe structural compromise to both cabins, occupants aboard the bus escaped with non-fatal injuries, averting what could have been a catastrophic casualty toll on the highway.

With term breaks releasing thousands of learners into inter-county transport networks, regional referral facilities in Kisumu and Kisii continue to monitor dozens of hospitalized survivors as traffic authorities assess vehicle mechanical conditions and driving protocols on the affected corridors.
