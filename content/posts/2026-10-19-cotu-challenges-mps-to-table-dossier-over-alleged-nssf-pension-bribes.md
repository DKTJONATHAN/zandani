The Central Organisation of Trade Unions stated on Monday that sweeping parliamentary bribery accusations against the National Social Security Fund lack documented backing, intervening to shield millions of contributors and aging pensioners from destabilising political interference ahead of the 2027 General Election.

According to the umbrella labour federation, the National Assembly’s recent claims that Fund officers routinely demand kickbacks to process retirement dues have not been matched by a single documented petition, leaving the welfare of contributors exposed to unsubstantiated floor rhetoric rather than statutory accountability.

COTU Secretary General Francis Atwoli, whose union commands two permanent seats on the NSSF Board of Trustees, maintained that none of its 47 affiliated worker syndicates had received reports of delayed payouts or extortion from retirees.

"First, COTU (K) wishes to state that we have not received any report from our contributing members or our 47 affiliated trade unions complaining about denial of benefits alleged during the parliamentary proceedings," Atwoli said in an official statement.

![A side-by-side image of COTU SG Francis Atwoli and NA Majority Leader Kimani Ichung'wah](https://i.ibb.co/8nPqVHhf/1e2f1bb412b9.webp)

### The Floor Accusations

The dispute follows an October 14 parliamentary sitting where National Assembly Majority Leader Kimani Ichung'wah led legislators in attacking operational practices at the state pension manager. The Kikuyu lawmaker alleged that Fund staff members systematically solicit bribes from exiting civil servants before clearing their pensions, arguing that administrative rot originated directly from executive leadership.

"It is not ordinary staff members at NSSF who are demanding bribes, because fish always rots from the head," Ichung'wah told the House. "If you see staff at NSSF demanding bribes, then the management must relook at what they are doing at NSSF. And I think, listening to members of the committee, listening to members of this house, I want to ask the management at NSSF to shape up or ship out."

COTU, however, insisted that generalized assertions made under parliamentary privilege fail to serve affected senior citizens. The union maintained that if lawmakers possess credible records of public servants subjected to cash demands, they must submit contributor names, trace transaction records, and trigger formal investigative channels rather than casting collective aspersions across the pension agency.

![NSSF building](https://i.ibb.co/bMdMrY86/4b29c3c1aa72.webp)

"It is, therefore, unacceptable for politicians to make generalised accusations about claims of demand of cash from retirees by officers at NSSF without presenting evidence, identifying the affected contributors, or initiating the appropriate processes to ensure accountability," COTU stated.

### Guarding an Expanding Balance Sheet

Beyond dismissing the bribery claims, the trade union positioned the administrative health of the Fund as an asset that requires safeguarding from external disruption. According to COTU, structural adjustments have driven the NSSF investment portfolio from approximately Ksh312 billion to Ksh814 billion over a three-year span.

Union leadership argued that the Ksh502 billion asset expansion positions the agency to breach the Ksh1 trillion threshold, placing Kenya among the largest statutory social security pools across the continent. Atwoli cautioned that politicizing the institution's operations threatens to undermine worker confidence precisely as the fund scales up its asset management footprint.

COTU issued a direct caution to the NSSF executive management and the Board of Trustees, urging the agency’s leadership to resist institutional blackmail and executive intimidation as political factions begin recalibrating their influence ahead of the 2027 election cycle. Lawmakers who made the floor allegations have not yet signaled whether they will formalize their bribery complaints with investigative agencies or present specific pensioner petitions to the House.
