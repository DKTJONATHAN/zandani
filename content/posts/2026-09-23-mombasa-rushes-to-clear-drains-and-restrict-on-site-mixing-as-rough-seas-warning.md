Mombasa County is racing to clear blocked urban waterways and choke off construction site debris before a prolonged period of rough seas and heavy rainfall peaks across the coastline.

The municipal push coincides with a formal advisory issued by the Kenya Maritime Authority (KMA), warning operators and residents that adverse weather, high waves, and strong winds will persist through December. While the maritime agency has directed vessel owners to secure moored craft, mandate life jackets, and monitor weather updates from the Kenya Meteorological Department (KMD), local authorities are tackling the land-side drainage bottlenecks that compound coastal flooding.

![An image showing strong winds along the Kenyan coast, forcing palm trees to bend.](https://i.ibb.co/wZFvDhPf/e095bcdb3110.webp)

Parts of Mombasa—including Ziwalang’ombe, Inuka, Railway, and Ziwani—have already experienced disruptive flooding that cuts off access to neighborhoods. To counter further blockages, Mombasa Governor Abdullswamad Nassir mobilized 3,000 young people across the county's six sub-cities to clear obstructed drains, culverts, and water channels.

Alongside the manual clearing campaigns, the county government has enforced strict operational limits on private construction sites. Developers are now barred from mixing ballast, sand, and cement directly on open ground—a common practice that previously allowed loose aggregate to wash into municipal gutters during downpours, instantly sealing off drainage systems.

![Rescue Boats](https://i.ibb.co/rKfzs0F7/efba5ba3cab0.webp)

For mariners and fishers relying on the ocean, KMA has urged the delay of non-essential activities when visibility drops and winds escalate. Operators already on the water have been directed to seek immediate shelter and coordinate emergency responses through the Regional Maritime Rescue Coordination Centre via 0737719414 or 0721368313.

With both marine safety protocols and municipal drainage enforcement now running concurrently, the next test for the coastal corridor rests on whether the mobilized labor force and new construction restrictions can prevent urban waterlogging through the peak of the seasonal rains.
