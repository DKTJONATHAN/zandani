A sharp meteorological divide will govern Kenya’s transit and economic corridors through mid-next week, as convective thunderstorms target western counties while Nairobi and its surrounding metro hubs absorb prolonged daytime heat reaching 29°C.

The five-day outlook released by the Kenya Meteorological Service Authority (KMSA), covering October 18 to October 22, 2026, places the Lake Victoria basin, the western highlands, and sections of the Rift Valley under sustained daily alerts for afternoon and evening thunderstorms.

At the same time, the central plateau faces unseasonably dry warmth marked by steep day-to-night temperature swings, leaving the capital largely shielded from precipitation until late in the cycle.

### Thunderstorm Corridor Along the Lake Basin

![A billboard toppled onto a vehicle during a heavy storm in Kisumu, October 8, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/HfCDZzdr/4930b2df61c4.webp)

The most severe physical exposure identified by forecasters sits across western transport hubs and agricultural zones. KMSA predicts that Kisumu, Siaya, Homa Bay, Kisii, Nyamira, Vihiga, and Bungoma will experience recurring afternoon and evening downpours marked by active electrical storms.

The repeated onset of lightning and moderate downpours threatens lakeside logistical routes and outdoor commerce, areas that have historically suffered infrastructure and vehicle damage during sudden squalls.

"We urge communities and sector stakeholders in affected zones to make use of these timely updates to safeguard lives, protect livelihoods, and adapt effectively to shifting weather patterns," said Edward Muriuki, the Acting Director of the KMSA.

### Metropolitan Heat and Night Chills

![An undated image of Kencom bus stop along City Hall Way, Nairobi.](https://i.ibb.co/LhspRGb1/e92e1764fc79.webp)

In stark contrast to the wet western belt, Nairobi is projected to remain predominantly dry through Wednesday, with solar heating driving daytime temperatures up to 29°C on both Monday and Thursday. The capital's only anticipated precipitation is restricted to isolated light showers on Thursday night.

Across the wider metropolitan area, satellite counties will navigate significant thermal variances rather than disruptive downpours:

* **Kiambu:** Dominated by sunny intervals with daytime highs peaking at 26°C, though brief light showers are projected during Monday and Tuesday afternoons and nights.
* **Machakos:** Highs will climb to 29°C under clear morning skies, balanced by overnight drops to 13°C and scattered showers across Tuesday and Wednesday.
* **Kajiado:** Daytime temperatures will range between 27°C and 28°C, punctuated by isolated showers on Wednesday afternoon. Nighttime minimums will plunge to 10°C early in the week, marking an 18-degree diurnal swing.

Along the coast, maritime activity will contend with typical tropical humidity. Mombasa is forecast to reach daytime highs of 31°C and overnight lows of 24°C, with brief early morning marine showers clearing rapidly into sunny intervals before partly cloudy nights set in.

With heavy electrical storms concentrated west of the Rift Valley and elevated temperatures desiccating central urban corridors, the weather authority's advisory leaves regional emergency desks and transport operators facing two entirely different operational realities through October 22.
