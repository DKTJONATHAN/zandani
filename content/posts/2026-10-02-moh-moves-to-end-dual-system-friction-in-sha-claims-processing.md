Healthcare providers operating under the Social Health Authority (SHA) framework will no longer be required to manually copy patient records across two separate software platforms, as the Ministry of Health moves to streamline hospital administrative workflows nationwide.

Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale clarified on Friday, October 2, that the government is not scrapping SHA in favor of a new software platform. Instead, the ministry is integrating facilities' existing Health Management Information Systems (HMIS) directly into the national health insurance architecture, eliminating a redundant dual-entry process that has created operational friction since SHA was established.

![Health CS Aden Duale and KMPDU Secretary General Davji Atellah during a meeting on July 2, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/qLZ3N5Ty/b4c6b70ea205.webp)

Under the initial rollout, the Digital Health Agency (DHA) provided a temporary SHA Provider Portal to receive and transmit medical claims. However, because public and private healthcare facilities were already using their own internal HMIS to track patient visits, facility staff were forced to enter identical information into both systems. That duplicate workflow added significant administrative burden to health workers, slowed down patient consultations and admissions, and contributed to prolonged waiting times in facility reception areas.

By positioning existing hospital HMIS as the primary administrative engine, healthcare workers will manage admissions, medical consultations, billing, and claims submissions from a single interface. The internal data will then automatically sync with SHA and broader health-sector platforms.

According to the ministry, the technical integration relies on digital health infrastructure that has already been procured and paid for, rather than requiring fresh capital investment in new software. Duale emphasized that the policy shift addresses systemic fragmentation rather than changing the statutory authority managing universal healthcare coverage.

![SHA Building](https://i.ibb.co/rf27LZ2K/2a812b437f95.webp)

Beyond easing staff workloads, the direct linkage between hospital point-of-care software and national insurance databases is designed to tighten claim verification. By matching claims automatically to verified patient encounters and actual clinical services rendered, the system is structured to flag duplicate billing and clerical errors before claims reach the payout stage.

The ministry indicated that full deployment will require ongoing technical support for facilities and training for healthcare personnel. The immediate focus turns to rolling out technical APIs and system integrations across participating hospitals to phase out manual paper trail verification and measure improvements in facility processing times.
