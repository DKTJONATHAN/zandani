Interior Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen took the stage at Nandi Hills Stadium for a teachers’ empowerment function and quickly turned the podium into a defensive launching pad against his political detractors. Facing rising public friction over the government's aggressive multi-agency security operation in the North Rift, Murkomen made no apologies for the heightened police presence across Samburu and Isiolo counties, framing the mounting political outcry as a sign that his ministry's strategy is finally hitting the mark.

Addressing attendees at the Nandi East/Tinderet Kenya National Union of Teachers (KNUT) Branch Empowerment Ceremony, Murkomen addressed the escalating political debate surrounding the crackdown. He attributed the public pushback directly to the government’s uncompromising stance against armed networks operating in the region. According to Murkomen, political pressure would not deter the ministry from pursuing criminal elements responsible for repeated attacks on civilians, police officers, and livestock.

“The noise that is there is because we are tightening the nut,” Murkomen told the crowd at the Nandi County venue. He added a clear timeline to the administration's security campaign, vowing: “We will continue until we liberate those areas before the next election.”

The Cabinet Secretary insisted that ongoing multi-agency operations remain strictly intelligence-led, designed to disarm illegal gun holders across Samburu, Isiolo, and the broader Kerio Valley belt. To back his defense of the campaign, Murkomen pointed to concrete operational recoveries, revealing that security forces have already seized more than 3,000 illegal firearms and 8,000 rounds of ammunition from criminal networks in the Kerio Valley corridor.

Yet alongside his defiant tone toward political opponents, Murkomen offered a rare public concession regarding the execution of the crackdown. The CS expressed regret over recent operational incidents that led to the accidental destruction and loss of civilian livestock during security raids. Acknowledging that collateral damage threatens to erode public trust, Murkomen announced that he has issued direct orders to field officers and security commanders to exercise maximum restraint.

“We will continue with the operations to ensure that all people who have illegal firearms, and those targeting our officers, civilians, and livestock, are dealt with decisively,” he stated, while instructing ground commanders to refine their tactical execution to safeguard non-combatant lives and property.

The fiery security address came as a striking pivot during an event primarily organized to celebrate local educators. Before steering his remarks toward regional security, Murkomen praised the KNUT leadership for its sustained advocacy on teacher welfare and reiterated the government’s commitment to advancing comprehensive reforms across Kenya’s public education sector.

By linking the timeline of the North Rift disarming operation directly to the upcoming election cycle, Murkomen has tied the success of his docket to a visible reduction in banditry. With field commanders instructed to minimize civilian fallout and thousands of illicit weapons already out of circulation, the Interior CS has made it clear that the security apparatus will keep turning the screw across Samburu and Isiolo regardless of political friction.
