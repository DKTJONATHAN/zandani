Raila Odinga Junior has walked straight into Nairobi’s brewing political heavyweight clash, throwing his support behind Lang’ata MP Phelix Odiwuor, popularly known as Jalang’o, just twenty-four hours after his sister Winnie Odinga laid claim to the exact same senatorial seat.

Appearing on Friday, October 16, alongside Jalang’o and Nairobi Woman Representative Esther Passaris, Junior bypassed family courtesies to offer an explicit party endorsement to the comedian-turned-lawmaker under the Orange Democratic Movement (ODM).

“I would like to thank Jalas for offering himself for the senatorial position. I would like to assure you that we (ODM party) are behind you. We are supporting you,” Junior said during the gathering.

The move delivered an immediate jolt to Nairobi’s political grapevine. Only a day earlier, on Thursday, October 15, Winnie Odinga had seized the public podium during the first anniversary commemoration of their father, former Prime Minister Raila Odinga, to confirm that she was stepping into the capital’s senatorial ring.

Addressing mourners and supporters at the memorial, the East African Legislative Assembly (EALA) MP framed her decision around the impending departure of incumbent Senator Edwin Sifuna, who is widely expected to seek higher office.

“Let it not be a rumour that as Edwin Sifuna searches for higher offices, I see there is some space in Nairobi, and that is why I have decided I will vie for Senator of Nairobi County,” Winnie declared.

Instead of rallying behind that announcement, Junior used Friday’s appearance to make it clear that bloodlines would not translate into a free pass within Orange House. Beyond endorsing Jalang’o, he took direct aim at the culture of handed-down direct nominations, insisting that anyone seeking to carry the ODM banner into the 2027 General Election must earn it through competitive party balloting.

“Baba left us inside ODM, and that is where we are, and our brother belongs to ODM, and I am solidly behind these two,” Junior said, anchoring his position in his father’s institutional legacy while insisting that party processes take precedence over individual entitlement.

That hard stance on the ballot comes with skin in the game. Junior revealed that he is not sitting on the sidelines as an advisor or kingmaker, announcing his own intention to launch a parliamentary bid for Kibra constituency—a seat that carries decades of historical significance for his family’s political roots in the capital.

Rather than seeking a curated pathway to the Kibra ticket, Junior maintained that he is fully prepared to subject himself to the rigours of the ODM nomination machinery against any challenger who steps up.

For Jalang’o, who secured Lang’ata in 2022 and has navigated turbulent party dynamics ever since, Junior’s vocal backing provides significant capital in an ODM heartland. It places the vocal legislator firmly inside the party tent at a moment when senatorial hopefuls are actively jockeying for position ahead of Sifuna’s eventual vacancy.

With Winnie staking an early claim at her father’s memorial and Junior matching it on the campaign trail with Jalang’o, Nairobi’s Senate race is already testing internal party allegiances long before the first primary ballot is printed.
