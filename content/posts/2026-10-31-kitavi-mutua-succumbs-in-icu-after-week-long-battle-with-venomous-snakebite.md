A week-long medical battle to counteract systemic envenomation ended on Friday night when veteran *Daily Nation* correspondent Kitavi Mutua died in the Intensive Care Unit at Kenyatta University Teaching, Referral and Research Hospital (KUTRRH).

Mutua had been transferred to the national referral facility in Kiambu County after venom from a snakebite suffered at his home in Kitui South entered his bloodstream, progressively impairing vital organ function despite days of clinical intervention.

Preliminary reports show the veteran journalist was bitten while asleep in his bedroom. Although he sought medical assistance shortly after the incident, his condition deteriorated over seven days as systemic toxicity spread through his internal organs, ultimately forcing his admission into critical care.

### Decades Documenting Ukambani

![The late Nation Media Group correspondent Kitavi Mutua.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/mutavi.JPG.webp?itok=_AkW-iYm)

Mutua's death marks the close of an influential regional reporting career anchored in the lower Eastern belt. Based in Kitui for years as a dedicated correspondent for the Nation Media Group, he acted as a primary conduit between rural communities across Ukambani and the national news agenda.

His portfolio cut across local politics, public accountability, county infrastructure rollouts, and the everyday hardships confronting arid settlements in Kitui County. Colleagues regarded him as an institutional memory bank for the region's shifting political alliances, often helping younger correspondents navigate the complexities of local administration and rural community reporting.

### Mentorship in Regional Politics

Beyond newsroom circles, Mutua’s guidance intersected directly with the region's elective leadership. Kitui Woman Representative Irene Kasalu cited his personal counsel as central to her initial steps in public life a decade ago.

![The Nation Centre building in Nairobi's Kimathi Street where the Nation Media Group offices are located. Monday, October 21, 2019](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/The%20Nation%20Centre%20building%20in%20Nairobi%27s%20Kimathi%20Street%20where%20the%20Nation%20Media%20Group%20offices%20are%20located.%20Monday%2C%20October%2021%2C%202019.jpg.webp?itok=nPB8MaGZ)

Kasalu noted that Mutua spent substantial time assisting her in decoding public communication strategies and managing relationships between elected representatives and media platforms during her early political career.

“Beyond being a journalist, Kitavi was a mentor, a friend, and a professional who believed in helping others. I will forever be grateful for the role he played in shaping my understanding of communication and political engagement,” Kasalu said in a weekend tribute.

Kasalu characterized Mutua’s death as an acute loss to both his family and the wider public in Kitui, whose regional concerns he elevated into the national conversation for decades.

Arrangements to transport his body and organize burial plans in Kitui County are currently underway, as the media fraternity and county leadership mourn the abrupt loss of one of the region's most enduring field correspondents.
