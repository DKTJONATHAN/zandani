President William Ruto has ordered the mandatory inclusion of citizen representatives on public hospital boards, seeking to insulate patients from persistent prescription shortages caused by primary health centres diverting Social Health Authority (SHA) disbursements into cosmetic compound upgrades.

Speaking during a church service at ACK Kapsabet in Nandi County on Sunday, October 18, the Head of State revealed that facility managers have been intercepting capitation funds sent straight to grassroots health centres to bankroll perimeter projects rather than stocking vital medical supplies.

“When money is sent to a dispensary, the first responsibility is to buy medicines,” Ruto said. “Some people, when we send SHA money, buy gates or install cabro.”

### Borrowing the School Governance Framework

![President William Ruto during a church service at ACK Kapsabet Church on October 18, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/BKdJrqLG/b0434fe57e66.webp)

To curb the unvetted diversion of public money, the executive is seeking to replicate the statutory oversight structure applied in the education sector under the Basic Education Act. Under that framework, public school Boards of Management (BOMs) integrate parents, local community leaders, and institutional sponsors alongside education administrators to oversee institutional operational spending.

Ruto stated that primary healthcare units require an identical supervision matrix to prevent facility heads and health officials from exercising unchecked authority over newly decentralized medical revenues.

“Health centres must be supervised by doctors and members of the public, just as we supervise schools,” Ruto said. “Doctors, government officers, and county officers must also be presented.”

Under recent SHA financing guidelines, the national government disburses insurance reimbursements straight into facility accounts rather than routing lump sums through consolidated county health treasuries. While the policy was intended to eliminate bureaucratic delays and ensure dispensaries maintain continuous cash flow for basic patient care, facility boards have retained wide spending discretion that has sidelined pharmaceuticals.

![undated image of the entrance of Wamalwa Kijana Teaching and Referral Hospital](https://i.ibb.co/spy2kjJv/3a675c9bd6da.webp)

“Because we are now sending money directly to every hospital, the Boards of Management in every hospital must prioritise purchase of medicines and health commodities over infrastructural developments,” the President noted.

### Devolution and Facility Autonomy

The presidential push intersects with wider, contentious reforms governing municipal health finances across the country. Devolved units have been overhauling facility administration, evidenced by Nairobi County’s Health Services and Management Act, 2026, which conferred financial and administrative autonomy to Level 5 county hospitals while requiring management boards to balance executive discretion against public accountability mechanisms.

Until now, low-tier dispensaries and Level 2 and 3 health centres have operated with minimal independent public oversight, leaving procurement decisions exclusively to facility-in-charge officers and sub-county medical teams.

By commanding the entry of civic and local community representatives into dispensary boards, the national executive is attempting to place community monitors in the room where expenditure warrants are signed. For sick citizens seeking basic care, the practical test of this directive rests on whether dispensary accounts immediately redirect SHA revenues back to empty pharmacy shelves instead of ornamental facility perimeters.
