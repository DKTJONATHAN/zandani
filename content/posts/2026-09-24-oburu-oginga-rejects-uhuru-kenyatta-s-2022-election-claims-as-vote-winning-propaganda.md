The political alliance that defined Kenya’s post-2018 landscape has hit a raw nerve, with Orange Democratic Movement (ODM) leader Dr. Oburu Oginga publicly calling out former President Uhuru Kenyatta for "exhuming" the ghosts of the 2022 general election.

At the heart of the friction is a sudden public claim by Kenyatta asserting that the late former Prime Minister, Raila Odinga, was the actual winner of the highly contested 2022 presidential race. Rather than welcoming the validation, Oburu has drawn a firm boundary, accusing the former head of state of turning historical pain into contemporary political rhetoric.

In a pointed statement on Thursday, September 24, 2026, Oburu made it clear that the Odinga family and the ODM party are not interested in retrospective sympathy that serves modern political interests. 

"The 2022 election and the sacrifices of our departed Party Leader must not be reduced to material for contemporary vote-winning propaganda," Oburu stated, shifting the focus from Uhuru’s claims to the former president's own actions during the transition of power.

### The Three Questions Uhuru Must Answer

Instead of letting the former president control the narrative of the 2022 polls, Oburu demanded full transparency, laying out three direct questions that challenge the logic of Uhuru's late-stage revelations.

First, the ODM leader questioned the timing of the disclosure, wondering why a truth of this magnitude was kept under wraps for four years, only to be brought up now when the political landscape is shifting ahead of the next electoral cycle.

Second, and most critically, Oburu took aim at Uhuru's constitutional role during the 2022 transition. If the former president was genuinely convinced that Raila Odinga had secured the presidency, Oburu demands to know why his administration willingly facilitated the official transfer of the instruments of power to William Ruto.

Finally, Oburu put a pragmatic question to the retired president: what exactly does he expect Kenyans to do with this piece of history today, years after the administration was established?

### Remembering the Cost of the Handshake

For the Odinga family and the ODM fraternity, the legacy of the 2022 election is tied to significant political compromises. Oburu pointedly recalled the steep concessions his party made following the famous 2018 Handshake.

To accommodate the Jubilee coalition and keep the peace with Uhuru's administration, ODM walked away from major political battlegrounds, including sacrificing key elective positions such as the Nairobi gubernatorial seat. Seeing these sacrifices treated as casual political talking points has clearly upset the party's leadership.

By demanding that Uhuru "tell the country the whole story," Oburu has signaled that the Odinga family is no longer willing to play the quiet partner in political post-mortems. As the political class begins maneuvering for future alliances, the ODM leader’s message is clear: the memory and sacrifices of the late Raila Odinga are off-limits for anyone looking to score quick political points.
