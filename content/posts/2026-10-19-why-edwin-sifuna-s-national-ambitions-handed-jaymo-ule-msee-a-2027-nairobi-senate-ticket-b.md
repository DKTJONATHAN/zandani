When content creator Wilson Muirani Gathoni—better known as Jaymo Ule Msee—began plotting his transition to elective politics, he did not look to a blank policy slate. Instead, he watched the desk of Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna.

Publicly ing the genesis of his campaign on Monday, October 19, 2026, Jaymo revealed that his decision to enter the 2027 Nairobi senatorial contest took root in late 2025. The core motivation, according to the creator-turned-politician, was an apparent vacuum left by an incumbent he accused of chasing national political horizons rather than tending to county obligations.

“The journey for the Nairobi senatorial bid started late last year when it also became apparent that our senator was more focused with ‘national ambitions’ at the expense of our city senatorial obligations & responsibilities,” Jaymo stated online.

Insisting that the capital city demands undivided administrative attention, the creator framed his campaign as a direct antidote to what he branded as political moonlighting. “Our city requires leaders who are not double dealers but optimists who can commit fully until NAIROBI VILE INAFAA,” he added, capping his declaration with the sign-off, “The journey continues, Mashallah.”

The critique sets a distinct tone for Jaymo’s positioning in a race that has steadily transformed into a magnet for high-profile figures, media personalities, and political veterans. His entry into the fray gained further momentum following East African Legislative Assembly (EALA) MP Winnie Odinga's reported moves toward the same seat.

On Thursday, October 15, 2026, Jaymo dropped his formal campaign markers, rolling out a digital poster stamped in the yellow-and-green colors of the ruling United Democratic Alliance (UDA). The graphic, featuring a side-by-side portrait with President William Ruto alongside the slogan “Vote Jaymo Ule Msee for Senator Nairobi County 2027,” marked his official bid for the ruling party's nomination.

By pegging his campaign rationale directly to Sifuna's perceived distraction, Jaymo joins a growing and eclectic pack of aspirants queuing up to claim the Nairobi Senate seat. Aside from Winnie Odinga, the lineup features Lang’ata MP and fellow entertainment industry alumnus Jalang’o, who has declared his readiness for the ballot. 

Entrepreneur KRG The Don is pursuing the ticket under the National Economic Development Party linked to former Governor Mike Sonko, while former Makadara MP Reuben Ndolo is eyeing a political comeback. Lawyer Cecil Miller, United Opposition youth leader Ken Babu, and Nominated Senator Crystal Asige have also surfaced in ongoing discussions surrounding the crowded contest.

With political camps drawing battle lines and party primaries looming, Jaymo’s gambit hinges on convincing Nairobi voters that an outsider armed with a creator’s platform can out-focus a distracted incumbent.
