Kenya's Capital Markets Authority (CMA) has moved to curb the domestic distribution of share offering materials for the Dangote Petroleum Refinery, warning that the historic cross-border share sale has not received domestic statutory clearance.

The market regulator issued a public notice on Monday clarifying that the public offering is strictly regulated under Nigerian law and has not been submitted for review or licensing under Kenya’s capital markets framework.

The advisory comes amid an aggressive pan-African push for the listing, which has circulated widely on domestic digital channels and social platforms following marketing efforts positioning the transaction as a borderless retail opportunity.

### The Cross-Border Regulatory Gap

![Kenyan CNN journalist Larry Madowo, in an interactive session with Africa's dollar-billionaire Aliko Dangote on the Dangote Petroleum Refinery IPO on September 21, Nigeria.](https://i.ibb.co/B2pZxSCZ/b2c94a823b08.webp)

Under Kenyan securities law, any entity marketing, soliciting, or aggregating funds for equity share sales to domestic retail investors must secure formal authorization from the CMA or distribute through licensed domestic intermediaries.

“This public offer is regulated in Nigeria and has not been submitted for consideration and approval by CMA under the applicable Kenyan legal and regulatory framework,” the regulator noted, cautioning that unverified intermediaries expose buyers to financial loss and data harvesting.

The regulatory intervention highlights the growing friction between pan-African digital investment campaigns and territorial securities oversight. Without local registration, Kenyan buyers transacting through unauthorized platforms lack recourse through domestic statutory investor compensation funds or dispute resolution mechanisms.

### Scale of the Nigerian Listing

The Dangote Petroleum Refinery and Petrochemicals FZE launched its public sale on the Nigerian Exchange (NGX) on September 14, seeking to raise over Ksh 211 billion ($1.63 billion) through 4.1 billion new ordinary shares priced at Ksh 5 each.

The capital call values the mega-refinery at roughly Ksh 6.4 trillion, with a minimum subscription threshold set at 10 shares (Ksh 511) to encourage mass participation. The offering is scheduled to run through October 13, ahead of planned secondary market trading in late November.

![Africa's richest man, Aliko Dangote, at his Nigerian Oil Refinery on June 21, 2025.](https://i.ibb.co/0R2xggGY/cede3ab55f3f.webp)

Speaking on the public campaign, industrialist Aliko Dangote stated that the transaction was designed to bypass traditional institutional barriers via fintech channels, targeting 10 million individual shareholders across the continent under a "People's IPO" model.

### Investor Protection Directives

Despite the continental appeal, Kenyan regulators maintain that accessibility does not override national compliance mandates. 

The CMA has instructed the public to refrain from making payments, sharing banking credentials, or committing funds through unofficial conduits circulating documentation online.

Prospective market participants have been directed to cross-reference any intermediary against the official directory of authorized brokers, fund managers, and investment banks published on the regulator's portal (`licensees.cma.or.ke`) before entering foreign capital allocations.
