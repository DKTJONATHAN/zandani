Foreign embassy personnel, international mission staff, and foreign nationals in Nairobi can now secure Police Clearance Certificates on the day of application, following the installation of live biometric processing terminals at the Diplomatic Police Unit headquarters.

The decentralisation removes the requirement for diplomatic personnel to queue at the Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) central complex on Kiambu Road or route paperwork through public Huduma Centres. Instead, processing will occur entirely on site at the specialized unit's station.

The move marks an operational milestone for the agency's Multi-Biometric Identification System (MBIS), a digital platform introduced to eliminate the traditional fortnight-long waiting cycle and phase out ink-based finger-print impressions.

### The Shift from Ink to Multi-Modal Scans

![An officer capturing the biometrics of a good conduct certificate applicant.](https://i.ibb.co/G38ncY0S/e8c4bb17f362.webp)

Under the upgraded framework, vetting workflows bypass paper records entirely. The automated system captures digital fingerprints, palm prints, and facial profiles, matching them instantly against the agency’s centralized criminal archives.

The technical switch directly targets historical administrative bottlenecks. By retiring manual paper handling and physical courier transit of fingerprint cards, the DCI reports that certificate turnaround times have compressed from an average of 14 days down to same-day clearance.

Beyond processing velocity, the platform represents a massive computational overhaul of the state's criminal tracking infrastructure. The MBIS architecture expands the DCI's active forensic database from a previous ceiling of two million files to a capacity of 10 million individual records.

Simultaneously, the agency's operational search engine has been scaled to handle up to 20,000 identity verifications each day—more than four times the previous threshold of 4,500 daily queries.

![DCI headquarters off Kiambu Road, Nairobi](https://i.ibb.co/GvH31S17/4bfc29dd8700.webp)

### Mapping the 63-Centre Network

The activation at the Diplomatic Police Unit represents the latest step in a phased national transition that began when the biometric architecture first went live on July 23, 2026. 

Following its capital launch across six primary stations in Nairobi, the DCI rolled out the second implementation phase in September, linking 20 provincial stations concentrated within the Central Region and adjacent northern corridors. That phase extended live terminals to administrative hubs including Kerugoya, Mwea, Embu, Chuka, Marimati, Maara, Kathwana, Meru, Isiolo, and Samburu-Maralal.

With the addition of the diplomatic post, 26 out of the 63 targeted police clearance stations across Kenya are now connected to the real-time biometric network.

While the expansion significantly eases logistical burdens for multinational employers and diplomatic missions that require frequent background vetting for personnel, the broader rollout remains an active transition. More than half of the agency's designated regional clearance desks—37 stations—are still operating on legacy processing paths as technical teams work to bring the remainder of the nationwide network onto the 10-million-record digital grid.
