President William Ruto holds a 16.1 percent lead over his closest challenger as the 2027 General Election cycle kicks into gear. Data released this Tuesday morning, September 8, 2026, by the Bobea Report, places the Head of State at 35.2 percent support. Field researchers conducted the survey across Kenya between August 26 and September 2, collecting views from markets and estates to gauge the mood ahead of the vote.

Edwin Sifuna follows the President with 19.1 percent, while former Cabinet Secretary Fred Matiang’i polls at 14.0 percent. Wiper leader Kalonzo Musyoka trails at 9.0 percent. The survey highlights a massive undecided block, with 17.7 percent of Kenyans surveyed yet to declare their preference. Researchers maintained a 95 percent confidence level with a margin of error of +/- 3.1 percent.

Nairobi’s gubernatorial race shows a tightening field. Embakasi East MP Babu Owino leads the pack with 26.7 percent, while Governor Johnson Sakaja sits at 23.2 percent. James Gakuya remains within striking distance at 21.9 percent. This three-way split suggests the capital will see heavy campaign spending as incumbents and challengers battle for control of City Hall.

In the parliamentary contests, Dagoretti North remains fluid. Beatrice Elachi currently polls at 32.2 percent, keeping incumbent Robert Alai at 27.9 percent. Dagoretti South is even less settled, where 33.8 percent of voters remain undecided. Dennis Waweru leads John Kiarie by 2.3 percent in that constituency, leaving the seat wide open for a late-campaign shift.

Elsewhere, Kiambu Governor Kimani Wamatangi maintains a dominant position, polling at 59.7 percent. His nearest challenger, James Mwaura Kogi, sits at just 4 percent. Meanwhile, in the Nairobi Women Representative race, Esther Passaris leads with 37.9 percent, ahead of Millicent Omanga at 29.2 percent and Karen Nyamu at 11.3 percent.

### The Nairobi read

If you are stuck in the morning jam on Waiyaki Way, look at the passenger in the matatu next to you. That person is the reason these numbers are so volatile. Nairobi is not voting on party lines alone anymore. The Bobea Report confirms what we feel on the ground: the urban voter is bored with the status quo. Babu Owino leading the gubernatorial race is not just a popularity contest; it is a clear signal that the city’s youth are aggressive and ready to disrupt established hierarchies. Sakaja is feeling the heat, and he knows it.

For the incumbent, the proximity of Gakuya and Owino turns the city into a survival game. Every project launch at the county level will now be scrutinized for votes, not just utility. The massive undecided block in Dagoretti South proves that the people are waiting for a compelling reason to show up. When politicians start moving through these estates, they are not just looking for crowds; they are looking for the 33 percent of people who have not yet decided if they even care to queue. The message here is simple. The era of the "safe seat" in Nairobi is effectively over. If you are a politician relying on history to carry you to 2027, you are already walking into a trap. Expect more grassroots movement and, inevitably, more money being pumped into the constituencies to sway those fence-sitters before the campaign officially hits the high gear.
