For a photographer whose entire creative output depends on visual precision, Faustine Lipuku—known widely as Baba Talisha—is confronting the reality he spent weeks hoping to outrun.

On Wednesday, November 18, 2026, the digital content creator confirmed that he has permanently lost sight in his left eye. The irreversible damage marks the culmination of an excruciating two-month medical ordeal, one compounded by an elusive parasitic infection that clinicians initially failed to identify.

Taking to his Instagram Stories, Lipuku laid bare the outcome of his latest specialist review, confirming that local doctors have exhausted standard interventions and recommended an urgent overseas evacuation.

"My heart is so broken; my worst fear is here," Lipuku wrote. "My worst fear has already happened. I have lost one of my eyes. Surgery is scheduled soon, as we plan to travel for further treatments."

### The Cost of the Diagnostic Lag

The loss of vision in Lipuku’s left eye was neither immediate nor sudden. Instead, it was the byproduct of a critical 60-day window during which the underlying cause went undetected.

According to his wife, Miss Wanjey, medical teams spent roughly two months treating him for a conventional ocular condition. During that period, Lipuku endured persistent, severe pain while the real pathogen continued unchecked beneath the surface.

It was only earlier this month, following more than a dozen targeted medical screenings, that laboratory results pinned down the actual culprit: *Acanthamoeba Keratitis* (AK). 

The rare and aggressive infection is triggered by a microscopic, free-living amoeba commonly found in water and soil. Because its early clinical markers closely mimic routine bacterial or viral keratitis, the pathogen frequently evades early detection. By the time clinicians in Nairobi adjusted their treatment protocol to confront the amoeba, the infection had already compromised the structural integrity of his eye.

### Cornea Deterioration and the India Directive

By mid-November, the tissue damage had advanced beyond what pharmacological treatment alone could arrest.

Fellow creator and philanthropist Karangu Muraya stepped in on Wednesday to outline the severity of the situation to their combined audiences. Muraya revealed that the cornea of Lipuku’s left eye had deteriorated extensively—a clinical stage often referred to as corneal melting—leaving no local recourse for recovery.

Faced with the imminent danger of the infection spreading further or permanently destabilizing the surrounding ocular tissue, attending physicians issued a direct referral for specialized medical care in India. The primary objective abroad is an urgent corneal transplant, an intricate surgical procedure designed to excise the damaged tissue, eradicate the persistent infection, and safeguard his remaining eye.

Muraya has since launched a public appeal across digital platforms, urging well-wishers and the creative industry to rally behind Lipuku to finance the travel logistics, hospital admission, and specialized surgery in India.

### Mobilizing to Protect What Remains

Lipuku’s disclosure has sparked an immediate response across Kenya’s influencer ecosystem, where he has long been a familiar fixture both behind the lens and as an online personality.

Colleagues, fans, and digital creators have begun coordinating support efforts to expedite his departure, recognizing that timing is critical in halting any secondary complications.

With travel arrangements and pre-surgical evaluations now underway, Lipuku’s immediate path rests on whether the planned procedure in India can contain the aggressive amoebic damage and stabilize his visual health before further complications emerge.
