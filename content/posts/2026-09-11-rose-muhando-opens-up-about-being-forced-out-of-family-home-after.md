Gospel artist Rose Muhando has publicly disclosed that she and her mother were evicted from their family home by relatives immediately following the death of her father.

The singer, known for her prominent career in East African gospel music, detailed a period of extreme personal hardship during which she and her mother were rendered homeless. Muhando stated that following her father’s burial, relatives seized the property, forcing her mother to relocate with the family under dire conditions.

Reflecting on the displacement, Muhando recounted that her mother endured significant verbal abuse during the takeover. According to the artist, her mother exercised extreme restraint during the confrontation to avoid lashing out at those evicting them.

"When my father died, my mother and I were chased away from home," Muhando stated.

The artist further revealed that during this period of instability, she was responsible for raising her three children-Gift Sheheba, Nicholas Winton, and Maximilian-while experiencing homelessness, noting that the family was at one point forced to seek shelter near a river.

The disclosure also addressed family tensions regarding a sibling who allegedly aligned with the relatives during the dispute. Muhando claimed that this sibling later died under distressing circumstances, an event she interprets through her religious convictions as a consequence of dishonoring a parent.

In her remarks, Muhando issued a public caution against the mistreatment of parents. "Do not let your father seal his mouth and curse you badly. Do not let your mother seal her mouth and curse you badly. God is not foolish," she said.

This account marks the first time the singer has provided specific details regarding the circumstances surrounding her upbringing and early family life. Muhando continues to maintain her career as one of Tanzania’s most recognized gospel performers.
