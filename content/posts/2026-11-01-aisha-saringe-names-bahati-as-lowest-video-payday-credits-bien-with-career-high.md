Aisha Saringe has placed two of Kenya’s most visible musicians on opposite extremes of her career ledger, publicly naming singer Bahati as the client who paid her the lowest fee for a music video and Bien as the one who offered her highest paycheck.

The seasoned video vixen made the disclosure while ing her experiences across several production sets in Nairobi, offering a rare glimpse into the largely private negotiations that determine what supporting talent earns in the country's commercial music industry.

“My lowest paid video was with Bahati. Highest paid video was with Bien,” Saringe stated.

While Saringe stopped short of publishing the exact monetary figures attached to either booking, her remarks put on-record confirmation to an open secret in Kenyan showbiz: standard rate cards for screen talent remain virtually nonexistent, leaving compensation dependent on who controls the budget.

### The Production Economy of the Music Video

Music videos serve as the visual backbone of song releases, functioning both as marketing collateral and algorithmic drivers across streaming networks. To secure high retention and social media traction, recording artists regularly contract dancers, actors, fashion models, and dedicated video vixens like Saringe to anchor the creative direction.

Yet the financial agreements that bring those performers onto the set are traditionally negotiated informally behind closed doors. With no unionized minimum wage or institutional baseline governing video appearances in Kenya, individual payouts vary wildly across productions.

Saringe's contrasting experiences demonstrate how two artists operating at the pinnacle of mainstream appeal can value on-screen talent differently within their operational budgets.

### Divergent Budgets at the Top

Bahati, who built a prolific catalogue through high-tempo collaborations and headline-grabbing rollouts, sits at the lower end of Saringe’s pay scale. Despite commanding substantial public attention and frequently investing in high-concept productions, his project represented the smallest cash return for Saringe's time in front of the lens.

At the other end stands Bien, whose career as a solo hitmaker following his foundational run with Sauti Sol has maintained a reputation for meticulous production standards. Saringe identified the singer-songwriter as her most lucrative employer on set, indicating that his production setup allocated substantially more resources to featured talent.

By withholding the specific sums, Saringe avoided a full audit of both projects, but her assessment crystallizes the unpredictable earning environment facing video performers. A single day on set can yield a negligible return under one marquee brand or establish a career-best payday under another, keeping screen models reliant on personal negotiation rather than standard industry rates.
