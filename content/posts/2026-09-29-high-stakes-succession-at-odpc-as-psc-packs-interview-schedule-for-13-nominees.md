The race to succeed Immaculate Kassait as Kenya’s Data Protection Commissioner has entered a high-pressure administrative phase, with the Public Service Commission setting a single-day vetting schedule for 13 shortlisted candidates drawn from senior regulatory and security dockets.

Following an application window that closed in mid-September after drawing 152 submissions, the commission has compressed the final selection into October 5 at its Nairobi offices. The shortlist reflects a heavy institutional crossover, featuring sitting regulators, state legal directors, and public sector governance specialists.

![Data Commissioner Immaculate Kassait speaking during the Data Privacy Day in Kisumu County on January 26, 2023.](https://i.ibb.co/Gv1TKr9g/6510bf1af4d2.webp)

Among the top contenders is Media Council of Kenya Chief Executive Officer David Omwoyo, alongside Otieno Oscar Onyango, the Senior Deputy Data Commissioner at the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC), and Lydia Chelagat Sitienei, Director of Legal Services at the Communications Authority of Kenya. Other state-linked applicants include Jasper Mbiuki from the Office of the President and Duncan Kubahatisha Nyale, chairperson of the Kilifi Transport and Infrastructure Committee.

The administrative convergence of figures from core regulatory bodies highlights the institutional gravity surrounding the ODPC as Kassait concludes her foundational six-year tenure, which began with her appointment in November 2020.

![MCK CEO David Omwoyo speaking in an engagement](https://i.ibb.co/twWyNGcv/8b59c865be57.webp)

Beyond the executive experience represented on the shortlist—which also includes technology policy specialist John Walubengo, university researchers, and AI policy analysts—candidates face a stringent pre-interview compliance gate. The commission requires applicants to produce comprehensive clearance documentation ranging from tax compliance and anti-corruption verification to credit reference ratings and criminal record checks.

Simultaneously, the commission has opened a narrow public accountability window, inviting citizens to submit sworn affidavits containing credible information regarding any of the 13 nominees ahead of the October 1 deadline, leaving little margin for extended public scrutiny before the panel sits on Monday morning.
