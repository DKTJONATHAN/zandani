The United States Department of Justice announced on Thursday, October 29, that a transnational syndicate orchestrated from Nairobi compromised more than 830,000 federal student aid accounts, funnelling Ksh16.8 billion out of US education loan coffers through money mules, cryptocurrency wallets, and front business entities.

The findings, coordinated under a multi-agency crackdown designated Operation Ghostbusters, established that illicit proceeds from federal loans and grants were siphoned out of educational institutions before being wired directly to beneficiaries in Kenya.

According to Assistant Attorney General Colin McDonald, the operation systematically intercepted student financial disbursements intended for genuine learners, redirecting them through layered offshore banking pipelines.

“After loan and grant disbursements, the refunds were diverted to bank accounts controlled by foreign actors and facilitated by U.S.-based money mules, including Kenyan nationals,” McDonald said. “Whether you are in Nebraska, New Jersey, Virginia, or Nairobi, we will pursue you.”

### Offshore Routing and the 70–30 Split

![The United States Department of Justice addressing the press regarding the ghost student fraud scheme involving Kenyans on Thursday, October 29, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/u.s-department-of-justice-1.jpg.webp?itok=wUQTPIU8)

Court filings outline a sophisticated operational division of labour between recruiters on American soil and coordinators operating in Kenya. Federal authorities disclosed that they have already seized approximately Ksh97 million in cryptocurrency held in an account belonging to a Kenyan citizen tied to the syndicate.

In an indictment unsealed in Nebraska on October 19, prosecutors charged a Kenyan national with five counts of theft of government money and five counts of aggravated identity theft. The suspect allegedly operated between November 2025 and May 2026, receiving a weekly stipend of Ksh258,000 while trying to recruit additional intermediaries.

The operational structure required the Nebraska-based mule to retain a 30 per cent commission on intercepted funds while wiring the remaining 70 per cent balance back to primary orchestrators in Kenya, who supplied the stolen identities used to enrol in academic programmes.

### Safari Fronts and High-Volume Transfers

Similar financial diversion channels emerged across other federal jurisdictions. In Virginia, an indictment returned on October 21 linked another Kenyan citizen to more than 700 fraudulent student aid refund claims totalling Ksh186 million.

Federal investigators documented thousands of individual banking transactions through which the suspect moved at least Ksh168 million directly into connected bank accounts across the United States and Kenya.

![The United States President Donald Trump addressing the press at White House in June 2025.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/trump-addressing-the-press.jpg.webp?itok=B5-DVlYZ)

In New Jersey, federal agents arrested three suspects accused of collaborating with Kenyan operators to draw Ksh129 million in fraudulent aid packages. Charging documents detail how the trio moved the funds through domestic commercial bank accounts before transmitting the capital to Kenyan entities masquerading as a safari enterprise.

Prosecutors confirmed that active proceedings are underway against two additional co-conspirators based in Kenya connected to the New Jersey safari conduit.

### Administrative Exposure and Account Lockdown

Beyond individual prosecutions, the findings have triggered emergency structural safeguards within the US educational financing architecture.

After identifying 830,840 compromised Federal Student Aid (FSA) identification profiles exploited by the ring, US security agencies formally petitioned the Department of Education to disable the entire cluster of accounts immediately.

The massive account deactivation aims to halt active recurring disbursements and prevent further balance leakage across the targeted institutional portals, even as cross-border tracking of downstream recipients in Nairobi continues.
