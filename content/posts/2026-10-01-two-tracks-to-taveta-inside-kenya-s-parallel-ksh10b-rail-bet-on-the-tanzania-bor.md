Kenya’s transport strategy along its southern corridor is developing into a high-stakes, dual-technology experiment. Rather than choosing between restoring legacy rail systems or building modern standard-gauge lines, the government is concurrently executing two separate, non-interoperable railway projects along the exact same 130-kilometre corridor connecting Voi to the Tanzanian border.

Speaking to residents of Mwatate in Taita Taveta County, President William Ruto announced that the government will establish four new stations at Mwatate, Bura, Mwakitau, and Taveta towns. The stations are planned as part of a proposed Standard Gauge Railway (SGR) extension designed to link the existing network at Voi directly to the Tanzanian border. 

According to the President, physical progress on the SGR line is already moving westward, with the alignment having reached Mwakitau. The state has earmarked approximately Ksh5 billion for this SGR extension, which aims to facilitate the movement of cross-border cargo and passengers.

![President William Ruto flagging off construction materials for two Last Mile Connectivity projects in Mwatate, Taita Taveta County on Thursday, October 1, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/Kp9vmGym/aedd4ae4a791.webp)

"Recently I stood here, and you told me you want business between you and Tanzanians to run smoothly," President Ruto said. "Besides the roads in this era, you urged me to build you a railway. The rail is already underway. Some had discouraged me from building it, but the rail is already in operation, and it is in Mwakitau."

This newly proposed SGR corridor runs parallel to another active railway investment. In April 2026, the government launched a separate Ksh5.5 billion rehabilitation of the Voi-Taveta Metre Gauge Railway (MGR)—a legacy line that had remained dormant for nearly two decades. 

By July 2026, Kenya Railways reported that 70 percent of the earthworks on the Voi-Taveta MGR rehabilitation were complete. This means that within the same transit strip, the state is committing a combined capital outlay of over Ksh10.5 billion across two distinct rail standards. 

### The Logistics of a Dual Corridor

![Ongoing works at the Voi Transhipment Hub before the launch of the revitalisation of the Voi-Taveta Metre Gauge Raiway, April 9, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/ynvsZmv7/6831b254720b.webp)

While running two parallel lines to the same border point presents coordination questions, the immediate beneficiaries of this dual investment are local traders and agricultural producers in Taita Taveta County. The proposed SGR stations in Mwatate, Bura, Mwakitau, and Taveta are designed to act as commercial hubs, allowing agricultural goods to enter the national and regional logistics chain far more quickly than by road transport alone.

The MGR rehabilitation offers a lower-cost, conventional freight option designed to revive historic trade flows, while the proposed SGR extension is engineered for higher speeds and direct integration with modern regional logistics. 

However, the long-term utility of the dual-track system depends heavily on what happens at the border. While Kenya is designing its SGR extension to run from Voi to Taveta, Tanzania is concurrently planning its own SGR expansion to meet the Kenyan network at the border post. 

Until both countries align their operational timelines, the division of traffic between the restored MGR line and the newly laid SGR tracks remains an open logistical question for Kenya Railways. For now, the physical work continues on both lines, transforming the Voi-Taveta corridor into a critical test of whether two distinct rail networks can successfully co-exist and justify their concurrent multi-billion-shilling budgets.
