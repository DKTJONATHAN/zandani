President William Ruto has thrown down a direct challenge to Coast political and community leaders, telling them to stop standing on the sidelines and begin actively mobilizing local youth for an impending wave of 60,000 jobs tied to major industrial developments.

Addressing regional stakeholders during an engagement along the coastal belt, the Head of State warned that opportunities connected to Nigerian billionaire Aliko Dangote’s KSh 2.2 trillion East Africa Oil Refinery in Lamu and other regional mega-projects will slip past local talent if leadership fails to organize artisan and technical training in advance.

Ruto singled out three industrial anchors driving the regional employment push: the proposed Dangote refinery in Lamu, the Dongo Kundu Special Economic Zone (SEZ) in Likoni, and the expanding Vipingo Industrial Park in Kilifi County.

“We will get job opportunities for 60,000 youth from the Coast and across Kenya,” President Ruto stated during the meeting. “Over in Lamu, there are job opportunities that will be available, and there is a slot for the youth of Mombasa right there. Over in Dongo Kundu, there are opportunities, over in Vipingo, there are opportunities… Let each one of us prepare, organize these young people properly, and connect these youth with those job opportunities.”

The marquee investment in the regional lineup is the KSh 2.2 trillion East Africa Oil Refinery project in Lamu. Backed by Dangote as a public-private venture, the facility is designed to process energy products for Kenya and the wider East African region, generating roles ranging from heavy construction and engineering to specialized port logistics and facility maintenance.

Further south in Likoni, the Dongo Kundu Special Economic Zone near the Port of Mombasa is designed as a primary manufacturing and trade node. The zone is set to host free trade facilities, manufacturing plants, and logistics parks intended to create both blue-collar work and specialized technical roles.

In Kilifi County, the Vipingo Industrial Park is expanding its footprint across agro-processing, light manufacturing, and construction, targeted at pulling skilled labor from Kilifi and neighboring counties.

Ruto emphasized that securing local placement requires structured coordination between government agencies, local administration, and grassroots leaders. Rather than waiting for contractors to begin recruiting independently, he urged regional leadership to put skills alignment frameworks in place so young people across Mombasa, Lamu, and Kilifi are verified and ready as project phases launch.
