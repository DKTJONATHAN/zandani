Importers navigating Kenya's trade compliance corridors will no longer need to rely on physical desk visits or manual interventions by National Environment Management Authority officers to clear their KRA PINs for Extended Producer Responsibility permits.

Beginning Monday, September 28, 2026, the PIN opting process will shift entirely to electronic handling through the Trade Facilitation Platform, commonly known as the Single Window System. The automation is being rolled out jointly by NEMA and the Kenya Trade Network Agency.

![cargo ship mombasa kpa](https://i.ibb.co/9zXgZyj/3aa2bdb0d3f1.webp)

Under the previous arrangement, businesses seeking to comply with waste management mandates faced procedural friction as officers manually processed PIN opt-ins. The upgraded system leverages KenTrade’s infrastructure to allow direct electronic lodging around the clock, cutting out intermediate clearance delays.

The adjustment is designed to deliver uninterrupted service availability and streamline compliance for goods entering the country. Both agencies noted that the transition aims to eliminate manual bottlenecks, enhance user experience, and speed up overall permit processing times.

![NEMA offices in Nairobi, Kenya.](https://i.ibb.co/dsrwcxHQ/deed93b1bfdb.webp)

The regulatory push stems from the Sustainable Waste Management Extended Producer Responsibility Regulations of 2024. Those rules place direct environmental accountability on manufacturers and importers for product packaging introduced into the local market. In March 2026, NEMA built on that framework by making an EPR Import Certificate a mandatory prerequisite for cargo entry through the National Electronic Single Window System.

With the automated platform going live on Monday, the system shifts from a compliance hurdle dependent on administrative queues to a continuous digital workflow as importers race to meet ongoing environmental vetting requirements.
