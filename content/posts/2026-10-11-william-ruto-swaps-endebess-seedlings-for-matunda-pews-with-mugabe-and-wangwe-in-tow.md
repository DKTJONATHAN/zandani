President William Ruto kept his relentless weekend pace in full view on Sunday, swapping the muddy conservation fields of Trans Nzoia for the polished pews of St Mark’s Catholic Church in Matunda, Likuyani Constituency.

Fresh off leading national Mazingira Day duties just a day earlier, the Head of State made his entrance into the Kakamega County sanctuary flanked by two prominent regional legislators: area host Innocent Mugabe of Likuyani and Navakholo MP Emmanuel Wangwe.

The Matunda church service served as the centerpiece of Ruto’s latest swing through the Western belt, showcasing his well-honed routine of pairing high-visibility spiritual appearances with an unforgiving travel itinerary.

### From Seedling Podiums to the Matunda Altar

Only twenty-four hours before kneeling at St Mark’s, Ruto was anchored in Endebess, Trans Nzoia County, leading tree-growing activities and rallying the country behind his administration’s ambitious target of planting 15 billion trees by 2032.

Yet by Sunday morning, the outdoor conservation gear was traded for formal Sunday attire as he touched down in Kakamega to touch base with parishioners and consolidate his visibility in a county that has become a staple on his official diary.

Flanked by Mugabe and Wangwe, Ruto settled into the service, using the sanctuary visit to reaffirm his personal presence across a region where local leaders continue to jostle for close positioning beside the presidency.

### The Enduring Western Circuit

Sunday's stop in Likuyani is far from an isolated outing. Western Kenya has commanded sustained presidential attention throughout the year, turning what used to be sporadic political check-ins into an ongoing regional tour.

Earlier this year, Ruto spent five consecutive days crisscrossing Kakamega, Busia, Bungoma, and Vihiga counties. That extensive run saw him launch multiple road projects, break ground on affordable housing schemes, and conduct walk-throughs of transport infrastructure designed to open up inter-county trade.

Beyond church engagements and road ribbon-cuttings, his routine in the region remains heavily anchored around the economic heartbeat of the local electorate: the long-troubled sugar belt.

His administration has consistently placed the revival of regional millers at the top of its Western docket, combining direct factory visits with feeder-road upgrades meant to cut transportation costs for sugarcane farmers.

As the Matunda service wound down, Ruto's presence alongside Mugabe and Wangwe signaled that the administration's Western calendar is nowhere near concluded, with infrastructure pledges and grassroots stops remaining firmly intertwined.
