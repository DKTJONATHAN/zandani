President William Ruto is set to launch the newly transformed Kamukunji Recreation Park in Nairobi’s Eastlands, as civil works on the historic 7.27-acre public parcel reach 95 percent completion ahead of an official state unveiling.

The extensive urban redevelopment represents a major physical overhaul of one of Kenya’s most iconic historical landmarks. Revered nationally as the symbolic birthplace of the country's multi-party democracy movement and the staging ground for the landmark 1990 Saba Saba rally, the location is being repurposed to balance heritage preservation with modern civic and sports amenities.

At the center of the upgraded layout is an array of new sports facilities designed to foster youth engagement and nurture local athletic talent across Eastlands. Crews are making final installations on a standard 11-a-side main football pitch, accompanied by a secondary 7-a-side field for training and smaller matches. The grounds also feature multi-purpose basketball courts and a permanent boxing ring, offering local combat sport athletes and neighborhood football clubs access to dedicated facilities within their immediate corridor.

To safeguard the 7.27-acre footprint against structural damage and waterlogging, contractors implemented comprehensive climate and drainage engineering through the ongoing Nairobi Rivers Regeneration initiative. Because the Kamukunji site sits within a low-lying zone highly vulnerable to heavy surface runoff during rainy seasons, engineers constructed upgraded subsurface drainage networks and installed reinforced concrete retaining walls along key borders. These measures are designed to curb flooding, stabilize the soil, and protect the newly landscaped surfaces during heavy downpours.

Beyond athletics and environmental engineering, the park incorporates a major empowerment hub aimed at expanding educational and creative access for local residents. The public center houses a community library, a fully fitted IT laboratory, and a modern music recording studio designed for young artists. Outside the main center, an amphitheatre has been erected to host civic meetings and cultural performances, surrounded by landscaped gardens, children’s play areas, paved walkways fitted with solar lighting, and multiple water points distributed throughout the park.

Despite the comprehensive architectural redesign, the space maintains its official legal status as a protected national heritage site. The national heritage shield ensures that the location’s political monument status remains protected while operating daily as an active community park.

With contractors carrying out final touch-ups across the remaining five percent of the project, the venue is undergoing its final inspection stretch prior to President Ruto's formal ribbon-cutting ceremony.
