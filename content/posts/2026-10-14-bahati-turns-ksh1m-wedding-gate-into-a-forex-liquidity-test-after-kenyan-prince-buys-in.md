Kevin Bahati has turned the guest list for his November nuptials into an open financial audit of Kenya's loudest online currency traders, using a single seven-figure purchase to demand that the entire sector prove its proclaimed wealth.

Taking to his social channels on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, the musician celebrated trader Kenyan Prince—known in social trading circles as K.P. Forex—as the first currency operator to pay the KSh 1 million fee demanded for a seat at his wedding to Diana Marua. Rather than simply issuing an acknowledgment, Bahati immediately leveraged the transaction into a public dare, asking whether any other self-styled market titan actually holds the liquidity to secure a chair.

“IT’S OFFICIAL; KENYAN PRINCE IS THE ONLY FOREX TRADER WHO CAN AFFORD & HAS PURCHASED THE BAHATI’S 1 MILLION INVITE,” Bahati stated. He followed the claim with a direct taunt aimed across the local trading scene: “NYINYI MNAONA KUNA FOREX TRADER MWINGINE ATA AFFORD KWELI??? NANGOJA NIJIONEE.”

### The KSh 100 Million Guest List

The stunt marks the latest escalation in a rollout strategy that has kept the couple’s ten-year union at the center of entertainment chatter since early October. On Wednesday, October 7, Bahati and Marua announced that their formal wedding would take place on November 6, 2026, strictly capped at 100 attendees.

Instead of issuing conventional invitations to family and industry colleagues, the couple placed a flat admission tariff of KSh 1 million per head. If fully realized, the gate fee alone would generate a gross KSh 100 million pool from the selected hundred seats.

“On this special day we will only have 100 people in attendance, and anyone who would like to attend must pay Ksh1 million to get the invite,” Bahati announced during the anniversary reveal.

Diana Marua quickly seconded the terms, endorsing the official date and reminding prospective attendees that entry remains strictly tied to payment confirmation rather than social proximity. The announcement immediately met widespread online pushback, with critics dividing between viewing the move as sheer commercial audacity and brushing it off as an engineered social media spectacle.

### Pushing Back on the Pulpit and City Hall

Bahati has spent the days following the announcement batting away resistance from high-profile voices who questioned the etiquette of charging guests for access to a personal ceremony.

By Friday, October 9, the singer went on the record to draw a sharp boundary around his intended audience, declaring the event explicitly reserved for what he termed “ballers.” He dismissed speculation that the steep entry barrier was merely an opening bid designed to drum up buzz before an eventual price correction, questioning whether skeptics were simply sitting on the fence hoping for a discount that would never materialize.

The pushback had gathered momentum after attracting criticism from both religious and civic quarters. Nairobi County official Geoffrey Mosiria and televangelist Pastor Victor Kanyari publicly weighed in against the concept, with Kanyari questioning the theological and social sense of asking ordinary or wealthy congregants to finance a fellow couple's vows with six-figure contributions.

Bahati dismissed both critiques, keeping the price fixed and shifting his promotional apparatus toward the high-risk, high-display world of forex creators, where screenshots of trading accounts and lavish spending routines serve as daily marketing collateral.

By singling out Kenyan Prince, Bahati has effectively tied social standing in the digital currency niche to his wedding door. With less than a month remaining before the November 6, 2026 ceremony, the singer has left the remaining 99 seats open as a public meter of who else in Nairobi's ostentatious influencer economy is prepared to part with a cool million shillings for an evening meal.
