Media personality and pastor Dr Ofweneke has issued a sharp critique of the traditional "full-time ministry" model, warning that abandoning secular careers for the pulpit can foster an unhealthy financial dependency on church resources. 

In a reflective address shared on Tuesday, October 6, 2026, Ofweneke challenged the common expectation that answering a spiritual calling requires individuals to discard their hard-earned professional skills and rely entirely on congregation-funded coffers.

For Ofweneke, who balances a prominent media career with his pastoral duties, the insistence on shutting down external income streams under the guise of full-time service is a flawed approach to ministry. 

"For me, that’s why I’m still fighting with the statement full-time ministry," he stated, arguing that the label is frequently used to justify an excessive reliance on church finances instead of active, self-sustained service.

### Repurposing the Secular Hustle

To back his argument, Ofweneke pointed to biblical history, specifically analyzing how Jesus Christ managed his disciples. He noted that the disciples’ professional backgrounds were not erased when they transitioned into ministry; instead, their existing secular abilities were integrated into their new spiritual roles. 

"And it hit me that actually when God calls you, he doesn’t kill your skills," Ofweneke shared. 

He cited the example of the disciples who worked as fishermen, explaining that their practical vocation was directly repurposed for their spiritual mission. "The ones who were fishermen were now used as fishermen, and they shall become fishers of men," he observed, suggesting that professional experiences are meant to complement spiritual work rather than compete with it.

### The Self-Sustaining Portfolio

Ofweneke used his own extensive career in the Kenyan corporate and entertainment sectors as a case study for this hybrid approach. Rather than shedding his public persona to fit a traditional pastoral mold, he argues that his diverse professional identity serves as an asset to his ministry.

His current professional portfolio spans a wide array of roles, including:
* Public speaking and corporate hosting
* Public relations practice and branding consulting
* Radio and television presentation
* Voiceover narration and entertainment
* Leadership and mindset coaching

Instead of viewing these commercial ventures as distractions from his spiritual calling, Ofweneke maintains that they provide the financial independence and cultural reach necessary to conduct ministry without becoming a burden on church resources.

"God’s intention is to use who I am as Doctor Ofweneke for the kingdom of God and my skills," he explained, asserting that his established brand remains fully active.

By advocating for a model where ministers maintain their active participation in the marketplace, Ofweneke's remarks challenge the conventional divide between secular work and spiritual service. His perspective encourages believers and clergy alike to view their professional talents, creative outlets, and corporate experience as tools for self-reliance and broader social impact, rather than liabilities that must be abandoned at the altar.
