Fraudsters targeting Kenyan jobseekers have moved beyond fake online job listings to directly impersonating state regulatory officers over mobile phone calls, prompting fresh enforcement alerts from the National Employment Authority.

In a public notice released on Wednesday, September 30, the authority flagged a specific mobile line—0747****26—used by individuals falsely claiming to represent the state body. The regulatory agency explicitly disowned the line, stating it holds no administrative connection to state operations and urging immediate reporting from anyone contacted by the caller.

The alert underscores a tactical shift in recruitment fraud, where swindlers leverage direct phone contact to project regulatory authority and extract money or personal details from applicants.

### Digital Auditing via the NEAIMS Database

To counter direct impersonation and illicit recruitment agencies, the regulator is directing jobseekers away from private phone interactions and toward official digital registry verification. 

Before entering contractual engagements or paying preliminary processing fees, applicants are required to cross-check recruitment entities through the National Employment Authority Integrated Management System (NEAIMS) online portal. The system maintains an active directory of legally recognized agencies and displays their operational status.

Key compliance checks for jobseekers include:
* **License Validation:** Verifying that a recruitment firm holds a current, unexpired operating license on the NEAIMS database.
* **Channel Authentication:** Refusing direct financial transfers requested via personal mobile numbers claiming affiliation with state boards.
* **Foreign Deployment Checks:** Confirming government clearance for agencies recruiting for overseas placements prior to submitting travel documents.

### Rising Diplomatic and Public Agency Fraud

The authority’s warning coincides with parallel misrepresentation schemes targeting international migration channels. The Australian High Commission in Kenya recently alerted the public to fraudulent employment and visa advertisements falsely tied to its diplomatic staff, aimed at individuals seeking overseas work in Australia.

Similar deceptive schemes have increasingly targeted public sector recruitment and state-linked vocational programs, exploiting widespread youth unemployment across urban and rural centers.

To prevent further breach of communications, the regulator maintains that all official administrative inquiries must go through its registered landline at 020 8610000 or via email at info@nea.go.ke, bypassing unverified mobile numbers claiming state clearance.
