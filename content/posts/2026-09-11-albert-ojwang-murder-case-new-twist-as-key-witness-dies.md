NAIROBI, September 11, 2026 - The high-profile murder trial of teacher Albert Ojwang faces a major procedural hurdle following the sudden death of Corporal Stephen Maina Nderitu, a key prosecution witness who was expected to provide testimony regarding the alleged tampering of evidence at the Central Police Station.

Corporal Nderitu, who was under witness protection, collapsed at his Nairobi home shortly after completing his shift on Wednesday. A postmortem examination conducted on the officer attributed his death to a combination of acute ulcers, cardiac arrest, and depression. Burial arrangements for the late officer have been scheduled for September 18.

Nderitu’s testimony was central to the prosecution’s case against former Central Police Station OCS Samson Talaam and five other co-accused. The officer had recorded a statement on May 18, 2025, which prosecutors intended to use to establish a chain of accountability regarding the alleged deletion of CCTV hard drives following Ojwang’s death in police custody.

Following the death, the prosecution team has signaled its intent to apply to the High Court to have Nderitu’s recorded statement admitted as evidence under existing legal provisions, aiming to preserve the integrity of his eyewitness account despite his passing.

The underlying case involves the death of Albert Ojwang, who died while in police custody. While initial official reports attributed his death to self-inflicted head injuries, subsequent investigations by the Independent Policing Oversight Authority (IPOA) and independent medical examiners concluded that the victim had been tortured and strangled.

Evidence previously presented to the High Court included CCTV footage showing Ojwang entering the station alive, followed by images of individuals changing uniforms and the eventual removal of his motionless body from the cell area.

The death of Corporal Nderitu has prompted increased security measures for those involved in the trial. Prosecutors have confirmed that six other key witnesses have been moved into heightened protective custody due to identified risks of interference.

The High Court is slated to resume the hearing of the murder trial in December.
