Regional media giant Nation Media Group has restructured its top editorial leadership across East Africa, reassigning veteran executives to anchor operations in Kenya, Uganda, and Tanzania following months of boardroom shifts and political pressure in Kampala.

### What we know

* Senior editorial directors are stepping directly into high-risk regional outposts to stabilize cross-border operations after severe state interference.
* Sending top executive firepower to Kampala underscores the urgent need to rebuild trust with political authorities following recent newsroom raids.
* The rapid succession of board and editorial shakeups points to intense shareholder pressure for immediate digital turnaround.
* Interim leadership across Nairobi newsrooms gives rising talent a temporary mandate to cut operational costs while maintaining audience trust.

Group Chief Executive Officer Geoffrey Odundo announced the sweeping reassignments, dispatching Group Editor-in-Chief Joe Ageyo to take direct charge as Editor-in-Chief of NMG Uganda. Joined by Washington Gikunju as Acting Managing Editor, Ageyo’s relocation to Kampala comes barely two months after Ugandan security personnel ended a tense 39-day blockade that paralyzed the company’s broadcast and print platforms from late June into July. Ageyo had previously spearheaded high-level negotiations to secure regulatory clearance and get key Ugandan outlets back on air.

The ripple effects of the cross-border shuffle triggered a wide-ranging overhaul at the Kimathi Street headquarters in Nairobi. Harry Misiko assumes the role of Acting Editor-in-Chief for Kenyan operations, backed by Dann Mwangi in Broadcast and New Media, and John Kiplagat leading Publishing. Broadcast journalist Ben Kitili steps up as Acting Lead Editor for Broadcasting, while Justus Wanga takes over the Weekend Edition and Michael Owuor assumes the reins at the Daily Nation. The Business Daily will now be headed by Michael Omondi, with Alex Ndegwa managing the Content Hub. Down south, James Smart crosses into Dar es Salaam to serve as Acting Managing Editor for Broadcast and New Media at Mwananchi Communications Limited.

This editorial realignment directly mirrors an aggressive corporate overhaul launched in August, when the board brought in six prominent directors-including Juliana Rotich, Bharat Thakrar, Julie Gichuru, Julius Kipng’etich, Wilfred Musau, and Georgia Mutagahywa-to redirect the business. Facing an increasingly difficult print market across three East African nations, the media conglomerate is banking on these interim setups to streamline operations and salvage audience numbers.

On the newsroom floor, the message is plain: the old playbook no longer pays the bills. Parachuting top-tier talent into Kampala shows how critical Uganda’s recovery is to the group’s bottom line, especially after 39 days of lost airtime and advertising revenues. Meanwhile, in Nairobi, an all-acting slate of editorial leaders faces the steep challenge of producing sharp daily journalism while pushing traditional audiences onto digital channels. For rank-and-file reporters, these constant administrative shifts bring both uncertainty and a clear mandate-deliver digital traffic fast or watch the newsroom shrink even further.
