Bishop Erick Mwangi of New Breed City Chapel has taken to TikTok to demand that Hiram Gitau, popularly known as Tash, immediately relinquish custody of the late Betty Bayo’s two children to their biological father, Pastor Victor Kanyari, injecting divine warnings into an active, court-regulated custody transition.

In a sermon video published on Tuesday, November 10, 2026, the Nairobi preacher asserted that he received a spiritual message while praying, specifically cautioning Tash against continuing to live with the children following the singer’s death on November 10, 2025.

“I was in prayer and God said, son, a hedge has been broken,” Mwangi stated in the broadcast, directing his remarks at Tash despite confirming he had never met him personally. “If you’re not careful, you’re about to fall a bit. It will happen in broad daylight. Take your hands off the beacons of another man.”

Mwangi went further to claim that Tash had previously experienced dreams regarding his own death, urging him to take the warning seriously and step away from what he characterized as another man’s domain.

“God is saying, return the children to the father. Return them,” Mwangi declared, describing the delayed transfer as an act of wickedness.

However, Mwangi’s pulpit ultimatum directly clashes with the legal parameters established by the judicial system just two weeks earlier.

On October 26, 2026, Dagoretti Magistrate Casmir Obiero delivered a decision on Kanyari’s petition for custody. While the court awarded legal custody to Kanyari as the biological father, Magistrate Obiero explicitly declined to order an immediate physical handover of the minors.

Instead, the magistrate directed that the children temporarily remain under Tash’s care while a Kiambu Children Officer formulates a formal transition plan. That process was mandated to explicitly address the children’s welfare, continuing education, and emotional stability before any change of residence occurs.

The court order also provided for structured contact between Kanyari and his children during the interim. Crucially, evidence presented during the court proceedings showed that the children themselves expressed a clear preference to remain living with Tash, who has maintained their day-to-day care since Bayo’s passing.

Mwangi’s intervention effectively demands an immediate bypass of that judicial timeline, substituting the court-ordered transitional welfare assessment with a spiritual imperative. As it stands, the legal baseline established by the Dagoretti court remains in effect under the supervision of the Kiambu Children Department.
