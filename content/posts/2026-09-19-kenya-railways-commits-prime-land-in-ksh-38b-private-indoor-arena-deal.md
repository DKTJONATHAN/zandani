Kenya Railways has committed central Nairobi land under a long-term lease to anchor a $294.5 million (Ksh 38.2 billion) indoor sports and entertainment complex, injecting private commercial capital into the state corporation's long-planned Railway City redevelopment.

The project, spearheaded by Pan-African investment firm Zaria Group alongside US-based Helios Sports & Entertainment Group, centers on a 10,000-seat multipurpose indoor arena built within the Nairobi Central Railway Station precinct. 

According to regulatory disclosures cited by Bloomberg, the partners have finalized the long-term land lease with Kenya Railways, clearing the ground for a privately financed anchor tenant designed to convert rail yards into a high-density mixed-use entertainment district.

### A Concession-Led Master Plan

The agreement marks a structural shift in how large-scale public assembly infrastructure is funded in the capital. While the national government continues to deploy public resources on developments such as the Talanta Sports City, the Railway City arena relies on a commercial concession model.

![An undated aerial view of Nairobi's Upper Hill side.](https://i.ibb.co/Cpq2nwmH/9ca1c503a892.webp)

Under the plan, the facility will stage indoor basketball tournaments, international music concerts, cultural festivals, and corporate gatherings under the Meetings, Incentives, Conferences and Exhibitions (MICE) banner.

Zaria Group co-founder Masai Ujiri, who serves as president of NBA franchise Dallas Mavericks, said the investment intends to establish commercial infrastructure capable of sustaining elite athletics and commercial entertainment in East Africa. The facility is envisioned as a regular stop for touring performers and continental sporting circuits that routinely bypass Nairobi due to a lack of enclosed, acoustically engineered venues with modern ticketing and broadcast capacity.

### Projected Labour and Operating Footprint

Developers project that the construction phase will generate approximately 3,500 direct jobs, followed by 1,500 permanent operational roles once the gates open.

The consortium's operational projections further estimate that secondary activity across live production, security, hospitality, catering, and transport will generate up to 25,000 temporary positions annually. 

![Construction workers install roofing at the Bomas International Convention Complex construction site in Nairobi, April 20, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/jvfckNhH/b0c4643c39b4.webp)

Those returns depend heavily on the venue maintaining a dense calendar of ticketed dates beyond traditional weekend athletics. The surrounding district is planned to host complementary retail, dining, and public plazas meant to sustain footfall during non-event days.

### Replicating the Kigali Playbook

The venture directly mirrors the commercial strategy executed by Zaria Group in Rwanda. In Kigali, the firm backed the $26 million (Ksh 3.37 billion) Zaria Court mixed-use urban district, operating alongside its management and revenue-generation contracts at the 10,000-seat BK Arena and the redeveloped Amahoro Stadium.

The consortium treats Kigali as an operational proof-of-concept for a broader regional portfolio that aims to connect venue networks across Nairobi, Lagos, and Johannesburg. By controlling both venue operations and adjacent hospitality assets, the developers seek to capture spending on food, lodging, and retail that standard municipal stadiums typically surrender to surrounding neighborhoods.

For Kenya Railways, the transaction represents one of the most lucrative real estate mobilizations in its historical urban landbank, trading underutilized rail tracks for a guaranteed long-term commercial leaseholder. 

The operational test now shifts to how smoothly the private development can be integrated into the Central Station’s existing transit operations, turning a crowded commuter interchange into a functioning, ticket-controlled entertainment hub.
