Kevin Bahati has pulled back the curtain on the personal tipping point that drove his move from commercial stages into electoral contests, recalling how pocketing corporate-scale performance fees while watching childhood peers risk their lives as campaign muscle in 2017 shattered his comfort with showbiz neutrality.

Speaking during a live broadcast interview on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, the recording artist explained that his controversial plunge into the Mathare parliamentary race was born out of profound moral friction on the national campaign trail rather than a sudden craving for office.

### The VIP Stage and the Street Reality

During the 2017 general election cycle, Bahati was among the headline entertainers booked to rally tens of thousands of supporters for national political figures. Yet, the view from the VIP stage brought an uncomfortable reality home.

While he was being paid to endorse candidates who had little personal interest in informal settlements, he regularly spotted former classmates from Mathare down in the dust, deployed on the front lines as hired goons.

“Unaletwa u-endorse mtu unajua huyu mtu hajasaidia mtaa,” Bahati recalled, pointing out the contradiction of lending his celebrity endorsement to politicians who abandoned slum quarters the moment the ballot boxes closed. “And that’s how I made a last minute decision in 2022.”

That realization, he admitted, pushed him to mount an eleventh-hour campaign for the Mathare parliamentary seat on a Jubilee Party ticket in 2022. While he maintains the motivation was justified, the execution was practically compromised from day one.

“I feel like it’s the best decision I made, but politically was a mistake because I came in so late,” he noted.

### Coalition Quotas and Parachuted Leaders

That rushed entry left Bahati with 8,166 votes, placing him third in a crowded, high-stakes contest. Looking back at that tally, the artist argued that a substantial portion of Mathare's registered voters went into polling booths entirely unaware that his name was even on the ballot.

Yet, beyond his own tactical blunders, Bahati took direct aim at the machinery of Kenya's major political coalitions, accusing party secretariats of treating Mathare as an electoral dumping ground for political allies who cannot win elsewhere.

According to Bahati, parliamentary tickets in informal settlements are routinely traded off to satisfy coalition balance sheets rather than local representation. This institutional habit, he argued, leaves Mathare perpetually managed by leaders who have no lived understanding of its day-to-day crises.

“Mathare has never had an MP or rather a leader that is born, raised, schooled, bred in Mathare,” Bahati stated. “Because of political alignment, unakuja unaletwa unaambiwa there is a constituency hatuna candidate and that’s how we get our MPs.”

### The Learning Curve of Slum Representation

The direct fallout of parachuting political outsiders into informal settlements, Bahati argued, is an administrative learning curve that stalls basic constituency services for years.

When a candidate with no background in the informal settlement secures power through top-down coalition arrangements, their early tenure is spent simply figuring out the physical and social terrain.

“After elections, huyu mtu anaanza kutafuta sasa nitaanzia wapi kwa hii slum,” Bahati explained. “You don’t know the issues facing the youth, the issues facing the kids, women. That’s why development imekuwa very slow.”

For Bahati, who grew up within Mathare's community networks before rising to mainstream musical fame, closing that gap requires someone who does not need an orientation tour to recognize where the pipeline bursts, where schools lack space, or why local youth get pulled into political violence.

With his sights firmly set on the 2027 General Election, the singer-turned-politician maintains that the lessons of his third-place 2022 finish have already reshaped his ground strategy. Instead of a hurried late-stage declaration fueled by frustration, Bahati is banking on an extended runway to convince Mathare that home-grown familiarity outweighs party-brokered machinery.
