Thousands of Junior Secondary School (JSS) teachers will see automatic deductions from their September salaries after a Nairobi court solidified a top-level administrative truce between Kenya’s rival teaching unions.

Justice Monica Mbaru of the Employment and Labour Relations Court has ordered the Teachers Service Commission (TSC) to deduct and remit September 2026 agency fees from all JSS teachers. Crucially, the interim directive, stemming from a petition originally filed by the Kenya National Union of Teachers (KNUT), instructs the commission to route these payments directly to the rival Kenya Union of Post-Primary Education Teachers (KUPPET) in accordance with a joint consent order brokered between the two unions.

The TSC has been given a 14-day window from September 16 to respond to the motion. The commission must then appear in court on October 5, 2026, to formally report on its compliance with the payroll deduction order.

### The Mechanics of the Truce

![JSS interns participating in demonstrations in Nairobi.](https://i.ibb.co/hJDCLcHY/f6f38a93ad04.webp)

While KNUT and KUPPET regularly clash in public over who represents the expanding junior secondary cadre, the court order reveals a pragmatic, top-level legal consensus on how non-union cash is handled. 

Under Section 49 of the Labour Relations Act, teachers who choose not to register with a union must still pay a statutory "agency fee" if they benefit from collective bargaining agreements (CBAs) negotiated by that union. KUPPET has maintained that because JSS teachers enjoy salary adjustments and terms secured under its existing CBA, they are legally obligated to pay these deductions regardless of active membership.

Yet this top-down financial agreement contrasts sharply with mounting friction at the grassroots level. Just recently, KNUT’s Nyamira branch raised administrative alarms, accusing the TSC of unilaterally migrating its registered members to KUPPET or subjecting them to unauthorized agency-fee deductions. The branch demanded that the employer reverse these transfers and produce the authorization paperwork used to alter teacher payrolls.

### A Broader Battle for JSS Control

![Teachers Service Commission Acting CEO Evaleen Mitei during a session with the National Assembly’s Departmental Committee on Education, May 13, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/Zp55s3H2/3e613b095798.webp)

Beyond the immediate battle for September's agency fees, the two unions remain deeply divided on how the junior secondary school system should be structured:

* **KUPPET's Position:** Advocates for junior secondary sections to operate as autonomous schools, complete with their own independent management structures and budgets.
* **KNUT's Position:** Demands that JSS classes remain fully integrated within existing primary school administrations under the comprehensive school model.

Despite these fundamental structural disagreements, the national offices of both unions have cleared the path for immediate financial deductions. For JSS teachers, the practical result is a reduced September payslip, authorized by a court order that effectively bypasses local branch protests over forced payroll migrations.

The focus now shifts to the TSC headquarters, where administrators must implement the court-ordered deductions across the JSS payroll before presenting their compliance report to Justice Mbaru on October 5.
