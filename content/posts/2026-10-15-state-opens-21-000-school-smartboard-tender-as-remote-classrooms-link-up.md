According to the Ministry of Information, Communication and the Digital Economy, public junior secondary learners in historically under-resourced counties stand to tap specialist instruction across county boundaries in real time, following the rollout of interactive classroom links funded under the World Bank’s Kenya Digital Economy Acceleration Project (KDEAP).

The cross-county learning network was formally demonstrated on Wednesday, October 14, when President William Ruto conducted a live National Digital Class session from Kiminini Junior Secondary School in Trans Nzoia County. The session established simultaneous two-way audio-visual feeds with three other public schools situated hundreds of kilometres apart, testing whether interactive digital smartboards can replicate standard in-person teaching environments without moving students or staff between regions.

The initial network linked Kiminini to St Georges Junior Secondary School in Ruiru, Kiambu County, where Information, Communication and the Digital Economy Cabinet Secretary William Kabogo coordinated the Kiambu classroom. Two further remote stations operated concurrently: Gender Cabinet Secretary Hannah Cheptumo and Musa Sirma participated alongside learners at Kabimoi Junior Secondary School in Baringo County, while ICT Authority Board Chairperson Lily Ngok joined the live link at Kericho Township Junior Secondary School.

### Bypassing Inter-County Transit Overhead

![An image of President William Ruto leading a live National Digital Class Experience at Kiminini Junior Secondary School in Trans Nzoia County, on Wednesday, October 14.](https://i.ibb.co/994JCKDw/748fe847acd6.webp)

The central operational objective behind the smartboard rollout is the eradication of geographical isolation for remote institutions. By pairing high-definition digital boards with persistent internet connectivity, classrooms in different administrative zones can conduct unified practical sessions, share single-teacher presentations, and coordinate joint revision exercises.

For administrators and parents, the model targets immediate institutional savings. School communities frequently shoulder substantial transport and boarding expenses to shuttle learners across counties for academic symposiums, joint practicals, and peer exchanges. State planners argue that real-time digital linking eliminates those transit overheads entirely, allowing institutions to anchor national collaboration inside their existing physical classrooms.

The deployment forms the operational anchor of the government's Digital Skills and Markets programme, a core pillar under KDEAP designed to standardise foundational digital literacy across primary and secondary tiers.

### Curriculum Readiness and the Grade 9 Gap

While the hardware demonstration established functional transmission between Trans Nzoia, Kiambu, Baringo, and Kericho, the broader educational rollout remains contingent on instructional content delivery.

State education authorities have fully formatted digital curriculum material for Grade 7 and Grade 8 learners. However, content for Grade 9 remains under development as curriculum developers work to complete the digital syllabi needed to support continuous progression through senior secondary preparation.

![A collage of President William Ruto leading a live National Digital Class Experience at Kiminini Junior Secondary School in Trans Nzoia County, and Cabinet Secretary for Informatio](https://i.ibb.co/VYybV0dG/853fff8f5080.webp)

In tandem with content digitisation, the ICT Ministry on Tuesday, October 13, initiated a separate procurement process inviting qualified consultancy firms to submit expressions of interest for integrating artificial intelligence into the national syllabus. Bidding firms must demonstrate verifiable technical experience in building structured AI coursework.

Ministry officials clarified that while the AI curriculum design exercise remains functionally distinct from the live hardware installations seen in Kiminini, both programmes operate under the broader national digital literacy mandate.

### The 21,000-School Expansion Sprint

The long-term viability of the initiative now shifts from Wednesday's four-school demonstrator to a mass procurement drive. The ministry confirmed that procurement procedures for phase two have formally started, setting a target to install interactive smartboards and network access across approximately 21,000 schools countrywide by April 2027.

Achieving that footprint requires navigating substantial logistical realities across off-grid and peripheral rural zones, where electrical stability and local network infrastructure must match the transmission requirements demonstrated in Kiambu and Trans Nzoia. The coming procurement rounds will determine how rapidly private contractors and network providers can deliver uniform classroom hardware before the April 2027 deadline closes.
