Public university lecturers have taken their push for the Ksh20 billion Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA) to Parliament, drawing a hard line against state attempts to link academic staff compensation to institutional student enrolment numbers or fee collection levels.

Appearing before the Parliamentary Committee on Labour and Education on Wednesday, November 4, Universities Academic Staff Union (UASU) Secretary General Constantine Wesonga warned that treating public university payrolls as contingent on tuition revenue creates structural instability across higher education.

### Parliamentary Scrutiny Following Strike Suspension

The union's submission to lawmakers follows the suspension of a nationwide strike that shut down teaching and research across public campuses for more than 20 days. The industrial action, which began on October 2, ended after UASU signed a Return to Work Formula on October 30, paving the way for lecturers to resume duties on Monday, November 2.

![Secretary General of the Universities Academic Staff Union (UASU), Dr. Constantine Wasonga, at a meeting with Education CS Julius Migos on July 22, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/MDgDqxz1/91638e5cf3e5.webp)

Addressing lawmakers, Wesonga stressed that the operational crisis facing public universities extends beyond concluding a single bargaining cycle, pointing directly to the national government’s core obligation to fund tertiary institutions directly rather than pushing labor liabilities onto cash-strapped campus accounts.

UASU explicitly rejected administrative proposals suggesting that staff employment stability and negotiated CBA disbursements should track individual university student tallies or fee clearance rates. Union leadership told the committee that expecting public institutions to maintain academic standards in teaching, research, and innovation while operating under variable, fee-dependent funding models is untenable.

### Structure of the Ksh20 Billion Settlement

At the center of the dispute is a Ksh20 billion total financial package negotiated to end the October strike. Under the signed agreement, the Treasury is required to release the funds in three structured tranches:

* **Financial Year 2026/2027:** Ksh6.7 billion
* **Financial Year 2027/2028:** Ksh6.7 billion
* **Financial Year 2028/2029:** Ksh6.6 billion

The terms of the deal provide for a 3.5 per cent annual increase in basic salary for public university lecturers over four years, backdated to take effect from July 1, 2025. The package also incorporates an automatic 5 per cent annual salary increment, enhanced harmonized allowances, and a structured timeline to clear accumulated salary and allowance arrears.

![UASU Ogamba](https://i.ibb.co/NgRFZTCS/3745718826af.webp)

To safeguard returning staff, the October 30 agreement includes explicit protection against victimisation or disciplinary action for union members and officials who participated in the strike.

### Fiscal Realities and the Next Legislative Test

By pushing back against enrolment-linked compensation formulas, UASU is pressing Parliament to ensure that the agreed tranches are integrated directly into national budget estimates as unconditional statutory transfers rather than discretionary university capitation.

While UASU reiterated its commitment to the signed dispute-resolution framework, union officials made clear that industrial peace hinges on strict compliance with the agreed payout schedules.

The immediate focus now turns to the parliamentary budget planning cycle, where lawmakers must decide how the initial Ksh6.7 billion tranche for the 2026/2027 financial year will be appropriated into national expenditure estimates.
