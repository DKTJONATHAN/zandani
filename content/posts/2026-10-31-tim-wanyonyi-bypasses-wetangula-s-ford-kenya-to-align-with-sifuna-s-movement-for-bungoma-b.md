Westlands MP Tim Wanyonyi has officially drawn a sharp line through family political calculations, stepping away from his brother’s sphere of influence to join Edwin Sifuna’s Linda Mwananchi Movement.

Wanyonyi made his definitive entry into Sifuna’s political fold on Friday, October 30, 2026, during the grand opening of the movement’s secretariat and headquarters in Nairobi. The move brings months of intense speculation over his 2027 political vehicle to a definitive close, while instantly complicating the local chessboard in Western Kenya.

For months, grassroots supporters in Bungoma anticipated that Wanyonyi would launch his gubernatorial ambitions under FORD Kenya, the party led by his elder brother, National Assembly Speaker Moses Wetangula. By aligning instead with Sifuna—an outspoken ODM secretary-general who has positioned the Linda Mwananchi outfit as an opposition force—Wanyonyi has opted for an independent political path that diverges sharply from Wetangula’s current backing of President William Ruto’s re-election bid.

The sibling divide is hardly new, but its electoral implications have rarely been this sharp. Throughout previous coalition cycles—including CORD in 2013 and NASA in 2017—Wanyonyi maintained his loyalty to ODM while Wetangula steered FORD Kenya. However, the 2027 Bungoma gubernatorial contest places the brothers in a uniquely delicate position. Wanyonyi’s campaign for the county seat sets him up against opponents like Kimilili MP Didmus Barasa, who is expected to fly the United Democratic Alliance (UDA) flag.

Sifuna, whose home county is Bungoma, has previously pulled no punches in criticizing Barasa, raising strong expectations that the ODM secretary-general could throw his considerable regional weight behind Wanyonyi's bid. That potential endorsement offers the Westlands legislator a strategic edge, anchoring his campaign within a network that cuts directly across Western Kenya's shifting voter blocs.

Even with Wanyonyi's high-profile entry into the movement, the race for Bungoma remains wide open. FORD Kenya retains deep institutional roots in the county, built largely on the incumbency of Governor Ken Lusaka, leaving the possibility open for the party to mount its own fierce defense of the seat. As the 2027 election cycle gathers momentum, Wanyonyi’s calculated gamble outside his family’s traditional party machinery marks the opening salvo in what promises to be one of the region’s most closely watched gubernatorial battles.
