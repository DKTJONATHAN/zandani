Filing an inquiry or attempting to trace a missing colleague in Nairobi has emerged as a direct security hazard for human rights advocates, as the sequential abduction and roadside abandonment of two activists this week demonstrates.

Activist Collins Otieno was found injured, weak, and disoriented along Thika Road at approximately 4:30 a.m. on Saturday, September 26. He had vanished earlier in the week after attempting to establish the whereabouts of his colleague, Boniface Mulinge Muteti, who had disappeared under similar circumstances four days prior.

A passing boda boda rider discovered Otieno on the roadside and transported him to Neema Uhai Hospital in the Kasarani area, where he was admitted for medical treatment.

### Sequential Targetings

![Activists Julius Kamau and Collins Otieno arrested in Nairobi CBD on September 21, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/PvDyt5PB/013549ad1313.webp)

The developments mark the second pre-dawn roadside recovery of a missing activist in under 24 hours. Muteti was located alive on Friday, September 25, in the Lukenya area of Machakos County, also displaying signs of disorientation and physical trauma.

Both men were among a group of civil society figures arrested in Nairobi’s Central Business District on Monday, September 21, during demonstrations against the ongoing detention of Ugandan opposition leader Kizza Besigye. 

While Muteti disappeared shortly after stepping away from fellow protesters on Monday to print an official petition, Otieno’s disappearance occurred after he began visiting authorities and contacts to trace Muteti's location.

"Comrade Collins Otieno has been found. He was dumped along Thika Road," stated Hussein Khalid, Chief Executive Officer of civil society organisation Vocal Africa, on Saturday morning. "Like comrade Muteti, Collins is disoriented, weak, and injured. He is presently at Neema Uhai Hospital around Kasarani receiving treatment."

### Extralegal Custody and Highway Abandonment

The timing and geographical spread of the abandonments point to a tactical pattern designed to avoid formal station custody logs. Rather than processing detainees through standard police station entry books, those held are deposited along major arterial transit routes—Lukenya on the Nairobi-Mombasa highway to the south, and Kasarani along Thika Road to the north—during off-peak hours.

![Activists Julius Kamau and Collins Otieno arrested in Nairobi CBD on September 21, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/cSRhfhXW/515e1d6679a5.webp)

This pattern forces civilian bystanders and informal transport operators to act as emergency first responders. In both instances, local residents and boda boda operators were responsible for securing medical assistance for the victims before human rights networks could establish contact.

The specific location where Otieno was held between his disappearance and his drop along Thika Road remains unaccounted for. No official security agency has issued a statement explaining his detention or identifying the vehicles involved in his transport.

### Unresolved Inquiries

Otieno remains under observation at Neema Uhai Hospital as medical personnel evaluate his injuries.

With both activists now accounted for and receiving care, rights groups are demanding formal disclosures regarding who ordered the initial Monday arrests, how missing persons reports were handled by station commanders during the week, and why injured citizens continue to be left along major highway corridors without public records of arrest.
