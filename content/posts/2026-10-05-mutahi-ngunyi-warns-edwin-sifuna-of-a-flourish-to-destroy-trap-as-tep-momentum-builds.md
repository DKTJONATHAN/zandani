Political analyst Mutahi Ngunyi has issued a sharp reality check to Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna, warning that his current wave of popularity is a classic setup designed to let him peak too early and crash before the 2027 General Election.

Sifuna, who has been riding high on the momentum of his newly launched outfit, The Equitable Party (TEP), and his "Linda Mwananchi" movement, is being cautioned that his rapid ascent might not be as organic or safe as it appears. 

Ngunyi pointed to fundamental rules of power dynamics to explain the senator's current trajectory, suggesting that his political competitors are intentionally giving him room to expand before pulling the rug out from under him.

"If you want to DESTROY someone, you must first make him FLOURISH," Ngunyi shared, arguing that Sifuna's rivals are strategically allowing him to exhaust his political momentum long before voters head to the ballot.

This cautionary take highlights the growing unease within the broader opposition ranks as Sifuna positions TEP as a national vehicle capable of fielding candidates across the country. The rapid expansion has drawn both skepticism and outright pushback from established leaders within the Orange Democratic Movement (ODM) and government-allied circles.

Among the skeptics is Homa Bay Governor Gladys Wanga, who has publicly questioned the staying power of Sifuna’s current political wave. Wanga and other establishment figures have characterized the excitement surrounding the "Linda Mwananchi" movement as a temporary phase of political euphoria, pointing to visible pockets of friction and resistance during some of the senator’s recent public rallies as early signs of fatigue.

However, Sifuna is not without defenders who reject Ngunyi’s psychological trap theory. 

Suba North MP Millie Odhiambo-Mabona has pushed back against the establishment’s narrative, arguing that critics are consistently underestimating the senator’s genuine grassroots appeal. According to Odhiambo-Mabona, any coordinated attempts by political insiders to sideline Sifuna have only served to energize his supporters, turning establishment resistance into a catalyst for his rapid growth.

As Sifuna moves forward with plans to institutionalize TEP nationwide, the debate over his political shelf life remains open. Whether his current popularity represents a sustainable, long-term movement or a carefully managed bubble waiting to be popped will be one of the defining dynamics of the pre-2027 political landscape.
