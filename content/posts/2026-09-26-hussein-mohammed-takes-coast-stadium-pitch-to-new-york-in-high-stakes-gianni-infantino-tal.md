Football Kenya Federation President Hussein Mohammed took Kenya’s coastal sports ambitions straight to world football’s top executive in New York, joining President William Ruto for high-level bilateral talks with FIFA President Gianni Infantino.

The meeting, held on Thursday, September 24, on the sidelines of the 81st United Nations General Assembly, saw Mohammed and Prime Cabinet Secretary Musalia Mudavadi flank the Head of State to push for an international-grade stadium in Mombasa designed to position the Coast region as an elite destination for global sports tourism.

For Mohammed, who took the federation’s reins with a mandate to professionalise local football infrastructure and talent pipelines, the New York summit marked a decisive effort to anchor global governing support to Kenya's expanding domestic venue blueprint.

### The Coastal Arena Pitch

Central to the discussions was the formal pitch to construct a certified FIFA-standard stadium in Mombasa. While Kenya’s primary international fixtures have traditionally concentrated in the capital, the delegation framed the coastal facility as both an athletic necessity and a commercial engine.

“We also discussed progress on the possibility of a FIFA-standard stadium in Mombasa to serve the Coast region and position Kenya as a hub for international football and sports tourism,” Hussein Mohammed confirmed in an official statement outlining the New York engagement.

If realized, the facility would give the coastal belt its first globally accredited football venue, opening the door for international friendlies, continental tournament hosting, and training camps integrated with the region’s hospitality sector.

### Nationwide Infrastructure Expansion

The Mombasa proposal arrives amid an aggressive national sports facility overhaul. The government is currently executing construction and modernization work across 32 regional venues.

Chief among those active developments is the Raila Odinga International Stadium, which is being constructed to satisfy the strict joint specifications of both FIFA and the Confederation of African Football (CAF). The New York delegation utilized the broader 32-facility footprint to demonstrate administrative commitment before pressing Infantino on technical endorsements for the Coast project.

### Elite Academies and the FIFA Arena Program

Beyond concrete and seating capacities, the bilateral discussions focused on restructuring player pathways from the grassroots level to elite competition. President Ruto and Mohammed reviewed institutional partnerships with Infantino, prioritizing decentralized scouting networks and coaching capacity.

“We discussed regional technical centres to identify and nurture young talent, strengthen grassroots football and create clear pathways for the next generation of Kenyan players,” President Ruto said following the closed-door talks.

According to FKF leadership, the talks also advanced operational frameworks for the FIFA Arena project in Kenya, alongside the establishment of a state-of-the-art national technical center. The proposed national hub will centralize coaching education certifications and high-performance training modules for national junior and senior squads.

With diplomatic groundwork now established in Manhattan, the focus shifts to joint technical assessments between FKF engineering teams and FIFA’s stadium development division to map site specifications and implementation timelines for the Mombasa venue.
