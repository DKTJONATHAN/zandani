NAIROBI - Veteran KBC Radio Taifa broadcaster Edmond Salasya died early Thursday morning following a sudden lung collapse, hours after completing his final live radio shift at the state broadcaster.

**What we know:**
*   Salasya, known to listeners as Edmonds Selassia, was on air from 8:00 p.m. to midnight on Wednesday, September 9, 2026.
*   The cause of death has been confirmed by colleagues as a lung collapse.
*   He served as a primary anchor for the KBC Kiswahili Service and hosted the drive-time program *Tafrija za Kutwa*.
*   Funeral arrangements are pending, with former colleagues including Bonnie Musambi pledging support to the family.

News of the veteran journalist's passing circulated through Kenyan newsrooms Thursday morning, prompting tributes from industry peers. Mathias Momanyi, a long-time colleague, paid tribute to Salasya as both a professional associate and a close personal friend.

Former colleague Bonnie Musambi expressed shock at the sudden loss, noting the two shared a professional history at Radio Taifa and a mutual interest in rhumba music. "My former colleague at Radio Taifa and such a dear friend," Musambi said. "We always called each other 'Manze Osuofia.' My fellow rhumba enthusiast."

Former co-host Caroline Mramba also acknowledged the loss, stating, "My heart is heavy... My personal person and former co-host Edmonds Selassia is no more."

Enock Bosire, who arrived at the KBC studios on Thursday morning, noted that the atmosphere among staff was one of collective grief. Bosire remembered Salasya for his signature Lingala-inflected greetings, which became a staple of his nightly broadcasts.

Salasya’s career at the Kenya Broadcasting Corporation established him as a prominent voice in Swahili-language broadcasting. He remained active in his role as a presenter until the hours preceding his death. Further details regarding burial plans are expected to be announced by the family in the coming days.
