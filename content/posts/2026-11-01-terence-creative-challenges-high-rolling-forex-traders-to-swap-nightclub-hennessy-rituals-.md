Content creator Terence Creative has thrown down a heavy financial challenge to Kenya’s self-proclaimed forex traders, urging them to stop washing their hands with top-shelf liquor in high-end nightclubs and instead pool their money to purchase a fleet of 100 school buses.

The comedian stepped directly into an escalating social media controversy surrounding viral clips of young traders putting on extravagant displays of wealth in nightlife spots across Nyali and Nakuru.

"I am challenging all rich Forex traders, badala ya kuoga na pombe, hiyo pesa nunueni basi za shule zisaidie watoto and donate them," Terence stated in a public video, confronting the trend head-on. "Am challenging you guys to buy 100 buses, kwani what is money?"

![Kenyan Prince // Instagram](https://i.ibb.co/hxj5qD2c/498cad8838c8.webp)

### The Nightlife Ritual Under Fire

Terence’s intervention targets a specific online trend where traders, including prominent figure Chuka Kenyan Prince, capture themselves treating luxury bottles as high-priced hand sanitizer. Video footage circulating across platforms shows individuals at venues such as Al Capone in Nyali having premium liquor continuously poured over their hands and onto VIP tables.

The displays routinely feature expensive labels including Hennessy VS, Hennessy VSOP, and Clase Azul tequila. In several clips, full 700ml bottles of Hennessy VSOP are uncorked and poured out entirely, accompanied by slogans like "washing away the sins."

The public rituals have drawn mixed reactions across Kenyan digital spaces. While some onlookers dismiss the antics as classic new-money theater or aggressive marketing for online trading mentorships, critics have condemned the trend as unnecessary waste and clout-chasing, with several online commentators branding the behavior "village mentality."

### Accountability in the Attention Economy

By attaching a specific vehicle target to his challenge, Terence Creative shifted the narrative from moral outrage to a direct test of actual financial capacity. The challenge puts pressure on the traders to prove that the ostentatious wealth displayed in VIP lounges reflects real capital rather than short-term stunt budgets.

The ongoing debate has also dragged in other prominent digital figures, including social media creator Thee Pluto, as audiences debate the line between personal financial freedom and public displays of excess.

Whether any of the high-rolling traders will take up Terence’s 100-bus challenge remains to be seen. For now, the public spotlight remains firmly fixed on whether the nightlife big spenders will respond with real social investments or simply order another round of spirits at the bar.
