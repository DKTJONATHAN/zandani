Nairobi Woman Representative Esther Passaris has used an appearance on content creator Elijah’s *Young Millionaire Talks* to map out the financial trajectory of her early corporate career, contrasting her rapid earnings at age 24 with a warning to young Kenyans who view political office as a shortcut to personal wealth.

Recounting her entry into commercial enterprise, the 62-year-old lawmaker disclosed that she hit her first KSh 1 million milestone at 24. Passaris attributed that early financial success to an aggressive stint in corporate sales and production, driven by a desire to travel globally and a strong aptitude for high-stakes pitching.

### Corporate Hustle and Sales Ethics

Describing her early confidence in marketing, Passaris recalled how her persuasion skills were once sharp enough to "sell ice to the Eskimos and oil to the Arabs." However, she cautioned young entrepreneurs against confusing high-energy pitching with deceptive tactics, insisting that long-term commercial success relies on genuine product value.

"If you don’t believe in a product, you can’t sell it," Passaris advised during the conversation, emphasizing that sellers owe it to their clients to deliver genuine value for their money.

### Modesty in Office and the 2017 Car

Transitioning from private enterprise to public administration, the lawmaker drew a firm boundary between business ambition and elective leadership. She warned young individuals eyeing public office against viewing elective positions as instruments for wealth accumulation.

"Don’t go into politics because you want to make money. Go into politics because you want to serve," she stated.

To underline her stance on financial discipline within public life, Passaris revealed a personal detail about her everyday routine: she still drives the same vehicle she purchased back in 2017, rejecting the frequent lifestyle upgrades often associated with parliamentary office.

### Aging and Spiritual Grounding

Beyond business dynamics and asset choices, Passaris addressed public interest surrounding her physical appearance at 62. Bypassing talk of complex cosmetic regimens, she attributed her mental and physical well-being to her spiritual practices and daily discipline.

She described her wellness routine as rooted in "loving God and understanding that there’s a superior being that created us and is worthy of respect," adding that she approaches her daily routine by counting her blessings and maintaining a foundation of personal gratitude.

Her reflections on the podcast provide a rare glimpse into how one of Nairobi's most visible politicians balances her legacy as an early corporate high-earner with her current public posture on political modesty.
