Rail development along Kenya's southern frontier is proceeding on two separate technical tracks, even as state engineers press ahead with the long-delayed restoration of colonial-era steel.

Kenya Railways confirmed on Tuesday that rehabilitation of the 120-kilometre Voi-Taveta metre-gauge railway (MGR) has reached 40 per cent completion. Managing Director Phillip Mainga stated that the Ksh5 billion project is advancing through critical subgrade preparations and cross-drainage construction along the dormant corridor, which has sat idle for over two decades.

![Kenya Railways Managing Director Philip Mainga inspecting the Voi-Taveta MGR rehabilitation project on October 13, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/ds0SB5cv/8d5f306bf4cb.webp)

The steady advance of the MGR works, however, intersects with fresh state commitments for the same economic artery. Just days prior to the 40 per cent milestone update, President William Ruto announced plans to construct a separate Standard Gauge Railway (SGR) line along the exact same route, earmarking stations for Mwatate, Bura, Mwakitau, and Taveta.

The concurrent pursuit of both a rehabilitated metre-gauge line and a heavy-gauge SGR extension creates an overlapping infrastructure footprint linking Kenya's coastal interior directly to Tanzania. While the MGR relies on upgrading existing earthworks and installing lighter tracks to jumpstart immediate freight and passenger links, the planned SGR layer points to a longer-term heavy logistics overhaul.

A central anchor for both transport modes is the planned development of a dry port in Voi. The logistics facility is engineered to handle incoming and outgoing cargo, transforming the inland town into a primary customs and transit hub connecting the port of Mombasa with East African Community trade corridors.

![A worker at the Voi-Taveta Railway project on October 13, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/PzbbdVtJ/3ba54ed52b66.webp)

Local economic activity has seen an immediate uptick from the MGR civil works, with construction teams opening up employment for nearby communities and driving demand for local building supplies. However, the ultimate economic viability of running parallel narrow- and standard-gauge networks over the same 120-kilometre stretch remains to be tested once heavy freight begins moving toward the Tanzanian border.

Kenya Railways has not yet released a definitive commissioning date for the completed MGR line, though progress currently hinges on finishing the foundational subgrade and drainage networks before final track-laying accelerates.
