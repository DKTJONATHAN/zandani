Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna’s Friday tour of Trans Nzoia County was built like a slow-burning political thriller that refused to wind down quietly.

By the time the National Police Service released an official communique on Friday, October 16, warning that no elected leader would hide behind rank or party colors, Kitale had already witnessed a full spectrum of political theatrics: a muzzled funeral podium, a street-level standoff between rival motorcades, and a nightfall rally that unraveled in a cloud of police teargas.

The day’s friction started far away from Kitale’s commercial avenues, in the solemn setting of a funeral service for Senior Assistant Inspector General of Police Lazarus Simiyu Opicho. Sifuna, accompanied by Trans Nzoia Governor George Natembeya, arrived to pay his respects alongside local leaders. But what was meant to be a dignified send-off for a senior law enforcement officer quickly turned into an administrative standoff.

Tensions flared across the seating gallery after earlier political remarks provoked an intervention by on-site security personnel. When the program moved forward, security commanders took no chances with the microphone. Sifuna was explicitly denied an opportunity to address the mourners, a public slight that visibly agitated the opposition camp.

Rather than retreating to the capital, Sifuna and Natembeya regrouped and took their political grievance straight into Kitale town.

### The Convoys Intersect

The timing could hardly have been more combustible. President William Ruto was already in Trans Nzoia concluding an official development tour, sweeping through the town with his full executive motorcade and a heavy deployment of protective officers.

As the presidential convoy maneuvered to exit the town, Sifuna’s entourage—aligned with his Linda Mwananchi platform—surged into the same urban corridor. Videos captured by bystanders showed the two massive political formations encountering each other along the town's choked transit routes.

The proximity immediately transformed the street into an arena. Pockets of rival youth aligned with the ruling United Democratic Alliance (UDA) and Sifuna’s faction squared off. As the presidential motorcade pressed through the corridors, segments of the crowd began chanting "one term," escalating an already volatile public encounter into an open contest of volume and presence.

### Speaking in the Dark

The street friction did not abate when the presidential security detail cleared the town center. Instead, Sifuna and Governor Natembeya doubled down, gathering crowds in Kitale town as dusk turned into nightfall.

Determined to deliver the speech denied to him earlier in the afternoon, Sifuna mounted an impromptu address under the cover of darkness. The assembly, however, drew immediate pushback from regular and riot police tracking the swelling crowd. Within minutes, law enforcement moved in with teargas canisters, sending supporters scattering into side alleys and cutting the nocturnal rally short.

By then, the damage had moved beyond hurt pride and political barbs.

### The NPS Files an Audit of the Chaos

The National Police Service confirmed that the skirmishes left a trail of physical destruction. Two police officers sustained injuries while attempting to restore order during the confrontations, and two government vehicles were significantly damaged.

In a Friday statement condemning the defiance of the Public Order Act, police headquarters avoided naming Sifuna or the local organizers directly, but signaled that the velvet gloves were off.

“Preliminary reports indicate that the individuals concerned engaged in actions that undermined public order and the rule of law, thereby endangering the safety and security of the public,” the NPS stated.

The service added that formal investigations are actively underway to pinpoint which individuals orchestrated the disorder, declaring that "no individual would be exempt from the law because of their political position, social status or party affiliation."

For Sifuna, the Kitale expedition delivered undeniable viral visibility, but it has also landed his inner circle squarely in the crosshairs of state investigators seeking accountability for two battered vehicles and two hospital-bound officers.
