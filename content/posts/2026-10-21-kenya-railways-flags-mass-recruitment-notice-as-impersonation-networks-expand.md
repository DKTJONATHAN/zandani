A wave of online impersonation targeting Kenya Railways has shifted from passenger ticketing fraud to predatory employment schemes, prompting the state corporation to formally disown a viral notice advertising hundreds of non-existent public sector jobs.

The alert, published on Wednesday, October 21, cautions that syndicates are circulating fabricated employment notices designed to extract cash payments and harvest sensitive personal data from job seekers across the country.

### Manufactured Vacancies Across Multiple Cadres

The fraudulent circulation, circulated under the heading "Job Opportunity 2026," purported to open recruitment across administrative, operational, and commercial departments within the rail agency. 

![An undated image of the boarding area at Voi SGR station.](https://i.ibb.co/v42tMPwR/21ed4b86e9ff.webp)

Among the fictitious openings were roles for sales and marketing managers, passenger services managers, data entry clerks, drivers, office assistants, and mechanical technicians. To create an impression of large-scale intake, the scammers claimed individual cadres had expansive vacancies, with certain entry-level positions advertising upwards of 70 available slots.

Kenya Railways moved to invalidate the document in its entirety, warning that none of the listed vacancies originated from its human resources division.

"We wish to alert members of the public about a fake job advertisement doing rounds on various social media platforms," the corporation stated. "We strongly advise job seekers to be cautious and verify any job adverts through our website."

### Escalation From Booking Portal Scams

![Trains Railway](https://i.ibb.co/vxCdj7KH/1a81f3b52ca6.webp)

The emergence of the fake recruitment drive underscores an expanding digital footprint for cyber rings impersonating the national rail operator. 

In July, the agency issued an urgent public advisory after rogue actors established duplicate websites and social media handles posing as official booking portals for the Madaraka Express. Unsuspecting commuters who attempted to secure passenger seats through the counterfeit domains suffered direct financial losses through rerouted payment channels.

While the mid-year scheme focused on skimming transit fares from daily commuters, the October recruitment notice reflects a broader pivot toward exploiting youth unemployment through credential harvesting and application processing fees.

Kenya Railways maintains that formal vacancy declarations, submission instructions, and shortlist releases are strictly restricted to its official institutional web domain, rather than unverified digital fliers.

Whether security agencies—tasked in July with investigating the network of counterfeit ticketing platforms—have traced the digital infrastructure underpinning these coordinated rail scams remains an unanswered operational question.
