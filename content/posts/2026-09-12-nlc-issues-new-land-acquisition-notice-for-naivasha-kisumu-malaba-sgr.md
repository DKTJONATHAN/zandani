**NAIROBI** - Kenya’s land regulator has launched a fresh wave of compulsory land acquisitions, targeting more than 300 parcels along the western corridor to clear the path for the Ksh700 billion Naivasha-Kisumu-Malaba Standard Gauge Railway extension.

### What we know

* Kenya Railways is accelerating corridor clearance across Nyanza to meet its ambitious July 2027 completion target for the Kisumu route.
* The inclusion of contested ancestral plots highlights unresolved ownership battles that threaten to draw the state into prolonged court fights.
* Swelling acquisition lists in Kisumu Municipality reveal an expanding footprint where high-density plot displacement will drive up compensation costs.
* State land officials are pressing forward with compulsory takeovers even as succession gaps and encumbered titles complicate legitimate payouts.

The National Land Commission (NLC) published the notice of intention to acquire the land on behalf of the Kenya Railways Corporation, revising previous schedules with new additions, deletions, and spatial corrections along the planned 370-kilometre rail line.

The new schedule heavily impacts Kisumu County, taking in extensive rural tracks in Chiga and Sidho West alongside numerous small urban plots in Kisumu Municipality measuring approximately 0.0206 hectares each.

According to the gazetted document, the targeted properties represent a legal minefield. The schedule covers private land, unregistered plots, properties registered under deceased persons, and parcels weighed down by active financial cautions and bank charges.

Among the high-stakes inclusions is a 4.9252-hectare parcel that triggered a high-profile 2021 court battles between Kenya Railways and members of the Nubian community claiming historical ownership rights.

NLC instructed affected property owners to review the survey maps ahead of formal public inquiries into compensation claims.

“Interested persons should inspect plans of the affected land at the offices of the Commission at Ardhi House 3rd Floor, Room 305, 1st Ngong Avenue, Nairobi and County Co-ordinator’s Offices in Kisumu County during official working hours,” the notice stated.

The gazette notice follows an announcement by Kenya Railways that valuation and corridor inspection teams had been deployed to secure the right-of-way. Mainline track construction began in July 2026, with state engineers working under a tight timeline to bring operational freight and passenger service to the port city of Kisumu by July 2027.

While the state is eager to demonstrate momentum on its flagship infrastructure project, pushing steel rails across encumbered estates and disputed community land without resolving underlying tenure grievances risks transferring the battlefield from the field into slow-moving courtroom corridors.
