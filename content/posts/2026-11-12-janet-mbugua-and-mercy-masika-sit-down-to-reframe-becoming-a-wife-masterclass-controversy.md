Janet Mbugua and Mercy Masika have taken their high-profile debate over marital roles from social media comment sections onto a television studio set, sharing a joint sit-down weeks after a public exchange regarding Masika’s upcoming *Becoming a Wife* masterclass.

The media personality and the gospel artist published a joint Instagram update featuring the two seated opposite one another on set, confirming they had recorded a direct conversation to address their contrasting perspectives on marriage ministry, domestic safety, and gender responsibilities.

Rather than extending the initial public friction, the joint post outlined a shared position focused on safety within domestic relationships and family structures.

"Marriage ministry isn’t new," the joint caption noted, acknowledging the long-standing prevalence of religious marriage coaching in Kenya. "Making sure every marriage it reaches is safe and supported is what matters now."

The two creators further noted that public figures can hold differing opinions on how marital support is structured while remaining aligned on the broader goal of protecting and supporting families. Neither Mbugua nor Masika disclosed the exact filming location or announced a scheduled release date for the recorded sit-down.

The studio meeting comes after weeks of intense online discussion sparked by Mbugua’s public pushback against Masika’s planned program. When the gospel singer initially announced the course, which was crafted specifically for women preparing for marriage, Mbugua openly challenged the focus of the initiative.

Mbugua argued that religious leaders, faith-based platforms, and influential creators should actively leverage their reach to confront pressing social issues, including systemic concerns over femicide, sexual abuse, and gender-based violence. She specifically questioned the ongoing trend of organizing masterclasses that exclusively coach women on how to become better wives, while comparable efforts and accountability measures directed at men remain largely absent.

In her initial critique, Mbugua maintained that marriage requires equal participation from two partners, emphasizing that women should not be expected to shoulder the entire burden of sustaining a relationship or ensuring its success.

Her comments resonated across Kenya’s digital space and drew explicit backing from several prominent figures, including actress Kate Actress and content creator Raych Gitau, who echoed concerns regarding unequal expectations in domestic coaching.

At the same time, Masika’s project garnered strong support from other notable women in the media landscape. Personalities such as Lillian Ng’ang’a and Lynn Ngugi publicly defended the initiative, arguing that adult women should have the autonomy to seek personal growth and marital guidance if they choose. Religious leaders also weighed in, with Lady Bishop Kathy Kiuna publicly encouraging the gospel singer to proceed with the program as planned despite the surrounding controversy.

By stepping onto the same studio set, Mbugua and Masika have shifted their dialogue from distant social media commentary into a structured exchange. With Masika’s seven-session *Becoming a Wife* masterclass scheduled to run from November 21 to December 12, 2026, public attention now turns to when their recorded studio session will be broadcast.
