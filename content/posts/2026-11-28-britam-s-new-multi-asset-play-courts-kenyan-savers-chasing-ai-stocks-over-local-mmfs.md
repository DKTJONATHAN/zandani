For years, the local money market fund was the undisputed default for any Kenyan trying to keep cash ahead of inflation without locking horns with traditional banking fees. But that familiar financial routine is undergoing a rapid reality check as urban savers look past standard fixed-income accounts.

That shifting appetite is writ large in the latest figures from the Capital Markets Authority. By June 2026, assets under management across Kenya’s collective investment schemes climbed to Sh948.7 billion—nearly nine times where the industry sat less than six years ago. Even more striking is how retail cash is moving. While money market funds commanded over 90% of the market in the mid-2010s, they now represent less than half of total assets. Special Funds have surged to capture Sh252.8 billion, accounting for 27% of the industry and establishing themselves as the second-largest category.

Capitalizing on this hunger for alternative exposure, Britam rolled out its Multi-Asset Special Fund to capture investors looking beyond domestic borders. Regulated by the CMA as a Special Fund under the Collective Investment Schemes Regulations, 2023, the Kenya shilling-denominated vehicle is built to bundle local bonds, international equities, commodities, and currency strategies into a single portfolio designed for a three-to-five-year horizon.

Behind the product launch lies a striking divergence in asset performance that has caught the eye of yield-hungry investors. While the S&P Kenya Sovereign Bond Index climbed to nearly 3.5 times its initial value in US dollar terms between January 2010 and August 2026, international technology stocks blew past domestic benchmarks. In dollar terms, tech investments outperformed Kenyan bonds by 5.2 times, while dollar-to-shilling conversions showed technology posting a staggering 31-fold return compared to 6.6 times for local bonds.

Britam’s broader strategy leans heavily into this digital pivot, backed by internal data citing a 97.5% AI adoption rate across Kenya in a comparative study of 54 countries—placing the local market well ahead of the global average of 81.2%. Through its Enhanced Global Equity Fund, the firm is steering local capital directly into artificial intelligence, semiconductors, and digital infrastructure.

Yet, financial analysts point out that expanding into global equities and alternative assets like gold and commodities introduces entirely new layers of risk, from currency volatility to sharper price swings. With Standard Chartered Bank Kenya acting as trustee and custodian, the fund relies on active management and foreign exchange strategies to buffer against potential shilling depreciation as economic indicators, including headline inflation at 6.6% in the third quarter of 2026, continue to test consumer portfolios.
