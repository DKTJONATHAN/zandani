Kenya’s push to mandate local mineral processing is colliding directly with an intensifying international scramble for industrial deposits, even as Beijing cautions against turning African resources into diplomatic proxy battlegrounds.

The domestic policy objective—designed to secure technology transfer, industrial expansion, and local employment rather than exporting raw unprocessed ore—faces its most acute test at Mrima Hill in Kwale County.

![A photo collage of President Donald Trump (US) and Xi Jinping (China) with Mrima Hills in Kwale in the background, October 26, 2025.](https://i.ibb.co/WNCJf9RZ/32517e71137a.webp)

Six separate corporate entities from the United States, Australia, and China are currently locked in competition for the rights to develop the site's deposits. The ground contains significant reserves of niobium, utilized in high-strength specialty steel, and rare earth elements essential for permanent magnets in electric motors, electronics, and defense technologies.

The high-stakes bidding coincides with a broader diplomatic push by Washington to establish critical-mineral supply chains outside of China's processing ecosystem. Mining Cabinet Secretary Hassan Joho has previously confirmed that Nairobi's ongoing discussions involve multiple U.S. departments, including Commerce, State, and Defence, as the state seeks lucrative partnerships without forfeiting resource ownership.

Against this backdrop, Chinese President Xi Jinping outlined a four-point framework for continent-wide mining cooperation on Tuesday, October 13. Delivered via Ministry of Foreign Affairs spokesperson Mao Ning and state-run *Xinhua News Agency*, the framework insists that African mineral resources must belong to Africa, promotes mutual economic benefits, advocates high-quality development, and calls for a fair, open investment environment.

![A collage of US President Donald Trump (left) and President Willian Ruto (right), with a hand holding rare-earth minerals in the middle.](https://i.ibb.co/nqrdyRdp/2dd8d37af010.webp)

“Africa’s resources should not become tools of geopolitical struggle,” Xi stated, noting Beijing's willingness to maintain predictable frameworks for resource development.

While the four-point stance outwardly mirrors Kenya’s domestic ambitions to retain economic value locally rather than shipping raw material abroad, it underscores the delicate diplomatic tightrope Nairobi must walk. Juggling concurrent overtures from Washington and Beijing while vetting competing multinational bidders will test the administrative and regulatory capacity of the Ministry of Mining as the Mrima Hill procurement moves toward a decision.
