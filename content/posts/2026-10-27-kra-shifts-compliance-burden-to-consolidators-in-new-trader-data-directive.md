Cargo consolidators operating at Kenyan ports are facing a major administrative overhaul after the Kenya Revenue Authority mandated them to capture and submit granular tax and personal details for every individual trader using their services.

Under the new directive issued on Tuesday, October 27, 2026, intermediaries handling shared shipments can no longer process consignments under blanket entries. Instead, consolidators must furnish the taxman with the full names, KRA Personal Identification Numbers (PINs), national identity card numbers, and active telephone numbers of every underlying importer.

![An Image of a cargo clearance officer supervising clearance at Mombasa port](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/an_image_of_a_cargo_clearance_officer_supervising_clearance_at_mombasa_port_.jpg.webp?itok=EDTRlkAK)

Traders will also be required to provide precise descriptions and financial values for individual items inside shared containers. According to the tax authority, the collected data is designed to feed into the Electronic Tax Invoice Management System (eTIMS), enabling eligible value-added tax (VAT)-registered traders to claim input VAT on imported goods.

The directive represents a sharp pivot in how the state monitors informal and small-scale commercial importing, turning logistics intermediaries into front-line tax compliance officers. For years, consolidated cargo allowed smaller traders to pool freight volumes and share clearance costs without direct individual engagement with revenue systems. Under the new framework, that institutional anonymity is removed.

The data requirement does not stand alone. It arrives on the heels of parallel regulatory tightening by the tax authority. In September, KRA rolled out the Ksh2 million cargo clearance threshold directed by President William Ruto, forcing closer scrutiny of mixed-consignment values. That move is set to be compounded on November 1 by the mandatory enforcement of Digital Marine Cargo Insurance (MCI), which requires all importers and clearing agents to secure underwritten policies from insurers licensed by the Insurance Regulatory Authority (IRA).

![cargo ship mombasa kpa](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/cargo-ship-kpa.jpg.webp?itok=aigQpRf8)

For consolidators and clearing agents, the cumulative effect is a compressed compliance window. Verifying individual PINs, matching mobile numbers, and detailing descriptions for dozens of fragmented parcels per container introduces significant processing drag at customs points.

KRA maintains that the measures are tailored to level the playing field, eliminate tax evasion loopholes, and inject transparency into multi-trader shipments. However, the operational reality of enforcing real-time eTIMS integration across thousands of small-scale informal traders remains squarely in the hands of the consolidators tasked with gathering the data.
