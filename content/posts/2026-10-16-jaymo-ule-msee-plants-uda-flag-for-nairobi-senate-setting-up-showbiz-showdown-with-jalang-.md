Digital creator Wilson Muirani Gathoni, widely known across Kenyan social feeds as Jaymo Ule Msee, has made his political ambitions unmistakable, rolling out official campaign branding for Nairobi County’s 2027 Senate race under the ruling United Democratic Alliance (UDA).

Taking to his social media platforms on Thursday, October 15, 2026, Muirani published a campaign poster rendered in UDA’s signature yellow and green palette. The poster features Muirani positioned directly beside President William Ruto, stamped with an unambiguous voter appeal: “Vote Jaymo Ule Msee for Senator Nairobi County 2027.”

The public rollout moves Muirani from speculative commentary straight into party-branded alignment, marking a deliberate escalation in his transition from viral sketch comedy into countywide electoral politics.

### The Creative-to-Parliament Pipeline

Muirani’s formal entry sets up a collision course with fellow entertainment veteran Phelix Odiwuor, popularly known as Jalang’o. The Lang’ata Member of Parliament, who successfully leveraged mainstream morning radio and digital interview platforms to capture his parliamentary seat in 2022 on an Orange Democratic Movement (ODM) ticket, wasted no time asserting his own countywide claim.

Reacting to the emerging field, Jalang’o took to X (formerly Twitter) to confirm that his sights remain locked on the broader Nairobi legislative seat.

“To the wire – I am ready!” Jalang’o posted, telegraphing that he does not intend to cede space to newer crossover aspirants or party rivals without a full contest.

The standoff between Muirani and Jalang’o brings two distinct branches of Kenya’s online and broadcast entertainment ecosystem into direct political rivalry. Where Jalang’o pioneered commercial morning radio hosting before shifting into grassroots constituency logistics, Muirani built a dedicated digital following through character-driven short-form video before stepping into political communications.

### Winnie Odinga’s Youth Counterweight

The race is complicated further by East African Legislative Assembly (EALA) MP Winnie Odinga, who used a major platform in Bondo, Siaya County, during the first anniversary commemoration of her late father, former Prime Minister Raila Odinga, to declare her own transfer to city politics.

Odinga framed her entry as a direct response to incumbent Senator Edwin Sifuna’s apparent decision to step away from the county legislature to pursue higher national office.

“Let it not be a rumour that as Edwin Sifuna searches for higher offices, I see kuna nafasi hapo Nairobi,” Odinga announced in Bondo. “That is why I have decided that I will stand for the senator of Nairobi County.”

Significantly, Odinga pitched her candidacy as an umbrella vehicle for younger entrants, adding: “Lakini siendi peke yangu, I am going with young people.”

That framing puts Odinga, Muirani, and Jalang’o in direct competition for the exact demographic each has historically engaged through social feeds, media broadcasts, and party youth wings.

### The Vacated Seat Dynamic

The sudden clustering of high-profile personalities around the Nairobi Senate seat stems from the vacuum left by Edwin Sifuna. Sifuna’s growing focus on national platform-building has signaled to rivals and allies alike that the capital's senatorial position will be an open seat come 2027.

With Muirani firmly securing his UDA colors, Jalang’o signaling an uncompromising campaign, and Odinga mobilizing a youth-centric county bid, Nairobi’s legislative contest is shaping up not as a quiet backroom negotiation, but as an aggressive public audition where digital visibility and street-level organizing will be tested side by side.
