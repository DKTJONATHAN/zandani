Threats to zone Nandi County against opposition sympathisers collapsed into a public retraction on Saturday, after Kemeloi-Maraba MCA Turfosa Kuto faced immediate peer revolt and an official summons from the National Cohesion and Integration Commission (NCIC).

Kuto, elected on a United Democratic Alliance (UDA) ticket, found herself politically isolated after a video clip recorded at a local funeral showed her warning residents that backing Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna carried the risk of forced eviction across county lines.

"Here in Nandi, there is no saying Sifuna," Kuto had told mourners. "If you say Sifuna, we will take you back to Hamisi. Are we together?"

The explicit threat to expel supporters to Hamisi—the neighbouring constituency across the border in Vihiga County—sparked swift condemnation from her colleagues in the Nandi County Assembly, who moved quickly to distance the devolved legislature from unlawful profiling.

### Assembly Peers Draw the Line

![Kemeloi-Maraba MCA Turfosa Kuto](https://i.ibb.co/KxFX7cyH/7b96a540cd41.webp)

Rather than rallying around their party colleague, a section of Nandi county legislators publicly denounced the remarks as an unconstitutional breach of basic political freedoms. 

The ward representatives warned that targeting individuals based on their political preferences risked destabilising inter-communal coexistence in a county that borders Western Kenya and hosts diverse populations.

"In Nandi, we have the face of Kenya, where every community lives in peace," the legislators said in a joint statement demanding an immediate retraction. "She needs to apologise; if she fails to do so, we will seek the law to take charge."

Faced with the threat of formal assembly censure and direct legal complaints, Kuto addressed the media on Saturday, November 7, to plead that her comments had been taken out of context.

"When I spoke at that funeral, I did not think it would hurt other people," Kuto stated. "For those I have wronged, I urge you to forgive me. Let us continue living together as the people of Nandi and Vihiga as we have been living."

The ward representative insisted that her statement was not designed to sow hostility along ethnic or geographical boundaries, urging that competitive politics remain civil.

![UDA delegates attending the National Governing Council (NGC) meeting at State House, Nairobi, on January 26, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/VWGGJ7tw/38fea42dd166.webp)

"My intention was never to promote division, hatred or violence between any communities," she said. "Our political differences should be pursued through peaceful, democratic and lawful means, without undermining the harmony that has long existed among our people."

### Cohesion Agency Steps In

The apology, however, came after the NCIC had already stepped in. The statutory cohesion monitor issued formal summons requiring the MCA to appear and account for the viral remarks.

The regulatory scrutiny lands at a sensitive moment for local politicians, with less than 11 months remaining before the August 2027 General Election. The watchdog has tightened surveillance on public rallies, where funeral addresses and local gatherings frequently serve as informal campaign platforms.

Sifuna, who serves as the secretary-general of the Orange Democratic Movement (ODM) and leads the Linda Mwananchi civic initiative, has maintained a high-visibility profile across the country, making political posturing in rural ward politics increasingly combustible.

Kuto's upcoming appearance before the cohesion commission will test whether a public walk-back shields elected officials from formal prosecution when rhetoric crosses into territorial profiling.
