Salaried graduates could see smaller deductions chipped from their monthly pay slips if lawmakers adopt stakeholder recommendations to lower the statutory ceiling on higher education loan repayments.

Meeting in Nairobi on Tuesday, October 27, the National Assembly Committee on Education commenced public hearings on a raft of legislative proposals governing post-secondary education. Among the most closely watched adjustments is a push to lower the maximum recovery rate from 25 per cent of a loanee's basic salary down to 15 per cent.

![University students accessing HELB services.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/helb_loan.jpg.webp?itok=Hia8gjyR)

Under the current statutory framework, employers are legally required to deduct up to a quarter of an employee's earnings through the check-off system and remit the funds directly to the Higher Education Loans Board (HELB). Clause 49(4) of the pending Tertiary Education, Placement and Funding Bill, 2026, retains that 25 per cent threshold, designating the incoming Tertiary Education Funding Authority (TEFA) as the successor entity tasked with recovering the funds once HELB is replaced.

Lobby group Mizizi Elimu Africa told the committee that trimming the ceiling to 15 per cent would ease financial pressure on young professionals facing heavy tax obligations while simultaneously encouraging voluntary compliance among borrowers.

The legislative review extends beyond loan recoveries. The Elimu Bora Working Group pressed lawmakers to allocate 2.5 per cent of Kenya Revenue Authority tax collections directly to the education fund, advocating for a hybrid model that introduces direct grants to complement repayable student loans. Separately, the Elimu Yetu Coalition called for formal recognition of refugee teacher qualifications to integrate them into the national labor pool.

![HELB offices in Nairobi](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/helb_2.jpg.webp?itok=8rQAw6xK)

On institutional administration, the Kenya Catholic Secretariat urged the committee to preserve disciplinary mechanisms such as suspensions and expulsions while upholding the absolute ban on corporal punishment. Meanwhile, World Vision Kenya recommended that meals for learners in private schools be provided free and free of separate levies charged to parents.

The parliamentary committee is compiling submissions from the Nairobi hearings alongside public participation forums conducted across counties. The panel will factor the divergent proposals into its final report on the seven education bills before presenting the legislative package to the full House.
