Kevin Kioko, the musician widely known as Bahati, has laid out the strict recovery rule governing his decade-long marriage to Diana Marua: when heated arguments spill over into packed suitcases and sudden exits, his personal protocol demands an immediate, same-day intervention.

Speaking during a sit-down interview with media personality Oga Obinna on Monday, November 9, 2026, the artist spoke openly about how the couple manages friction inside their high-profile union. Far from describing a seamless lifestyle, Bahati revealed that domestic walkouts remain a periodic reality in their home—including an incident earlier in 2026.

When Obinna pressed him directly on whether Diana had ever gathered her bags and left after a severe disagreement, Bahati did not sidestep the question.

“Mara ngapi. Hata this year,” Bahati confirmed, making clear that packed-bag departures have taken place repeatedly over their decade together. “Let me tell you, of course fighting is part of the game. And part of the journey. Usidanganywe na mtu. Watu hupigana, watu uhama. But you choose to stay together.”

For Bahati, however, a spouse walking out of the house is not an invitation to cool off separately or spend days in silence. His personal marital standard rests on a strict rule against overnight separation, placing the burden of immediate resolution directly on himself the moment a dispute turns physical.

“Akienda namufuata,” Bahati emphasized during the sit-down. “Bibi kwanza hafai kulala nje. Umeharibu. Unamfuata. Kabisa, yaani unafuata.”

Under Bahati’s framework, letting a wife spend the night away from the matrimonial home after a fight represents a fundamental failure of marital responsibility. Rather than waiting for emotions to settle over several days, he treats the physical departure as an urgent breakdown that must be repaired before nightfall, insisting on following her immediately to ensure she returns home that same day.

Beyond the mechanics of the chase, Bahati used the interview to reframe relationship friction as a mandatory metric of authenticity. Rather than viewing periodic arguments as warning signs of a fragile union, he argued that a marriage entirely free of confrontation usually indicates hidden dishonesty or mutual disengagement.

“Actually, when you guys don’t fight, there is something wrong,” Bahati told Obinna. “Someone is lying to the other. You have to disagree on a few things.”

The disclosure comes at a significant milestone for the pair. Having recently celebrated both their 10th wedding anniversary and Diana’s 38th birthday, the couple has built one of Kenya’s most visible celebrity brand ecosystems around their family life. Yet Bahati’s comments strip away the polished online presentation to reveal a dynamic anchored in intense disagreements and hands-on reconciliation.

By treating suitcase-packing walkouts as a predictable feature of long-term partnership rather than a prelude to divorce, Bahati’s interview underscores a commitment to active conflict management. For the singer, surviving ten years in the public eye has depended less on avoiding heated disputes and more on enforcing a rigid rule: never let the sun set on an empty room.
