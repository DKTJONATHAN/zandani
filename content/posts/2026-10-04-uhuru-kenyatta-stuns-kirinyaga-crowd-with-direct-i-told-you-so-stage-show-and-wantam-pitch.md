Former President Uhuru Kenyatta stepped back into the public spotlight in Kirinyaga County on Saturday, October 3, 2026, turning a regional gathering into a fiery, highly interactive stage show where he delivered a blunt "I told you so" to Mt Kenya voters over their 2022 election choices.

Rather than delivering a scripted policy address, Kenyatta leaned into direct crowd engagement, taking aim at the regional fallout from the last presidential contest and openly backing the opposition’s single-term "WANTAM" campaign aimed at President William Ruto ahead of 2027.

Diving straight into his pre-2022 warnings, Kenyatta reminded the Kirinyaga audience that he had explicitly cautioned them against the political path they chose when they rejected his preferred Azimio la Umoja ticket. He pointed out that the economic and political consequences of that decision now rest squarely on the shoulders of those who ignored his counsel.

"I am happy, even after you people refused to listen to me. That is upon you," Kenyatta told the gathering, reflecting on the 2022 race where he campaigned for the late Raila Odinga and Gichugu native Martha Karua. "When I told you, you were making the wrong decision, you all thought you knew better. I knew that if the Old Man, late Raila Odinga, and your daughter, Martha Karua, united, Kenya would have made great progress."

The defining moment of Kenyatta’s appearance came when he paused his speech to conduct an impromptu poll, challenging the audience to publicly acknowledge whether they regretted their 2022 ballot markers. Demanding a physical show of hands, he put the crowd on the spot regarding the upcoming 2027 contest.

"A mistake is not a mistake unless it is repeated. Kirinyaga residents, will you make the same mistake? Kirinyaga residents, will you repeat the mistake?" Kenyatta asked, watching hands rise across the venue. "Let me see by a show of hands, so that they don’t accuse me of saying this. Sasa sijui ni makosa gani mlifanya, lakini kama nyinyi wenyewe mnajua, sasa si ni shauri yenu, ama nimesema aje?"

The theatrical exchange highlighted Kenyatta's readiness to re-enter active political conversation in his home region. His endorsement of the "WANTAM" movement—a campaign slogan calling for President Ruto to serve only a single presidential term—marks his most explicit campaign alignment since stepping down from office.

The confrontation reopens a long-standing political drama between Kenyatta and Ruto. After serving two terms together as president and deputy president under the Jubilee banner, the two leaders experienced a bitter fallout that saw Kenyatta bypass his own deputy to endorse Odinga and Karua in 2022. Ruto ultimately won the tightly contested poll, sweeping a significant portion of the Mt Kenya voting block that Kenyatta once anchored.

Kenyatta's Saturday appearance in Kirinyaga signals that he has no intention of remaining a quiet retired statesman as the region scrambles to realign ahead of 2027. By forcing Kirinyaga residents to answer directly for their past vote in public, Kenyatta signaled that Mt Kenya's political stage remains very much open for his commentary.
