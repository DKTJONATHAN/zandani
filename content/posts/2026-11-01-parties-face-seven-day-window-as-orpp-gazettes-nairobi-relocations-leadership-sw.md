A strict statutory clock is ticking for four Kenyan political outfits after the Office of the Registrar of Political Parties (ORPP) gazetted sweeping structural overhauls that reach well beyond routine paperwork. 

The formal notifications published on Friday, October 30, set off a seven-day public review window during which any voter or stakeholder can contest proposed constitutional amendments, revamped executive rosters, revised ballot symbols, and the physical relocation of national headquarters across Nairobi.

The filings capture a deliberate effort by mid-tier and fringe outfits to shake off dormant institutional postures. Rather than maintaining static registers, the parties are actively retooling their visual branding and operational infrastructure ahead of pre-coalition bargaining.

### Migrating Nairobi Footprints

Among the most practical shifts detailed in the Gazette notices is the relocation of physical nerve centres away from older commercial hubs toward distinct suburban and diplomatic nodes across the capital.

The Party for Peace and Development (PPD) is vacating its long-standing operational base at Muthaiga Flat along Thika Road. In its filing, the party announced plans to establish its national headquarters at House No. 54 along Mukima Drive in Roasters, nestled just off the same transport corridor.

![Registrar of Political Parties John Lorionokou in an engagement.](https://i.ibb.co/1YzZbq5S/87bb6fa54a95.webp)

Further west, Usawa Kwa Wote Party (USAWA) has resolved to abandon its Muthaiga address entirely. The outfit has applied to anchor its operations along Muiri Lane in Karen, situating its national seat directly opposite the main gates of the Bomas of Kenya—a geographic corridor steeped in national political assemblies.

The Democratic Union (TDU) is charting a similar northward pivot. The party plans to vacate Victoria Plaza along Parklands Road, seeking regulatory sign-off to move its national headquarters to Whispers Avenue in Gigiri, near Nairobi’s diplomatic hub.

### Overhauling Visual Identifiers

Parallel to these logistical migrations is an aggressive scrub of ballot symbols and official colours, addressing the visual marks that define party identity on election materials.

PPD is seeking regulatory clearance to abandon its traditional triangle emblem in favour of a jug. Along with the symbol, the party is casting aside its four-colour scheme of red, white, green, and black to adopt an orange, sky blue, and white palette.

TDU has similarly applied to retire both its house icon and its founding slogan, *Mwanzo Mpya*. The outfit intends to contest upcoming elections under a unity kite insignia, backed by the motto *‘Our People, Our Purpose, Our Power’*. Its colour registry is slated to transform from the conventional red, blue, and white blend into a darker configuration of red, dark brown, and green.

These visual overhauls reflect an acute awareness among party strategists that ballot iconography remains one of the primary mechanisms for voter differentiation once field alliances begin taking shape.

### Executive Reshuffles and Statutory Deadlines

The gazetted updates also introduce notable changes to national leadership structures, most visibly inside the Movement for Democracy and Growth (MDG), which sits within the Azimio La Umoja Coalition framework.

MDG notified the registrar of plans to adopt constitutional amendments alongside a refreshed slate of eight national officeholders. The roster places Monica Apio Mungála as National Chairperson, Hillary Adolwa as Deputy National Chairperson, and Julius Ouma Jwan in the strategic post of Secretary General.

PPD submitted a parallel reorganisation across its operational hierarchy. The party proposed Phylis Kambita to head its National Women’s League, succeeding Florence Wangui. Judith Kwamboka is designated as Deputy National Chairperson in charge of Operations, stepping into the position previously held by Faith Mbogo, while Trizza Kithitho takes over Strategy from Dahabbo Darro.

PPD’s restructuring further fills specialised administrative slots, forwarding Brian Ooga as Secretary for Devolution and County Governments and Omari Samwel as National Chairperson in Charge of Diaspora.

None of the proposed constitutions, executive names, symbols, or headquarters relocations take legal effect immediately. Under the Political Parties Act, the registrar’s gazettement opens the process to public challenge.

Written representations and objections must reach the ORPP within seven days of the October 30 publication date. In the absence of sustained legal or public challenges before that deadline lapses, the registrar will formally amend the national political party records, cementing the new executive slates, geographic addresses, and campaign iconography into law.
