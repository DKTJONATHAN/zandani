Inside the Kibera High Court, a rigid legal veil surrounds eight teenage students from Utumishi Girls Academy as judicial authorities enforce strict child-protection safeguards in one of the country's most sensitive criminal proceedings.

High Court Judge Diana Kavedza rejected the bail application for the eight subject minors, who face 16 counts of murder alongside charges connected to the late May 2026 dormitory fire in Gilgil, Nakuru County. The fast-moving inferno claimed the lives of 16 students and left 79 others injured.

Under directives issued by the bench, all parties, legal counsels, and media entities are legally mandated to refer to the accused exclusively as “subject minors.” Public disclosure of any identifying details has been strictly prohibited to shield the teenagers from exposure while the legal process unfolds.

State prosecutors successfully argued against temporary freedom by pointing to the extreme gravity of the capital offenses and the fragile condition of those affected by the tragedy. The court heard that survivors of the Nakuru County blaze and the bereaved families are still undergoing critical psychological support to process the scale of the loss, rendering the immediate release of the accused inappropriate.

The eight students initially entered a plea of not guilty before Justice Kavedza in July 2026, following mandatory psychiatric evaluations that cleared them as fit to stand trial. With the bail application formally dismissed, the murder trial will continue under the supervision of the Kibera High Court bench, operating under strict procedural constraints designed to manage the unique legal and social dimensions of minor defendants facing capital charges.
