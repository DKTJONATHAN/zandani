Kenya Power has earmarked key commercial and residential corridors in Nairobi and Meru counties for temporary disconnection on Friday, October 16, 2026, as the national distributor carries out routine maintenance on local transmission lines.

The scheduled utility works will establish an operational pause across two completely different economic landscapes: Pangani’s densely built urban transit routes in Nairobi and a string of vital agro-processing factories, trading outposts, and learning institutions in rural Meru County.

### Pangani’s Urban Commuter and Residential Strip

In Nairobi, the maintenance program concentrates entirely on Pangani, targeting an interconnected network of busy roadways that handle heavy commercial delivery traffic, mid-rise residential housing, and roadside small enterprises.

The utility confirmed that premises along Forest Road, Chai Road, Agoi Road, and Pamba Road will experience electricity cuts throughout the duration of the maintenance window. Adjacent domestic and retail customers linked to these low-voltage and medium-voltage feeders will also sit without grid power.

The affected Pangani streets host a mix of long-standing residential properties, auto garages, bakeries, small-scale eateries, and transit retail spaces that flank the Forest Road access lanes into the capital’s central business district. The planned disconnection forces small businesses operating on tight margins along Chai and Agoi roads to rely on secondary power generation or alter their Friday operational shifts.

### Meru Agro-Industrial and Educational Hubs

Outside the capital, Kenya Power's maintenance deployment shifts into Meru County, landing squarely on agricultural processing infrastructure, rural trade centers, and schools within Mikumbune and Ntemwene.

Unlike the service-heavy urban strip in Nairobi, the outage in Meru interrupts several critical production facilities during active working hours. Among the primary installations lined up for the power cut are the Kandigi Coffee Factory and the Kieru Coffee Factory—two essential collection and primary processing stations that serve local smallholder coffee growers.

The blackout footprint also covers Macikine Secondary School, alongside the commercial hubs of Mikumbune Market and Gaatia Market, where local trading, small-scale refrigeration, and food kiosks depend on continuous grid supply. Surrounding centers including Kiorone, Ntemwene, Kagwampungul Kabyoo, and the National Oil service station will face similar downtime, together with neighboring homesteads and feeder-line consumers.

### Alternative Arrangements for Affected Pockets

Kenya Power regularly undertakes planned maintenance to clear overhead lines, upgrade aging distribution transformers, replace damaged utility poles, and reinforce feeder capacity ahead of seasonal weather shifts. While necessary for long-term grid stability, scheduled outages routinely test the resilience of local establishments that lack dedicated backup diesel plants.

The utility has notified customers within the listed zones to implement contingency plans to safeguard daily operations and perishable inventory until network engineers conclude maintenance works and restore the feeder lines.
