**NAIROBI, Kenya - September 14, 2026** - Nairobi County is aggressively pursuing Ksh2.7 billion in uncollected revenues from defaulting institutions, including churches and government security departments, as City Hall moves to plug budget holes through tighter enforcement. Appearing before the Senate Investment, Trade and Industrialisation Committee, Governor Johnson Sakaja revealed that long-standing tax defaults across high-profile institutions continue to hamper the capital’s effort to expand its internal revenue base.

### What we know

* City Hall is turning up the heat on sacred cows, putting religious houses and disciplined forces on notice over uncollected tax billions.
* Digital payment platforms like the USSD gateway have already pushed county revenues up ninety-five percent, proving technology closes chronic corruption gaps fast.
* Senators are demanding full breakdown figures because recovering these massive arrears is crucial for funding devolved urban infrastructure without raising local charges.
* Nairobi's aggressive revenue drive mirrors a national push to digitise essential public services and eliminate cash-handling leakages across government ministries.

***

### The Senate Showdown

During his appearance before the Senate committee, Governor Sakaja faced intense questioning regarding Nairobi’s own-source revenue trajectories and debt recovery mechanisms. The county chief confirmed that non-compliant institutions across the metropolis owe City Hall upwards of Ksh2.7 billion in land rates, parking fees, single business permits, and utility charges.

Among the most prominent defaulters identified were faith-based institutions and security departments-entities that have historically operated under an informal veil of administrative leniency.

While Sakaja did not immediately table a itemised breakdown specifying the exact amounts owed individually by religious institutions versus state security installations, he assured the Senate panel that active debt collection strategies are underway to ensure all property owners and operators contribute to the county basket.

### The Numbers: Digitisation Drives a 95% Surge

To curb revenue leakages that previously bled millions through manual collection and kickbacks, City Hall has shifted almost entirely to digital channels.

* **Platform Access:** Taxpayers now process payments exclusively through the `*647#` USSD code or the `nairobiservices.co.ke` web portal.
* **Three-Year Growth:** Own-source revenue has surged by **95 per cent** over the last three years, an increase Sakaja attributes directly to automated receipting and reduced human contact.
* **Single-Day Peak:** Demonstrating the impact of system automation, the county recorded a single-day revenue collection peak of **Ksh256 million** on March 26.

Beyond payment automation, the county executive has expanded decentralised customer service centers across Nairobi's sub-counties, aiming to bring administrative resolution closer to traders and property owners.

### Street-Level Reading: Ending the Era of Untouchables

For years, the average *mama mboga* in Muthurwa or a small kiosk operator along River Road faced immediate arrest or premises closure over delayed single business permits. Meanwhile, sprawling church complexes running commercial parking lots and multi-story security installations accrued massive unpaid municipal bills without consequence.

Sakaja’s public disclosure before the Senate signals a crucial policy shift: the tax net is widening to include entities that once considered themselves immune to municipal enforcement. Recovering Ksh2.7 billion would significantly ease City Hall’s cash flow constraints, funding stalled ward-level projects and road maintenance without forcing the county to slap higher levies on ordinary residents.

This crackdown aligns with a broader national trajectory. Across Kenya, public sectors-from education and healthcare to agricultural input tracking-are migrating to digital frameworks to eliminate paper trails and plug systemic graft. In Nairobi, where cash used to disappear into private pockets at the bus ranks and market gates, the fight for the city's financial sanity is now being fought on servers and USSD codes.
