The circulation of an unaccounted AK-47 rifle and 30 live rounds in Mombasa South has prompted local security agencies to issue night movement restrictions, following a deadly pre-dawn assault on a municipal revenue barrier in Likoni.

The 3:40 a.m. ambush on Monday along Barabara ya Mchanga, located roughly three kilometres west of Inuka Police Station, left one county enforcement officer dead and two others hospitalized with severe injuries. Patrol officers who rushed to the scene after hearing gunfire found that an armed group carrying crude weapons had overwhelmed the station team, disarmed a police officer, and escaped with the automatic firearm.

![An image of the Likoni attack in Mombasa County on October 26, 2026](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/likoni-police-attack-october.jpg.webp?itok=wvKZt2l0)

The incident underscores the growing operational hazards facing hybrid enforcement teams stationed at isolated revenue barriers during late-night shifts. The municipal checkpoint was actively collecting county cess taxes when the gang launched the sudden attack.

During the confrontation, one county enforcement officer sustained a fatal gunshot wound to the back. His body was subsequently moved to the Coast General Morgue. Two other officers suffered critical injuries: one was admitted to Diani Beach Hospital with deep cut wounds and a gunshot wound to his left thigh, while another was treated for deep cuts to his head and right thigh. A fourth county enforcement officer escaped the station without major physical injuries.

The sudden loss of a service rifle in the area has drawn immediate operational concern from security chiefs. Mombasa County Police Commander Stella Jerono confirmed that while investigators have not yet established a motive for the assault, tracking down the missing firearm and neutralising the armed group remains the immediate priority.

![Kenyan Police Vehicle.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/police%20car%20.jpg.webp?itok=rJFMSgZV)

"We urge the public, who may have any information that could assist the investigation, to report it to the police," Jerono said, reassuring residents that tracking teams had been deployed across the sub-county.

Following the attack, security agencies issued targeted travel and operational cautions for Likoni and broader Mombasa South. Residents have been advised to limit non-essential night travel, particularly around Barabara ya Mchanga and designated municipal collection points. Concurrently, corporate entities and non-governmental organisations with field teams or physical assets in the zone have been instructed to harden their facility security and maintain real-time headcount accounting of night-shift personnel.

The Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) branch in Likoni has taken charge of the case. Detectives are currently analyzing physical evidence from the crime scene and coordinating with multi-agency units to track the route taken by the attackers, prioritize the recovery of the missing firearm, and identify the assailants before the weapon can be deployed in subsequent crimes.
