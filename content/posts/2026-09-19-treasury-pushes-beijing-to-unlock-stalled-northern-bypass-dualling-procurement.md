The National Treasury is attempting to break a year-long administrative gap between loan approvals and physical construction on the Northern Bypass, dispatching senior officials to Beijing to nudge Chinese financiers into releasing funds for procurement.

During high-level consultations in Beijing on Friday, September 18, National Treasury Principal Secretary Chris Kiptoo pressed the China International Development Cooperation Agency (CIDCA) to help move the 20.2-kilometre road upgrade and two sister infrastructure schemes from diplomatic commitments into active civil works.

"We will continue working closely with CIDCA to move these and other priority programmes from commitment to implementation," Dr Kiptoo said following a meeting with the agency’s vice chairman, Liu Junfeng.

### The 20-Kilometre Arterial Squeeze

The push centers on converting the Northern Bypass—which stretches from Ruaka in Kiambu County across northern Nairobi to its junction with the Eastern Bypass at Ruiru—into a full dual carriageway. While the Cabinet approved the dual-lane conversion in June 2025, and China EximBank cleared financing under a Preferential Buyer’s Credit facility, the corridor has remained stuck in preparatory stages rather than moving to site mobilization.

The single-carriageway design has long struggled with severe traffic congestion generated by rapid residential and commercial development across the Runda, Kahawa West, and Membley belts. 

Engineering designs for the pending upgrade specify eight grade-separated interchanges, dedicated overpasses, underpasses, segregated pedestrian walkways, cycle lanes, and modernized drainage networks to match the already dualled Eastern Bypass.

### Contrasting Timelines Across Nairobi Corridors

The Treasury’s bilateral mission exposed uneven progress among Kenya's China-backed urban transport projects. While the Northern Bypass dualling remains pinned at the financing-to-procurement juncture, the nearby Muthaiga-Kiambu-Ndumberi Road upgrade has cleared those administrative hurdles far more swiftly.

The Kenya Urban Roads Authority (KURA) signed an Engineering, Procurement, and Construction (EPC) contract for the 23.5-kilometre Muthaiga-Ndumberi arterial in March 2026. That project, which traverses heavy commuter zones across Nairobi and Kiambu towns, is already locked into its delivery cycle, providing the Treasury with a procedural template it hopes to replicate on the Northern Bypass.

### Expanding Beyond Transport to Water and Health

Beyond urban roads, Nairobi’s delegation placed the long-delayed Bosto Dam Water Supply Project in Bomet County squarely on the Beijing negotiating table. The water scheme represents a critical rural utility project that the national government has sought external concessionary backing to deliver.

The bilateral discussions also audited ongoing grant-funded interventions, including Chinese state-backed rehabilitation works at Londiani Hospital in Kericho County and Baringo County Referral Hospital in Kabarnet, alongside emergency drought relief assistance.

For the Treasury, the primary test remains turning signed credit facilities into deployed heavy machinery before mounting commuter pressure along the northern peri-urban belt completely outpaces planned road capacity.
