The planned Ksh2.2 trillion petroleum refinery in Lamu is no longer being pitched purely as a petroleum processing asset. It is now slated to double as an independent power producer capable of feeding 500 megawatts of electricity directly into the Kenyan national grid.

Disclosing the operational scope during President William Ruto’s tour of the Lekki Petroleum Refinery in Lagos, industrialist Aliko Dangote revealed that the Lamu complex will feature an on-site 1,000-megawatt power generation plant. Under the proposed model, the facility will consume half of the output for its own refining units while selling the remaining 500 megawatts directly to the Kenyan government.

The off-take arrangement introduces a commercial electricity dimension to an infrastructure undertaking initially framed around processing crude oil from the Lokichar basin in Turkana County.

### Expanding Beyond Petroleum Processing

![resident William Ruto (left) talking to Billionaire Aliko Dangote during their visit to the Dangote Petroleum Refinery and Petrochemical Company in Lekki, Nigeria, on Friday, Septe](https://i.ibb.co/wrK2BZvB/914d2b2195df.webp)

By integrating a 1,000-megawatt generation plant into the industrial footprint, Dangote Group is positioning the coastal installation to operate completely insulated from grid shortfalls while monetizing surplus generation. An injection of 500 megawatts would place the facility among the single largest generation sources feeding the state's transmission network.

“The refinery is actually the gate,” Dangote stated during the inspection. “Once you open the refinery, you will be shocked at how many people will come to invest in Kenya.”

The technical design for Lamu outlines processing capacity of 700,000 barrels of crude oil per day, targeted at domestic distribution and broader East and Central African markets. Dangote noted that the Kenyan facility will incorporate refining units absent from his flagship Lagos plant, including an industrial coker and an expanded Residual Fluid Catalytic Cracker (RFCC) engineered to maximize fuel yields from heavy crude grades.

Backing for the project spans both sovereign and institutional capital, with development estimates ranging between Ksh1.94 trillion and Ksh2.20 trillion alongside technical and structuring support from the Africa Finance Corporation (AFC). The project was redirected to Lamu after Dangote Group bypassed an earlier proposal for Tanga in Tanzania, citing transport logistics, pipeline corridors, and market access.

### Upstream Pipeline and Land Commitments

![Africa's richest man, Aliko Dangote, at his Nigerian Oil Refinery on June 21, 2025.](https://i.ibb.co/mrJNLPCx/a8702fdf3d62.webp)

For the Kenyan government, accommodating the scale of the complex has required direct administrative interventions. Ruto confirmed that the state has completed compulsory acquisition and boundary planning for the coastal site.

“We are not looking at this as just a refinery. We are looking at it as a refinery that is also going to elevate our industrial scale,” Ruto said. “We have already secured the land for this project.”

The refining and power plans coincide with concrete technical shifts upstream. On Friday, a 1,500-horsepower GW70 onshore drilling rig valued at Ksh2.6 billion ($20 million) docked at Kilindini Port in Mombasa aboard the vessel MV Transit Sedanka from Duqm Port in Oman.

Leased by Gulf Energy E&P BV SEZ from China's Great Wall Drilling Company, the heavy rig is scheduled to be hauled overland to the South Lokichar basin ahead of a November 1 spud date. Gulf Energy has committed to delivering the first phase of commercial crude production by December 2026—supplying the feedstock required to feed the coastal refinery.

As technical preparations shift to the coastal corridor, Dangote’s electricity proposition places formal power purchase discussions on the state’s agenda, forcing energy planners to evaluate how a 500-megawatt private thermal-industrial feed will integrate into the country's long-term generation dispatch plans.
