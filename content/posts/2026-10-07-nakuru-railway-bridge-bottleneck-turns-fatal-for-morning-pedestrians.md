A routine decision by morning commuters to abandon stalled public transport vehicles and walk into Nakuru’s town centre ended in tragedy on Wednesday when an out-of-control haulage truck ploughed into pedestrians at the Railway Bridge.

The collision along the Nakuru-Nairobi highway killed two people on the spot, near Medi Junction, right at the eastern threshold where long-distance highway freight merges with dense morning foot traffic heading into the Central Business District (CBD).

According to witnesses at the scene, the victims—identified as a pupil from St. Joseph Primary School and a local boda boda rider—had both disembarked from public service vehicles caught in crawling morning queues. Attempting to complete their journeys on foot along the road shoulder, they were struck from behind when an oncoming commercial trailer suffered suspected brake failure and veered off its lane.

### Lethal Friction at the CBD Gateway

The impact crushed one victim beneath the vehicle's chassis while leaving the second critically wounded before succumbing to injuries as first responders arrived. The heavy truck eventually came to a halt with visible damage to its frontal bumper, blocking critical inbound lanes and locking thousands of motorists in gridlock stretching kilometres east of the town.

Local residents have long categorised the Railway Bridge section as one of Nakuru’s most treacherous infrastructure choke points. Motorists descending toward Medi Junction frequently contend with sudden braking and narrow carriageways, while pedestrians have little physical barrier separating walking paths from high-tonnage transit trucks navigating the regional trade route.

When traffic backs up entering the CBD, commuters routinely alight from matatus to avoid delay, placing school children, office workers, and informal sector operators directly in the path of heavy hauliers contending with steep approaches and mechanical fatigue.

### A String of Transit Corridor Disasters

Wednesday’s fatalities represent the third severe transport incident recorded within Nakuru County's trunk roads over a four-day span.

On Tuesday, an LPG gas tanker burst into flames adjacent to a General Service Unit (GSU) camp at Sachangwan near Salgaa on the Nakuru-Eldoret stretch, leaving two individuals severely injured and halting cross-country traffic for hours. That blast came just two days after an October 4 multi-vehicle collision at Mau Summit, where a pickup truck crashed into two oncoming vehicles, leaving multiple casualties.

While traffic police cleared the damaged trailer to restore the flow of goods and passenger vehicles into Nakuru town later in the morning, authorities had not yet issued a formal report detailing the truck's mechanical inspection or the definitive casualty count.
