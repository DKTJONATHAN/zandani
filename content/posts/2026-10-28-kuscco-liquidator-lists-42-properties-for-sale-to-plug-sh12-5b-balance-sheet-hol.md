According to statutory sale notices issued by court-appointed receiver Waithaka Ngaruiya, the Kenya Union of Savings and Credit Co-operatives (KUSCCO) will auction 42 prime properties across eight counties in November, marking the first tangible recovery drive for affiliated SACCOs stranded in a Ksh12.5 billion balance sheet deficit.

The public disposals represent an aggressive effort to convert physical assets into liquid cash after financial filings presented during recent legal proceedings revealed the union carries Ksh17.7 billion in outstanding obligations against an asset book valued at Ksh5.2 billion.

The auction roster encompasses high-value residential developments, bare commercial plots, and stalled construction projects scattered across Nairobi, Kajiado, Kiambu, Machakos, Nakuru, Kisumu, Mombasa, and Kilifi.

### Staggered Bidding Across Nairobi and Kisumu

![Kenya Union of Savings & Credit Cooperatives (KUSCCO) Offices located along Kilimanjaro Road, Upper Hill, Nairobi](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/kuscco_office-location_0.jpg.webp?itok=IAlCOi0Q)

Watts Auctions has scheduled the sales across three phases, opening on Wednesday, November 11, at View Park Towers in Nairobi. That opening session will place properties from Nairobi, Kajiado, Kiambu, and the wider Mount Kenya zone on the block.

Among the flagship listings in the capital is a residential apartment within Kirichwa Gardens in Kileleshwa. In neighboring Kajiado County, the liquidator is offloading a mix of four-bedroom bungalows, three-bedroom maisonettes, and large undeveloped parcels in Kitengela and Kisaju.

The liquidator has also put up unfinished developments to cut holding costs. The auction portfolio contains a motor vehicle workshop, a mixed-use commercial and residential complex, and a multi-unit residential project currently stalled at roughly 30 percent completion.

A second regional auction will follow on Friday, November 20, at a public park in Kisumu town, covering holdings across Nyanza, Western Kenya, Kisii, and parts of the Rift Valley. Coastal properties on offer include residential plots and apartment blocks situated in Mombasa and Kilifi locations such as Bamburi, Bombolulu, Shanzu, and Kiembeni, alongside further parcels in Meru, Bungoma, and Siaya.

### The Legal Route to Dissolution

The asset sell-off follows a regulatory intervention triggered on August 28, when KUSCCO delegates voted to dissolve the apex umbrella body after years of deepening cash crunches. Commissioner for Co-operative Development David Obonyo formally cancelled the union's registration on August 31, invoking statutory powers under co-operative legislation to initiate liquidation.

Obonyo subsequently gazetted Waithaka Ngaruiya of Waithaka and Associates on September 23 to oversee the wind-up process under a one-year mandate. The administrative path became the sole legal avenue for asset recovery after High Court Judge Rhoda Rutto struck out a commercial creditor petition on September 28.

That petition, filed by an aggrieved member SACCO demanding Ksh108.85 million in defaulted payments, had sought to wind up KUSCCO under the Insolvency Act. Justice Rutto ruled that because KUSCCO was incorporated as a co-operative society rather than a limited liability company, it remained exempt from corporate insolvency laws and could only be unwound through the Office of the Commissioner for Co-operative Development.

With commercial court avenues dismissed, the proceeds realized from the upcoming November auctions will serve as the primary liquidity pool to determine how much of the Ksh17.7 billion creditor claims can be settled before Ngaruiya's 12-month gazetted mandate concludes.
