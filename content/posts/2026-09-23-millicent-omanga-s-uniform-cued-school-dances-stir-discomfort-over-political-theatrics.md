Millicent Omanga has traded power suits for pleated skirts and school sweaters once too often, turning standard educational visits into choreographed social media spectacles that have left online observers distinctly uneasy.

In a trending video clip, the former nominated senator is seen matching the attire of secondary school students—complete with a red sweater, collared shirt, and white socks—while joining in on a synchronized routine. While public figures regularly court younger demographics through school drop-ins, Omanga’s persistent adoption of actual student uniforms has crossed from quirky outreach into territory that critics increasingly label as boundary-blurring media theatrics.

This latest performance is part of an escalating sartorial pattern rather than an isolated incident. Back in July 2026, Omanga debuted a lavender school uniform during an outreach visit to Njathaini Primary School within Roysambu Constituency. That particular appearance marked the third distinct school uniform she had worn over a four-day span as part of her menstrual hygiene initiative.

While those earlier visits were framed around charity and sanitary towel distributions, the reliance on full student cosplay has drawn sharp pushback. Social media commentators and education observers have questioned the necessity of adults dressing as minors for public engagement, arguing that it shifts the focus away from student learning environments and toward political brand maintenance.

As the footage circulates across digital platforms, the debate centers less on the charity work itself and more on the optics of staging viral classroom choreography. Educational advocates maintain that schools should remain focused on student empowerment rather than serving as backdrops for political performance art, leaving politicians like Omanga facing mounting scrutiny over their choice of stagecraft.
