The National Transport and Safety Authority (NTSA) has opened a formal probe into dangerous driving on the Nairobi Expressway, relying on public social media submissions to help identify the registration numbers of two high-end vehicles caught racing along the toll corridor.

In an open appeal issued Monday, October 26, the transport authority requested members of the public holding vehicle registration details or footage to send information directly through social media direct messages. The solicitation marks an operational push by regulators to convert viral citizen surveillance into actionable enforcement leads on a closed-access dual carriageway.

The investigation centres on social media footage capturing a Mercedes-Benz C-Class and a Lamborghini executing high-speed overtaking manoeuvres across multiple lanes. Video circulated online shows the vehicles squeezing through narrow gaps between traffic and narrowly avoiding a collision while passing on the left, with onboard collision-warning alarms sounding inside the vehicle as occupants reacted to the close calls.

![A screengrab of famous Nairobi influencers racing along Nairobi Expressway, overtaking dangerously that has caused online uproar](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/_dangerous-driving.jpg.webp?itok=rvLgIK0T)

Online reports linked the occupants to individuals active in forex trading, drawing widespread public concern over stunt-driving on the modern infrastructure asset.

NTSA condemned the conduct, stating that the drivers had put themselves, their passengers, and other road users at severe risk despite ongoing national efforts to curb road trauma.

"These drivers not only put their own lives at risk, but also endangered passengers and other road users," NTSA said in its statement. "It is deeply unfortunate that while comprehensive measures are being implemented to address road safety challenges, individuals still engage in such risky actions."

![Nairobi Expressway](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/nairobi-expressway_0.jpg.webp?itok=dG888P4R)

The authority’s request for private digital tips highlights the role public digital recording plays in triggering regulatory action. While the Nairobi Expressway operates as a monitored toll road, NTSA is leaning on eyewitness registration data sent via social media channels to establish clear ownership records and initiate regulatory action.

The probe coincides with a period of heightened enforcement intensity across the national transit network. Just a day prior to NTSA’s public call, 25 people were killed in a major crash along the Nairobi-Mombasa Highway near the Kanga area in Mtito Andei, prompting the Ministry of Roads and Transport to declare stricter compliance mandates across key transit arterial routes.

NTSA stated that once vehicle registration details provided by the public are verified, it will proceed with targeted enforcement action against the registered owners and drivers involved.
