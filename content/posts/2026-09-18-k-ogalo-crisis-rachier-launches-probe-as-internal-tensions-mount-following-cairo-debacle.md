Gor Mahia chairman Ambrose Rachier has officially launched an internal inquiry into the club’s disastrous CAF Champions League exit, describing the team's performance in Egypt as a humiliation. The Kenyan champions were soundly beaten 5-1 by Pyramids FC in the second leg of their preliminary round tie in Cairo, resulting in a 7-2 aggregate defeat that has left the club hierarchy searching for answers.

The heavy loss in Cairo stood in stark contrast to the first leg in Nairobi, where K’Ogalo had been praised for their competitive effort despite a 2-0 defeat. In Egypt, the team’s defensive structure collapsed, allowing the hosts to score at will, while goalkeeper Byrne Omondi was identified as having made several costly errors during the encounter.

Speaking to journalist Eric Njiru, Rachier expressed his shock at the nature of the defeat, noting that the club management believed the squad was fully prepared for the continental challenge. "It is difficult to say anything positive about what happened in Cairo. We are still trying to grapple with the results because we got shocked," Rachier stated.

The chairman emphasized that the investigation is necessary to understand why the team’s performance levels plummeted so drastically. "If anything, we got humiliated and the issue is, why? That is what we want to look into. When we were going, we thought we were already very prepared but alas! We were not quite prepared yet," he added.

The aftermath of the Cairo trip has sparked significant unrest at the club, with reports emerging that the management has moved to suspend several players deemed responsible for the poor display. This decision has reportedly created a volatile atmosphere within the dressing room, casting a shadow over the team’s preparations for the domestic campaign.

The situation has escalated further with reports that the remaining players, who were not suspended, have threatened to boycott training sessions. This internal friction poses a major challenge for head coach Charles Akonnor, who is now tasked with steadying the ship ahead of the club's new season opener.

Gor Mahia is scheduled to face Murang’a Seal this Saturday, a match that was intended to be a fresh start for the side. However, the combination of the ongoing inquiry, player suspensions, and the reported training boycott threatens to disrupt the team's momentum as they look to put the continental disappointment behind them.

As the club navigates this turbulent period, the pressure remains on the leadership to resolve the internal disputes quickly. For now, the focus remains on the findings of the inquiry and whether the squad can overcome the lingering tension to deliver a performance on the pitch this weekend.
