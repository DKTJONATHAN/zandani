Ferdinand Waititu’s fight to maintain his freedom outside prison walls collapsed on Wednesday, October 14, 2026, pulling his wife, Susan Wangari, directly back into the legal crosshairs beside him.

In a comprehensive ruling delivered at the High Court, Justice Rose Ougo dismantled the former Kiambu governor’s hopes of overturning his 12-year prison term. More decisively for the couple's household, the judge wiped away the legal safety net Wangari had secured during their initial trial, overturning an earlier acquittal and convicting both Waititu and Wangari on three counts of money laundering.

The reversal delivers a heavy personal blow to the flamboyant political figure, widely known across Nairobi and Kiambu social and political circuits as "Baba Yao." While Waititu had spent months navigating the appellate corridors on revised bail terms, the High Court’s ruling ended that reprieve in a single stroke, cancelling his bail and directing that he be taken back into custody immediately.

### The Overturned Acquittal in the Family Ledger

The central surprise of the High Court's determination rested on the State’s cross-appeal. While the lower trial court had initially cleared both Waititu and Wangari of money laundering allegations, Justice Ougo determined that the acquittals were flawed.

By finding the couple guilty on the three money laundering charges, the High Court has tied their fates together once again in the criminal justice system. The judge referred the matter back to the Chief Magistrate’s Court, where both Waititu and Wangari must now present mitigation before receiving fresh sentences for the offenses.

Under legal provisions noted in the judgment, any new custodial sentences handed down by the magistrate for the laundering counts are expected to run concurrently with the prison terms already imposed. However, the verdict strips away the partial victory Wangari had maintained, turning what began as Waititu’s singular appellate gamble into an expanded legal trap for his family.

### Bail Revoked and the KSh 588.7 Million Tender

Waititu’s central appeal had challenged the severity of the 12-year sentence handed down over the irregular award of a KSh 588.7 million road construction contract during his tenure at the helm of Kiambu County. In his filings, the former county chief argued that the penalty was excessively harsh, claiming the lower court had glossed over evidentiary contradictions that favored his defense.

Justice Ougo rejected those arguments outright, ruling that the prosecution had proved its case and affirming the original conviction alongside the custodial term. Co-accused persons who had joined the appeal similarly saw their challenges dismissed.

With the confirmation of the conviction came the immediate revocation of the revised bail terms that had allowed Waititu to move freely during the appellate review. The court’s order to remand him back into custody closes an extended window where the politician remained a visible presence outside prison.

### A Decade in the Political Cold

Beyond the prison gate, the High Court also slammed the door shut on any near-term political comeback for Waititu. Justice Ougo upheld the trial court’s order disqualifying the former governor and his co-convicts from holding elective or appointive public office for 10 years.

The decade-long bar neutralizes any prospect of the veteran grassroots mobilizer re-entering frontline politics in Kiambu or Nairobi, cementing his removal from the public stage.

Waititu and Wangari now await their date before the Chief Magistrate’s Court, where their defense teams will plead for leniency during mitigation before the court formally pronounces their sentences on the money laundering counts.
