Importers leaving commercial shipments to gather dust in state facilities are running out of runway, as the Kenya Revenue Authority moves to liquidate thousands of long-abandoned consignments sitting across regional customs warehouses.

Under a gazette notice issued by the tax authority, hundreds of tons of industrial materials, electronics, and commercial goods face permanent forfeiture. Among the stalled shipments is a massive consignment of 1,472 packages of mineral fibre ceiling materials and ceiling grids housed at the Naivasha Inland Container Depot (ICD). Unless cleared within a strict 30-day window, the goods will be formally classified as abandoned and fed directly into a four-day public auction scheduled to run from December 28 to 31, 2026.

![Photo of Naivasha Inland Depot](https://i.ibb.co/fVQYhvMY/79c9fa19fd70.webp)

The inventory slated for the hammer highlights deep logjams in customs clearance across the country. At the Lodwar Customs Station Warehouse, the taxman flagged 665 empty beer crates distributed across four consignments, some of which have occupied storage space since April 2022. Meanwhile, Eldoret customs facilities are processing the disposal of multiple drum batches of ethanol—including a single 54-drum lot of 250-litre containers—alongside 470 smartphones, five laptops, a piano, 49 Plantronics Blackwire earphones, and 320 assorted perfumes held at the airport warehouse.

In Nakuru, the targeted inventory includes 927 cartons of modern tiles and 125 bags of sugar, underscoring that the clearance defaults span both heavy industrial inputs and fast-moving consumer goods.

![Directional signage for an auction.](https://i.ibb.co/mpTwTmj/c6538c41d0e5.webp)

This sweep is backed by Section 42 of the East African Community Customs Management Act, 2004, which empowers customs authorities to dispose of goods left uncleared in designated warehouses once a 30-day final notice lapses. The statute also covers merchandise where owners have defaulted on required import duties and taxes, shielding warehouse operators from indefinite storage liabilities while recovering lost revenue.

Prospective buyers have been given a brief two-day inspection window on December 23 and 24 to review the lots on-site during official working hours. Final bidding for all listed consignments will be conducted entirely through KRA's digital auction platform before the close of the year.
