Lady Bishop Kathy Kiuna did not offer polite comfort when addressing the online storm surrounding Mercy Masika. Standing at the pulpit in a video published on Friday, October 30, the preacher issued a direct, unvarnished instruction to the gospel singer: do not back down, do not hesitate, and execute the programme with absolute precision.

Addressing the recording artist by her married identity, Kiuna cut through days of intense social media debate over gender expectations in marriage with an authoritative directive from the altar.

“Mercy Mugoro, you better start that class and start it well. You better start it well,” Kiuna said, dismissing the chorus of online skepticism that has trailed Masika’s upcoming project.

Rather than interpreting the public criticism as a reputational liability or a cue to retool the syllabus, Kiuna argued from the pulpit that hostility is an inherent confirmation of spiritual significance.

“If you know that God is in something powerfully, look at the attack,” she told her congregation. “You better start it and push very clearly.”

### The Pulpit Defense of Elder Mentorship

At the center of Kiuna’s intervention is the institutional doctrine of generational coaching within the church. Grounding her position in scripture, Kiuna argued that older women carry an explicit mandate to guide younger generations through the realities of marriage and domestic life, regardless of shifting cultural sentiments outside the sanctuary.

“Older women are advised by the word to train the younger women. That is the word. And that’s what we need to do,” Kiuna said. She doubled down on the mandate of her own platform, adding, “We are raising wise women in this house. We are raising wise women in the name of Jesus.”

The public backing carries substantial practical weight for Masika’s initiative. Kiuna is not merely an external observer offering vocal support; she is scheduled to serve as one of the headline guest mentors within the curriculum itself.

### Inside the Seven-Session Curriculum

The initiative that sparked the confrontation is titled the *Becoming a Wife Masterclass*, organized under Masika’s Women of Light Ministry. Formatted as an intensive seven-session programme, the masterclass is scheduled to run between November 21 and December 12, 2026.

According to the ministerial prospectus, the sessions are structured around traditional Christian marital tenets. The outlined coursework covers foundational biblical marriage principles, relational construction, intimacy management, and the prescribed functional responsibilities of women within marriage.

Yet the moment Masika unveiled the initiative, public reaction split sharply along philosophical lines, exposing a widening rift between traditional church teachings and contemporary perspectives on relationship equity.

### The Gender-Burden Debate

Critics immediately questioned why instructional platforms overwhelmingly target women while comparable training initiatives for men remain sparse. The primary pushback centered on the persistent cultural habit of burdening women with the entire responsibility of sustaining marriage stability.

Former Citizen TV news anchor Janet Mbugua emerged as one of the most prominent voices challenging the premise of the masterclass. Mbugua questioned the concentration of instructional burden on women, contending that healthy, durable marriages demand shared responsibility and bilateral accountability rather than unilateral coaching.

Other media figures, however, pushed in the opposite direction. Lifestyle personality Lillian Nganga defended Masika’s right to curate specialized faith-based content for her target audience, describing the mounting outrage across social media as disproportionate and unwarranted.

With Kiuna now throwing the full weight of her pulpit authority behind Masika, the ideological confrontation between digital critics and traditional ministry mentorship has reached a standoff. Masika's Women of Light Ministry remains on course to open the seven-session class on November 21, 2026, backed by a roster of senior mentors determined to let the curriculum speak for itself.
