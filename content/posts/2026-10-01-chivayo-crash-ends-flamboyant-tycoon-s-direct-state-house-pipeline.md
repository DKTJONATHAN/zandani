The mid-air crash in Marondera, Zimbabwe, that killed corporate tycoon Wicknell Chivayo has abruptly closed one of the most unusual and highly publicised informal channels to Kenya's State House.

The disaster, which claimed six lives on Wednesday evening, terminates a rapid cross-border networking trajectory that saw the controversial Zimbabwean businessman move from social media notoriety to direct, personal access to President William Ruto.

### Helicopter Down in Marondera

Police in Zimbabwe confirmed that the aircraft went down in the Marondera Rural district at approximately 5:00 PM on Wednesday, September 30. Emergency response teams dispatched to the site found no survivors among the six individuals on board.

![chivayo-ruto.jpg](https://i.ibb.co/d4tfYrCq/dff10cadc44b.webp)

Family statements and law enforcement officials subsequently confirmed that Chivayo and his wife, Lucy Muteke, were among the deceased.

Reacting to the news, President Ruto issued a personal tribute mourning Chivayo as a close friend whose drive and conviction in African continental development stood out.

"I am deeply saddened by the tragic passing of my friend, Wicknell Chivayo, together with his wife, Lucy Muteke, and the other lives lost in the helicopter crash in Zimbabwe," Ruto stated, offering condolences to Zimbabwean President Emmerson Mnangagwa and the families of the victims.

### Unprecedented Executive Access

That personal bond had become a focal point within Kenyan political and trade circles. Chivayo, who built a massive social media presence under the moniker 'Sir Wicknell,' spent recent months documenting an extraordinary degree of proximity to the Kenyan presidency.

His interactions went beyond formal state receptions. Chivayo was frequently hosted at State House in Nairobi and visited Ruto's private residence in Kilgoris, where video recordings captured the President personally driving the Zimbabwean businessman across his estate to a waiting helicopter.

![President William Ruto speaking during a church service in Kisumu on September 13, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/QvQvGxdj/3af138b07d61.webp)

Throughout these visits, Chivayo publicly framed his presence in Kenya around continental trade, positioning himself as an investor exploring energy and infrastructure deals across East Africa.

### Energy Dealmaker Under Scrutiny

Behind the high-profile hospitality lay a complex corporate footprint in Southern Africa. As the founder of Intratrek Zimbabwe, Chivayo was a central figure in major public infrastructure projects, most notably the long-contested Gwanda Solar Project.

While his aggressive procurement style and deep political ties drew persistent scrutiny and legal challenges in Harare—allegations of wrongdoing he consistently rejected—Chivayo leveraged his capital and high-level relationships to project influence well beyond Zimbabwe's borders.

As aviation investigators in Marondera work through the wreckage to determine the mechanical or atmospheric factors behind the crash, Chivayo's death leaves several private cross-border investment discussions without their chief architect. The accident brings a sudden end to an era of unmediated access to Kenya's top leadership, leaving Intratrek's regional growth strategy without its primary political promoter.
