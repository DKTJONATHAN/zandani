For gospel entertainer Samuel Muraya, widely known as DJ Mo, a routine morning drive down Thika Road turned into a courtroom appearance after a single day of missed paperwork derailed an international travel schedule.

The media personality was pulled over by traffic authorities near Roasters in the morning hours, setting off a chain of events that culminated before Senior Principal Magistrate Geoffrey Onsarigo at the Milimani Chief Magistrate’s Court on Wednesday, November 4.

Appearing before the bench, DJ Mo entered a guilty plea to two distinct traffic infractions. The primary charge stemmed from operating his Jaguar vehicle without a valid third-party motor vehicle insurance cover. According to proceedings, the policy had lapsed just the previous day, an oversight the entertainer attributed to a lapse in memory amid a pressing family emergency.

Addressing the court, the DJ explained that he had been rushing to collect his child from school following a sudden illness when police intercepted him. That high-stress dash, however, compounded into a second legal hurdle involving a charge of disobeying verbal instructions from a uniformed officer.

Prosecution records indicated that Corporal Chelimo had directed the motorist to drive the vehicle directly to a police station after the initial stop. Instead, the entertainer reportedly continued driving toward Thika, prompting further police escalation.

Magistrate Onsarigo delivered a total fine of KSh 5,000 for the dual infractions, breaking it down into a KSh 3,000 penalty for the expired insurance cover and an additional KSh 2,000 fine for failing to comply with the officer's roadside directives.

Beyond the courtroom fines, the morning encounter created immediate logistical fallout for the entertainer. DJ Mo disclosed to the court that the prolonged detention and subsequent legal processing severely disrupted his travel itinerary, forcing him to miss a scheduled flight to the United States.

Following the court session, the entertainer shared a lighthearted video clip from inside the courthouse premises, using the incident to caution fellow motorists about the strict penalties tied to administrative deadlines.

"Forgetting to renew insurance just for a day has landed me in court. Anyway, are these courts for men or not? 😂 Remember to check yours," he posted online, turning his brush with the judiciary into a public reminder on vehicle compliance.
