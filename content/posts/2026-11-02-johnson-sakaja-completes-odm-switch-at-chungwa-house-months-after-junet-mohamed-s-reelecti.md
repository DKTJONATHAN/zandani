Nairobi Governor Johnson Sakaja has officially folded his UDA umbrella, completing his long-teased jump to the Orange Democratic Movement (ODM) during a high-stakes reception at the party’s Chungwa House headquarters in Nairobi.

Sakaja was formally ushered into the party on Monday, November 2, 2026, by ODM Deputy Party Leader Simba Arati alongside a delegation of senior party brass. The ceremony brings a decisive end to months of heavy speculation and escalating internal friction within the ruling United Democratic Alliance, officially positioning Sakaja to seek his second term as Nairobi chief on an ODM ticket in the 2027 General Election.

The move marks the ultimate realization of a blunt public warning issued to Sakaja during an empowerment function in Kileleshwa by Suna East MP and National Assembly Minority Leader Junet Mohamed. At the time, Junet openly told the city chief that remaining within the UDA fold would doom his political survival in the capital. Junet insisted that Nairobi remained an unshakeable ODM stronghold and argued that any candidate wishing to govern the city required the explicit backing of Chungwa House.

"If Sakaja desires to be re-elected as Nairobi governor in 2027, then he should come to ODM. If he decides to remain where he is… nobody will elect him," Junet told the gathering, laying down an ultimatum that ODM leadership spent months reinforcing through steady persuasion.

Yet Sakaja’s political pivot traces back beyond recent party pressure. The foundation for his transition was laid in late 2025, when the late former Prime Minister Raila Odinga publicly endorsed Sakaja’s county leadership and called on ODM members to support his governance and reelection efforts. Sakaja himself dropped explicit hints about his political exit in August 2026, publicly reflecting on his close personal relationship with the late ODM leader and confirming that wide-ranging consultations were underway across the city.

The final momentum for Monday's handover gathered speed over the weekend during the emotional climax of ODM’s 'Mwezi wa Baba' rally at Jacaranda Grounds on Saturday, October 31. Sakaja took to the podium and directly polled the crowd on his next political move. When the audience responded with overwhelming chants demanding his entry into ODM, Sakaja made his decision on the spot, publicly asking party leaders present on stage to begin his formal onboarding process immediately.

Forty-eight hours later, that public pledge materialized into administrative reality as Sakaja stood beside Simba Arati at Chungwa House to seal his ticket, setting up a completely redefined political contest for the capital in 2027.
