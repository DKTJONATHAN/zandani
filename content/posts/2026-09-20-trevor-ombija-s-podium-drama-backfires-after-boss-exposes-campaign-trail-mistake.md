Citizen TV prime-time news anchor Trevor Ombija took his parliamentary campaign to Nyakach Constituency with a dramatic account of standing up to state pressure, only to have his own newsroom chief publicly set the record straight.

Addressing residents during a political engagement in Kisumu County on Friday, September 18, 2026, the news presenter claimed that former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua had petitioned top management at Royal Media Services (RMS) to have him fired. Ombija, who is actively campaigning for the Nyakach constituency seat in the 2027 General Election, told the crowd that the alleged move followed a contentious studio interview, claiming further that his ethnic background was introduced into disputes concerning his job security.

“As I stand here to address you, the former Deputy President Gachagua once called for my dismissal from work because we confronted each other,” Ombija told the political gathering.

The hero's narrative on the stump was short-lived. Royal Media Services Editorial Director Linus Kaikai quickly refuted Ombija’s account, clarifying that no request for the anchor’s dismissal was ever made by the former Deputy President. Kaikai reinforced that even if an influential political figure had placed such a call, it would carry no weight against the media house’s established editorial independence.

To clear up the record, Kaikai took the matter directly to Ombija. The editorial director revealed that when questioned, Ombija conceded that his statements on the podium were untrue and admitted he did not anticipate his rally comments reaching the broader media space.

“I have asked Trevor about the remarks, and he says it is ‘a mistake and I regret it’,” Kaikai stated. “Trevor has also told me he ‘didn’t think the remarks would be picked up.’”

The underlying tension between the anchor and the former Deputy President traces back to a fiery live broadcast of *Monday Report* in March 2026. During that interview, Gachagua openly criticized Ombija, accusing the host of bias and unprofessionalism while labeling the segment a “tribal show.” Ombija immediately countered the attack on air, informing viewers that Gachagua had specifically requested the broadcast appearance himself.

Ombija joined Royal Media Services in 2018, rising to become one of the network's recognizable faces on prime-time news. However, his decision to balance anchor duties with active political ambitions in Kisumu County creates a challenging dual role. Campaign rallies often incentivize candidates to craft compelling stories of political victimhood to rally local support. But as Ombija’s Nyakach speech revealed, campaign trail rhetoric can quickly unravel when it collides with verifiable facts within his own newsroom.

As the road to the 2027 elections shortens, Ombija faces the double burden of maintaining broadcast credibility while pitching himself to voters in Nyakach. His unverified rally statement may have aimed to showcase political resilience, but the swift correction from his own editorial director delivered an unmistakable reminder: in modern political coverage, off-the-cuff podium claims are always on the record.
