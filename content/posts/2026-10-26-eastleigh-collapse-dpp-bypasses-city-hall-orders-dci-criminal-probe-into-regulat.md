State prosecutors have wrested the investigation into the Eastleigh building disaster out of City Hall's regulatory hands, assigning the Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) to scrutinize municipal approval desks alongside private contractors under standard penal law.

The shift, announced on Monday, October 26, effectively short-circuits the administrative blame-shifting that followed the multi-storey residential building's collapse on Friday, October 23. By bringing in external state detectives, the Office of the Director of Public Prosecutions (ODPP) has widened the inquiry to examine whether acts or omissions by Nairobi County officials during planning, statutory approval, and structural supervision carry criminal culpability.

### The Missing Paper Trail

The move followed a formal clarification by the ODPP dismissing claims that City Hall had sought prosecutorial backing against the project before the disaster occurred. 

According to the prosecution agency, Nairobi City County had never transmitted any inquiry file regarding the site—neither targeting the private developer, registered construction professionals, nor inspecting municipal officers.

"The Director of Public Prosecutions wishes to clarify that, as of the date of this statement, no inquiry file relating to the construction of the building has ever been forwarded to the Office by the Nairobi City County Government regarding malfeasance by the private developer, professionals or county government officials for perusal and directions," the agency confirmed.

Without a formally forwarded docket, prosecutors had remained legally blocked from assessing the site's structural warnings, issuing arrest directions, or initiating High Court or magistrates' court enforcement.

### Sakaja’s Prosecutorial Bottleneck

The absence of an active inquiry docket stands in sharp contrast to City Hall’s posture over municipal enforcement. Documents indicate that Governor Johnson Sakaja had formally protested prosecution constraints in an April 16, 2026 letter addressed to the ODPP.

In that communication, Sakaja argued that City Hall’s ground-level regulatory teeth had been blunted since early 2024, when the national gazettement of special county prosecutors was revoked. The governor documented repeated instances where municipal charge sheets targeting illegal construction and zoning violations were declined, delayed, or summarily thrown out in subordinate courts.

To restore enforcement capacity, Sakaja had requested the ODPP to gazette special county prosecutors, adjust prosecutorial charging guidelines, and officially clear six county-nominated legal officers to pursue municipal infractions.

Yet, while City Hall pointed to systemic prosecution bottlenecks to explain regulatory delays across the capital, the ODPP's revelation establishes that no specific case file concerning the Eastleigh development was ever placed before state attorneys for action.

### Detectives Step Into City Hall's Approval Chain

With rescue efforts continuing at the site, the criminal investigation ordered by the DPP bypasses the dispute over county prosecutorial powers entirely. 

The DCI mandate instructs detectives to track the project's entire documentary footprint from inception. This includes zoning clearances, architectural approvals, structural audits, ground inspection sign-offs, and compliance enforcement notices.

Crucially, the ODPP affirmed that criminal charges will apply evenly across the private-public divide if negligence or corruption contributed to the structural failure.

"Criminal proceedings will follow if the evidence established sufficient grounds to hold any of those involved criminally responsible, regardless of their position or status," the prosecution agency stated, cautioning against routine flouting of building standards.

Once the DCI concludes its inquiry, the completed file will be forwarded directly to the DPP for independent review and formal plea preparation.

The forensic turnaround comes against an urgent human toll. By Sunday, October 25, search teams and government monitors had tracked 47 people reported missing following the collapse, with only nine traced back to their families while rubble clearance continues.
