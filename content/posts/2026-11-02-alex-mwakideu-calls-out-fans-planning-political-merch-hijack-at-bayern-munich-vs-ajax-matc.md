Radio host Alex Mwakideu has drawn a firm boundary between matchday entertainment and political activism, calling out supporters who plan to turn an upcoming international football showcase into a political rally.

The veteran media personality voiced his frustration after online fans loyal to Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna suggested that spectators attending the Bayern Munich versus Ajax Amsterdam fixture should turn up wearing "Linda Mwananchi" branded merchandise.

### Calling Out Fan Zealotry

In a video clip published on his Instagram page, Mwakideu zeroed in on what he viewed as an unnecessary urge to drag stadium culture into the ongoing political tension between rival camps.

He argued that partisan followers were increasingly viewing every public venue through the prism of political battles, forcing ordinary entertainment events into the ongoing "Wantam versus Tutam" debate.

"Kuna watu siwaelewi, you are either for Sifuna or you are spoiling for Sifuna," Mwakideu said, questioning why an international exhibition match had to serve as a backdrop for party loyalty.

### Defending Sports Fandom

Mwakideu emphasized that politicians themselves are entitled to enjoy live sports purely as fans, separate from their public office or party movements. Pointing to Sifuna's own known interest in football, the presenter expressed doubt that the senator would even support turning a neutral sporting event into a sea of political gear.

"You know mpaka nikajiuliza, even Sifuna himself cannot do this. Infact ata Sifuna mwenyewe ni fan wa ball, politics aside," Mwakideu stated.

He added that fanatical supporters were overstepping by trying to turn every public appearance involving political figures into a platform for partisan mobilization, rather than letting spectators celebrate the game.

### Keeping Stadium Stands Free of Slogans

The upcoming clash between German giants Bayern Munich and Dutch side Ajax Amsterdam was officially announced by President William Ruto, with the fixture set to take place at the Raila Odinga International Stadium.

With political online factions increasingly organizing around public gatherings ahead of upcoming electoral cycles, Mwakideu insisted that live sports must remain an arena for relaxation and entertainment.

"Politics aside, it doesn’t always have to be 1 term and 2 term," Mwakideu added, urging sports enthusiasts across the political divide to leave political slogans at the gate and focus on the action on the pitch.
