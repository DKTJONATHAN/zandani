The Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority (EPRA) has directed all petroleum and liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) traders to transition to mandatory electronic clearance, tapping the state’s trade portal to eliminate manual border approvals.

The regulatory shift, implemented in partnership with the Kenya Trade Network Agency (KenTrade), routes cross-border cargo handling through the National Electronic Single Window System (NESWS). Compliant bulk importers and established logistics firms stand to gain faster cargo movement across inland borders, while rogue operators face an automated compliance wall that cross-references operational licences before cargo can be cleared.

Under the framework, cross-border hauliers will no longer rely on physical paperwork when moving petroleum products, cooking gas, and solid mineral fuels across land frontiers.

"Stakeholders are advised to ensure that all applicable licences and related compliance documentation are valid and up to date in preparation for the proposed rollout and subsequent submission through the platform," EPRA said in a public notice on Friday, October 30.

### Automated Checks for Drivers and Fleet Plans

The central operational change falls on licensed transport firms. Instead of tendering hard-copy route documentation at regional transit checkpoints, hauliers must upload digital journey plans directly to the single window before departure.

EPRA compliance inspectors will use the unified KenTrade system to verify the validity of commercial operating licences held by importers, exporters, logistics carriers, and individual transit drivers in real time. Any credential lapse or route variance logged on the system will immediately freeze consignment processing at frontier stations.

The rollout will proceed in stages. EPRA confirmed that the first phase will apply exclusively to petroleum products and bulk LPG consignments entering or exiting the country. Coal consignments will be integrated during a subsequent phase, though the regulator has not set a formal date for the mineral fuel rollout.

### November Public Review Across Six Hubs

To manage the transition, EPRA has structured a month-long public participation programme running through November 2026. The regulator plans to hold direct stakeholder sensitisation sessions across six key transit and industrial centres: Nairobi, Mombasa, Kisumu, Eldoret, Nakuru, and Meru.

Affected logistics operators, importers, and industry associations can also present written memoranda directly to the authority's headquarters, regional operational centres, or through dedicated submission channels.

The regulator indicated that an official enforcement date for mandatory digital compliance will be established once stakeholder representations are reviewed and system integration tests with border post terminals are complete.
