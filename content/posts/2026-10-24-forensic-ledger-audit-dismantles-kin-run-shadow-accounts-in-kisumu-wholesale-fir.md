A coordinated attempt to mask commercial theft as normal trade collapsed in a Kisumu court after forensic reconciliations showed how an enterprise accountant systematically falsified software records while routing Ksh113.2 million in customer receivables into private family accounts.

Delivering the verdict at the Kisumu Law Courts, Senior Principal Magistrate Fatuma Rashid convicted three members of the same family across all six counts submitted by the Office of the Director of Public Prosecutions (ODPP). The ruling brings formal closure to a 10-month diversion scheme executed against a regional wholesale distribution business between October 2020 and July 2021.

At the center of the prosecution’s case was an evidentiary trail showing that one of the accused abused internal system privileges at the firm to construct fictitious accounting entries. The fabricated records were used to explain away inventory shortages and hide client payments that never reached the company’s operating accounts.

![Undated file image of a gavel on the bench in the courtroom](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/Gavel%20on%20the%20bench%20in%20the%20courtroom.jpg.webp?itok=R_vCVJzN)

Instead of flowing into the distributor’s verified bank facilities, customer payments for wholesale consignments were routed into external mobile money and bank accounts registered under the names of the accountant's two relatives. 

Defence counsel had argued that the financial flows represented valid commercial arrangements within ordinary trading parameters. Magistrate Rashid rejected that position, holding that the state had proved an intentional, coordinated enterprise designed to extract company assets and cleanse the proceeds through external accounts.

A formal forensic investigation and audit report presented during the trial verified that exactly Ksh113,227,529 vanished from the business over the period under review. 

![Person Working on a laptop](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/some-with-laptop.jpg.webp?itok=n-IADNwt)

To substantiate the trail, the prosecution called an array of witnesses that included company colleagues, wholesale buyers who made the payments, commercial bank representatives, forensic accountants, and investigating officers who mapped the digital payment destinations. Their collective testimony documented how physical stock left the company's premises while the corresponding electronic payments were deliberately siphoned out of corporate oversight.

The magistrate determined that the two non-employee relatives actively facilitated the enterprise by receiving the diverted capital and laundering the stolen funds through personal channels.

All three defendants were convicted of conspiracy to commit a felony, money laundering, and acquiring or possessing proceeds of crime. The former accountant was additionally convicted on separate charges of theft and fraudulent accounting under the Penal Code.

The three convicts have been remanded pending sentencing, which Magistrate Rashid scheduled for October 29, 2026.
