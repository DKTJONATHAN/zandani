Wiper Party leader Kalonzo Musyoka has captured the top slot in prospective opposition ticket pairings, anchoring a three-way combination with Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna and Jubilee leader Fred Matiang’i that pulled 48% support in a new survey.

The findings, released on Saturday, November 14, by Swiss Poll International, place Kalonzo at 28% in overall presidential preferences, trailing President William Ruto at 37%. When pollsters narrowed the question specifically to an opposition flagbearer, Kalonzo's support climbed to 45%, with Sifuna following at 28%, Matiang’i at 11%, Rigathi Gachagua at 9%, and Kiharu MP Ndindi Nyoro at 4%.

Beyond individual rankings, the survey tested specific coalition tickets. A configuration pairing Kalonzo, Sifuna, and Matiang’i emerged as the clear frontrunner with 48% backing. An inverted arrangement featuring Sifuna at the top with Kalonzo as his running mate managed 23%, while a Kalonzo-Matiang’i-Sifuna lineup secured 12%. Kalonzo’s presence in all three leading combinations underscores his central positioning in current alliance math.

Despite the strong numbers for joint tickets, Swiss Poll International injected a note of caution into the data. The pollster emphasized that while the five featured opposition figures collectively drew 59% support compared to Ruto’s 37%, these figures should not be interpreted as a guarantee that opposition factions will successfully consolidate behind a single candidate.

The survey also probed separate political party popularity, revealing a distinct gap between personal preference and party affiliation. Ruto’s United Democratic Alliance (UDA) led party rankings at 34.1%, followed by the Linda Mwananchi Movement at 15.1%. Gachagua’s Democratic Change Party registered 9.8%, narrowly edging out Kalonzo’s Wiper Party at 9.1%, while the Orange Democratic Movement (ODM) recorded 7.5% and the Jubilee Party stood at 5.3%.

Conducted between November 5 and 10, the research relied on telephone interviews with 4,113 respondents across Kenya’s eight former provinces. The sample balanced demographic representation, with men accounting for 53.1% of participants and women making up 46.9%, while respondents aged 26 to 35 formed the largest single age bracket at 30%.
