The High Court has initiated a high-stakes transparency check on the government's financial plumbing, fast-tracking a legal challenge that demands the full disclosure of how public resources are being committed to the newly launched Dangote East Africa Petroleum Refinery.

Justice David Mburu on Tuesday, October 6, certified the petition as urgent, ordering immediate priority for a case that threatens to pull back the curtain on the state's newly minted investment vehicles. The directive comes less than a week after the high-profile September 30 groundbreaking of the multi-billion-shilling facility in Lamu, turning the celebration of the regional mega-project into an immediate battle over public financial accountability.

At the heart of the legal dispute is the state’s plan to acquire a 10 per cent stake in the refinery. Rather than direct treasury allocations, the government reportedly intends to fund this stake using public assets and resources managed by the National Investment Fund (NIF). Because the NIF operates largely outside the public spotlight, the petitioner is demanding that state agencies lay bare its legal framework, investment mandates, feasibility studies, and the exact financial models used to justify the partnership.

![President William Ruto and Nigerian billionaire Aliko Dangote during the East African Oil Refinery grounbreaking in Lamu on September 30, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/d0tKHQD5/67afc51a2b98.webp)

The scope of the requested disclosure is sweeping. The petitioner wants all records of agreements, valuation reports, environmental assessments, and due diligence documents preserved and made public. Crucially, the Capital Markets Authority (CMA) has been dragged into the suit as an interested party. The regulator is being forced to state whether it has quietly authorized or processed any fundraising schemes or investment offers tied to the Lamu refinery project.

While the court declined to issue immediate temporary injunctions to halt non-routine government spending on the project, the legal timeline has been highly compressed. Justice Mburu ordered the petitioner to serve the state agencies and the CMA within two days. The respondents will then have just seven days to submit their formal defences, with the case scheduled to return to court on November 12 to verify compliance.

This High Court petition is not an isolated legal hurdle for the state's partnership with Nigerian billionaire Aliko Dangote. It forms part of a widening, multi-front squeeze on the project’s financial and physical footprint.

![dangote-oil-refinery-nigeria.jpg](https://i.ibb.co/6RKpX8nr/819962f71818.webp)

Separately, the Consumers Federation of Kenya (COFEK) has filed a petition with the Public Private Partnerships (PPP) Petition Committee. COFEK is demanding specific details regarding the state's proposed Ksh65 billion stake in the refinery, which reportedly includes a Ksh21.5 billion initial seed allocation and the allocation of approximately 7,000 acres of land.

At the local level, the physical foundation of the project remains highly contested. Residents of Chandavai are actively challenging the acquisition of the land earmarked for the refinery in the Environment and Land Court in Malindi. The community cites unresolved ancestral claims, lack of compensation, and inadequate resettlement frameworks. That case is set for a critical hearing on October 14.

By forcing the state to defend its financial commitments in open court, the legal actions shift the conversation from the geopolitical triumph of attracting West Africa's wealthiest industrialist to the strict procedural rules of Kenyan public finance. The upcoming weeks will determine whether the state can keep its transaction structures private, or if it will be forced to expose the inner workings of the National Investment Fund before the High Court on November 12.
