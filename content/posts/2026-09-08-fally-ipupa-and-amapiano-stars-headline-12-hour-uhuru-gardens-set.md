Congolese rumba icon Fally Ipupa led a 12-hour musical showcase at Uhuru Gardens this past weekend, drawing thousands to the Langata venue. The event featured a cross-genre lineup, including South African amapiano stars Kamo Mphela and Nomfundo Khumalo, alongside local performers. DJ Joe Mfalme managed the decks throughout the evening, while the Kodong Klan opened the stage with a 100-minute set.

The Congolese star arrived at Jomo Kenyatta International Airport on Thursday evening. Traditional Maasai dancers greeted him on the tarmac before his transit to the city center. Ipupa spoke to the media upon his arrival, noting his long-standing connection to his Kenyan fanbase and promising a performance defined by high energy and technical precision.

Kamo Mphela and Nomfundo Khumalo joined the headline act after their own arrivals at Jomo Kenyatta International Airport. Organizers positioned the 12-hour show as a fusion of regional sounds, combining the rhythmic structure of Congolese rhumba with the percussive heavy beats of South African amapiano.

Kodong Klan maintained a rigorous pace during their early evening slot. The collective utilized pyrotechnics and coordinated leather stage costumes, setting a frantic tempo for the crowd before the transition to the international headliners. Fans remained at the Uhuru Gardens grounds well into the early hours of Sunday morning, marking one of the more significant concert attendances at the venue this year.

### What it costs you

If you were stuck in the inevitable crawl of traffic heading toward Langata or trying to exit via the Southern Bypass, the price of this concert was measured in more than just the gate fee. While promoters boast of 12-hour spectacles, the cost to the average Nairobi resident is the cumulative toll of logistics in a city that is struggling to move.

Fally Ipupa fills the house because he understands the currency of nostalgia and dance. He is not just selling a ticket; he is selling a night where the economy of the streets feels irrelevant. Yet, consider the fan who spent KSh 5,000 for a regular ticket, another KSh 1,500 on transport, and the inevitable markup on drinks once inside the venue. That is a significant slice of a monthly salary for a massive segment of the youth.

When international acts land at Jomo Kenyatta International Airport, the promoters gain the prestige, and the artists gain the paycheck. The fans gain a few hours of escape. But look at the empty parking lots at Uhuru Gardens on Monday morning and the fatigue on the faces of those shuffling back to work. We are reaching a point where these marathon events demand too much from a consumer base that is already exhausted. If you are sitting on Thika Road today, staring at the taillights ahead, ask yourself if the three minutes of a hit song you finally heard live was worth the ten hours of your life you lost to the logistics of the event. We are paying for the rhythm, but the silence afterward is starting to feel expensive.
