A sharp quarterly reallocation of retail and institutional cash has rearranged the top tier of Kenya’s collective investment market, propelling Standard Investment Trust Fund past long-term leader Sanlam to claim the industry's largest portfolio.

Fresh regulatory disclosures published in the Capital Markets Authority’s Quarterly Statistical Bulletin for the third quarter of 2026 show Standard Investment expanded its portfolio by 23 percent in three months. Its assets under management jumped from Ksh153.15 billion in March to Ksh188.62 billion by June 30, capturing a 19.88 percent share of the entire sector.

The surge unseated Sanlam Unit Trust Scheme, which grew at a comparatively modest six percent over the same period to close at Ksh171.38 billion, representing an 18.06 percent market share.

### Apex Concentration

The shake-up occurred during an aggressive expansion across the broader collective investment industry. Total funds under management rose by 11 percent between March and June, swelling from Ksh851.7 billion to Ksh948.7 billion. The three-month inflow added roughly Ksh97 billion to the pooled savings pool.

![CMA Chief Executive FCPA Wyckliffe Shamiah](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/cma-chief-executive-fcpa-wyckliffe-shamiah.jpg.webp?itok=fuVYgurS)

The regulator attributed the Ksh97 billion expansion to a mix of organic growth among legacy portfolios, newly registered sub-funds under established umbrella schemes that began reporting in the second quarter, and aggressive promotional campaigns by fund managers competing for yield-seeking retail capital.

Yet the latest figures demonstrate that capital inflows remain intensely concentrated at the top. Standard Investment and Sanlam together account for Ksh360 billion—nearly 38 percent of all money invested across Kenya's collective schemes.

CIC Unit Trust Scheme maintained third position with Ksh105.24 billion in assets, commanding an 11.09 percent market share after recording two percent growth over the quarter. Combined, the top three fund managers hold nearly half of the entire industry's wealth.

### Uneven Fortunes for Institutional Rivals

Beyond the top three, institutional performance diverged sharply as fund managers defended their market footprints against rising competition.

Britam Unit Trust Scheme held fourth place, registering a 15 percent quarterly expansion to push its portfolio to Ksh71.35 billion, which translates to a 7.52 percent market share.

In contrast, NCBA Unit Trust Scheme saw its asset base shrink. NCBA, which closed the quarter in fifth place with a 5.33 percent market share, suffered a seven percent contraction as its assets dropped from Ksh54.29 billion in March to Ksh50.54 billion in June.

![Money](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/money_2_1.jpg.webp?itok=z9Hyk8tP)

The lower half of the top ten remained dominated by institutional players and commercial bank-sponsored funds competing within narrower margins:

* **Old Mutual Unit Trust Scheme:** Ksh38.29 billion
* **Jubilee Unit Trust Scheme:** Ksh32.90 billion
* **Absa Unit Trust Funds:** Ksh31.39 billion
* **Coop Unit Trust Scheme:** Ksh27.09 billion
* **KCB Unit Trust Scheme:** Ksh26.03 billion

### Chasing the Mid-Market

Further down the regulatory register, independent asset managers and specialized vehicles maintained steady accumulation, even as they operate with significantly smaller market slices.

Etica Unit Trust Fund ranked 11th with Ksh24.87 billion, edging out ICEA Unit Trust Scheme, which closed at Ksh23.74 billion, and Madison Unit Trust Funds at Ksh20.56 billion. Ziidi Money Market Fund held 14th place with Ksh19.89 billion, sitting just ahead of Faida Unit Trust Funds with Ksh19.42 billion and Nabo Africa Funds with Ksh14.79 billion.

With collective schemes adding Ksh97 billion in a single quarter, the sector sits just Ksh51.3 billion short of the historic Ksh1 trillion milestone, setting up an intense contest for retail liquidity as managers enter the final quarter of the year.
