Andy Deh has found himself at the center of a fierce online backlash after asserting that romantic respect is tied directly to a man's physical capacity to use violence against his partner.

Known as the son of the late, revered reggae entertainer MC Fullstop, the digital creator drew immediate condemnation when he took to social media to weigh in on relationship dynamics. In a candid post that quickly circulated across local platforms, Andy framed physical dominance as a baseline for authority.

![Andy Deh //Courtesy](https://i.ibb.co/9HmdYFW8/f2a31fd00af7.webp)

"Hakuna vile dem atakurespect na anajua huwezi mpiga kibare," he stated in a post that loosely translates to, "There is no way a woman will respect you when she knows you cannot beat her."

The remarks landed during a particularly sensitive period for digital audiences in Kenya. The comments arrived right as internet users and commentators were actively grappling with a wave of discussions surrounding domestic violence, amplified by recent high-profile allegations involving digital creator Nihan and her boyfriend, Flossin.

Rather than passing quietly, Andy's assertion split social media users into polarized camps. While some commenters, such as user James Mokua, argued that the statement reflected an unvarnished truth when viewed without emotional filters—writing, "ukiskiza huyu jamaa bila feelings utajua anasema ukweli"—the overwhelming majority pushed back.

Other users pointed to mutual affection and autonomy as the true bedrock of partnership. User phillipe countered the narrative by noting, "dem akikupenda atakuheshimu hata kama we ni kiwete; respect doesn't come by force it comes automatically when there's genuine love." Meanwhile, user Waitherero underscored the friction the remarks caused with a sharp warning about physical confrontation: "Siku mwanaume.atanigonga itakuwa third world war."

The public clash over Andy's statements has once again thrust the broader conversation surrounding how romantic respect is perceived and policed online into the spotlight. While digital spaces frequently host provocative takes designed to drive engagement, relationship experts and commentators have continually stressed that physical intimidation or violence should never serve as a mechanism for conflict resolution.

As the debate continues to unfold across various digital feeds, Andy Deh’s remarks remain a focal point for discussions regarding creator responsibility and the normalisation of violence in contemporary Kenyan relationships.
