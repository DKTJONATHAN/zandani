Janet Mbugua has drawn a sharp line in the sand over the growing habit of political figures treating grassroots health advocacy as campaign rallies, pointing directly to the chaotic scenes at the recent Endometriosis Warriors event at the Kenyatta International Convention Centre (KICC).

The media personality stepped into the ongoing public debate following the September 27, 2026, gathering hosted by creator and activist Natalie Githinji. The event, designed to center on living with endometriosis, hit a wall of friction when nominated Senator Karen Nyamu took to the microphone and was met with sustained, disruptive “Wantam” chants from sections of the thousands-strong crowd.

While Nyamu’s appearance triggered immediate digital chatter, Mbugua shifted the focus from the heckling itself to the mechanics of how politicians infiltrate citizen-led campaigns. In a public statement on Wednesday, September 30, Mbugua contrasted the raw, lived reality of Githinji’s advocacy with the performative nature of political stagecraft.

“Natalie Githinji is the example of what politicians could never be. She used her own story as the door and walked the whole country through it to endometriosis,” Mbugua noted, before adding a blunt warning to political figures: “Politicians, stop making awareness events about you.”

Mbugua argued that the intrusion of elected officials threatens to drown out the very people the events are meant to uplift, drawing a parallel to how Members of Parliament have increasingly converted solemn community spaces like funerals into political pulpits.

The KICC showdown has since exposed a deeper rift behind the scenes regarding how public figures navigate non-partisan causes. Comedian and activist Eric Omondi, who was involved in organizing the gathering, revealed that organizers had established a strict preliminary understanding to keep politicians off the podium to preserve the integrity of Githinji’s campaign.

According to Omondi, Nyamu had reached out prior to the event expressing support for Githinji and subsequently sent a KSh 100,000 contribution. However, Omondi stated that the senator did not disclose her intention to physically attend and take the floor, short-circuiting the organizers' firewall against political optics.

Despite the hostile reception and the public scolding from both Mbugua and event organizers, Nyamu defended her presence at the venue. Maintaining that her participation stemmed from a genuine legislative interest in tackling endometriosis through Parliament, the nominated senator shrugged off the hostile chants as an anticipated occupational hazard.

“I had a choice to come or not to come. And for me, I cannot stop coming because there will be ‘One Term’ chants,” Nyamu stated following the incident.
