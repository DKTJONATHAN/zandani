Agricultural productivity in Central Kenya faces a sharp test over the next five days as the Kenya Meteorological Service (KMSA) warns of a significant cold snap affecting key production zones.

While much of the country anticipates moderate rainfall through Thursday, September 24, the primary concern for farmers in Nyandarua is the mercury. Forecasts indicate night temperatures will plummet to 5°C on Monday, hovering near that threshold through the remainder of the week. This level of cold poses a direct risk to sensitive crops and livestock, which are often vulnerable to sudden temperature drops in high-altitude regions.

The cold conditions are not isolated to Nyandarua. Across the Central region, including Nyeri, Murang’a, and Kiambu, night temperatures are expected to oscillate between 13°C and 15°C. Laikipia will see slightly lower ranges, between 11°C and 13°C. In the Rift Valley, specifically Nandi, Kericho, and Bomet, residents should prepare for consistent nocturnal temperatures between 12°C and 13°C.

### The Coastal and Eastern Outlook

Contrasting the chilly conditions in the highlands, the coastal strip is entering a period of intermittent moisture. Mombasa, Kwale, Kilifi, and Lamu are slated for moderate showers. In Mombasa, the rain is expected to persist across the morning, afternoon, and night on Sunday. Kilifi’s showers are projected to be more consistent, stretching from Sunday through the end of the five-day window.

Further inland, the pattern remains varied. Embu is forecast to experience the most persistent rainfall, beginning with morning showers on Sunday and continuing with moderate to light activity through Thursday. Meanwhile, the eastern lowlands of Kitui and Machakos are expecting light morning rain for the first three days of the forecast period.

### Broad Precipitation Coverage

Beyond these regions, the KMSA has identified a wide swath of the country expected to receive rainfall. This includes counties across Western and Nyanza, such as West Pokot, Trans Nzoia, Uasin Gishu, Kakamega, Vihiga, Bungoma, Homa Bay, Kisii, and Nyamira. Central counties, including Nairobi, will also see light rains, primarily concentrated between Sunday and Tuesday.

For residents in the affected areas, the primary challenge remains the contrast between the daytime weather and the rapid cooling at night, particularly in Nairobi, where temperatures are expected to settle between 13°C and 14°C. With the forecast spanning through Thursday, the meteorological authority has signaled that the current conditions are part of a broader, multi-day weather pattern rather than isolated storms.
