Kevin Bahati is stepping away from the studio to launch his campaign for the Mathare parliamentary seat—and he is handing his digital microphone directly to his eldest son.

In a public announcement on Friday, November 20, 2026, the veteran musician confirmed he is taking a one-year hiatus from recording and live performances. The 12-month break is designated entirely for his political machinery in Mathare, where he intends to mount a concentrated campaign for the National Assembly seat.

Rather than leaving his streaming channels dark during the sabbatical, Bahati announced he is officially turning his audience over to his 15-year-old son, Morgan Bahati. The teenager makes his professional debut with a solo single titled *Zoza (Nikifika 18)*, scheduled for a midday release on the official Bahati Kenya YouTube channel.

### The Strategic Holdout

According to Bahati, Morgan’s interest in music was not a sudden decision. The teenager grew up watching his father’s studio routines and tour schedules, expressing a desire to enter the booth from an early age.

However, the singer revealed he intentionally stalled his son’s professional entry until the teenager reached a stage of personal maturity where he could make independent decisions about his public life. Praising Morgan’s growth, spoken delivery, and self-assurance, Bahati poked fun at his own linguistic skills, noting that his son’s spoken English had already surpassed his own.

The timing aligns Morgan's entry into the music industry directly with Bahati’s departure for the political trail, converting the family's established channel infrastructure into a platform for the next generation.

### Fifteen Years in the Making

The debut marks a milestone in a parenting journey Bahati described as a 15-year "God-given assignment." Reflecting on his personal growth while raising Morgan, the singer acknowledged past mistakes and personal flaws, maintaining that his primary goal has been to position his son to eventually eclipse his own achievements in the creative industry.

With *Zoza (Nikifika 18)* entering distribution, Morgan becomes the third member of the household to actively release commercial music. He follows his father and his mother, Diana Marua—who records under the stage name Diana B—solidifying the family's multi-creator presence in Kenya's pop culture ecosystem.

As Bahati pivots his daily schedule to grassroots mobilization and town halls across Mathare, the immediate focus shifts to how the young artist handles the high-volume streaming audience built over a decade of his father's career.
