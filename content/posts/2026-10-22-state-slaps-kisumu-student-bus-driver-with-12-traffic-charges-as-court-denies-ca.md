State prosecutors have instituted a 12-count criminal indictment against the driver behind the wheel of a Technical University of Mombasa (TUM) student bus that overturned in Kisumu County, securing tight bail terms that block immediate cash release.

Appearing before the Nyando Law Courts, the driver denied all charges stemming from the August 7 crash at Boya along the Ahero–Awasi Highway. Principal Prosecution Counsel Douglas Moseti structured the state’s case to hold the driver liable not only for the double fatality, but individually for each passenger critically hurt during the transit.

### The Breakdown of the 12 Charges

The charge sheet filed by the Office of the Director of Public Prosecutions (ODPP) separates the casualties into two distinct statutory violations under the Traffic Act, Cap. 403.

![Bus ferrying Technical University of Mombasa students involved in an accident along Kisumu-Awasi highway on Friday, Aufust 7, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/GQZb2sX1/853b8d366027.webp)

The driver faces two primary counts of causing death by dangerous driving under Section 46 of the Act. The counts name Evance Odiwour Otieno and Rooney Owek Gakumba, the two TUM students who died when the coach lost control and veered off the carriageway. Under the statute, prosecutors argued the driver maintained a speed and manner of travel that jeopardised other motorists and passengers before leaving the road.

Beyond the fatalities, Moseti mounted 10 separate counts of driving without due care and attention, contrary to Section 49(1) of the Traffic Act. Each count represents an individual passenger who sustained severe physical harm when the vehicle rolled over.

### Journey to a Funeral

The bus was ferrying TUM students from the coast to Kisumu to attend the burial of Elvis “Beast” Otieno, a former student leader at the institution. 

![First responders trying to rescue injured Technical University of Mombasa (TUM) students involved in an accident along Kisumu-Awasi highway on Friday, August 7, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/nMRJLZhK/ea9954d9249a.webp)

Footage retrieved in the immediate wake of the incident documented severe passenger indiscipline moments before the vehicle lost control. Occupants were recorded sitting atop the exterior roof rack, hanging from window frames, and leaning across the carriageway toward passing motorists along the highway corridor. Seconds later, the bus left the paved surface.

The resultant wreckage overwhelmed local medical capacity across Nyando and Kisumu sub-counties. While Lisa Hospital treated and discharged 14 students within 24 hours of admission, Jaramogi Oginga Odinga Teaching and Referral Hospital admitted 15 victims with complex trauma, and one critically injured student required urgent transfer to the High Dependency Unit at Aga Khan Hospital.

### Bond Secured Without Cash Option

Following the driver's not-guilty plea, the magistrate granted release on a bond of Ksh500,000 accompanied by a surety of similar value. The court declined to extend an alternative cash bail provision, meaning the accused remains in remand until verified property or personal sureties are filed and cleared by court registries.

The case is scheduled to return to the Nyando Law Courts for pre-trial directions and witness scheduling on November 5.
