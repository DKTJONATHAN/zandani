Public service vehicle operators in Kenya are facing an expanded regulatory net that moves past simple roadside compliance into corporate governance, requiring SACCOs and business owners to maintain exhaustive internal audit trails for every vehicle, route, and crew member.

The National Transport and Safety Authority (NTSA) outlined the sweeping directives, asserting that business management systems must explicitly demonstrate responsibility for enterprise operations. Under the framework, operators are legally accountable for proving their crews are verified and supervised, business entities are properly registered, and maintenance records are rigorously archived.

![NTSA officers assisting drivers with free motor vehicle inspection services](https://i.ibb.co/4ZRf2c34/3fa2998c5e5b.webp)

The updated administrative posture is backed by severe statutory penalties under the Traffic Act. Motorists operating unroadworthy vehicles face fines of up to Ksh400,000 or a maximum two-year prison sentence, while unlicensed vehicles carry a penalty of up to Ksh10,000 or three months behind bars. Additionally, picking up or dropping passengers outside authorized bus stops or designated terminals attracts an immediate Ksh3,000 fine.

Beyond technical vehicle roadworthiness, the Authority is targeting terminal discipline and route adherence. Operators must ensure their fleets operate strictly within authorized corridors and designated stopping points, effectively holding company managements responsible for driver detours and informal terminal operations.

![NTSA Director General Nashon Kondiwa, in a meeting with senior management and staff in Nairobi on February 23, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/5WmvSDZ1/c87f36eb7378.webp)

The compliance push follows closely on the heels of a joint initiative launched with the Transport Ministry. That pilot programme, which kicked off on Monday, September 21, 2026, targeted more than 4,000 PSV drivers, SACCO managers, operators, and supervisors through free nationwide capacity-building sessions designed to bridge operational gaps in the transport sector.

By demanding verifiable paper trails for business registration, crew supervision, and vehicle maintenance, the regulatory framework leaves little room for informal management structures as transport authorities step up enterprise-level oversight.
