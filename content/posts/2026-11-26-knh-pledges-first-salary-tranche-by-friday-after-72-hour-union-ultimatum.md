Faced with a strict 72-hour ultimatum from medical practitioners, the Kenyatta National Hospital (KNH) management has committed to settling outstanding salary arrears in two distinct phases, beginning with an initial disbursement on Friday, November 27, 2026.

The concessions follow intensive late-night negotiations held on Tuesday, November 24, and continued talks through Wednesday, November 25. Health workers had downed their tools at the start of the week to protest delayed salaries alongside a backlog of unremitted statutory and third-party deductions that left medical staff stranded.

According to the management's revised payout schedule, the first batch of arrears will hit accounts on Friday, with the remaining balance scheduled for clearance on December 8, 2026. The financial commitment directly addresses union grievances concerning partial payments and frozen statutory obligations, which include employee bank loan repayments, insurance premiums, SACCO contributions, and pension remittances.

The industrial action was triggered following a high-stakes press briefing by the Kenya Medical Practitioners and Dentists Union (KMPDU), where union officials laid bare the financial strain placed on doctors receiving fractured salaries while mandatory deductions went unpaid by institutional accounts.

In an official communication issued as dialogue progressed, KNH administration sought to reassure staff regarding workplace welfare while pressing for a return to normalcy. Management emphasized that ongoing engagements with union representatives aim to permanently resolve outstanding administrative hurdles.

"As discussions continue, Management appeals to all staff to resume duty as we work together towards resolving the outstanding issues," the hospital stated, underlining the fragile balance required to maintain operations for thousands of patients relying on the national referral facility.

With union officials holding firm on accountability and the first disbursement window just hours away, the immediate operational stability of the hospital rests on whether the Friday financial tranche satisfies the demands laid down in the KMPDU ultimatum.
