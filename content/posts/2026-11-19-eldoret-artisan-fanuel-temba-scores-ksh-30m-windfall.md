When Fanuel Anyanga Temba set up his metal fabrication shop in Eldoret with just three machines, he was running a modest local operation. By the time President William Ruto took the podium for his State of the Nation Address, Temba had become a case study in how state-backed housing contracts can transform an artisan's bottom line.

Addressing lawmakers, President Ruto revealed that Temba secured subcontracts worth KSh 30 million under the government’s Affordable Housing Programme. The capital injection allowed the Eldoret-based artisan to expand his workshop equipment from three machines to 20, while scaling his payroll to employ more than 25 young Kenyans.

Temba was not the only worker to earn a direct shout-out on the nation's biggest political stage. The President used his speech to pull individual site workers out of the statistical background, positioning personal income turnarounds as the primary metric of the housing push.

Among those highlighted was Moses Wasike, a resident of Bungoma who previously struggled to secure consistent casual work. Under the ongoing housing projects, Wasike transitioned from intermittent site labor to supervising a crew of more than 150 workers.

In Nairobi, Diana Mwange from Mukuru experienced a similar shift in her daily earnings. Ruto noted that Mwange moved from irregular domestic work into regular site shifts as a painter on housing developments, securing predictable income through trade skills on active building sites.

These individual trajectories form part of a broader government ring-fencing strategy aimed at the informal manufacturing sector. Ruto disclosed that the state has reserved more than KSh 61 billion in supplier orders specifically for Jua Kali artisans, ensuring doors, windows, and fittings for state housing projects are sourced directly from local workshops.

The administration argues that this supply-chain integration is central to justifying the scale of the rollout. According to figures presented in the address, more than 313,000 housing units are currently at various stages of construction across all 47 counties, with some developments already handed over to buyers.

Beyond site labor and fabrication, the government reports substantial traction on the consumer side. More than 1.37 million Kenyans have registered their details on the Boma Yangu digital platform, seeking access to the housing units. To date, over 18,000 applicants have been formally allocated homes.

To make ownership accessible to lower-income earners, the program incorporates social housing arrangements that start with monthly payments of KSh 4,000, allowing households to transition gradually into homeownership.

The broader economic footprint of the initiative spans multiple technical and trade disciplines. Construction sites nationwide are actively drawing on engineers, architects, masons, plumbers, electricians, painters, welders, carpenters, and material transporters.

In total, the President maintained that the Affordable Housing Programme has generated over 1.1 million employment opportunities nationwide. As building work progresses across the counties, local manufacturers, Jua Kali operations, and site crews remain tied directly to ongoing project delivery timetables.
