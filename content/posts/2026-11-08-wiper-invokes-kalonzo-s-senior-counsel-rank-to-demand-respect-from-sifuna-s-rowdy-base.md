Stephen Kalonzo Musyoka’s camp is drawing a line under the recent chaos that shadowed his public appearances, and they are using professional pecking order to enforce boardroom decorum on the street.

Following an embarrassing incident at a Linda Mwananchi Movement rally where hecklers targeted the veteran politician, Wiper leaders in Mombasa delivered a pointed reality check to Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna: remind your followers who holds the Senior Counsel rank before letting them heckle the top table.

Wiper National Executive Council member Sheikh Twaha Omar laid out the grievance directly during a press briefing in Mombasa, framing the podium clash as a breakdown of professional and political protocol. While careful not to pick a full-blown personal fight with Sifuna, Twaha made it clear that the younger politician's energetic base had crossed an unwritten boundary.

“We respect Sifuna as a leader. However, whether he has already done so or not, he should tell his supporters to respect Kalonzo Musyoka,” Twaha said. “Sifuna is a lawyer, but Stephen Kalonzo Musyoka is a senior counsel and also a lawyer. He deserves respect.”

### The Linda Mwananchi Flashpoint

The friction traces back to an infiltration at a recent gathering organized under the Linda Mwananchi banner—the rising political lobby closely linked to Sifuna and other youth-driven figures in the opposition. What was meant to be a show of shared defiance against the Kenya Kwanza administration curdled into uncomfortable scenes that visibly unsettled the former Vice President.

Coast region Wiper chairman John Chamia stepped in to lower the personal temperature around Sifuna, making sure the party did not burn bridges with the Nairobi senator himself, but firmly pinned the blame on undisciplined infiltrators who hijacked the gathering to embarrass Kalonzo.

“I’m not here to condemn Linda Mwananchi but to denounce the act that embarrassed our party leader,” Chamia said. “Way forward. We are in the process of trying to unite to make one strong front. That was our waking call. We must be vigilant.”

Chamia also escalated the stakes around Kalonzo’s safety and status, claiming that the Wiper boss had landed on an active political “wanted list.” The alleged targeting, Chamia argued, means allied youth groups cannot afford to turn friendly podiums into hostile turf.

### No Running Mate Slot

Beyond policing platform etiquette, the Mombasa caucus made sure their opposition partners understood that Kalonzo's gentlemanly patience has expired ahead of the 2027 race. The party dismissed any suggestion that Kalonzo might play second fiddle again, ruling out another deputy slot under any coalition banner.

According to Twaha, Kalonzo had demonstrated sufficient loyalty to the opposition cause by turning down lucrative enticements from the state, including an alleged offer of a Cabinet secretary slot. That sacrifice, Wiper insists, guarantees him the front of the line.

“We are going with Kalonzo all the way to the ballot. If they refuse to name him the flag bearer, he will still vie against the others,” Twaha insisted. “He is not backing down for anyone this time round. We will not accept any other thing.”

Chamia also shut down backchannel chatter hinting at a possible detente between Kalonzo and President William Ruto, brushing the rumor off as political fantasy.

“Kalonzo and Ruto are like North and South; they can’t meet. Whoever is thinking that they can work together should be daydreaming,” Chamia said, repeating that Wiper’s sole agenda remains ousting Ruto with Kalonzo leading the charge.

Opposition principals now have about a month to sit across the table and untangle their internal rivalries before a planned joint summit. Until then, Wiper’s message to younger players like Sifuna is blunt: coordinate the messaging, manage the crowds, and remember the pecking order.
