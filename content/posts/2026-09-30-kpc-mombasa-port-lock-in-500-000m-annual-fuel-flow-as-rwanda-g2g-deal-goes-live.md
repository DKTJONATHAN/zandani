Energy and Petroleum Cabinet Secretary Opiyo Wandayi announced on Tuesday that Kenya's Northern Corridor fuel infrastructure will handle more than 500,000 additional cubic metres of petroleum annually, following the operational rollout of a government-to-government import framework with Rwanda.

The commercial activation of the route began at the Port of Mombasa, where the tanker *MT Sea Wolf* discharged a maiden 40,000-tonne consignment of gasoline and gasoil at the Kipevu Oil Terminal 2 (KOT 2).

The delivery sets in motion three bilateral pacts finalized on June 29, 2026, which reposition Kenya’s state logistics network—comprising the Kenya Ports Authority (KPA) and the Kenya Pipeline Company (KPC)—as the primary bulk procurement, storage, and transit channel for Rwanda’s national fuel supplies.

### Transit Volumes and Storage Integration

![Ongoing construction of a new berth at the port of Mombasa on June 7, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/JRWf2Nbh/2b94a1117b94.webp)

Under the framework, Rwanda’s petroleum flow through the Northern Corridor is projected to expand tenfold. The operational shift relies on utilizing Kenya’s coastal discharge and inland pipeline grid to move refined products from Mombasa toward the western transit hubs bordering the Great Lakes region.

Rwanda’s Minister of State in the Ministry of Infrastructure, Armand Zingiro, who received the vessel alongside Kenyan officials at the port on September 29, stated that the arrangement provides essential supply-chain stability as Kigali broadens its own internal fuel depots.

According to Zingiro, the extended holding capacity provided within Kenya's storage network gives Rwanda operational flexibility, cushioning its market against supply disruptions while domestic strategic reserves are constructed.

For Kenya, the arrangement secures recurring transit volume across KPC’s mainline assets, increasing throughput fees and anchoring refined product distribution within the Northern Corridor.

![A photo President William Ruto and President Paul Kagame in Kigali, Rwanda on April 4, 2023.](https://i.ibb.co/xtZnVrrL/eb83652054ff.webp)

### Terminal Capacity and Corridor Turnaround

The logistics schedule anticipates Mombasa receiving three dedicated oil tankers each month to service Rwanda’s market requirements.

Kenya Ports Authority Managing Director Captain William Ruto confirmed that the port has configured its offloading schedules to handle the recurring traffic. He noted that the multi-berth capacity at the modern Kipevu Oil Terminal 2 accommodates large-capacity tankers simultaneously, reducing vessel turnaround intervals and cutting demurrage exposure on regional bulk orders.

Once discharged at KOT 2, the consignments enter KPC’s pipeline network for onward pumping to regional distribution depots, where licensed cross-border hauliers collect the fuel for overland transit to Rwanda.

With the first 40,000-tonne volume now entering the pipeline, the two states will monitor monthly discharge pacing as Mombasa prepares for the arrival of subsequent chartered tankers scheduled under the tripartite procurement accords.
