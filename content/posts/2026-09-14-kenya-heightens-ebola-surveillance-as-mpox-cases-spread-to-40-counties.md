Kenya's public health machinery is currently fighting on multiple fronts, stretching screening teams along key transport corridors as an Mpox surge reaches 40 counties while health officials simultaneously prepare for potential Ebola threats along the western border.

### What we know

* The Interior Ministry is bracing for Ebola before a single local case arrives, deploying mobile testing labs and multi-agency border patrols.
* With 1,298 Mpox cases and 19 deaths recorded, urban hubs like Mombasa and Nairobi remain the epicentre of community transmission.
* Military and police involvement at informal transit points marks a significant shift from routine hospital-level containment to national security protocol.
* Concurrent outbreaks of measles in northern counties and dengue along eastern trade routes leave county health networks severely overstretched.

The sudden activation of the National Ebola Virus Disease Incident Management System on Monday underscores the government’s growing anxiety over regional disease vector movements. Although the Ministry of Interior confirmed that every suspected Ebola case tested so far has returned negative, the state is taking no chances. Testing infrastructure has been split across four strategically positioned facilities: KEMRI Nairobi, KEMRI Kisumu, the National Public Health Laboratory, and a dedicated mobile laboratory stationed along the busy Busia and Bungoma border circuit.

This aggressive posture against Ebola comes as health workers struggle to ring-fence an escalating Mpox outbreak. Official Ministry of Health data shows the virus has now established a foothold in 40 out of Kenya's 47 counties. As of September 6, the nation had documented 1,298 confirmed Mpox infections and 19 fatalities. Coastal transit hubs and capital commerce routes carry the heaviest burden-Mombasa leads the nation with 452 cases, Nairobi follows with 320, and the border entry of Busia has logged 115.

The geographic footprint has prompted Principal Secretary for Public Health and Professional Standards Mary Muthoni to identify seven counties-Mombasa, Nairobi, Busia, Makueni, Kiambu, Kilifi, and Nakuru-as high-risk zones requiring strict vigilance due to their density and connection to regional logistics arteries.

What makes this response distinct is the militarisation of border health controls. Multi-agency units incorporating the Kenya Defence Forces, National Police Service, and National Government Administration Officers are now stationed at informal porous crossings and long-haul truck stopovers. These teams are conducting real-time health screenings and conveyance inspections to intercept symptomatic travelers before they reach major population centers.

The strategy, however, is being tested by concurrent medical emergencies in peripheral counties. Even as national resources pool around Mpox and Ebola preparedness, local health units in Garissa and Wajir are battling Dengue fever outbreaks, while surveillance teams in Baringo, Marsabit, Wajir, Mandera, and Garissa are working to contain clusters of measles. With personal protective equipment and infection control stock now being stockpiled nationally, the pressure on county-level isolation facilities will remain high in the coming weeks.
