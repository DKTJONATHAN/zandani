Cleophas Malala has framed his withdrawal from Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP) central rallies as an orderly geographic division of executive duties, seeking to neutralize mounting evidence of an ideological breach at the top of the outfit.

Appearing on Citizen TV on Sunday, November 1, the DCP deputy party leader insisted that his visible distance from high-profile campaign circuits—particularly while party leader Rigathi Gachagua was touring the United States—reflected tactical delegation rather than an internal purge.

According to Malala, administrative logistics required party principals to partition the country rather than duplicate mobilization efforts in single bastions.

"We were given a responsibility to keep the party moving," Malala stated. "The country is too big. I took charge of the Western region, while [Secretary General John] Methu took charge of the Central region. What business will I be doing to mobilise people in Karatina?"

### The Mechanics of Regional Partition

![Malala and Gachagua DCP](https://i.ibb.co/YTQ6s3r9/b61ae41b8fce.webp)

Under that arrangement, Nyandarua Senator John Methu served as the visible custodian of the party's Mt Kenya heartland, spearheading rallies across Central Kenya during Gachagua's overseas tour. Malala, conversely, restricted his operations to Kakamega and surrounding western counties.

Malala contended that stepping away from camera-heavy platforms in Central Kenya allowed him to manage foundational party compliance, which he claimed included drafting the DCP manifesto and formalizing grassroots branch registers from polling-station committees up to county secretariats.

He pushed back against assertions that Methu had superseded him as Gachagua's de facto second-in-command, describing claims of an internal downgrade as misplaced commentary by observers unfamiliar with party operations.

"I do not feel sidelined. Why would I feel so?" Malala posed, maintaining that his role remains protected by his status as a founding stakeholder. "It is the only party I am a founder member. All these other parties I have joined them, but for DCP I am a founding member."

### Cross-Party Endorsements and the Sifuna Stumble

Yet Malala's tidy administrative explanation collides directly with political positions he adopted while operating independently on his Western turf. 

While camping in the region under the banner of the Linda Mwananchi Movement, Malala publicly declared support for Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna's prospective presidential bid. The endorsement ran diametrically counter to DCP's internal architecture, which positions Gachagua as its titular flagbearer for the top seat.

![DCP Deputy Party Leader Cleophas Malala during an event in Kakamega County on Octiber 24, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/j977wH37/c38676172e4c.webp)

The maneuver drew sharp rebuke upon Gachagua’s return from the United States. Rather than accommodating Malala's outreach as broad coalition-building, the party leader dismissed his deputy's statements as unauthorized personal views that held no institutional weight within DCP.

Gachagua went further, publicly labeling Malala a "mini Ruto"—an acute characterization signaling deep skepticism over Malala's loyalty and motives within the opposition outfit.

### Fractured Command Ahead of Coalition Talks

Malala dismissed the attacks and party-squabble reports as the work of "inferior-minded" detractors, maintaining that his standing inside the executive council remains secure.

However, the friction exposes the structural fragility of DCP’s command structure. By explicitly delegating Mt Kenya to Methu and retreating behind Western regional boundaries, Malala has established a territorial detachment that makes unified national messaging difficult to sustain.

With Gachagua seeking to consolidate DCP as an undisputed political vessel for Mt Kenya and beyond, Malala's open promotion of external contenders tests whether the nascent party can enforce internal discipline on its founders—or whether the regional demarcation marks the beginning of an irreconcilable leadership fracture.
