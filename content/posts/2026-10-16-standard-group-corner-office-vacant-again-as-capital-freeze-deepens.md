Standard Group’s attempt to rebuild its battered balance sheet faces renewed executive disruption after Chief Executive Officer Chaacha Mwita opted to step down on November 1, 2026, vacating the corner office just seven months after securing substantive confirmation.

The announcement, issued in a market notice signed by Company Secretary Catherine Kawira, leaves Kenya’s oldest multimedia house without a named principal officer. Mwita also relinquishes his seat as an executive director, with the board offering no explanation for the abrupt departure beyond standard corporate gratitude for his dedication and contribution.

The resignation exposes acute institutional instability at the Mombasa Road-based publisher. Mwita was elevated to the substantive chief executive post on April 1, 2026, following a nine-month caretaker stint that began on July 1, 2025. That interim run was itself triggered by the unexpected exit of his predecessor, Marion Gathoga-Mwangi, who walked away on June 30, 2025, after lasting barely a year in the role. 

Standard Group will now seek its third chief executive in under 16 months.

### A Board Reconstructed in Days

Mwita’s impending exit arrives in the middle of a wholesale reconstitution of the company’s non-executive oversight. 

Just days earlier, on October 12, the firm confirmed that two veteran non-executive directors, Edward Mwasi and Dr James McFie, had resigned their seats, with their departures backdated to August 31, 2026. Dr McFie, a fixture on corporate boards, had long provided governance and audit oversight during the media house's worsening fiscal cycles.

To fill the breach, the listed firm quickly drafted new non-executive directors. Effective October 15, 2026, Anthony Ambaka Kegode, Tom Odongo, and Elishah Njeru took up seats as independent non-executive directors. The newcomers arrive at an enterprise facing operational fatigue across its primary assets, including *The Standard* newspaper, flagship television broadcaster KTN, and Radio Maisha.

### The Frozen Ksh 1.5 Billion Lifeline

![File image of Standard Group Plc's Mombasa Road Headquarters in Nairobi](https://i.ibb.co/67hv2kck/84a62310b546.webp)

The rotating personnel in the boardroom and executive suite highlight a harsher underlying commercial reality: the collapse of the company's financial turnaround timetable.

Standard Group has struggled under continuous operational losses, weighed down by industry-wide disruptions and a crippling liquidity crunch tied to delayed commercial receivables. Crucially, the company has had to suspend a proposed rights issue that was intended to raise approximately Ksh 1.5 billion from shareholders.

The equity call was designed to provide the working capital necessary to restructure high-cost debt, settle mounting legacy liabilities, and modernize broadcast production. With that rights issue frozen, management's room to maneuver narrowed sharply.

Compounding the capital-raising roadblock is an unyielding cash-flow choke point: outstanding advertising payments owed by the state. Chronic payment delays through the Government Advertising Agency have drained cash liquidity across commercial newsrooms in Nairobi, leaving legacy media houses struggling to meet everyday overheads, clear vendor debts, and stabilize payrolls.

With no interim successor named and the rights issue parked, the newly remade board under Kegode, Odongo, and Njeru faces an immediate mandate: finding an executive willing to steer a severely capital-constrained operation through deep structural restructuring.
