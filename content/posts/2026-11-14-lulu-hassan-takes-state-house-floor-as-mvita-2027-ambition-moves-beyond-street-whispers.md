Lulu Hassan took the floor at State House, Mombasa, addressing President William Ruto and a gathering of cross-party figures on Thursday, November 12, taking her long-rumored transition into elective politics directly into the presidential spotlight.

The Citizen TV news anchor, who has been repeatedly linked to the Mvita parliamentary seat ahead of the 2027 General Election, was among the select attendees given an opportunity to speak during the session. Her active involvement in the high-level meeting moved what had been months of street-level branding and online speculation into a formal political venue.

The gathering brought together senior representatives of the broad-based administration, including ODM chairperson and Homa Bay Governor Gladys Wanga, Mombasa Governor Abdulswamad Nassir, Mining Cabinet Secretary Hassan Joho, Suna East MP Junet Mohamed, and former Public Service Cabinet Secretary Aisha Jumwa.

Should Hassan formally step into the race, she is positioned to challenge incumbent ODM MP Mohammed Soud Machele for the Mvita seat. Reports have pointed to internal backing within the United Democratic Alliance (UDA) positioning her as a preferred candidate for the urban constituency, though the party has not issued an official endorsement.

Speculation regarding her political trajectory first gained public traction in May 2025, when reflector jackets and printed T-shirts bearing the slogan “Lulu Hassan, Mama Mvita” began circulating across social media platforms and local community networks in Mombasa. Her presence at the Thursday State House forum marks the clearest indication yet that those early promotional signals reflected concrete political moves.

Hassan’s appearance in Mombasa places her at the front of a expanding line of broadcast personalities from Royal Media Services (RMS) preparing to trade prime-time studio slots for campaign trails.

That migration is already underway. Reporter John Wanyama formally separated from RMS in August 2026 to launch his campaign for the Malava parliamentary seat, having submitted his resignation notice to human resources on July 3. Wanyama had spent nearly 11 years with the broadcaster, rising from an Eldoret-based camera operator and reporter in 2015 to senior correspondent for Uasin Gishu County.

Several other high-profile RMS journalists are actively navigating similar transitions. Citizen TV anchor Trevor Ombija has signaled intent to contest the Nyakach parliamentary seat, currently held by Joshua Aduma since 2013. Reporter Stephen Letoo is preparing a bid for Kilgoris Constituency, while Citizen Radio host Tina Ogal has been linked to Embakasi East. Former news anchor Willis Raburu is pursuing the Kisumu Senate seat, and Inooro TV’s Michael Njenga has declared his candidacy in Kabete Constituency.

The steady drift of active anchors and reporters into elective politics has forced regulatory action from the Media Council of Kenya (MCK).

In May 2026, MCK Chief Executive Officer David Omwoyo confirmed the regulator was finishing updated guidelines to enforce strict boundaries between newsroom duties and political campaigns ahead of 2027. The framework mandates immediate suspension from all on-air roles—including hosting, anchoring, and field reporting—the moment a journalist declares intent to run, alongside fixed resignation deadlines.

The council also instructed media organizations to enforce balanced coverage standards to prevent newsrooms from giving favorable treatment to former colleagues on the ballot. Final gazettement of the MCK rules is expected following an editorial validation workshop with newsroom executives, setting strict compliance deadlines for broadcast stars eyeing public office.
