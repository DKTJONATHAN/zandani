The High Court in Mombasa has formally rejected the defense of voluntary religious fasting, handing down a 200-year prison sentence to Good News International Ministries leader Paul Mackenzie on findings that the Shakahola deaths were driven by an organized system of violent coercion.

Lady Justice Diana Kavedza Mochache delivered the sentence on Friday, November 6, 2026, following the conviction of Mackenzie and 28 co-accused on 191 counts of murder. The court established that the severity of the punishment was designed to ensure retribution, denunciation, and deterrence following the death of 191 people, 190 of whom were children.

In her sentencing remarks, Justice Kavedza stated that judicial objectives require punishment to be experienced as a tangible loss by the offender, given the scale of extreme suffering inflicted on the victims and their families.

![Controversial doomsday preacher Paul Mackenzie at the High Court in Mombasa for sentencing on Friday, November 6, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/Dsf5QbH/4d7f37c21ab6.webp)

### Systemic Intimidation Over Spiritual Devotion

The sentencing builds on the court’s October 19, 2026 murder judgment, where the prosecution proved beyond reasonable doubt that Mackenzie and his co-defendants orchestrated a structured murder enterprise rather than a permissive spiritual retreat.

The judicial record established that Mackenzie directed his followers to starve themselves to death under the pretext of meeting Jesus. However, the court found that the deaths were far from voluntary acts of faith. Instead, the ministry deployed a coordinated system of religious indoctrination, physical intimidation, and violence to enforce starvation.

The findings detailed a methodical progression: children were targeted first for forced starvation before the mandatory fasting regime was systematically expanded to young people, women, and men within the compound.

![Controversial doomsday preacher Paul Mackenzie at the High Court in Mombasa for sentencing on Friday, November 6, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/3YC9wGBL/388e4f0d6e1e.webp)

### Accountability for Mass Coercion

By ruling that enforced religious fasting constitutes murder, the court established that leaders using institutional discipline to deprive followers of basic survival needs face full criminal liability for deliberate homicide.

The conviction of all 29 accused persons across 191 counts marks one of the most comprehensive judicial hold-to-account determinations regarding cult-related deaths in Kenya's legal history.

Justice Kavedza affirmed that where indoctrination is combined with physical containment and violence to prevent escape or access to food, the resulting fatalities represent calculated murder, stripping away any claim of individual choice or religious freedom.
