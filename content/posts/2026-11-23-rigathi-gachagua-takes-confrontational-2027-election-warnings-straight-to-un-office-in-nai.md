Former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua has escalated his political warnings concerning Kenya's 2027 General Election, taking his grievances directly to the United Nations Office in Nairobi (UNON) where he accused the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) of partisanship and the current administration of widespread human rights abuses.

Gachagua, who leads the Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP), met with UNON Director-General Zainab Bangura in Nairobi on Monday, November 23, 2026. The high-profile consultation, held at what was described as the Office of the Second Deputy President, focused on Kenya's political climate, the state of human rights, and institutional preparedness for the upcoming polls scheduled for August 10, 2027.

In a statement following the meeting, Gachagua detailed his claims against the electoral body, arguing that its conduct had eroded public trust and created conditions ripe for potential electoral malpractice. He urged the UN to press the IEBC for impartiality and constructive engagement with all political stakeholders.

“We informed the UN that the IEBC as it were, has lost the confidence of the people of Kenya, which is a precursor to suspected mischief in the forthcoming polls,” Gachagua stated, emphasizing that the commission must remain objective and “not stay in bed with this regime.”

Beyond electoral integrity, the DCP leader presented a damning assessment of the current administration, alleging a deterioration in the rule of law. His claims included instances of political violence, abductions, extrajudicial killings, brutal attacks against young people, and the purported operation of state-sponsored militias.

Gachagua's direct appeal to the international body underscores a significant shift in how domestic political tensions are being framed, particularly from a figure of his stature. He warned that unresolved electoral grievances risk plunging Kenya back into its historical cycle of election-related violence.

He welcomed the UN’s expanding footprint in Nairobi while simultaneously calling on the international body to pay closer attention to democratic governance within the country. The petition signals a determined effort to internationalize domestic political accountability discussions ahead of the critical 2027 polls.
