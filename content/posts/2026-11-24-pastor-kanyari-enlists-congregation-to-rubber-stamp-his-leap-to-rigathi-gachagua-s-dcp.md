Pastor Victor Kanyari turned his Sunday church service into an impromptu political referendum, orchestrating a call-and-response routine from the altar to validate his move into former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua’s Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP).

Addressing his Salvation Healing Ministry congregation, the preacher staged his political alignment not as an individual ambition for the Kasarani parliamentary seat in 2027, but as an obedience test to the demands of his flock.

“Si mlisema tusikize ground? Na tukasikiza ground. Na ground inasemaje? Ground inasema chama ni gani?” Kanyari shouted from the pulpit, directing the microphone toward worshippers who chanted the party’s name back to him.

### The Wamunyoro Validation

The pulpit performance served as the public post-mortem of a high-profile visit Kanyari made to Gachagua’s home in Wamunyoro, Nyeri County, on November 12, 2026. 

Having teased the trip to his followers earlier as a pending "surprise," the preacher used the sanctuary to deliver on the reveal, framing his entrance into the DCP as a shared decision between preacher and congregation.

“My week has been very good, and I told you I would surprise you,” Kanyari told the crowd. “Sasa hapa ni DCP, na hapa ni Rigathi Gachagua, na hapa ni Wamunyoro.”

He then pressed the audience for explicit confirmation, asking them to cheer their approval before formally declaring his intent to contest Kasarani on the party ticket.

### Sanctuary as Electoral Stage

By converting his church service into a political audit, Kanyari joins a growing list of public figures attempting to blend spiritual leadership with electoral strategy ahead of the 2027 General Election. 

Rather than issuing a standard press release or holding a conventional political rally, the preacher leveraged the physical structure of his ministry to project an image of popular mandate, grounding his decision in local precinct sentiments.

The public commitment formally binds Kanyari to Gachagua’s newly established political vehicle as party building and regional realignments pick up momentum across Nairobi and the wider Mt. Kenya region.

For Kanyari, the altar remains his primary broadcasting hub. As the 2027 campaign cycle draws closer, his Kasarani bid now moves from sanctuary chant to formal party organization.
