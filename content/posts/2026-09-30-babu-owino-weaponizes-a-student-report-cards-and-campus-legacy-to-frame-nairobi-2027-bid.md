Embakasi East MP Babu Owino is steering his brand away from simple street-level activism, positioning himself instead as a certified "A-student" of administration as he guns for Nairobi’s top job.

In a newly released campaign pitch, the firebrand lawmaker is using his top-tier constituency scorecard and his legendary campus leadership days to build an executive resume, framing current Governor Johnson Sakaja as an "incompetent" executive who has outsourced the capital’s keys to the national government.

The transition marks a deliberate rebranding for Owino, who is mounting his 2027 Nairobi gubernatorial campaign under The Mwananchi Party (TMP) within the Linda Mwananchi coalition. Rather than relying solely on populist rhetoric, Owino is anchoring his bid on a highly specific metric: his number-one ranking in the 2026 Infotrak CountyTrak Performance Index.

"So long as you can be given the responsibility of managing a constituency and you do it well, then it’s only fair that you can manage a county also fairly well," Owino argued in a video interview shared across his social media platforms on Monday, September 28, 2026.

The Infotrak survey, conducted between January and May 2026, awarded Owino an 80 percent approval rating—placing him first among Kenya’s 290 elected MPs. The evaluation, which graded legislators on service delivery, accessibility, and the utilization of the National Government Constituencies Development Fund (NG-CDF), has become the cornerstone of Owino’s argument that he possesses the operational discipline required to run a capital of over five million people.

To build his case for the City Hall job, Owino is targeting what he describes as a structural abdication of duty by the current administration. He pointed directly to the controversial KSh 80 billion cooperation framework signed by Governor Sakaja in February 2026. Under that agreement, Sakaja handed over significant financial and operational control of critical county dockets to national authorities.

For Owino, this deal is not a strategic partnership but an admission of failure.

“If a Governor is relieving himself from the responsibilities that he was given by Nairobians; responsibilities of doing roads, giving them fresh and sufficient supply of water, creating a good environment for business, creating jobs, dealing with health and education… if you see a governor releasing those responsibilities to the national government, it tells you that the governor is incompetent,” Owino declared.

Owino’s policy pitch promises a sharp return to direct municipal management. He has pledged a hands-on approach to stabilizing the city’s erratic urban water distribution, fixing broken infrastructure, and ensuring the timely payment of county healthcare workers who have frequently downed tools over salary delays.

But the MP’s path to the city’s top job requires him to dismantle two persistent narratives: his reputation as a chaotic student politician and accusations of relying on ethnic voting blocs.

Owino has leaned directly into his past, reframing his controversial, record-breaking four-term tenure as a student leader at University of London-affiliated institutions not as a period of campus disruption, but as an early masterclass in governance. To Owino, winning repeated endorsement from "intellectual peers" serves as verified proof of his capacity to lead and deliver under high-pressure environments.

He has similarly dismissed claims that his political appeal is ethnically polarized. Pointing to his electoral dominance in Embakasi East, Owino noted that his constituency victories rely heavily on a highly diverse voter base, including a substantial Kikuyu demographic that has repeatedly backed him.

As the road to 2027 takes shape, Owino’s campaign is setting up a stark stylistic clash for Nairobi’s voters: a choice between Sakaja’s collaborative, technocratic devolution and Owino’s highly personalized, hands-on administrative model.
