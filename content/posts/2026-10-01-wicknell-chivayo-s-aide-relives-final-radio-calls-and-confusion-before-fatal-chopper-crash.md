Long before radio silence consumed the airspace over Surrey, the security aide traveling just behind Wicknell Chivayo knew the journey was unraveling. 

Both helicopters had lifted off on September 30, 2026, from a rural site where construction on the prominent Zimbabwean businessman's home was underway. Roughly 40 minutes into the return trip, the skies turned hostile. Seeking safer passage, the escort aircraft's pilot floated a detour.

“On our way back, the weather became challenging, and our pilot suggested that we reroute and go via Bromley instead,” the aide recalled.

What followed was a tense, audio-only chronicle of aviation distress. As the two aircraft pushed through thickening skies, the radio frequency between them began to register mounting panic. The pilot carrying Chivayo grew increasingly isolated inside a wall of gray.

“What I observed during the pilots’ communication was that the pilot flying the boss kept saying: ‘I’m in bad weather, I haven’t exited the cloud,’” the aide recounted.

Those transmissions tracked the aircraft over Surrey, painting a picture of a pilot wrestling with instruments while completely blinded by altitude and weather. The margin for error narrowed as the escort helicopter neared the end of its transit. 

Ten minutes out from their intended touchdown, the final, fatal exchange played out across the airwaves. The escort pilot called in their impending arrival, prompting a disoriented response from the leading aircraft.

“The pilot flying the boss appeared confused, asking, ‘Which destination?’ Our pilot responded that Marondera, but no reply came,” the aide said. “All communication with the other helicopter was lost.”

That severed connection preceded the impact that claimed the lives of all occupants aboard, including Chivayo. 

In the wake of the disaster, the aide also sought to clarify the immediate casualty figures circulating in public reports. While initial accounts suggested six lives had been lost, he maintained that the actual death toll from the crash was five people, though he did not detail the full identities of all victims.

Official investigators have yet to issue a definitive finding on the exact mechanical or meteorological cause of the tragedy, leaving the final chapter of the high-profile businessman's journey anchored to those last desperate exchanges over the Zimbabwean countryside.
