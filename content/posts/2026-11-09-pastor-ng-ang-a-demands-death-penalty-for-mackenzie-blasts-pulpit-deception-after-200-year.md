Pastor James Ng'ang'a has rejected the landmark 200-year prison sentence handed to doomsday cult leader Paul Nthenge Mackenzie, insisting that a custodial term is far too lenient for orchestrating mass deaths under the guise of religious fasting.

Reiterating his long-standing disdain for deceptive spiritual leaders, the 73-year-old televangelist argued that High Court Judge Diana Kavedza Mochache should have handed down the maximum possible punishment given the scale of human loss uncovered in Kilifi County.

Ng'ang'a zeroed in on the core contradiction of Mackenzie’s doctrine, questioning how followers were persuaded to starve themselves to death while the cult leader remained unharmed.

“Jaji hapo hata ungemhukumu kunyongwa tu aende mtu anaambia watu wafunge yeye hakufi, agent wa shetani anatumia jina la Yesu,” Ng’ang’a said, directly accusing Mackenzie of hijacking Christian theology for destructive ends.

He pushed the critique further, challenging the theological logic that sold terminal starvation as a doorway to salvation: "Yesu gani huyo? Eh Yesu gani huyo? Watu wafunge wakufe waende mbinguni."

### Calling Out Pulpit Theatrics

Beyond his condemnation of the Shakahola tragedy, Ng'ang'a used the ruling as a platform to address what he views as a broader wave of counterfeit spirituality taking root across Kenya. He warned fellow preachers to dismantle theatrical deceptions, framing legal and divine consequences as inevitable.

"Utavuna kule ulikopanda. Waongo muache uongo. Me I don't support dramas. Manabii wa uongo," he stated.

When asked about potential legal scrutiny or threats of incarceration aimed at his own high-profile ministry, the veteran preacher shrugged off the pressure with characteristic indifference, citing his age as a buffer against institutional intimidation.

"Me, I'm 73. Hata ukanifuga hiyo mimi nitakuwa tu niko nyumbani tu," he noted.

### The Legal Scope of the Shakahola Verdict

The scathing reaction from the pulpit comes on the heels of one of the most grueling legal proceedings in Kenya’s modern history. At the Mombasa Law Courts, Judge Mochache convicted Mackenzie and 28 co-accused across 191 counts of murder—a charge sheet comprising 190 children and one adult.

The resulting 200-year prison sentence was split down the middle: 100 years for murder by starvation and an additional 100 years for direct homicide. The conviction was built on testimony from 120 prosecution witnesses following extensive exhumation operations in Kilifi County’s remote Shakahola Forest, where emergency teams unearthed a total of 429 bodies from shallow mass graves.

While residents of Furunzi village in Malindi welcomed the severe sentence, Mackenzie’s extended family in Machakos County rejected the High Court’s findings and signaled their intention to file an appeal.

### Personal Surrenders and Pending Trials

In the wake of the judgment, personal fractures within Mackenzie's immediate family have also surfaced. His son, Dan Mackenzie, publicly acknowledged that defying his father’s rigid teachings as a child ultimately saved his life.

"Escaping this cult was just rebellious. I didn't want to join or going to church when I was a kid. So, he just gave up on me and it didn't involve me to go to Shakahola," the younger Mackenzie revealed.

Despite the 200-year sentence, Paul Mackenzie's legal battles are far from concluded. The former taxi driver turned preacher remains in custody as state prosecutors prepare to take him through separate proceedings involving terrorism charges and additional deaths linked to his isolated forest community.
