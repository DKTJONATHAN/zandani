David Maraga has spent much of his public life defined by strict constitutional boundaries, but on Tuesday, the retired Chief Justice offered a rare, sweeping look into the private domestic dynasty that predates his legal career by decades.

Taking to his official X platform on October 6, 2026, Maraga announced the passing of his elder sister, Sabina Kemunto Otiambo, confirming she breathed her last at the extraordinary age of 107. Her departure draws the curtain on a century-long matriarchal reign that quietly linked colonial-era Kisii to a sprawling modern lineage counting 53 direct grandchildren and great-grandchildren.

Rather than framing the passing through solemn heartbreak alone, Maraga deliberately framed her departure around celebration, emphasizing a life that ran for over ten full decades across multiple generations.

“It is with heavy hearts, but endless gratitude for a life richly lived, that we share the passing of our beloved mother, sister, grandmother, and great-grandmother, Mrs. Sabina Kemunto Otiambo,” Maraga stated.

### A Century-Long Matriarchal Lineage

Sabina was the widow of the late Raphael Otiambo Nyangena, an union that anchored a massive household through the shifting political and cultural eras of modern Kenya. Her longevity meant that she did not simply witness history from the sidelines; she lived long enough to oversee five living generations within her own compound.

Over the course of her life, Sabina bore eight children. She is survived by six: Grace Moraa Ochoi, David Nyangau, Simon Monari, Eunice Kemuma, Job Miranga, and Florence Nyanchama. Two of her sons, Peterson Nyambane and Kamanda, preceded her in death.

Yet it is the sheer volume of her downstream descendants that paints the clearest picture of her role as the family's center of gravity. Across the extended family network, Sabina’s direct branches count 53 grandchildren and great-grandchildren, a formidable legacy that turned her into an institutional figure within the larger clan.

### The Last Standing Siblings

Beyond her role as a grandmother to dozens, Sabina’s death marks a poignant shift in Maraga’s own generational cohort. Her passing leaves the former Chief Justice and his brother, James Ochengo, as the only two surviving siblings of their immediate nuclear family.

Before her death, the expansive sibling circle had already buried six of their brothers and sisters: Onduko, Ongondi, Barake, Nyanduko, Okongo, and Kemuma. Sabina’s long lifespan made her the elder guardian of their shared childhood memories, outliving most of her peers by several decades.

For a public figure who stepped away from the Supreme Court bench in 2021 into an elder statesman lifestyle, Maraga's tribute highlights the deep cultural roots that anchored his home life away from Nairobi's judicial corridors. The family is expected to coordinate formal farewell arrangements in the coming days as relatives and the dozens of direct descendants gather to honor the centenarian.
