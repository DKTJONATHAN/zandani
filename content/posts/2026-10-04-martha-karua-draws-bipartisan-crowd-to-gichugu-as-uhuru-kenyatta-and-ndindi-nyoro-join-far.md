Martha Karua stood at the center of a rare political truce in Kirinyaga County on Sunday, as leaders from rival ends of Kenya’s political landscape gathered to stand with the People’s Liberation Party leader during the final send-off of her 98-year-old father, Mwalimu Jackson Karua.

The funeral service, hosted on the grounds of Mugumo Primary School in Gichugu, transformed the quiet local venue into a high-profile convergence of political heavyweights. Headlining the guest list was former President Uhuru Kenyatta, whose appearance alongside Wiper party leader Kalonzo Musyoka and former Interior Cabinet Secretary Fred Matiang’i marked a prominent public gathering of senior opposition and former executive figures.

Yet the turnout in Gichugu extended across political divides. Sitting within the same mourning precinct were key ruling coalition figures, including Kiharu MP Ndindi Nyoro, alongside prominent opposition lawmakers and governors. Among those in attendance were Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna, Siaya Governor James Orengo, Democratic Action Party of Kenya (DAP-K) leader Eugene Wamalwa, Embakasi East MP Babu Owino, and former Meru Governor Kawira Mwangaza, alongside several members of the National Assembly and Senate.

The solemn service in Gichugu marked the conclusion of a week of public remembrances following Mwalimu Jackson Karua’s death on Sunday, September 27, 2026. Before returning to his ancestral home for burial, the veteran educator was honored at a requiem mass on Thursday, October 1, 2026, at All Saints Cathedral in Nairobi, which initiated a series of high-level condolence visits to the Karua family residence.

Mwalimu Jackson Karua spent decades as a respected teacher and community pillar in Kirinyaga, remembered by attendees for his long service in education and his role as the family patriarch.

Sunday’s ceremony in Gichugu brought the public rites to a close, with attending leaders offering condolences to the Karua family before the procession moved to the private family interment.
