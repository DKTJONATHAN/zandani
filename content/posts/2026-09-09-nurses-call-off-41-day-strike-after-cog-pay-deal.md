Nurses across Kenya have called off their 41-day strike after union leaders signed a pay agreement with county governors in Nairobi. The Kenya National Union of Nurses and Midwives instructed its members to return to public hospitals immediately, ending a five-week standoff that had completely grounded healthcare services in 47 counties.

### What we know

- KNUNM Secretary General Seth Panyako directed all nurses to report back to work within 24 hours.
- The Council of Governors agreed to raise the monthly risk allowance by Ksh 8,000 and uniform allowance by Ksh 5,000.
- The total monetary increase in monthly allowances now stands at Ksh 13,000 per nurse across all counties.
- The deal averted a parallel nationwide strike by doctors under the Kenya Medical Practitioners, Pharmacists and Dentists Union.
- Nurses began their strike action on July 29, 2026, leaving public facilities without essential nursing care for nearly six weeks.

Seth Panyako announced the end of the industrial action following day-long closed-door negotiations with county bosses at the Council of Governors offices. Speaking to union members and journalists outside the venue, Panyako invoked his constitutional powers under the union charter to direct nurses back to work. He stated that duty schedules across county hospitals must normalize without further delay.

County leaders agreed to the deal following high-level discussions that touched on State House consultations. Council of Governors Chairman Ahmed Abdullahi confirmed that counties will implement the Ksh 8,000 increase in risk allowance alongside a Ksh 5,000 raise in uniform money. The agreement resolves the core financial demands that had locked both parties in a prolonged stalemate.

The breakthrough halted a worsening crisis across the public health sector. Over the past week, nurses had pitched camp directly outside the Council of Governors premises in Nairobi, promising daily sit-ins until county chiefs addressed their grievances. Patients in public health facilities including Coast General Hospital in Mombasa, Kisumu County Referral Hospital, and Mbagathi Hospital in Nairobi were forced to seek expensive private care or go without treatment.

The agreement also neutralized a major threat from medical officers. The Kenya Medical Practitioners, Pharmacists and Dentists Union had issued a seven-day strike notice over the crisis. Doctors argued that the prolonged absence of nurses placed an unsustainable workload on medical officers in busy casualty wards and maternity wings.

### Why it matters
If you are stuck in traffic on Thika Road tonight wondering whether to rush a sick relative to Muthaiga or pay private hospital rates in Parklands, this deal gives you breathing space. The nurses won their money, but county governments took 41 days of empty wards to concede basic allowances. That delay cost lives in places like Machakos Level 5 and Kakamega General while health executive officers traded press statements in Nairobi. Ahmed Abdullahi and his fellow governors settled only when doctors threatened to join the walkout. That tells you everything about county priority lists. The Ksh 13,000 monthly raise will land in nurses' accounts, yet county finance departments still delay basic medical supplies and equipment. A nurse back at work without oxygen cylinders in Mama Lucy Hospital is just a spectator in a white uniform. Governors saved their political face, doctors withdrew their strike threat, but the fragile state of public health facilities remains exposed.
