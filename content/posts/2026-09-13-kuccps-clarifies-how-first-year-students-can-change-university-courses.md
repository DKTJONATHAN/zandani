The Kenya Universities and Colleges Central Placement Service (KUCCPS) declared on Sunday that individual universities retain exclusive authority over internal course adjustments, handing immediate administrative relief to thousands of newly admitted first-year students eager to realign their academic paths without central agency red tape.

### What we know

* KUCCPS drew a firm operational line between campus-level programme switches and national admissions, decentralising administrative leverage directly to university academic registrars.
* First-year students dodge central bureaucracy if staying on the same campus, leaving individual faculty deans to determine seat capacity and departmental cut-off points.
* Cross-institutional movers who missed the August cutoff gain rare breathing space, as the 2025 examination cohort retains eligibility to migrate universities through 2027.
* The placement agency curbed a looming administrative bottleneck by offloading routine faculty swaps while it works through backlogged inter-varsity applications on its portal.

“Interested students should engage their respective universities as the process is initiated internally at the universities,” KUCCPS stated in an advisory directed at the September 2026 intake.

Addressing concerns from candidates seeking to shift academic departments under the same roof, the agency clarified that institutional senates dictate their own criteria, capacity quotas, and submission deadlines.

On the national front, the agency moved to calm tensions among candidates seeking to move between different higher learning institutions after the formal application window lapsed on August 14. KUCCPS confirmed that those locked out of the initial cycle will not forfeit their mobility rights.

"If they sat for KCSE in 2025, they are eligible for transfer this year, 2026, and/or next year 2027," the placement body noted, adding that ongoing inter-institutional applications are actively being audited.

Applicants awaiting inter-university decisions have been instructed to monitor their online dashboards, where validated approval letters will be transmitted directly once clearing concludes across participating institutions.

The operational separation unburdens both incoming undergraduates and tertiary administrators during an orientation season defined by logistical friction. By routing internal transfers to institutional academic boards, the agency frees its technical infrastructure to handle wider regional student distributions, allowing local campuses to manage lecture hall vacancies based on real-time attrition and cluster requirements.
