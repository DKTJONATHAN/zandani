Manyatta Member of Parliament John Gitonga Mukunji has formally fronted a push to rename Embu County to Embu-Mbeere County, asserting that dropping the community from the official identity has systematically entrenched economic and developmental neglect.

The proposed change targets direct benefits for the population of Mbeere, an expansive region that the lawmaker argues remains structurally starved of tap water, electricity, and basic public works despite generating critical resources for the devolved unit.

Mukunji, who represents the urban and comparatively developed Manyatta constituency, stated during a media briefing on Saturday that county resource allocations and administrative visibility have mirrored the single-name title to the detriment of outlying wards.

"I have given a proposal to change Embu County to Embu-Mbeere County. When you reach Manyatta, you will know you are in Embu County because there is enough water, electricity, and development at large. But when you extend to Mbeere, it is marginalised," Mukunji stated.

### The Infrastructure Deficit

![Manyatta MP John Gitonga Mukunji addressing the press at a past event](https://i.ibb.co/p6jyZ8t8/da7cd67b3695.webp)

According to the lawmaker, the disparity is sharpest in basic utility delivery. Mukunji pointed out that while freshwater sources within Mbeere supply substantial volumes of water distributed across the county, native households in the catchment basins still lack piped connections.

The lawmaker added that untapped mineral reserves in Mbeere have similarly failed to translate into local wealth or capital expenditure, creating an unequal regional dynamic within the same devolved boundary.

"If you are neglected by name, then even in terms of development, you are neglected," Mukunji argued. "That is why I have the proposal to bring equality for the whole county."

To justify the twin nomenclature, Mukunji cited the precedent of Taita Taveta County, where dual identity was preserved to acknowledge distinct native populations under a unified devolved administration.

"Mbeere people have enough resources but are still neglected. With immediate effect, we will demand Embu-Mbeere County, just like we have Taita-Taveta County," he said.

### The Constitutional Mountain Ahead

![A statue in the center of Embu town](https://i.ibb.co/C5WfRkbf/aa53ce31d140.webp)

Despite the political traction of local equity debates, altering a county’s registered identity requires navigating Kenya's most rigid legislative mechanisms. Under Article 6(1) of the Constitution, the national territory is bound to the 47 distinct devolved units enumerated in the First Schedule, where Embu is codified as County 14.

Changing that entry cannot occur through an executive order or a standard County Assembly resolution. The measure must take the path of a formal constitutional amendment bill.

Under parliamentary procedure outlined in Article 256, Mukunji would have to sponsor an amendment bill that secures a two-thirds supermajority vote across both the National Assembly and the Senate through multiple legislative readings. 

Alternatively, Article 257 provides for a popular initiative route, requiring at least one million registered voter signatures verified by the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC), subsequent endorsement by at least 24 county assemblies, and eventual passage by Parliament.

While the Constitution explicitly lists issues that automatically trigger a national referendum under Article 255(1)—such as changes to territory, devolution structures, or the bill of rights—it does not expressly clarify whether modifying an individual county's nomenclature in the First Schedule mandates a national plebiscite.

Mukunji's proposal now faces its primary political test in whether fellow legislators from the region and the wider bicameral House will expend scarce legislative capital to steer a First Schedule amendment through the floor.
