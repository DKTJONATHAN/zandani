Gospel entertainer Samuel Muraya, better known as DJ Mo, and his wife, gospel minister Linet Munyali (Size 8), have built a reputation for documenting the friction points of their marriage in full view of the public. Now, that refusal to project a pristine public image has created an unexpected secondary outcome: their personal phone lines have become an informal emergency desk for prominent figures struggling to keep their own relationships afloat.

Speaking on Thursday, November 5, 2026, during a recorded podcast session with gospel musician Daddy Owen, the couple disclosed that high-profile individuals across various social and professional spheres regularly reach out to them in secret when private marital crises escalate.

### The Peer-to-Peer Intervention Desk

According to DJ Mo, the decision to speak openly about their personal rough patches stripped away the intimidating barrier that often prevents public figures from seeking counseling. Rather than presenting themselves as marital experts with clean records, their documented stumbles signaled to peers that they could discuss severe friction without fear of judgment.

"There are people who call us. Prominent people, *kimeumana*, hata tunashangaa huyu tuta advice aje," DJ Mo explained during the session. "But anasema, whatever you guys are going through, is what we go through. Sisi huweka tu vile iko."

DJ Mo noted that this unvarnished approach has directly stabilized multiple high-stakes relationships whose participants felt unable to turn to formal channels or close associates.

"The testimonies zenye tumepata out of being real, zimesave marriage mob sana," he added, tying their peer interventions directly to their refusal to maintain a polished public narrative.

### Pride as a Structural Vulnerability

Reinforcing her husband's assessment, Size 8 pointed to institutional pride and the demand for digital perfection as the primary factors that turn manageable marital disputes into quiet breakdowns among high-status pairs.

In her view, public figures frequently trap themselves in untenable domestic situations because admitting failure damages the brand capital built around their personal lives.

"Pride is what makes people not want to show a part of their life because you want to look perfect," Size 8 told Daddy Owen. "Hakuna mtu perfect. Jesus ndio atusave; his nakedness was seen for the world."

She argued that dismantling the fear of public scrutiny is a prerequisite for resolving deep-seated relational hurdles. By encouraging high-profile callers to acknowledge their vulnerabilities rather than hiding behind curated social media updates, the couple aims to lower the stakes of private reconciliation.

### Shifting the Public Playbook

The revelation highlights a broader shift in how public figures navigate private adversity in Kenya’s digital entertainment ecosystem. Historically, public figures experiencing domestic instability have relied on swift denial, curated silence, or aggressive public relations suppression.

By openly discussing the distress calls coming into their private phones, DJ Mo and Size 8 are positioning their unscripted personal history not merely as past tabloid fodder, but as operational proof that high-profile marriages require active, vulnerable maintenance behind closed doors.
