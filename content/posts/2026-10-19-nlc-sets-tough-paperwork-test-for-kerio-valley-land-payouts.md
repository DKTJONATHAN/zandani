Communities settled along the 124-kilometre transport corridor cutting through the Kerio Valley face a demanding administrative hurdle next month, as the National Land Commission (NLC) conditions road-project compensation on formal banking, tax, and property documentation across land officially catalogued as unregistered community property.

The inquiry hearings, published under an October 16 gazette notice, will clear the right-of-way for a major Kenya National Highways Authority (KeNHA) highway linking Barpello, Tot, Sigor, and Marich Pass across Elgeyo Marakwet and West Pokot counties.

Yet the commission’s statutory conditions create immediate friction on the ground: pastoralist families and communal occupants must present individual Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA) PIN certificates, national identification cards, verified bank account details, and written land ownership claims before appearing before hearing officers.

### Three Construction Segments, Six Unregistered Belts

The KeNHA corridor runs through three successive segments across the remote, topographically challenging rift floor. 

![A section of Maralal–Suyani–Baragoi (A4) Road, October 1, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/DgS8Hx8R/553db33b5f64.webp)

The initial stretch covers 43 kilometres from Barpello (Moru) to Tot Junction. The second leg runs 46 kilometres, extending from Tot Junction through Chesegon to the Kopasi River. The final 35-kilometre section pushes north from Kopasi River to join the highway network at Marich Pass.

In its formal gazette disclosures, the commission identified six continuous clusters in Elgeyo Marakwet as unregistered community land: Chemonyo, Murtukwo, Ketut, Kibiriem, Koibirir, and Talai.

Across the county boundary in West Pokot, the inquiry route takes in Cheptelul, Kona, Lomut, Chester, Sigor, and Marich Pass. In several of these localities, the NLC noted that the precise hectarage earmarked for compulsory acquisition is yet to be determined, leaving residents to prepare compensation claims without finalized parcel boundaries.

### The November Chief's Camp Schedule

To manage the claims process, the commission has scheduled public hearings at local chiefs' offices from November 10 to November 27, 2026. 

Under the statutory timetable, individuals or groups claiming an interest in the acquired strip must lodge their paperwork in advance of their specific hearing dates. The reliance on chiefs' camps mirrors the standard administrative apparatus for remote infrastructure corridors, but the insistence on formal banking and tax clearance introduces substantial compliance demands in areas where land has historically been held under customary arrangements rather than registered individual titles.

Without valid KRA PINs and verified personal bank accounts, affected claimants risk having their awards withheld or redirected into escrow pending formal legal resolution.

![Athi Water Plaza, belonging to the Athi Water Works Development Agency (AWWDA).](https://i.ibb.co/qLGHfqYH/0748d102f013.webp)

### Contrasting Acquisition at Muka Mukuu

While the northern highway cuts across open communal tracts, the NLC is simultaneously executing a far smaller, titled acquisition in Machakos County for regional water infrastructure.

Under Section 110 of the Land Act, the agency intends to take possession of 0.0678 hectares carved out of the Donyosabuk/Donyo Sabuk West Block, owned by Muka Mukuu Farmers Co-operative Society. The parcel will host infrastructure for the Mwala Cluster Water Supply project, commissioned by the Athi Water Works Development Agency (AWWDA).

The commission has designated November 4, 2026, for the Muka Mukuu inquiry at the Donyo Sabuk Chief’s Office, where cooperative members and interested parties can inspect maps and challenge valuation terms.

The Machakos site forms a small puzzle piece in AWWDA’s broader Ksh206 billion 2023–2027 strategic expansion. That five-year capital blueprint targets 461,000 cubic meters in daily storage expansion and 318,500 cubic meters in water treatment capacity, flanked by 443 kilometres of bulk transmission lines and projects including the Thiririka Dam, Maragua 4 Dam, and the Northern Collector Tunnel II.

For residents in Machakos, the inquiry window opens in just over two weeks. In Elgeyo Marakwet and West Pokot, claimants have until November 10 to organize their paperwork and resolve communal boundary representation before the commission's traveling inquiry teams arrive.
