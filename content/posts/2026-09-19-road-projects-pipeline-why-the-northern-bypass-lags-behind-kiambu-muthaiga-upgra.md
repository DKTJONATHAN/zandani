The government’s latest push to accelerate infrastructure development in partnership with China highlights a widening gap between conceptual priority and active construction.

Following high-level talks in Beijing between National Treasury Principal Secretary Chris Kiptoo and the China International Development Cooperation Agency (CIDCA), the 20.2-kilometre Northern Bypass has been re-tabled as a key implementation target. Despite being approved for conversion into a dual carriageway in June 2025, the project remains in the procurement phase, struggling to transition from a list of government commitments to a physical worksite.

In contrast, the Muthaiga-Kiambu-Ndumberi road project-discussed alongside the bypass during the Beijing meetings-has already cleared the initial bureaucratic hurdles. The Kenya Urban Roads Authority (KURA) signed an Engineering, Procurement, and Construction (EPC) contract for the 23.5-kilometre stretch in March 2026. While the Northern Bypass remains tied to the complexities of the China EximBank’s Preferential Buyer’s Credit arrangement, the Kiambu corridor is already in the implementation stage.

For commuters navigating the heavy congestion between Ruaka and Ruiru, the distinction is significant. The Northern Bypass, currently a single carriageway, serves as a critical artery connecting Kiambu and Nairobi’s northern suburbs. The proposed upgrades, which include eight interchanges and dedicated pedestrian and cycle paths, have been on the table for over a year, yet they still await the final green light for contractor mobilization.

The Beijing meetings also served as a review of broader Chinese-funded initiatives, including the Bosto Dam Water Supply Project in Bomet County and ongoing health sector grants for hospital upgrades in Londiani and Baringo.

As the Treasury seeks to move these projects from the pipeline to the ground, the immediate challenge remains the financing and procurement bottleneck affecting the Northern Bypass. Unlike the Muthaiga-Kiambu-Ndumberi project, which has secured its contractual footing, the Northern Bypass continues to rely on the success of ongoing diplomatic and financial negotiations to break the current cycle of planning.
