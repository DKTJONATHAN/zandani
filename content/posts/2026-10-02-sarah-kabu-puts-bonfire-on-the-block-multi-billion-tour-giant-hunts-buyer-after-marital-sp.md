Sarah Kabu is ready to hand over the steering wheel of the multi-billion tour empire she built from scratch. Following months of personal turbulence and a finalised divorce from co-founder Simon Kabu, the Bonfire Adventures managing director has revealed that the company is actively shopping for an investor to buy out the business.

The disclosure marks a decisive turning point for Kenya’s most visible travel brand, which for over a decade served as both a commercial powerhouse and the primary backdrop for the couple’s shared public brand.

Speaking during an interview on Radio Jambo, Sarah confirmed that negotiations are no longer theoretical. The company has formally opened channels with prospective buyers across borders to take over the firm’s equity and direct its next chapter.

"Saa hii tunatafuta investor mwenye anaweza kununua kampuni and take it to the next level. So sasa tunaongea na watu kutoka nchi tofauti na pia huku Kenya," Sarah said.

### The Cost of a Public Separation

The decision to pursue an outright acquisition comes after the founders' domestic fallout spilled directly into the company’s day-to-day operations. When the marriage unraveled under intense public scrutiny, the fallout did not stay confined to social media commentary—it reached the balance sheet.

Sarah acknowledged that the high-profile separation rattled client confidence, with personal networks and long-standing customers effectively picking camps in the marital dispute.

"Tulipoteza wateja kwa sababu sasa walikuwa wamekosa imani na kampuni," Sarah explained, pointing out that several corporate and private bookers hesitated as rumors swirled around the company’s internal stability.

While neutral clients eventually cushioned the shock and bookings stabilized, the disruption exposed how deeply Bonfire’s commercial identity had been tied to the couple’s personal union.

### Billions on the Books

Despite opening the doors to a buyout, Sarah maintained that the agency is not negotiating from a position of distress. Bonfire Adventures, which began as a small group-travel outfit before scaling into a full-fledged international travel management agency, continues to post massive turnover.

"It is still doing well; it’s making billions," she stated, defending the commercial health of the enterprise as suitor discussions proceed.

The firm's valuation and operational strength now form the bedrock of pitch meetings with both regional funds and international hospitality investors looking for established market footprint in East Africa.

### Boardroom Divergence

The move to find an external buyer follows official confirmation from Simon Kabu, who previously stated on Radio Jambo that their divorce had been legally concluded. 

Since the dissolution of the marriage, the co-founders have publicly diverged over key operational and strategic decisions, making co-management increasingly untenable. While both built the business into a household name across Kenya, the current investor hunt represents the clearest signal yet that the Kabu era at the helm of Bonfire Adventures is moving toward a formal corporate exit.
