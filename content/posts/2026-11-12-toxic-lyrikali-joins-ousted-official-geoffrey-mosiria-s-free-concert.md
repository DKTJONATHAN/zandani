Rapper Toxic Lyrikali is taking his microphone to the KCB Rugby Grounds behind KCA University, stepping into a high-stakes political calendar where street-level charity meets electoral ambition.

The hip-hop artist used a TikTok broadcast alongside social media personality Obidan Dela to confirm his upcoming set at a free public event marking a decade of humanitarian work by former Nairobi County Chief Officer Geoffrey Mosiria. The gathering, slated for Sunday, November 15, 2026, is being pitched directly to Nairobi residents as an open-air celebration of community intervention without gate fees.

“I see there is an event here for Geoffrey Mosiria, and I see it is about celebrating 10 years of humanity,” Toxic told his followers online, urging fans to prioritize the Thika Road venue for live entertainment. Obidan reinforced the call, framing the gathering as a direct show of solidarity for an administrator known for documenting interventions among homeless families, struggling urban youth, and children requiring urgent medical assistance.

Beyond the festival atmosphere and the draw of live rap performances, the timing of the humanitarian milestone carries heavy political weight. The public celebration lands weeks after Mosiria exited City Hall following a bitter public fallout with Nairobi Governor Johnson Sakaja. Mosiria, who previously headed county operations covering health, environmental management, and citizen engagement, was dismissed from his Chief Officer post on October 21, 2026.

His departure from the county administration followed public criticism of internal county governance, including allegations of delayed salary disbursements for workers, budgetary allocations, and operational corruption within the county government. Rather than retreating from the public eye after his dismissal, Mosiria has leaned into the digital following he built documenting hands-on rescue missions across Nairobi's informal settlements.

That online footprint is now anchoring a wider political pivot. Mosiria has officially stated his intention to challenge Governor Sakaja for the Nairobi gubernatorial seat in the upcoming 2027 General Election. By pairing mass-appeal entertainment like Toxic Lyrikali’s live performance with a decade of recorded grassroots advocacy, the ousted official is attempting to convert his digital and street-level visibility into an active electoral constituency ahead of the ballot box.
