The opening of an emergency spillway at Nairobi Dam has averted immediate risks of water overtopping the facility’s reservoir embankment, but the resulting discharge is funneling heavy runoff directly into vulnerable settlements and transit corridors downstream along the Ngong River basin.

The Nairobi Rivers Commission (NRC) confirmed on Monday that civil works on the diversion channel, initiated on October 13, are complete and water is actively moving through the newly excavated structure. While the intervention prevents an unmanaged breach of the dam itself, state agencies have cautioned that the measure does not shield adjacent neighborhoods from seasonal inundation as national weather models forecast heavy El Niño rainfall.

NRC Chief Executive Officer Brigadier (Rtd) Joseph Muracia stated that the multi-agency project—executed alongside the Nairobi City County Government—operates as both an urgent flood control buffer and an initial stage of the broader Nairobi Dam regeneration programme. By establishing a fixed exit route for rising surface water, engineers aim to stabilize reservoir pressure and halt structural erosion along the earthen barrier that anchors the basin.

### Downstream Catchment Under Scrutiny

Yet the engineering solution transfers hydraulic volume directly toward heavily settled urban corridors. The commission issued formal vigilance notices to communities situated immediately below the dam walls, specifically highlighting Nyayo Highrise, Dam Estate, Nairobi West, Madaraka, and sections of Kibera.

NRC instructed residents in those sectors to keep clear of swollen river channels, stressing that while the spillway regulates reservoir levels, it cannot halt overland flooding if regional downpours overwhelm existing drainage paths. Further along the Ngong River's natural exit path, commercial nodes and residential zones—including Strathmore University, T-Mall junction, South C, South B, the Industrial Area, and informal settlements in Mukuru—face compounding flood risks during periods of concentrated precipitation.

![An undated image of Nairobi Dam](https://i.ibb.co/h3Jmf92/94be474f3762.webp)

This hydrological friction follows months of administrative unease over the structural integrity of the facility. During the March long rains, the Water Resources Authority (WRA) raised formal alarms that escalating water tables were placing unsustainable structural load on the dam’s embankment. Those warnings prompted multi-agency eviction directives ordering residents across Kibera, Dam Estate, Lang’ata, and Nairobi West to vacate riparian reserves within a 14-day window ahead of planned demolitions.

### Severe November Horizon

The activation of the spillway arrives just as the Kenya Meteorological Service projects a stronger than 90 percent likelihood that severe El Niño conditions will persist across the country into early 2027. Early manifestations of the weather pattern have already triggered property losses in Kisumu and dropped 147.8 millimeters of precipitation in Nyeri over the past week alone.

Meteorologists expect the October-to-December seasonal peak to arrive in November, with intense storm bands anticipated to deliver over 50 millimeters of rainfall within single 24-hour windows. With the Nairobi Dam spillway now discharging constantly to preserve upstream containment, the capacity of downstream riverbanks and municipal culverts to swallow that incoming November deluge remains the city's critical, untested vulnerability.
