Thirteen years on the run ended in a quiet Runda driveway when detectives handcuffed a Kenyan man convicted of killing a person in a California drink-driving crash. The Office of the Director of Public Prosecutions is now pushing to put him on a plane back to the United States to face his delayed sentence.

**What we know**
* The suspect skipped a mandatory sentencing hearing at the Superior Court of California in Madera County on October 28, 2013.
* California court records confirm his conviction for gross vehicular manslaughter while intoxicated, including three special statutory allegations.
* The United States Department of Justice submitted a formal extradition request to Kenya’s Attorney General in December 2019.
* Senior Principal Magistrate Teresia Nyangena remanded him at Gigiri Police Station after rejecting his application for bond at Milimani Law Courts.

State prosecutors told the court that the suspect fled the US in October 2013, just days before he was scheduled to receive his penalty in Madera County. His conviction fell under Section 191.5(a) of the California Penal Code, an offence covering the unlawful killing of a human being without malice aforethought while driving under the influence of alcohol and displaying gross negligence.

Assistant DPP Duncan Ondimu mounted a fierce opposition against bail during the arraignment. He argued before Magistrate Nyangena that someone who successfully dodged an American court for more than a decade represents a clear flight risk. The state maintained that pending extradition treaties outweigh any domestic bond terms.

Defence lawyers pleaded for leniency, offering to deposit the man's passport and submit to any strict monitoring conditions the court might impose. Magistrate Nyangena turned down the plea, ruling that the suspect must remain behind bars at Gigiri Police Station to guarantee his presence throughout the proceedings.

The extradition process has moved at a crawl since Washington first logged the case file in Nairobi back in late 2019. The Milimani Chief Magistrate’s Court eventually issued the arrest warrant that allowed detectives to trace the fugitive to his hideout in the capital. The court scheduled the next mention for September 15 to give directions on his extradition surrender.

### The take

Think about what it takes to live behind high walls in Runda for thirteen years, watching every unmarked car with a knot in your stomach. For the victim's family across the ocean in Madera County, closure stood frozen while Nairobi life moved on. For anyone sitting in the evening gridlock on Thika Road or Waiyaki Way watching reckless drivers overlap on the pavement, the case is a reminder that fatal negligence carries a tab that someone eventually has to settle. A wealthy leafy suburb can buy you silence and space, but it does not erase a criminal record. Our justice machinery often moves like cold molasses, yet once diplomatic pressure locks into place, the illusion of safety falls apart. When the handover plane finally departs Jomo Kenyatta International Airport, it sends a blunt signal: Nairobi is running out of room for fugitives looking for a quiet retirement from foreign courts.
