A breakdown in wage negotiations between public university staff unions and the state spilled onto Nairobi’s primary transport artery on Tuesday, after University of Nairobi learners barricaded Waiyaki Way to protest a month-long academic standstill.

The demonstration along the busy dual carriageway brought morning traffic to a halt, forcing motorists to turn around and hunt for detours through adjacent suburban lanes. Protesters hauled heavy stones across the lanes and set bonfires alight in the middle of the road before anti-riot police intervened, firing tear gas canisters into the crowds to scatter the students and clear smouldering barricades from the tarmac.

The unrest came barely 24 hours after conciliation talks between the government and public university unions collapsed on Monday, October 26. The two sides hit an impasse after union representatives rejected a state counter-offer of Ksh11.9 billion intended to cover the four-year 2025–2029 Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA).

### Stalled Ledger

The industrial action, which took effect nationwide on October 2, 2026, has entered its fourth consecutive week with no teaching underway across Kenya’s public universities. Staff walked out after prolonged conciliation failed to yield a funded framework for basic salary adjustments and associated benefits for the new cycle.

While the Ministry of Education attempted to break the deadlock on Monday by putting Ksh11.9 billion on the table, union negotiators deemed the figure insufficient to cover the accumulated shortfall across academic and non-academic staff cadres.

Universities Academic Staff Union (UASU) Secretary General Constantine Wasonga maintains that academic staff will not return to lecture halls on promissory notes alone. The union has insisted that any workable return-to-work agreement requires explicit, ring-fenced funding commitments signed off directly by the National Treasury, rather than indicative departmental envelopes that risk subsequent budget cuts.

![The University of Nairobi students protesting along Waiyaki Way on Wednesday, October 21, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/uon-students-protest.jpg.webp?itok=xW5iZ1Jo)

### Compounding Disruption

Tuesday’s clash marks the second time in a week that University of Nairobi students have taken to the streets outside the main campus, mirroring protests staged along the same corridor last week. With lecture halls shuttered, examinations indefinitely frozen, and semester calendars sliding into disarray, learner frustration has increasingly drifted from silent dormitories to public infrastructure.

For motorists on Waiyaki Way, the unrest turned routine morning commutes into multi-hour gridlock until police details pushed the student lines back toward campus gates and pushed the burning debris off the carriageway.

The government and university union representatives are scheduled to return to the negotiating table later this week in an effort to salvage the stalled 2025–2029 agreement. Until the National Treasury commits hard figures to match the unions' baseline demands, the deadlock leaves thousands of learners stranded between locked lecture halls and tear gas along the highway.
