George Natembeya has no intention of writing flattery into his gubernatorial itinerary.

The Trans Nzoia governor drew an uncompromising line under his public relationship with State House on Sunday, October 11, 2026, firing straight back at President William Ruto after the head of state used a weekend tour in Endebess to paint Natembeya’s political machinery as a roadblock to public works.

Addressing the accusations that his signature Tawe Movement has actively derailed state-funded infrastructure, Natembeya made it clear that regional development cannot be treated as a prize handed down exclusively to political cheerleaders.

“To bring development, the President should just follow the law. Equality and fairness are well addressed by the Constitution,” Natembeya said. “The law also allows us to be in the Opposition. It is not my work to praise you; my work is to keep you in check.”

### The Endebess Podium Provocation

The standoff flared up following Ruto’s public address in Endebess on Saturday, where the president took direct aim at local dissenters, accusing county leadership of weaponizing contrarian politics to stall marquee initiatives, including a regional hospital project.

Standing before local crowds, Ruto had framed the delays as a byproduct of regional resistance, pledging that his administration would push past local leaders to deliver healthcare upgrades and road networks.

“Even if you have some leadership problems of those opposing everything, I want to assure you that I will not allow Trans Nzoia to be left behind,” Ruto told the gathering, directly tying the area's stalled funding to political intransigence.

For Natembeya, framing standard opposition oversight as economic sabotage was a narrative he refused to let settle quietly on his home turf.

“He has come to Endebess and tells people Natembeya does not want development with his Tawe Movement,” the governor countered. “Development is for everybody, including the people of Trans Nzoia. They also pay taxes.”

### Representation Over Patronage

Rather than treating national allocations as executive favors, Natembeya turned the conversation back to the legal plumbing of public finance. 

He challenged elected legislators from Trans Nzoia to drop quiet compliance and actively fight for the county’s rightful cut of the national purse in Parliament, invoking historic accountability doctrine to remind them why voters sent them to Nairobi in the first place.

“We have lawmakers from this region who should ensure money from the national government comes to Trans Nzoia,” Natembeya stated. “That is why they say there shall be no taxation without representation.”

By anchoring his response in statutory entitlement rather than personal loyalty, Natembeya has firmly rejected the unwritten rule that county bosses must soften their tone to secure budget disbursements.

With Ruto determined to keep his infrastructure delivery promises visible in western Kenya and Natembeya refusing to tone down his Tawe banner, the political boundary lines across Trans Nzoia remain drawn with zero room for applause.
