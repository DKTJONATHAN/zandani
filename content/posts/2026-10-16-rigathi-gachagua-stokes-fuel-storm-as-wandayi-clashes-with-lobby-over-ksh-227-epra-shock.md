Former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua has thrust himself squarely into the middle of Kenya’s bitterest consumer flashpoint, claiming the government is artificially padding retail fuel margins just as pump prices scaled brutal new heights.

His high-decibel intervention immediately drew a sharp pushback from Energy Cabinet Secretary Opiyo Wandayi, setting off a high-stakes duel between the former second-in-command and the country’s top energy boss. But while Wandayi moved to dismiss Gachagua’s accusations, the numbers delivered by the Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority (EPRA) gave critics all the ammunition they needed to turn technical pricing into a public revolt.

By Thursday, the Motorists Association of Kenya (MAK) stepped directly into the crossfire, warning that the pricing dispute would not end on podiums or television studios. The lobby threatened to pour into the streets for peaceful protests if the regulatory body fails to explain the fuzzy mathematics behind the latest pump increases.

### The Math Under Scrutiny

EPRA’s mid-month review, which took effect on October 15, 2026, landed like a sledgehammer on household and commercial budgets across the country. In Nairobi, Super Petrol surged by KSh 12.93 to retail at KSh 226.96 per liter, Diesel climbed by KSh 9.90 to touch KSh 227.76, while Kerosene absorbed a massive shock of KSh 28.49 to settle at KSh 219.87 per liter.

The revised caps remain locked in through November 14, 2026. Yet it was the disproportionate hike on kerosene—the staple fuel for low-income households and micro-enterprises—that ignited forensic pushback.

In a detailed technical rebuttal, MAK blew a hole through EPRA’s retail formula. The association highlighted that while the landed cost of imported kerosene had edged upward by only 9.41 percent, the regulator permitted its retail pump price to jump by 14.89 percent.

The lobby demanded that energy regulators urgently publish a line-item breakdown accounting for the five-percentage-point gulf between import expenses and consumer billing. Without a transparent paper trail, MAK argued, the figures directly echo Gachagua’s charge that domestic margins are carrying hidden, state-sanctioned markups.

### The G-to-G Audit Demands

The dispute is now dragging the state's controversial government-to-government (G-to-G) oil procurement framework back into the spotlight. 

Beyond challenging EPRA’s monthly price-setting matrix, the motorists' lobby is calling for a full, independent forensic audit of the G-to-G arrangement, alongside total disclosure of the freight, insurance, and landing charges used to construct the domestic price structure.

Motorists argue that the compounding price shocks are ripping through Kenya's primary transport arteries and industrial sectors, accelerating retail inflation at a moment when ordinary citizens can least afford it. 

With Wandayi standing by the integrity of the pricing regime and Gachagua continuing to fan the public's suspicions, the dispute has moved beyond routine economic dissatisfaction into a combustible political standoff. The ball now rests with EPRA and the energy ministry, with motorists demanding a line-by-line accounting of every shilling before their November 14 deadline expires.
