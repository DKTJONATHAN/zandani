Power utility schedules across Kenya’s major commercial hubs reveal a stark operational divide, with Wednesday’s maintenance blueprint imposing heavy nine-hour blackouts on most regions while offering a brief reprieve to select pockets.

Under the notice issued for Wednesday, November 25, 2026, the utility’s maintenance teams targeted installations spanning nine distinct administrative zones. For the vast majority of households and commercial enterprises caught in the footprint—stretching from Nairobi and Machakos down to Kwale, Kisii, and Migori—the interruption is configured as a standard full-day lockdown running from 9:00 am to 5:00 pm, and stretching up to nine hours in parts of Nyeri.

Yet the scheduling architecture breaks its own uniform pattern at the margins. In Uasin Gishu County, residents and business operators in Munyaka, Hawai, and their immediate environs navigate a sharply compressed downtime window. Their supply interruption is slotted for a brief two-hour interval between 10:00 am and 12:00 noon, offering a stark logistical contrast to the prolonged shutdowns enforced elsewhere.

Other sectors face an even earlier disruption. Certain distribution lines in the wider maintenance roster kick off their downtime by 8:00 am, extending the operational paralysis for businesses relying on uninterrupted grid stability for morning commerce.

With nine counties simultaneously flagged for infrastructural servicing—including Kiambu and Trans Nzoia—the Wednesday exercise underscores the friction of managing aging transmission assets while businesses attempt to maintain baseline productivity through rolling regional outages.
