President William Ruto has moved to draw a line under the mounting scrutiny surrounding the ceremonial ensemble he wore during his daughter Charlene Ruto’s wedding in Mwanza, Tanzania, earlier this month. Standing before congregants at St. Mary’s Girls High School in Narok County on Sunday, the President characterized the recent debate over his wardrobe as a distraction born of petty political obsession.

Since the November 6 ceremony, the President’s choice of attire has been picked apart by opposition figures, most notably his former deputy, Rigathi Gachagua. Over the past weekend, Gachagua escalated the criticism, placing a staggering KSh 100 million valuation on the President’s appearance. In an itemized breakdown presented to the public, Gachagua claimed the coat was worth KSh 20 million, the trousers KSh 15 million, the shirt KSh 5 million, and the footwear KSh 25 million, an exercise that has fueled a week of heated political discourse.

Addressing the matter directly from the pulpit, President Ruto framed the debate not as an inquiry into state resources, but as a misplaced obsession with personal style. 

“People wear what they want to wear. I heard someone complain that I wore a certain type of coat to the wedding, now why do you have a problem with the coat?” the President asked. “Just like everything I do, I pay attention to what I wear. If you are casual about your dressing or casual about what you say or how you do stuff, that is your problem.”

The President’s remarks at St. Mary’s served as a pointed rejoinder to the claims made by Gachagua, who has been increasingly vocal in his critiques of the State House inner circle since his departure from the deputy presidency. By positioning his attention to detail as a virtue—contrasting it with what he termed the “casual” nature of his critics—Ruto sought to reclaim the narrative from the specific monetary figures cited by his detractors.

The exchange highlights a growing trend of personality-driven political sparring, where seemingly trivial domestic details are weaponized to question institutional accountability. While Gachagua’s valuation has provided fodder for intense social media debate, the President’s response in Narok suggests a strategy of dismissive redirection, refusing to engage with the specific math of the outfit while questioning the motives of those doing the accounting.

As the political temperature continues to rise, the dispute over the Mwanza wedding attire remains symptomatic of the deepening rift between the President and his former ally, with both sides using public forums to project contrasting images of discipline and excess.
