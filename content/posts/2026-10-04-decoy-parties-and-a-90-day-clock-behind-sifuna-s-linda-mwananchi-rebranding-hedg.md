A protracted regulatory battle inside the Office of the Registrar of Political Parties (ORPP) has forced promoters of the Linda Mwananchi Movement into a calculated operational hedge: securing a provisional 90-day window to rebrand The Equitable Party (TEP), while formally resolving to run under the existing TEP banner if fresh administrative roadblocks emerge.

The dual-track strategy, made public during the official unveiling of TEP on Sunday, October 4, in Nairobi, reflects a defensive posture shaped by months of institutional friction. Addressing principals and supporters in the capital, Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna announced plans to rebrand TEP into the Linda Mwananchi Movement, but drew an immediate procedural boundary against further regulatory interference.

"In the coming days, and as has been resolved by the principals of Linda Mwananchi, who you see here, we shall be seeking to rebrand this party, The Equitable Party, to the Linda Mwananchi Movement," Sifuna said. 

He stressed that the movement would refuse to get tangled in endless administrative disputes, committing to stay on the ballot under TEP if the renaming process hits another wall.

![Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna during the unveiling of TEP party on October 4, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/SDtFs3vd/cbe654dc8369.webp)

“I want to make it clear that in the event that more obstacles are put in the path of rebranding this party, called The Equitable Party, as we have experienced in the past, the party will continue with its present identity until the ballots are cast in 2027,” Sifuna added.

### The Decoy Strategy

The defensive posture follows an elaborate cat-and-mouse game with state oversight bodies. Kakamega Governor Boni Khalwale disclosed during the launch that the movement intentionally floated multiple political formations as decoys to deflect administrative scrutiny and disrupt political surveillance from President William Ruto’s camp, shielding Sifuna’s ultimate 2027 presidential ticket.

That tactical misdirection was prompted by repeated dead ends. An earlier attempt to secure the United Democratic Party (UDP) as a platform collapsed under factional disputes and petitions before the Political Parties Disputes Tribunal (PPDT). 

Efforts to register the Linda Mwananchi brand directly met stiff administrative resistance at the ORPP. Promoters of the outfit, among them Suba South MP Caroli Omondi, saw their application to reserve the name rejected by the Registrar on grounds that it posed public interest concerns and conflicted with the acronym of the inactive Liberty National Democratic Alliance (LINDA).

Procedural complications deepened in February 2026, when a rival claimant, Charles Wanyonyi, submitted an application for the Linda Mwananchi Party of Kenya, introducing competing administrative claims even as negotiations continued.

### The Tribunal Showdown

The breakthrough came through the courts rather than the registry counter. On September 30, 2026, the PPDT overturned the Registrar's rejection, ruling the administrative blockage unconstitutional and confirming that the earlier LINDA reservation had legally lapsed.

The tribunal issued a strict seven-day compliance order to the regulator. On October 1, Registrar John Cox Lorionokou formally notified Bansbury Associates Advocates that the reservation of the Linda Mwananchi name had been granted for a statutory duration of up to 90 days.

Sifuna framed the emerging political vehicle as an institutional platform built on public accountability, asserting that the movement envisions a governance model where state resources are held in trust and public grievances are settled through constitutional channels.

With the 90-day name-reservation clock now running, the transition from TEP to Linda Mwananchi enters an administrative sprint. Whether the movement completes the formal statutory changeover or is forced to trigger Sifuna’s fallback guarantee will depend on how cleanly the paperwork clears the Registrar’s desk before year-end.
