Mamboleo, Kisumu - President William Ruto arrived at the Voice of Salvation and Healing Church this Sunday to solidify the ruling administration’s partnership with ODM, positioning his government as the primary custodian of the late Raila Odinga’s development vision.

The President opened the service by framing the UDA and ODM union as a formal, long-term collaboration. Addressing the congregation, Ruto characterized himself as a former student of the late opposition leader, pledging that the current broad-based coalition would prioritize the late statesman’s unfinished agenda for Nyanza.

The sequence of the address focused on transitioning political cooperation into tangible infrastructure. Ruto explicitly credited the former Prime Minister for the conceptual push behind the extension of Za Ndani Gauge Railway from Narok to Kisumu. He further linked his national housing and universal health coverage initiatives to the shared mandate of the coalition, emphasizing that these efforts are designed to stabilize the economic gains achieved over his four-year tenure.

Homa Bay Governor and ODM National Chairperson Gladys Wanga confirmed the party’s stance, dismissing internal dissent regarding the alliance. Wanga maintained that the current development trajectory in the region serves as proof of the coalition's efficacy, signaling that the party remains committed to the broad-based framework.

Official figures shared by the administration highlight that the Social Health Authority has now reached an enrollment of 34 million Kenyans. Additionally, the President defended the current fiscal policy, citing the reduction of fertilizer costs to KSh 2,000 as a central pillar in the administration's plan to revive the tea, coffee, and sugar sub-sectors. The government currently ranks Kenya as the sixth-largest economy in Africa.
