Justice Kossy Bor has brought the state’s glossy architectural renderings crashing down to earth, halting the high-profile KSh 5 billion Kanduyi Airstrip development and laying bare an inverted procurement process that put multi-billion-shilling tenders ahead of basic constitutional procedure.

Delivering her judgment from the Environment and Land Court in Bungoma, Justice Bor nullified the entire 2,034-unit residential project, ruling that the push to turn an active aviation runway into an urban residential estate breached core constitutional requirements on environmental conservation, land-use planning, and citizen consultation.

Rather than an ordinary temporary injunction, the ruling arrived as a decisive repudiation of the state’s administrative sequence. Justice Bor pointedly highlighted that state planners had already inked and awarded the KSh 5.02 billion construction contract before bothering to convene local residents for a public participation forum on January 14, 2026.

### Cart Before the Horse at Kanduyi

For months, the planned smart city at Kanduyi was promoted as an ambitious regional facelift intended to introduce high-density modern living to Bungoma. Yet in the courtroom, the legal architecture underpinning the brick-and-mortar ambitions unraveled quickly.

Justice Bor’s findings revealed that the authorities treated mandatory legal gates as procedural afterthoughts. The court determined that the project moved forward without adhering to established physical planning statutes and applicable environmental impact assessment standards. 

By executing a commercial construction agreement valued at over KSh 5 billion before holding the January 14 consultation, the state effectively inverted the constitutional mandate. In Justice Bor's assessment, consulting citizens on a zoning transformation that had already been commercially committed rendered the entire public engagement exercise legally empty.

### Three Residents Derail State Machinery

The multi-billion-shilling rollout was not halted by rival corporate bidders or high-ranking political figures. Instead, the petition that froze the earthmovers was mounted by three local residents determined to defend the designated aviation space from arbitrary rezoning.

The three petitioners took the state to task for unilaterally erasing an active aviation reserve without demonstrating lawful compliance or obtaining the necessary statutory clearances to alter the land’s public utility. Justice Bor agreed with their petition, noting that the government completely failed to satisfy the court that the change of user from an airstrip reserve to high-density housing was pursued within the confines of the law.

The ruling marks a notable victory for localized public-interest litigation, demonstrating how ordinary citizens using constitutional checks can successfully challenge the momentum of mega-budget executive developments.

### The 45-Day Demolition and Restoration Bill

Beyond simply ordering a down-tools on the site, Justice Bor attached a tight financial and operational consequence to the ruling. The court barred the state from deploying or utilizing the Kanduyi parcel in any manner that contradicts the judgment.

Even more consequential for public coffers, Justice Bor directed the national government to fully restore the Kanduyi land to its original status within 45 days. Crucially, the court specified that every cent required to remediate the torn-up aviation grounds will be borne entirely at the state’s own expense.

With the 45-day restoration clock running, the state now faces the unenviable task of reversing structural interventions on an aviation reserve it had already committed billions to reshape—turning a flagship smart city showcase into an expensive lesson on legal compliance.
