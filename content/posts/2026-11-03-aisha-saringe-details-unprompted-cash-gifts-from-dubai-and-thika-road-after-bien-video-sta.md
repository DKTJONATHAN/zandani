Aisha Saringe is finding out firsthand how quickly a high-profile music video feature can transform a model's digital footprint into an operational inbox logjam.

Weeks after appearing in Bien-Aimé Baraza's *I'll Be Okay* music video, the Kenyan video vixen and model has pulled back the curtain on the financial realities of what she terms "pretty privilege"—specifically, unprompted cash transfers from admirers who decline to take her phone number in exchange.

![Aisha Saringe //Instagram](https://i.ibb.co/DHxYgJVR/2ebfc87c3465.webp)

Saringe pointed to two distinct encounters that illustrate the phenomenon outside standard dating or networking channels. In one instance at a club along Thika Road, a stranger handed her KSh 130,000 in cash without asking for her contact details. Later, during a trip to Dubai, she encountered a married man accompanied by his wife who gifted her KSh 300,000 under similar circumstances.

Those high-value encounters match a broader shift in her daily interactions following her stint in Bien's project. Saringe noted that her social media direct messages have swelled past the 100-message mark, bringing an eclectic mix of domestic offers and unusual proposals from online followers.

![Sandra Dacha Sparks Concern After Sharing Pre-Surgery Video in Hospital Gown](https://i.ibb.co/Zp1tjYgd/98d35125991f.webp)

Among the messages crowding her inbox are domestic pitches from young men volunteering culinary services, alongside direct inquiries from admirers attempting to buy their way into her attention. "Kuna mwingine anasema mimi nakuja na pesa yangu, nataka unitumie vibaya," Saringe said, acknowledging the heavy volume while noting she is unable to reply to every incoming message despite appreciating the attention.

The influx underscores the immediate visibility boost that Kenyan artists' music videos provide to featured talent, turning auxiliary casting choices into trending digital personalities overnight.
