When Chebet Ronoh first watched her earnings hit KSh1.5 million in a single month as a teenager, she turned to her mother with absolute certainty about her future.

Working with four to five brands concurrently at just 19 years old, the viral content creator believed she had cracked the code of Kenya's emerging digital economy. But the windfall arrived without a safety net or established industry pioneers to guide her through the sudden influx of cash and public attention.

Reflecting on that period during an interview with BBC News Africa, Ronoh recalled the precise moment her mother tried to steer her toward asset accumulation rather than immediate spending.

“I remember telling my mom, ‘Oh my God, this is it, I am going to be a star’ but my mom was trying to tell me we should invest,” Ronoh shared.

At the time, Kenya’s digital content space was vastly different from today’s structured creator economy. With only a handful of television comedians and digital pioneers to look up to, Ronoh found herself navigating adult financial decisions while still a teenager desperate for peer validation.

She admitted that her mother's advice fell on deaf ears. Instead of securing property or setting aside capital, a significant portion of the KSh1.5 million flowed into alcohol and trying to impress friends.

“I didn’t want to hear none of that advice. So, I remember a lot of my money went to alcohol. I was still a kid, I’d say I was a teenager desperate for validation. I didn’t even know who I was, I just loved that people liked me and I wanted to prove so much to my friends,” she explained.

That rapid introduction to nightlife and substance experimentation ultimately left a mark on her personal life, creative opportunities, and public persona. Looking back at the chaotic intersection of sudden wealth, weak peer boundaries, and a lack of mentorship, Ronoh described the period as a disaster that was nevertheless part of her formative journey.

The creator has since shifted her focus toward personal recovery and growth, using the hard lessons of her teenage financial missteps to reevaluate how she handles success and public pressure.
