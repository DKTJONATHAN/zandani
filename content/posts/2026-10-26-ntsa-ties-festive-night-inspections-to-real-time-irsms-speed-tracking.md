Nighttime public service vehicle operations on major Kenyan transport corridors are facing automated scrutiny under a newly activated enforcement framework that links roadside inspections directly to real-time national data feeds.

The National Transport and Safety Authority (NTSA), operating alongside law enforcement under the newly launched *Operation Msimu Bila Ajali, Usalama Barabarani* campaign, has made continuous data transmission a core requirement for commercial motorists. Central to the enforcement effort is the Intelligent Road Safety Management System (IRSMS), a digital tracking platform designed to verify that speed limiters are functioning and transmitting telemetry data without interruption while vehicles are in motion.

![An undated image of NTSA Alco blow safety check on a motorist.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/ntsa-safety-check.jpg.webp?itok=2bnsbqmO)

The clampdown targets a specific statistical vulnerability on local highways. Agency data shows that the hours between 7 pm and 10 pm account for roughly 30 percent of all road crashes nationally, and 26 percent within Nairobi. Within that window, the hour between 8 pm and 9 pm emerges as the most lethal period, driven by deteriorating visibility, driver fatigue, and alcohol impairment along poorly lit stretches where pedestrians and boda boda riders face heightened exposure.

Under the checklist enforced during day and night corridor checks, long-distance operators are required to present physical and digital compliance proof. Officers are verifying passenger manifests, driver duty logs detailing actual hours behind the wheel, valid Road Service Licences, and vehicle inspection stickers tied to approved routes. Motorists are also subjected to breathalyser screening, while overloading and passenger capacity limits are checked against vehicle registration ratings.

![ntsa-crackdown.jpg](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/ntsa-crackdown.jpg.webp?itok=nFTLewib)

Beyond roadside checks, the authority has sought to counter fraudulent notifications targeting motorists, clarifying that official speed violation alerts are dispatched exclusively through the SMS ID 'NTSA'. 

The heightened regulatory posture coincides with the authority's broader appeals for elevated state intervention, including a recent push for the executive to formally declare road fatalities a national disaster following a surge in casualties across the transport sector this year.
