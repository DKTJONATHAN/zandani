The High Court’s decision to find Inspector General of Police Douglas Kanja in contempt of court carries implications far beyond the immediate summons for his September 22 sentencing. By holding the police chief personally accountable for widespread road blockades erected during June protests, the court rejected a core operational defense long used by security organs: that decentralized police actions do not carry top-level personal liability.

Justice Patricia Nyaundi ruled that Kanja failed to show that reasonable measures were taken to ensure police officers complied with a binding court order. That order, issued in July 2025, required the National Police Service to issue advance public notifications before establishing barricades or roadblocks during public demonstrations marking the anniversary of the 2024 Finance Bill protests.

The ruling squarely addresses the limits of command responsibility within Kenya's security apparatus. During the proceedings, police leadership attempted to distance the Inspector General from individual street-level deployments. Nairobi Regional Police Commander Issa Mohammed Mohamud maintained in court filings that the security measures set up across the capital were not part of a centrally coordinated scheme designed to trigger personal liability for the Inspector General.

Mohamud further argued that the original conservatory orders did not constitute an outright ban on security checkpoints or traffic diversions. However, Justice Nyaundi observed that the defense failed to demonstrate any systematic effort by top leadership to enforce the mandatory public notice requirement established by the court.

The contempt application, brought by public interest rights body Katiba Institute, highlighted extensive barricades across major arteries into Nairobi’s Central Business District and surrounding areas. Rights advocates argued that by locking down routes such as Uhuru Highway, Moi Avenue, Parliament Road, Roysambu, Kangemi, Ngong Road, and several outer transit corridors without prior notice, police intentionally defied explicit judicial directives.

By ruling that Kanja must personally face mitigation and sentencing on September 22, 2026, the court reinforced a principle of institutional oversight: command authority comes with an affirmative obligation to enforce legal boundaries down the chain of command. Rather than treating police non-compliance as isolated field decisions, the court’s judgment asserts that overall responsibility for adhering to constitutional rights and judicial orders rests firmly at the top.
