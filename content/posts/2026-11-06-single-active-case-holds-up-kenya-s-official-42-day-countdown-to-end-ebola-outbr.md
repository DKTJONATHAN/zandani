Kenya’s push to formally declare an end to its Ebola Bundibugyo outbreak remains paused by a single isolation bed, even as health authorities record 18 consecutive days without detecting a new infection.

The discharge of a male patient on Friday, November 6—the country’s sixth recovery—brought the total active cases down to one. However, under strict epidemiological rules outlined by the Ministry of Health, the zero-infection streak is not enough on its own to initiate the official countdown toward an Ebola-free declaration.

### The Dual-Timeline Clearance Standard

Health Director-General Dr. Patrick Amoth clarified that the regulatory framework for ending the outbreak relies on two separate temporal benchmarks that run on different triggers.

![Health Director General Patrick Amoth addressing the press on Thursday, October 22, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/cc4Sw2fw/554ec1b5ddff.webp)

The first timeline is the 21-day monitoring window applied to exposed contacts. This mechanism isolates high-risk individuals for the maximum known incubation period of the virus to catch symptoms before community exposure can occur.

The second timeline—the formal 42-day national countdown—does not measure time from the last confirmed infection or the end of contact monitoring. Instead, international and national protocols mandate that the 42-day clock, representing twice the maximum 21-day incubation cycle, can only begin when the final confirmed patient tests negative and is physically discharged from the treatment facility.

Because one patient remains under active care, Kenya's 42-day outbreak clock has not yet started ticking.

### Ring-Fencing the Initial Transmission Chain

The current containment numbers reflect an outbreak that has been successfully restricted to its original entry point. Since Kenya confirmed its first imported case on October 6, 2026, health teams have recorded a total of nine confirmed infections and two deaths.

Crucially, all nine cases stem from a single, unbroken chain of transmission linked to the initial index patient. Public health teams intercepted every subsequent infection while the individuals were already in mandatory quarantine, preventing secondary exposure in the general population. The last confirmed positive test was recorded on October 19.

![Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale, during a meeting at Afya House, with an inset of an Ebola Virus Bundibugyo Strain under a microscope.](https://i.ibb.co/ZRfxZ7gH/292766be6dd4.webp)

Contact tracing operations are also approaching complete clearance. Out of the individuals identified during contact tracing, 66 people have completed their mandatory 21-day quarantine and returned home. The remaining 52 contacts currently under observation are scheduled to complete their monitoring period by November 11.

### Surveillance and Clearance Thresholds

While clinical management continues for the remaining patient, the Ministry of Health is maintaining high-level surveillance across the country's transport infrastructure. Port health officials have screened more than 760,000 travelers across 26 designated points of entry to prevent fresh reintroductions of the virus.

County-level emergency response teams remain operational, and the ministry has kept open public reporting channels—including toll-free line 719, USSD code *719#, and WhatsApp channel 0700 719 719—for rapid notification of suspected cases.

The immediate prerequisite for resetting Kenya's public health status now rests entirely on the medical recovery of the single remaining patient. Once that patient meets discharge criteria and leaves the treatment facility, the 42-day uninterrupted countdown will officially begin.
