Rigathi Gachagua is leaning into his self-described identity as an unvarnished "truth-teller," turning accusations of political arrogance back on his opposition allies and demanding that presidential hopefuls leave five-star hotel boardrooms for active grassroots mobilization.

Speaking on Tuesday, November 3, 2026, from his Wamunyoro residence in Nyeri, the former Deputy President pushed back against criticism mounted from within the United Opposition alliance, directly taking aim at Public Service Cabinet Secretary and former National Assembly Speaker Justin Muturi.

Muturi had publicly called out Gachagua for political bullying, double-speak, and self-sabotage following statements Gachagua made during the launch of the Linda Mwananchi party and a subsequent broadcast interview on Inooro TV. Refusing to soften his tone, Gachagua framed the personal friction inside the coalition as a refusal by established politicians to hear uncomfortable facts.

"Some members of the United Opposition have a problem with me. Just because I am a truthful man, and I tell them the truth all the time, they see that as arrogance and bullying," Gachagua stated.

To press his advantage, Gachagua drew a line between political strategy calculated in hotel meeting rooms and ground campaigns built in public spaces. He pointed to his own outfit, the Democracy for Citizens Party (DCP), claiming that while he was away in the United States, party youth leaders led by Nyandarua Senator John Methu organized and executed 27 rallies across the country.

He argued that rival alliance leaders aiming for the top seat cannot expect to secure the coalition’s endorsement through corporate-style discussions without doing equivalent heavy lifting among voters.

Beyond his exchange with Muturi, Gachagua put his foot down on the alliance's electoral timeline, rejecting a proposal floated by Wiper Party leader Kalonzo Musyoka to formally unveil a unified presidential flagbearer on November 13.

Gachagua warned that settling on a candidate more than 280 days before the general election represents a serious tactical misstep. Exposing the opposition's top ticket this far in advance, he argued, would give President William Ruto's administration ample time to study, target, and counter their political playbook ahead of the polls.

While Gachagua denied that the ongoing war of words points to an immediate collapse of the United Opposition, his stance exposes unresolved differences over strategy and candidate selection.

Insisting that his impeachment was legally flawed and maintaining that his own 2027 presidential ambitions remain active, Gachagua laid down a firm condition for his political weight in Mt. Kenya. He warned that he will not marshal the region’s voters behind any opposition contender who relies on boardroom endorsements rather than direct voter engagement.
