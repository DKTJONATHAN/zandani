Nominated Senator Karen Nyamu found herself at the centre of an unexpected public spectacle at the Kenyatta International Convention Centre (KICC) in Nairobi, where she was met with opposition chants during media personality Natalie Githinji’s Endometriosis Awareness Concert. Yet, Nyamu quickly pivoted from the hostile reception to outline a detailed legislative agenda aimed at tackling Kenya's significant deficits in endometriosis diagnosis and treatment.

Speaking after the September 28 event, Nyamu confirmed her attendance was driven by a core policy mission: to hear directly from women living with endometriosis and gather crucial information to inform a proposed Senate motion. She credited Githinji for elevating the conversation around the condition, asserting that 'If it was not for Natalie, we would not be having that motion in the Senate.' Nyamu even suggested Githinji deserved a Head of State Commendation for her advocacy efforts.

### Kenya's Deep Endometriosis Deficit

Nyamu's presence at the concert served as a platform for her to highlight what she described as critical shortcomings in Kenya's healthcare system. Her primary concern revolved around the severe lack of specialized medical capacity and comprehensive national data.

“We have only five gynecologists who are able to operate on a woman who needs surgery with endo,” Nyamu stated, underscoring the stark reality that out of approximately 700 registered gynaecologists in the country, a mere handful possess the expertise for complex endometriosis surgeries. This scarcity, she argued, creates immense barriers to effective treatment for countless women.

Further compounding the issue, Nyamu pointed out the alarming absence of reliable national statistics. “In Kenya we don’t have any endometriosis data,” she revealed, questioning how the government could adequately plan treatment centres and allocate necessary resources without a clear understanding of the condition's prevalence. She posed, “How do you plan, how do you budget, how do you put endo centres all over the country if you don’t know the number?”

### Proposed Senate Motion Seeks Answers

To address these identified gaps, Senator Nyamu's proposed Senate motion seeks to implement several key measures. Among them is the establishment of specialized endometriosis treatment centres across the country, aiming to decentralise care and improve accessibility for patients.

The motion also advocates for comprehensive medical insurance coverage for endometriosis treatment. Currently, the condition is often misclassified or treated as routine menstrual pain, leading to inadequate care and significant financial burdens for patients. Nyamu stressed the importance of reclassifying the condition to ensure it receives the appropriate insurance support.

Strengthening specialist capacity, collecting reliable national data, and improving overall access to treatment are central tenets of the motion, which Nyamu believes are critical steps towards alleviating the challenges faced by women living with endometriosis in Kenya.

### Ignoring the Noise, Focusing on the Mission

While the booing incident itself captured immediate attention, Nyamu downplayed its impact on her legislative focus. She admitted anticipating the negative reaction, attributing it to the political profile of individuals associated with the event, specifically comedian and activist Eric Omondi, who has been vocal in his critique of the government. “Well, I was kind of expecting it because hii event ni ya Eric Omondi and Eric Omondi has been in the forefront kufight government,” she remarked.

When asked how she handled the hostile reception, Nyamu stated she chose to ignore it rather than engage, a strategy she believes will not affect her parliamentary work or her commitment to issues she deems important. Her focus remains on pushing through the legislative changes to improve the lives of women battling endometriosis across the country.
