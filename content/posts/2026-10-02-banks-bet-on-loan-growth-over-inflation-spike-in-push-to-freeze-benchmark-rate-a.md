Commercial lenders have formally petitioned the Central Bank of Kenya (CBK) to freeze the benchmark lending rate at 8.75 per cent, arguing that protecting an emergent recovery in private enterprise borrowing outweighs the risks of an approaching consumer price ceiling.

The Kenya Bankers Association (KBA), through its Centre for Research on Financial Markets and Policy, issued the recommendation on Friday ahead of the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) rate-setting meeting scheduled for Wednesday, October 7, 2026.

According to the banking lobby, keeping the Central Bank Rate (CBR) unchanged is necessary to nurture private credit expansion and sustain broader domestic momentum. The lobby contends that the current interest rate environment, coupled with dropping Treasury bill yields and stable interest rate differentials, has created a viable window for commercial lending to rebound.

### Credit Ambitions vs. Inflation Upper Limit

![Kenya Bankers Association CEO Raimond Molenje speaking at a past meeting.](https://i.ibb.co/C5cNsb9m/27c935b0eeaa.webp)

The bankers' stance comes at a delicate point in the macroeconomic cycle. Official metrics show headline inflation accelerated from 4.3 per cent in February to 6.8 per cent in September, placing price growth within striking distance of the CBK’s upper target bound of 7.5 per cent.

Much of that pressure remains concentrated in essential household categories. Transport inflation reached 15.6 per cent in September, driven by domestic fuel costs, while food inflation climbed to 9.5 per cent.

Despite the jump, KBA researchers argue that aggregate demand pressures across the economy remain subdued. The association forecasts that upcoming crop harvests in major agricultural belts will ease food supply constraints in the final quarter of the year, pulling food costs back down before overall inflation breaches the regulatory ceiling.

“With inflation within the target range, and exchange rate stability sustained, sustaining the current stance of monetary policy, to anchor the recovery in private sector credit and economic activity is appropriate,” the KBA research centre stated in its note.

### Growth Buffers and Weather Uncertainties

![cbk-kamau-thugge.jpg](https://i.ibb.co/FvPkZp4/76a48033d270.webp)

The banking lobby pointed to broader output resilience as justification for holding policy steady. Kenya’s real gross domestic product expanded by 5.3 per cent during the first quarter of 2026, improving from 4.9 per cent recorded over the matching period in 2025.

, continued stability in the foreign exchange market has contained imported inflation, reducing immediate pressure on the CBK to enact an emergency rate hike.

Yet the banking sector acknowledged significant downside risks that could complicate the policy trajectory over the coming six months. These include stubborn fuel and food price increases, elevated domestic transport expenses, softening global trade growth, and anticipated El Niño rains running from October 2026 through the first quarter of 2027.

When the MPC last convened on August 11, policymakers opted to retain the CBR at 8.75 per cent, concluding that a steady hand was required to anchor price expectations while international commodity markets settled.

The MPC's upcoming decision on October 7 will test whether CBK leadership shares the commercial banks' confidence that agricultural supply and exchange rate stability can absorb fuel-driven price spikes without choking off business loan expansion.
