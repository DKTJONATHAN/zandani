A hostile custodial environment and compromised digital privacy forced the High Court's hand in denying bail to eight minors facing charges over the Utumishi Girls' Academy fire tragedy.

Justice Diana Kavedza dismissed the bail applications for the teenage suspects, ruling that releasing them back into the community presented immediate and appreciable risks. Crucially, the court's decision was anchored not only on the gravity of the May 28 tragedy—which claimed the lives of 16 students and injured 79 others at the Gilgil institution—but on the verified breakdown of safety within the state's own remand facilities.

![Justice Diana Kavedza, during the ruling of the 8 Utumishi girls at Kibra Law Courts on September 21.](https://i.ibb.co/93CfyNV5/38e07fb9c050.webp)

Pre-bail reports and administrative evaluations revealed that once the identities of the minors became public, they faced sustained bullying, physical assault, and discrimination from fellow inmates. Compounding the physical hostility, some of the children gained access to mobile phones while in custody, exposing them to intense online harassment and compounding their psychological distress.

“Detention is not imposed as punishment, but as a necessary measure to protect the subjects, victims, and witnesses and to preserve the integrity of the trial,” Justice Kavedza stated, noting that immediate release into a volatile environment would invite severe harm.

To mitigate the immediate danger, the court ordered a restructuring of their custody arrangements. Seven of the minors are to be transferred to the Kamae Girls Borstal Institution within Kamiti Prison, where specialized administrative oversight can better safeguard their welfare. A remaining minor was directed to the Kamaya Boys Home Institution.

![An partly burnt dormitory at Utumishi Girls in Nakuru on Thursday, May 28, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/xSDn0NZH/9ff309154d06.webp)

While keeping the suspects in custody, the court issued strict directives to address the psychological and developmental toll of their detention. Acknowledging reports of anxiety, peer conflict, and physical abuse, the ruling mandates continuous access to qualified counselors and psychologists. Authorities were also instructed to provide structured educational support and trial preparation to ensure their development as adolescents is not truncated by ongoing legal proceedings.

“Children in conflict with the law retain their rights as children notwithstanding the severity of the charges,” the court emphasized, highlighting the statutory mandate to protect juvenile welfare, dignity, and education.

The case proceeds under heightened security and administrative monitoring as investigators continue examining the institutional safety breaches—including overcrowding and locked evacuation doors—that deepened the catastrophe on the night of the fire.
