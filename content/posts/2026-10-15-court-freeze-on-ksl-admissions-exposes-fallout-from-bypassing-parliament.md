A bid by the Kenya School of Law (KSL) to overhaul bar qualification criteria through an annual intake advertisement has collapsed into a full institutional halt, following a High Court intervention that frozen all applications for the 2027/2028 academic cycle.

The freeze, formalized by KSL in a circular to prospective advocates on Thursday, October 15, leaves the training pipeline paralyzed just weeks ahead of the scheduled November 13 application deadline. The suspension stems directly from an administrative decision to alter entry criteria without taking the amendments through formal statutory rule-making or parliamentary vetting.

### The Administrative Short-Cut

The dispute landed before the High Court after petitioners challenged KSL’s revised intake notice, arguing that altering entry thresholds for the Advocates Training Programme (ATP) requires adherence to statutory procedures rather than internal executive memos. 

Under regular criteria, law graduates seeking entry into the ATP must demonstrate a minimum Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education (KCSE) mean grade of C+ alongside a B plain in English or Kiswahili, in addition to their undergraduate degrees. 

![The Kenya School of Law (KSL) located in Karen, Nairobi.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/kenya%20school%20of%20law.jpg.webp?h=f2af6d3c&itok=zeuXmvnI)

However, in its published notice for the upcoming class, KSL sought to lower that threshold unilaterally. The institution introduced alternative entry paths designed to accommodate graduates who fell short of the traditional secondary school cut-offs, including an avenue routed through a Pre-Bar examination.

Petitioners swiftly moved to court, arguing that introducing substantive adjustments to professional entry standards within an intake advertisement skirted the mandatory regulatory framework, which demands public participation and parliamentary review before taking effect.

### Judicial Brake

Concurring that the procedural shortcuts warranted urgent scrutiny, Justice John Onyiego issued conservatory orders on October 6, prohibiting both KSL and the Council of Legal Education (CLE) from processing or admitting candidates under the revised conditions.

The judge classified the contest as an urgent matter, giving the training school and its regulator seven days to submit formal responses and setting priority hearings that commenced on October 13.

Facing an active court injunction against its criteria, KSL had little operational leeway left except to shut down the intake portal altogether.

![Law](https://i.ibb.co/5xtHRYfq/348628cf979e.webp)

“Following the issuance of interim orders by the High Court in relation to the advertised ATP intake, the receipt of applications for admission to the ATP Class of 2027/2028 academic year has been temporarily suspended pending further directions from the Court and the determination of the matter,” the institution announced.

While KSL expressed regret for the disruption facing hundreds of law graduates who had already begun lodging papers, it did not state whether it would seek to defend the revised notice or revert to the previously established admission criteria.

### The Pre-Bar Gray Zone

The shutdown has created immediate practical fallout across law faculties nationwide. Applicants now face an open-ended postponement of their professional certification timeline, with no indication of when the judicial bench will settle the threshold dispute.

Compounding the confusion is an institutional disconnect in KSL's notices. While the ATP intake portal is now shut, KSL’s circular made no mention of altering the January 2027 Pre-Bar Examination intake—an assessment designed to qualify candidate eligibility, which opened in tandem with the ATP advert and carries an active registration deadline of December 11.

Whether that preparatory exam can legally proceed while the entry qualifications tied to it remain restrained under Justice Onyiego’s conservatory order remains one of the critical legal questions pending clarification before the bench.
