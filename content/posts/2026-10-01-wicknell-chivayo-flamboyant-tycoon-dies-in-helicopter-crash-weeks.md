Flamboyant Zimbabwean businessman Wicknell Chivayo, known as much for his penchant for high-end luxury vehicles and political networking as his sprawling infrastructure ambitions, has died alongside his wife and four others in a private helicopter crash in Marondera. The fatal accident unfolded on Wednesday evening just as his long-delayed renewable energy business appeared to finally clear years of legal friction—and instantly sent a 16-month-old social media post careening back into public conversation.

Chivayo, 43, was traveling in a private helicopter that went down at around 5:00 PM on Wednesday, September 30, 2026, in the Marondera Rural area, located southeast of Harare. According to reports confirmed by the Zimbabwe Republic Police, the aircraft caught fire upon impact, leaving no survivors among the six individuals on board.

Family spokesperson Herbert Chivayo subsequently confirmed that both the Intratrek Zimbabwe founder and his wife, Lucy Muteke, perished in the wreckage. Government spokesperson Nick Mangwana also verified the casualty figures as air disaster response teams arrived at the site.

### Fire on Impact in Marondera

The crash brought a abrupt end to one of the region's most polarizing public figures. First responders and air safety investigators deployed to the Marondera crash site faced a severe burn scene, working through the evening to secure the wreckage and initiate a formal inquiry into what caused the aircraft to go down.

While police confirmed the operational facts of the fatal impact, authorities noted that technical cause analysis remains in its preliminary stages, with no immediate indications of mechanical fault or environmental triggers publicly released.

### A Solar Breakthrough Cut Short

For Chivayo, the timing of the flight landed at a moment of significant corporate turnaround. As the founder and head of Intratrek Zimbabwe, he spent years locked in protracted legal disputes over the flagship 100-megawatt Gwanda Solar Power Project.

The high-stakes engineering contract had faced endless delays, court challenges, and regulatory holds before Intratrek received a formal notice to proceed earlier in September 2026. The green light represented a major commercial victory for Chivayo, who had consistently used his public platform to defend the project's legitimacy.

Beyond his commercial footprint, Chivayo built a massive digital presence defined by unrestrained displays of wealth. From displaying fleets of top-tier European cars to making high-profile philanthropic gifts and highlighting his access to political elites across southern Africa, his public persona rarely stayed out of the headlines.

### The Resurfaced 2025 Online Warning

As news of the Marondera crash spread across digital platforms, attention shifted sharply toward a resurfaced post published on X (formerly Twitter) on May 8, 2025, by Susan Mutami.

In the message written roughly 16 months prior, Mutami wrote: "He knows it’s the truth, and the truth hurts. Wicknell will die in a plane crash very soon. Save this post."

While the post offered no evidence, technical detail, or factual context regarding aircraft operations at the time, its explicit reference to an aviation death triggered widespread resharing across online communities once the helicopter crash was confirmed. There was no official indication from investigating authorities that the author had advance technical knowledge of the September 2026 flight.

As emergency personnel continue clearance operations in Marondera, civil aviation teams face the task of piecing together the flight path and mechanical status of the aircraft, leaving both the ultimate cause of the disaster and the future of Intratrek's solar operations unresolved.
