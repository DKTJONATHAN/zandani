Pastor Victor Kanyari paused his Sunday altar duties on November 8, 2026, to conduct an impromptu dress-code intervention, singling out a female congregant for wearing biker shorts to his Nairobi sanctuary.

The Salvation Healing Ministry founder halted the service after spotting the worshipper among the congregation, questioning how tight athletic wear made its way past the sanctuary doors and into a formal worship setting. Rather than issuing a quiet rebuke through his church ushers, Kanyari took the microphone to directly confront the choice of fabric from the pulpit.

He openly compared the congregant's biker shorts to beachwear, arguing that the outfit belonged in a pool rather than before the altar.

### The 'Swimming Costume' Rebuke

Addressing the seated congregant directly, Kanyari expressed open bewilderment at the styling choice, questioning who had cleared the outfit for church service.

“Ah, I am surprised. What outfit is this that you have decided to wear to church? Who told you this is how we dress up in church? Please, no, these are not church outfits. These outfits you have clearly look like swimming costumes,” Kanyari told the congregation.

He maintained that biker shorts were cut specifically for aquatic or recreational spaces, insisting that their presence in his church disrupted the expected reverence of Sunday worship.

“Yes, that biker short is for swimming,” he reiterated from the podium, steering the dressing-down toward whether the worshipper owned any alternative clothing at home.

### Altar Parading and the Cash Offer

Instead of leaving the matter at verbal correction, Kanyari escalated the interaction into an on-the-spot social intervention. Concluding aloud that the congregant might simply lack Sunday-best attire, he announced a direct financial subsidy.

“So that means you do not have church clothes? Oh no. If that is the case, then I will give you KSh 5,000 so you can buy proper church outfits. I am shocked that you do not have church clothes,” Kanyari said.

Before releasing the worshipper back to the pews, Kanyari instructed her to stand and spin around so the wider congregation could view the attire that had triggered his intervention.

“Please turn the other way so people can see how you are dressed,” he told her as fellow churchgoers looked on.

### Philanthropy Under the Spotlight

Once the 360-degree display concluded, the preacher directed the woman to return to her place, giving an explicit guarantee that the KSh 5,000 shopping allowance would be paid out before she left the church premises.

“Go and sit down and you will get the money. I like to support people when I have a chance,” he said.

The public exchange underscores Kanyari’s longstanding style of pastoral leadership, where spontaneous financial pledges and high-visibility congregational spectacles frequently share the pulpit with the sermon.
