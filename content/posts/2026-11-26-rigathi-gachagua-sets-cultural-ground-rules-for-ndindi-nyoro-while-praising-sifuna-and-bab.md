Rigathi Gachagua has made it clear that if Ndindi Nyoro wants an alliance, he will have to follow traditional cultural protocol and make the first move.

Speaking during a late-night interview on Citizen TV, the Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP) leader waved away swirling speculation of a behind-the-scenes truce with the Kiharu MP. The rumor mill had gone into overdrive hours earlier after the two influential figures crossed paths in broad daylight during a joint opposition press briefing in Nairobi.

The capital city gathering had brought together opposition heavyweights—including retired Chief Justice and United Green Movement leader David Maraga alongside Democratic Party chief Justin Muturi—to announce an upcoming boycott of a scheduled Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) meeting regarding 2027 polls. But for many watchers, the shared venue was instantly read as a potential Mt Kenya political reunion, especially given months of chilled relations since Nyoro aligned with the People’s Party of Kenya (PPK) back in August.

Closing the door on any premature talk of a coalition, Gachagua pointedly reminded observers of generational hierarchy. According to the former Deputy President, younger leaders seeking alignment cannot wait for senior figures to bridge the gap.

“I haven’t sat down with him. He hasn’t come to me. I am an elder. People come to me. That’s the way our traditions are. Anybody who wants mentorship, Riggy G is available,” Gachagua stated, maintaining that his door remains open only to those driven by national progress rather than self-interest or sabotage.

While keeping Nyoro at arm's length, Gachagua offered a striking contrast by turning his attention toward Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna and Embakasi East MP Babu Owino. Rather than dismissing the fiery opposition legislators, Gachagua singled them out as young leaders he is fully prepared to work alongside following deep, substantive talks.

Detailing his interactions, the DCP leader revealed that a rigorous ten-hour sit-down with Sifuna completely shifted his perspective. That extended engagement, he noted, left him persuaded that the youthful senator genuinely means well for the country and has earned a shot at leadership. He offered similar praise for Babu Owino, noting that separate consultations revealed policy ideas that caught his attention.

The sharp divide in how Gachagua handles his political peers highlights the evolving alliances taking shape ahead of the 2027 cycle, where cultural deference and marathon policy sessions carry as much weight as public appearances.
