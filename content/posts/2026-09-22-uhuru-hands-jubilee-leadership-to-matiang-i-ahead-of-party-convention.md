Former President Uhuru Kenyatta announced on Tuesday that he has surrendered the leadership of the Jubilee Party to his deputy, Fred Matiang’i, paving the way for a formal executive succession ahead of the party's upcoming National Delegates Convention (NDC).

Addressing an official Jubilee consultative meeting in Nairobi on September 22, Kenyatta instructed party organs to recognise Matiang’i as the new party leader to oversee operations during the interim period leading up to the NDC.

The directive immediately elevates the former Interior Cabinet Secretary to the helm of the party apparatus, placing him in operational control of party organs as the outfit prepares to convene its highest decision-making organ.

![uhuru jubilee](https://i.ibb.co/Fq3V55hP/cf490fcca75e.webp)

“While we wait for NDC, I have decided to let my deputy take over the party leadership. I want to ask you respectfully to accept Fred Matiang’i to take the position of Jubilee party leader,” Kenyatta told the assembled officials.

### Transition Roadmap

Under the arrangement outlined during the Nairobi meeting, Kenyatta will relinquish active day-to-day administrative command while maintaining formal affiliation with the party.

![uhuru jubilee](https://i.ibb.co/ymvBcZh7/ebdb2301ef1d.webp)

Kenyatta confirmed that he will remain an ordinary member of Jubilee and will continue to guide the political outfit in a senior advisory capacity.

The handover positions Matiang'i to steer party strategy, coordinate branch structures, and oversee delegate mobilisation in the run-up to the convention.

The National Delegates Convention is expected to officially ratify the leadership changes, formalising Matiang’i’s status as party leader under Jubilee’s constitution.
