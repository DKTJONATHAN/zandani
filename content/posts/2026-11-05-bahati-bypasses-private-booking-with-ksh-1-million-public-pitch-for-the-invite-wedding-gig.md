Kevin Bahati has upended the standard script for high-profile celebrity weddings by taking his event's entertainment procurement directly to the public, offering a KSh 1 million payout for a single live musical performance.

In a public call issued on Thursday, November 5, 2026, the musician bypassed traditional talent management agencies and behind-the-scenes event coordinators, putting out a direct invitation for local artists to pitch for a slot at his upcoming nuptials with Diana Marua.

“I have 1 million Ksh to pay 1 musician to perform at the Bahatis’ big day,” Bahati announced across his social platforms, framing the performance budget as a dedicated headline slot for their upcoming celebration, dubbed *The Invite*.

### Open Call vs Private Contracts

For major celebrity weddings of this scale, entertainment is typically handled through private agency retainers, undisclosed artist fees, or curated guest-star appearances arranged strictly behind closed doors. By contrast, Bahati’s open invitation converts a private event contract into a public pitch process, offering a rare high-value single-night performance engagement in the local live music ecosystem.

The upcoming event, branded as *The Invite*, is planned as a formal, black-tie affair expected to draw together close family, friends, and public figures. Rather than featuring a multi-artist lineup or relying solely on pre-recorded sets, the couple is structuring live music as a central focal point of the main program.

### The Brand Ecosystem Behind the Stage

Bahati and Diana Marua have spent years building a integrated digital footprint that spans mainstream music releases, reality television programming, and brand endorsements. Their wedding planning has followed a similar trajectory, turning key milestones into public engagement points while maintaining commercial interest around the event execution.

Setting a KSh 1 million price tag on a single wedding slot sets a notable precedent for private event bookings in the regional entertainment scene, where performance fees for private functions are rarely disclosed publicly at this level.

As of the initial announcement, Bahati has not revealed the shortlisting criteria, the application deadline, or whether a specific genre is being prioritized for the black-tie program. The identity of the musician who will land the KSh 1 million paycheck and take the stage at *The Invite* remains unconfirmed.
