Three years after ending her high-profile relationship with fellow content creator Flaqo, Keranta has altered one of the last permanent physical markers of their time together by covering up his name tattooed on her body.

Images shared across social media show the original name inscription replaced by a fresh artwork design spread across the same area of skin. The modification comes long after the initial public split of the duo, underscoring how physical romantic branding often lingers long past the digital dissolution of creator couples.

![Flaqo // Instagram](https://i.ibb.co/Dg1b9bjS/ab73a2c0cc4d.webp)

At the height of their joint visibility, Keranta and Flaqo stood among the most followed couples in Kenya’s digital entertainment scene. Their online presence was routinely built around collaborative content, public appearances, and shared milestones—a dynamic they anchored further through body art, including matching tattoos and the explicit lettering of Flaqo’s name on Keranta’s skin.

While public creator relationships can be scrubbed from social media feeds in minutes by archiving posts and untagging photos, permanent ink follows a much slower timeline. For three full years following their breakup, the body art remained intact while both creators built separate personal brands and moved into independent creative projects.

The newly revealed design uses a darker, detailed artwork overlay to completely obscure the lettering where the name previously sat. Neither Keranta nor Flaqo has issued a formal personal statement regarding the cover-up, leaving the visual transformation as the singular statement on the record.

The update resolves a long-standing residual detail from one of Kenya’s most visible online partnerships, proving that while digital footprints disappear with a tap, erasing physical markers of past relationships often takes a three-year countdown.
