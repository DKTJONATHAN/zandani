Kenya Power has formally notified 68 occupants on land parcel LR NO. JOMVU MV/222/2 in Jomvu, Mombasa County, that they must dismantle their homesteads, harvest standing crops, and relocate all livestock within 90 days or face forceful removal.

The statutory demand, issued Tuesday, October 27, 2026, aims to restore unencumbered utility control over the coastal parcel, allowing the power distributor to secure infrastructure corridors while shifting the entire financial and logistical burden of site clearance onto the residents.

### The Terms of the Notice

In the notice, Kenya Power declared the current settlement illegal under statutory land regulations, demanding that all private economic activity across the site cease immediately.

![An aerial view of a section of the 11km Mombasa-Kwa Jomvu Interchange.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/mombasa-kwa-jomvu-2.jpg.webp?itok=hnG0i0Us)

“Take Notice that you are in unlawful occupation of our land LR NO. JOMVU MV/222/2 situated in Jomvu, Mombasa County, without express lawful authority or any right or license under any law,” the company stated.

The directive requires residents to knock down both permanent structures and temporary shelters, strip out existing improvements, uproot agricultural plots, and clear out domestic animals. The three-month countdown expires in late January 2027, after which the state utility warned it will execute compulsory evictions at the occupants' own expense and peril.

Reclaiming utility land reserves, substation sites, and high-voltage transmission wayleaves is standard practice for Kenya Power, which routinely cites network maintenance access, electrocution risks, and grid reliability to justify clearing encroached corridors.

### Legal Recourse and Mounting Judicial Pushback

![Kenya Power engineers repair a collapsed pylon.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/kenya%20power%20engineers%20repair%20a%20collapsed%20pylon..jpg.webp?itok=VrR1gkJ2)

For the 68 occupants facing displacement, the utility pointed to the Environment and Land Court as the single official avenue to dispute the boundary or assert proprietary rights. 

However, Kenya Power’s ultimatum arrives amid heightened judicial resistance toward unilateral land reclamation by state corporations. Earlier this month, the Supreme Court threw out an appeal by Kenya Railways concerning the 2021 eviction of 3,500 Nubian residents in Kisumu, cementing appellate findings that the agency breached constitutional rights by failing to conduct humane, orderly relocations.

Similar state utility maneuvers have hit legal walls across the country this year. In July, the High Court intervened to halt planned demolitions across Nairobi's Muthurwa Estate, while Kenya Railways faced sustained legal challenges in May over its bid to recover prime residential plots in Lavington.

Whether the 68 Jomvu occupants assemble a collective legal challenge before the Environment and Land Court before the January deadline will determine whether Kenya Power secures undisputed access to the parcel or finds its reclamation tied up in protracted coastal land litigation.
