Kitutu Chache South Member of Parliament Antony Kibagendi has stepped into the spotlight with a sharp reality check for the executive, training his guns on President William Ruto’s ambitious KSh 2.23 trillion Dangote East Africa Petroleum Refinery project in Lamu.

Appearing on a televised interview on Monday, October 5, 2026, Kibagendi did not just question the administrative channels driving the multi-trillion-shilling venture; he took direct aim at the economic narrative being sold to the public. While President Ruto has championed the Mokowe groundbreaking as a masterclass in modern, innovative financing—leveraging public assets, prime land, and National Infrastructure Fund capital to draw institutional players—the Kitutu Chache legislator dismissed the accompanying hype around jobs and cost-of-living relief.

Drawing comparisons to Dangote’s established footprint in West Africa, Kibagendi noted that the Nigerian mega-facility runs on a lean workforce of roughly 3,000 to 4,000 employees. For the outspoken MP, using those figures as a baseline pours cold water on state projections promising tens of thousands of local jobs for Kenyans in Lamu. , he argued that the Nigerian plant has done little to meaningfully pull down local pump prices, giving him ample room to challenge the promised windfall for motorists.

Beyond the numbers game, Kibagendi’s fiercest jabs targeted the legal framework of the deal. He accused the administration of fostering an unacceptable pattern of opacity by committing state assets without the nod of the legislature. Reminding the country of Parliament's constitutional power of the purse, the lawmaker insisted that any heavy commitment of public resources demands rigorous legislative scrutiny rather than unilateral executive deployment.

“The opaque nature around investments involving public resources has become an unacceptable trend,” Kibagendi stated during the broadcast, framing the Lamu venture as a test case for executive accountability.

The high-profile pushback highlights a growing rift within political circles over the administration's alternative funding strategies. With opposition legislators raising persistent red flags over transparency, land allocations, and ultimate ownership structures, the path ahead for the Lamu refinery remains tied down in rising parliamentary friction.
