For an artist whose supporters know him as Mr Wonderful, the comment section of a live broadcast remains an unpredictable battleground where personal history and digital voyeurism collide. Akorino musician and philanthropist Karangu Muraya found himself navigating this exact friction during an appearance on the *Obinna Show Live* at Obinna TV Studios.

While the sit-down with host Oga Obinna was intended to traverse his ministry, charitable footprint, and personal journey, viewers repeatedly steered the live chat back to past marital disagreements involving his former partner, Triza Njeri. Rather than leaning into the digital curiosity, Muraya drew a firm boundary.

“Na siwezi ongelelea bibi ya mwingine hapa. Just ignore anyone who asks you about her. People do separate,” Muraya stated calmly on air, pointing out that Triza had since remarried and her current home deserved protection from public speculation.

The conversation also laid bare the personal toll of internet rumors. Muraya recalled a particularly stressful period in Dubai when online chatter regarding his marriage forced him onto a TikTok live stream to defend himself—a reactive move he now admits was a misstep born of immense pressure. Shifting focus to his actual domestic duties, he noted that he maintains active involvement in his children's education by keeping track of official school communication groups.

Away from family matters, the interview tracked the sheer scale of Muraya’s humanitarian travels. Revealing that his 64-page passport is nearly full, the singer explained that a significant portion of those international stamps represent medical trips accompanying sick Kenyans seeking specialized treatment in India.

“Passport ya 64 pages iko karibu kujaa na nikiangalia mahali nimeenda kwingi ni India,” he noted, contrasting his physical footprint on medical missions with the digital noise surrounding his private life.

The studio tension peaked toward the end of the broadcast when Obinna stepped in to counter online detractors questioning the musician's background and education. The presenter issued an open KSh 5 million cash challenge, giving critics one week to prove they had logged heavier international travel or handled larger monthly financial transactions than Muraya, underscoring the ongoing public scrutiny tracking the musician's dual life in ministry and entertainment.
