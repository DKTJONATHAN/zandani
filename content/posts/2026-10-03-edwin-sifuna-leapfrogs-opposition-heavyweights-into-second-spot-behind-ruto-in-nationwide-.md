Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna has dramatically reshaped the opposition pecking order, leaping past veteran political figures to claim the second spot behind President William Ruto in a new nationwide popularity survey.

In the Politrack Research Team survey conducted between September 15 and September 25, 2026, Sifuna recorded 19.6% of potential voter preferences. He finished second only to President Ruto, who topped the poll with 45.1% support. Sifuna's performance placed him comfortably ahead of several long-standing political heavyweights who have historically dominated presidential preference polls.

Wiper Democratic Movement leader Kalonzo Musyoka placed third with 10.3%, while former Interior Cabinet Secretary Fred Matiang’i registered 7.2%. Former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua logged 6.0% support. Sifuna’s 19.6% rating gave him nearly double the backing of Kalonzo and more than triple that of Gachagua among respondents polled across the country.

The nationwide exercise sampled 20,419 potential voters through a text-based technology platform, allowing participants to respond at their convenience. Politrack reported a 95% confidence level and a margin of error of plus or minus three percentage points. 

The survey also revealed that 8.4% of respondents remain undecided about their 2027 choices. Smaller shares of the vote went to Roots Party leader Prof George Wajackoyah at 1.1%, while former Justice Minister Martha Karua and former Chief Justice David Maraga tied at 0.8%. Siaya Governor James Orengo polled at 0.4%, and businessman Jimmy Wanjigi recorded 0.3%.

Sifuna’s emergence in the runner-up position follows months of heightened visibility through his Linda Mwananchi movement, which has been conducting grassroots mobilization drives across various counties. While President Ruto has been consolidating his base through nationwide infrastructure commissioning and development project inspections, Sifuna’s aggressive opposition outreach appears to be gaining traction among prospective voters.

Sifuna’s poll positioning coincided with a major regulatory win for his political outfit. The Political Parties Disputes Tribunal (PPDT) overturned a decision by the Registrar of Political Parties that had rejected the reservation of the name “Linda Mwananchi Movement” and its proposed abbreviation, “LLM”.

The tribunal ruled that the Registrar’s refusal lacked legal justification and ordered that the name be reserved within seven days. The applicants had successfully argued that the Registrar's initial rejection unconstitutionally restricted their right to form a political party.

While opinion poll ratings remain a snapshot of public mood during the survey window rather than a guarantee of election day outcomes, Sifuna’s double-digit rating and cleared party name mark a distinct shift in the opposition lineup heading into the 2027 election cycle.
