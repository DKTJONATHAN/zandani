President William Ruto has set November 2026 for the groundbreaking of the Ksh60 billion first phase of the Lake Victoria Ring Road, placing a firm timeline on a 612-kilometre littoral corridor that has lingered in state planning pipelines for fifteen years.

### What we know

* Mbadi’s confirmation of World Bank financing converts a perennial campaign talking point into an actionable, bankable capital project ahead of schedule.
* Circling five lakeside counties unlocks isolated fishing hubs and agricultural belts previously severed by chronic underinvestment in cross-border transport.
* Upgrading the project to a Class B2 highway with digital ducts reflects a modern shift toward multi-utility regional trade corridors.
* Carving Ksh60 billion out of the broader Ksh92 billion roads envelope tests the exchequer's execution capacity amid tightening infrastructural oversight.

Speaking during a grassroots consultative forum in Kisumu on Sunday, the President told local delegations that an agreement has been locked down to begin work on the multi-county arterial network before the close of the year. The initial Ksh60 billion injection forms the lion's share of a Ksh92 billion national trunk-road allocation, signaling an aggressive push to anchor state capital spending around the lake basin.

Treasury Cabinet Secretary John Mbadi told the gathering that multilateral capital is already secured. Mbadi confirmed that concessional financing negotiated with the World Bank has cleared key approval hurdles, removing the liquidity bottleneck that stalled earlier iterations of the project.

The corridor is designed to sweep through five counties-originating at the border county of Busia, cutting across Siaya and Kisumu, then tracking the southern shoreline through Homa Bay before terminating in Migori. Beyond connecting fish landing sites directly to regional trunk routes, the alignment provides a direct bypass around internal lake transit chokepoints.

The ring road's fiscal footprint has ballooned substantially over the decade. Initially mooted in 2016 under an estimated Ksh41.6 billion envelope, subsequent engineering reviews by the Kenya National Highways Authority (KeNHA) escalated the scope toward Ksh70 billion to match Class B2 highway specifications.

According to KeNHA's technical blueprints, the design incorporates a 7-metre-wide carriageway flanked by 2-metre paved shoulders to segregate pedestrian and bicycle traffic from heavy haulage. Ducting for fibre-optic infrastructure has also been built into the corridor’s right-of-way, converting the transport spine into a high-speed data route between the lakeside counties.

The November rollout will test whether the state can successfully shift the project from high-level political rhetoric to actual earth-moving, after more than a decade of false starts across three administrations.
