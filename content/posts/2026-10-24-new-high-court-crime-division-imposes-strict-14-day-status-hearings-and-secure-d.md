Judges assigned to Kenya's newly established International and Organised Crimes Division will be legally required to hold a status conference within 14 days of any fresh filing to establish rigid trial timetables and evidence disclosure parameters.

The division, created by Chief Justice Martha Koome via a Gazette notice dated October 19 and set to become operational on October 30, introduces specialised procedural rules that blend domestic criminal trial standards with International Criminal Court (ICC) rules of evidence where applicable.

![Martha Koome Online](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/martha%20koome%20online.jpg.webp?itok=_eI4A7ik)

Under practice directions issued by the Chief Justice on Friday, October 23, all new matters coming under the division’s mandate must be routed through its principal registry exclusively via the Judiciary’s electronic filing system. The framework incorporates a dedicated secure portal for submitting classified and confidential documents, alongside specific witness and victim protection safeguards such as closed hearings, voice distortion, opaque screens, and restricted records.

The court’s jurisdiction spans core international offenses under the International Crimes Act of 2008, as well as appeals in organised crime cases previously handled by subordinate courts. Its docket covers terrorism, human trafficking, migrant smuggling, kidnapping, arms trafficking, drug trafficking, piracy, wildlife offenses, environmental crimes, and related extradition and mutual legal assistance proceedings.

![The Judiciary](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/judiciary-image.jpeg.webp?itok=baKPzQ86)

Additionally, the division will exercise jurisdiction over constitutional petitions and judicial review applications alleging rights violations connected to matters within its mandate. Proceedings will generally be heard by a single judge, though the presiding judge retains discretion to assign certain appeals to a two-judge bench.

Cases already part-heard in existing High Court stations will remain anchored where they were filed, and subordinate courts will retain their current jurisdictional thresholds for active matters. Fresh filings submitted after October 30 will test whether the 14-day case management timeline can streamline complex transnational prosecutions through the Milimani registry.
