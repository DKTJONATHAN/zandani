The sudden seizure of three key airports in northern Ethiopia by Tigray forces has abruptly severed regional aviation links, forcing Ethiopian Airlines into indefinite flight suspensions and throwing travel corridors into disarray.

On Wednesday, September 23, 2026, the Tigray Defence Forces took control of airfield infrastructure in Mekelle, Axum, and Shire. The rapid operational takeover prompted an immediate response from the state carrier, which halted all scheduled flights to and from the three destinations. 

With no clear timeline for when the runways will reopen, the shutdown has effectively isolated a significant portion of northern Ethiopia from commercial air traffic.

### Grounded Flights and Travel Alerts

![Tigray People's Liberation Front (TPLF) being ferried in a truck amid escalating violence in the Afar region.](https://i.ibb.co/jkmRf9b4/1ceb5acab206.webp)

The air travel freeze prompted the U.S. Embassy in Addis Ababa to issue a security alert, advising foreign nationals—including Kenyans and Americans—to avoid both the Tigray and neighboring Afar regions. 

For regional travelers, particularly those from Kenya who rely on Addis Ababa as a primary aviation hub to connect to broader international networks, the closures introduce immediate logistical bottlenecks. Security officials have warned passengers booked on flights transiting through or near the affected zones to verify their schedules directly with carriers before arriving at departure terminals.

### A Fractured Peace and a New Alliance

This latest disruption marks a severe escalation in the power struggle between Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed’s administration and the Tigray People’s Liberation Front (TPLF). The conflict, which many hoped had settled after a peace agreement ended major hostilities nearly four years ago, has reignited with a coordinated military push.

The current offensive is bolstered by a newly formed coalition. The TPLF has aligned with six other armed groups, creating a unified front aimed at challenging federal authority in Addis Ababa. This expanded coalition significantly raises the stakes of the renewed conflict, threatening to destabilize transit routes that connect East Africa's largest economies.

### Diplomatic Backlash and Sanction Warnings

![trump-deport-plane](https://i.ibb.co/pBJnzYRk/ccdfa9c43c2c.webp)

The offensive has drawn sharp criticism from international mediators. In a statement, U.S. Ambassador to Ethiopia Ervin Massinga expressed deep disappointment over the TPLF leadership's decision to bypass diplomatic channels in favor of a large-scale military campaign.

Massinga noted that the TPLF, which was hit with Washington-backed sanctions earlier this year, had rejected African Union-led peace initiatives spearheaded by former Nigerian President Olusegun Obasanjo. 

"Rejecting dialogue sponsored by the AU under President Obasanjo and engaging in an unwinnable fight does nothing for the people," Massinga said.

The diplomatic fallout is expected to deepen. Washington has signaled that it is prepared to levy fresh, targeted sanctions against individuals and armed groups participating in the offensive. 

For now, the immediate focus remains on the regional skies. With three strategic northern runways under rebel control and commercial planes grounded, the conflict has shifted from a localized political dispute to a major threat to East African aviation and transport security.
