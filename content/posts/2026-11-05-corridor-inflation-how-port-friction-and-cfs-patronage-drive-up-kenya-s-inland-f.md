Shipping a 20-foot container thousands of nautical miles from China to the Port of Mombasa costs businesses approximately Ksh260,000 ($2,000). Moving that exact same container just 480 kilometers overland to Nairobi adds up to Ksh162,500 ($1,250) to the bill—a disproportionate inland haulage penalty driven by port delays, governance instability, and highway friction.

According to the World Bank’s November 2026 Kenya Economic Update published on Wednesday, November 4, moving cargo along the Mombasa-Nairobi corridor costs between Ksh230 and Ksh338 ($1.77 to $2.60) per kilometer. By comparison, moving goods over an equivalent distance on European transport corridors averages Ksh195 ($1.50) per kilometer.

The sharp cost disparity coincides with a significant decline in the port’s global operational standing. In the World Bank’s 2024 Container Port Performance Index (CPPI), East Africa’s largest seaport fell 49 positions to rank 375th out of 403 assessed ports globally, down from 326th out of 348 ports in the 2022 evaluation.

### Leadership Turnover and CFS Patronage

The World Bank tied Mombasa’s deteriorating efficiency directly to institutional instability inside the Kenya Ports Authority (KPA). The state corporation has had five managing directors over the past decade, including two acting chief executives between 2020 and 2023. The lender noted that executive volatility and rent-seeking have diluted the momentum needed to implement operational upgrades.

Administrative bottlenecks extend into off-dock infrastructure. The report raised concerns over the licensing of private Container Freight Stations (CFSs) operating around Mombasa, warning that facility concessions have frequently been awarded based on political connections rather than open market competition. This dynamic has heightened risks of collusion, mismanagement, and elevated costs for importers.

Cargo owners also face recurring IT system outages, rising port fees, and multiple administrative inspections. The World Bank documented widespread reports of illicit payment demands by officials across different government agencies responsible for clearing goods at the port gates.

### Highway Checkpoints and Expansion Plans

Beyond the marine terminal, physical transit along the Northern Corridor continues to slow down freight delivery. To address these delays, the government is rolling out the 2026 Roadblock Reduction Initiative. The policy targets a reduction in police checkpoints along the main Mombasa-to-Malaba transit route from between 22 and 27 current stops to a maximum of five gazetted stops, with the goal of cutting transit times from 80 hours down to between 36 and 40 hours.

Responding to the economic drag caused by port congestion, President William Ruto announced on Wednesday that the government is preparing to unveil comprehensive plans to expand the Port of Mombasa.

However, the World Bank’s findings indicate that capital-intensive berth construction alone will not restore Mombasa's regional competitive edge unless paired with executive stability at KPA, transparent CFS licensing, and the elimination of discretionary clearance touchpoints that inflate cost per kilometer across the country's main trade artery.
