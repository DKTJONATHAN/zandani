The security controls designed to protect asset-financed smartphones are facing an active challenge from regional repair centers, where illicit technicians are systematically stripping digital security locks to re-enter stolen units into the commercial market.

![A collection of mobile phones and motherboards seized by DCI detectives during a crackdown in Kisii Town.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/phones-recovered-by-dci-september-2026.jpg.webp?itok=TCtdGcri)

Evidence of the practice emerged in Kisii Town when a joint operation between local detectives and corporate fraud investigators from a mobile financing company exposed a high-volume hardware altering hub operating under the cover of a routine repair stall.

Officers from Kisii Central DCI and corporate risk analysts raided the shop located inside Market Plaza, arresting two suspects and seizing a cache of more than 500 mobile devices and standalone motherboards. Two individuals arrested at the scene remain in police custody pending formal charges.

### Bypassing Loan Lockouts

Mobile phone financing models rely heavily on remote software locks, which allow lenders to disable handsets when repayments default or when devices are reported stolen. However, the Kisii operation revealed that syndicates are circumventing these safeguards through physical component manipulation and security software overrides.

During the search at Market Plaza, investigators found multiple smartphones-previously flagged as missing by the credit provider-that had already been stripped of their factory security parameters. The presence of loose motherboards indicates that technicians were either harvesting unlinked hardware components or swapping system boards entirely to create untraceable devices.

According to the Directorate of Criminal Investigations, the facility served as a central clearing point where compromised phones were prepped for immediate resale to unsuspecting buyers.

### Systematic Hardware Exploitation

The Kisii seizure underscores a growing tactical shift by electronics syndicates across urban centers. Rather than attempting to sell locked devices directly, network operators are turning to technical workarounds that break the link between the handset IMEI and the lender's control database.

Law enforcement agencies have increasingly focused on repair outlets acting as intermediary processing nodes. In August, a similar tactical operation in Nairobi’s Central Business District led to the recovery of 38 smartphones, three laptops, and a desktop computer from a single commercial stall.

### Forensic Supply Chain Tracing

Detectives are currently subjecting the 500 seized devices and circuit boards to forensic analysis to establish where the units originated and identify the retail networks designated to receive the unlocked stock.

Police have advised consumers to verify device histories and purchase credentials before buying second-hand electronics from third-party vendors, as investigations into the supply networks feeding Market Plaza continue.
