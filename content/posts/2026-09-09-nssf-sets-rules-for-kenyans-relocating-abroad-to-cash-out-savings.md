The National Social Security Fund has issued clear conditions for Kenyan workers relocating abroad permanently to claim their accumulated pensions before reaching retirement age. The Fund directed applicants to show valid migration visas outside the East African Community, sworn affidavits, and termination papers to unlock their savings through Electronic Funds Transfer.

**What we know:**
* The Emigration Benefit applies only to contributors relocating permanently to countries outside the East African Community.
* Kenyan applicants must submit a sworn affidavit confirming permanent departure alongside an immigration visa and an NSSF card.
* Members who lose jobs but remain in Kenya cannot withdraw their funds until they reach age 50 and stop earning.
* Non-Kenyan citizens must provide a travel ticket, passport or Alien ID, and a certificate of service to process payout.

The Fund issued the guidance following public inquiries over when sacked or contract-terminated workers can access their contributions. NSSF reiterated that joblessness alone does not qualify a contributor under age 50 to withdraw money, unless they make a permanent exit across East African borders.

Under the outlined procedure, applicants must deliver their national ID or valid passport, a certified termination letter or certificate of service, and accurate bank details for Electronic Funds Transfer settlement. Foreign nationals leaving the country must attach a one-way travelling ticket to their application files.

The standard retirement route still requires local contributors to clock 50 years of age and fully exit formal employment before making a claim. Relocating within regional neighbours like Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda, Burundi, South Sudan, or the Democratic Republic of Congo locks contributors out of this early cash-out path.

NSSF stated that the migration visa must prove permanent residency intent abroad rather than short-term visitor travel, sealing loopholes for early withdrawals by workers who remain within the domestic economy.

### The Nairobi read

Let us be honest about what is driving this traffic at the pension desk. Young professionals trapped in Nairobi's volatile contract economy want liquid cash the moment an employer along Upper Hill or Westlands declines to renew a contract. Watching deductions leave your monthly pay slip while rent in Roysambu or Kasarani is due makes the NSSF vault feel like a holding cell for your own sweat.

The Fund's hard stance is standard practice for social security schemes, but it creates a bitter contrast on the ground. A laid-off clerk stranded on Mombasa Road cannot access a single shilling to start a small business. Yet a software developer holding a permanent residency visa for Canada or Australia gets their entire savings wired out before boarding. For the frustrated worker sitting in evening traffic on Thika Road, the signal is unmistakable: unless you survive to age 50, the only key to your pension is a one-way ticket through Jomo Kenyatta International Airport.
