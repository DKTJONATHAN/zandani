Industrial manufacturers of banned plastics in Kenya face more than just criminal prosecution; they risk the complete liquidation of their business assets. Under the country’s stringent environmental laws, a single raid can trigger the permanent forfeiture and destruction of entire production lines—a high-stakes reality highlighted by a major enforcement operation in Nairobi’s Eastlands.

National Environment Management Authority (NEMA) officers raided an active manufacturing facility in Njiru, Nairobi, seizing 90,685 single-use banned plastic bags. The operation resulted in the arrest of five suspects, including the plant's owner. The individuals were booked at the South B Police Station and subsequently released on cash bail ahead of their scheduled arraignment at the Kibera Law Courts.

![An image of the 90,695 single-use banned plastic bags seized by the National Environment Management Authority (NEMA) on October 7, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/m5JM3jYH/99fa1b4d26fe.webp)

While retail traders caught with banned plastics typically face inventory losses and minor fines, the legal framework reserves its most devastating penalties for the upstream supply chain. Under Section 144 of the Environmental Management and Coordination Act (EMCA), manufacturing, importing, or selling the prohibited bags carries a statutory fine ranging between Ksh2 million and Ksh4 million. Convicted individuals also face prison terms of one to four years, or both.

Crucially for industrial operations, the law empowers the state to permanently confiscate and destroy seized manufacturing equipment and raw materials. For factory owners, this clause elevates a NEMA raid from a routine compliance setback to an existential financial blow, as the loss of heavy machinery can permanently shut down an enterprise.

The Njiru raid marks an escalation in NEMA's enforcement strategy, which has recently balanced localized retail sweeps with targeted industrial audits. Just days prior, on Friday, September 25, enforcement officers arrested five traders in the Coast region for possessing banned carrier and flat bags. 

![Cartons of plastic bags impounded from a truck in Kitui on Wednesday, March 4, 2020.](https://i.ibb.co/dsNTjzKt/c55b4c4dc5f5.webp)

By shifting focus from regional retail markets to Nairobi's manufacturing hubs, the environmental watchdog is attempting to strangle the supply of single-use plastics at the source. Alongside these raids, NEMA has stepped up inspections of legitimately licensed industries. These are firms authorized to use plastic bags strictly for industrial and non-industrial primary packaging, a sector under constant surveillance to ensure licensed materials are not diverted into the illegal retail market.

The upcoming proceedings at the Kibera Law Courts will test these statutory measures. The court's decision will determine not only the personal liberty of the five accused but also whether NEMA will secure orders to permanently dismantle and destroy the machinery seized during the Njiru raid.
