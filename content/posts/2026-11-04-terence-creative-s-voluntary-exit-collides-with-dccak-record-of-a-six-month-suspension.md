Terence Creative framed his departure from the Digital Content Creators Association of Kenya (DCCAK) as a clean, self-directed exit on Monday, November 2, issuing an immediate resignation from his post as Secretary General. But the trade body’s leadership reacted almost instantly, stepping in to publicly reset the timeline with an administrative detail of their own: the comedian had actually been suspended six months prior.

In a formal statement circulated across his social media platforms, the veteran content creator addressed the association’s executive committee with a concise farewell. He expressed gratitude for his time in leadership while signaling that his departure from the executive table would take effect without delay.

"I hereby tender my resignation as Secretary General of the Digital Content Creators Association of Kenya (DCCAK), effective immediately," Terence wrote, adding that he planned to continue supporting creators through independent skills development and mentorship programs.

The narrative of a voluntary step-down stood for only a brief period before DCCAK issued a clarification to correct what it presented as an inaccurate depiction of his status within the organization. According to the association, Terence’s executive functions had been formally frozen on May 2, 2026, following an internal suspension.

To maintain administrative continuity, DCCAK revealed that Joan Wanjiku Waweru was installed as acting Secretary General on the same day the May suspension took effect—meaning the secretariat had been operating under new management for half a year by the time Terence published his resignation notice.

The public contradiction highlights the sharp friction that can emerge when high-profile personal brand management collides with official institutional governance in Kenya’s digital economy. While Terence’s public statement focused heavily on his ongoing commitment to sustainable creator livelihoods, the association’s prompt pushback prioritized organizational record-keeping, ensuring the industry understood his exit followed formal administrative action.

Terence established his wide public profile through comedy skits and relatable online characters like Kamami, eventually using his visibility to take up leadership roles within organized advocacy groups. His work with DCCAK had previously positioned him among the key figures speaking on behalf of digital content creators navigating commercial and regulatory changes.

Despite the back-and-forth over his official exit protocol, Terence maintained that his broader creative mission remains intact outside the association's structure. Whether DCCAK intends to disclose further details regarding the grounds for the May 2026 suspension remains unconfirmed, leaving the administrative transition in place as Joan Wanjiku Waweru continues in her acting secretariat role.
