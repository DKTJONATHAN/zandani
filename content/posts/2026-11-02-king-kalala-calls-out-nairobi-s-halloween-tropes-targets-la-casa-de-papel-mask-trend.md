Media personality King Kalala has drawn a firm line against uninspired Halloween disguises in Nairobi, taking aim at revellers who default to television props rather than thoughtful creative themes.

Targeting the crowd that treats the annual October 31 calendar date solely as an excuse for clubbing and social media content, Kalala called out the predictable emergence of specific pop-culture masks across local venues.

![Story Behind Eliud Kipchoge’s Custom Wrist-Watch Weighing 57 Grams](https://i.ibb.co/fY92tF5j/72a7a1aeadae.webp)

“Next Halloween msitokee na hizo mask za La Casa Di Pape. Kenyans have no idea what Halloween means,” Kalala warned, pointing directly to the widespread use of plastic masks borrowed from the Spanish television hit *La Casa de Papel* (*Money Heist*).

The Salvador Dalí facial mask, which became a global phenomenon through the Netflix heist series as a narrative signifier of anti-establishment rebellion, has frequently been repurposed in Nairobi as an off-the-shelf substitute for spooky attire.

Kalala’s intervention underscores a broader friction in Kenya's urban entertainment scene: the tension between embracing western seasonal observances and understanding the roots of what is being celebrated.

![Mbavu Destroyer and Truphena Muthoni Target 1B Trees in 24 Hrs](https://i.ibb.co/B2RXKqgB/2ee34141f532.webp)

Historically, October 31 originated from Samhain, an ancient Celtic festival that marked the conclusion of the harvest season and the arrival of winter. Celts held that the threshold separating the living from the dead thinned on that evening, prompting people to light bonfires and wear disguises to deflect wandering spirits. Over centuries of cultural evolution, those ritualistic practices transformed into modern global customs including trick-or-treating, themed pumpkin carvings, and commercial club nights.

In Nairobi's night spots, however, that historical evolution often gets reduced to quick accessory purchases. Instead of developing distinct concepts, many attendees reach for widely available plastic props like the Dalí mask, creating a repetitive visual routine across themed events.

By calling for an end to the *La Casa de Papel* look, Kalala has challenged local partygoers to either engage with the underlying themes of the holiday or elevate their effort when adopting international social trends.
