A massive overnight drone wave shut down the entire civil aviation perimeter around Moscow on Friday morning, bringing travel to a standstill just hours after Russian President Vladimir Putin hosted dozens of African heads of state, including Kenya's President William Ruto, for high-level economic negotiations.

The resulting nine-hour aviation lockdown forced the cancellation of more than 400 commercial flights across all four of the capital city's primary airports, exposing the acute operational vulnerabilities confronting Russia’s metropolitan infrastructure even as the Kremlin sought to project geopolitical normalcy and economic strength to visiting foreign partners.

### Nine Hours of Airspace Paralysis

Restrictions across Moscow’s airspace commenced late Thursday evening and persisted until Friday morning, when Russia's Federal Air Transport Agency began phased clearances. Flight operations at Sheremetyevo and Zhukovsky international airports resumed under standard parameters around 8:00 am, while Vnukovo and Domodedovo remained throttled under special clearance protocols well into the morning.

![President William Ruto (right) conversing with Russian Counterpart Vladimir Putin on the sidelines of the Third Russia-Africa Economic and Humanitarian Forum on Thursday, October 2](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/ruto-and-putin-1.jpg.webp?itok=mHD53-3R)

The commercial toll was immediate and heavily concentrated. Sheremetyevo recorded the most severe operational shock, logging 170 flight cancellations. Vnukovo posted 48 cancellations alongside 111 protracted departures, while Domodedovo and Zhukovsky registered four cancellations and one cancellation respectively.

With Moscow's air corridors obstructed, inbound international and domestic routes were redirected across northwestern Russia. Pulkovo Airport in St. Petersburg received at least 27 diverted flights, straining terminal processing capacities as transit passengers contended with multi-hour layovers on tarmacs hundreds of kilometres off course.

### Unprecedented Drone Volumes

The scale of the disruption reflected one of the heaviest aerial barrages directed at the Russian capital since the escalation of hostilities with Ukraine. Moscow Mayor Sergei Sobyanin reported that 862 drones had been targeted toward the wider Moscow region between Thursday evening and Friday morning.

According to Russian municipal and defence authorities, interceptor batteries destroyed dozens of unmanned aerial vehicles along outer approach corridors before they breached central metropolitan airspace. Nonetheless, civil aviation regulators enforced total ground stops to prevent mid-air collisions with incoming munitions and interceptor debris.

While commercial airlines bore the immediate logistical disruption, authorities confirmed by Friday noon that the drone waves did not directly penetrate the protected security sectors housing visiting African leaders or target official summit logistics.

### The African Diplomatic Backdrop

The security alert intersected directly with the conclusion of the Third Russia-Africa Summit, a two-day multilateral forum held in Moscow between October 28 and 29. The summit drew official delegations from 48 of Africa’s 54 nations, with 22 countries represented by their heads of state.

President Ruto had arrived in Moscow on Tuesday, October 27, leading a delegation that included First Lady Rachel Ruto and Prime Cabinet Secretary Musalia Mudavadi, who also oversees Foreign Affairs. The Kenyan mission focused heavily on bilateral trade talks, sovereign investment cooperation, and agricultural supply stability.

For African delegations seeking pragmatic commercial engagement, the overnight flight emergency provided a clear operational reminder of the domestic pressures encircling the host nation. While the Kremlin used the platform to advocate for non-Western trading alliances and alternative clearing systems, the morning’s grounding demonstrated that normal civilian logistics remain acutely exposed to the daily friction of the Ukraine conflict.

By midday Friday, normal scheduling had slowly begun returning across the Moscow hub network, as departing delegations and regular passengers navigated an extensive backlog of rerouted aircraft and rescheduled regional departures.
