National Treasury Cabinet Secretary John Mbadi has linked a planned overhaul of statutory payroll deductions directly to low-income workers earning Ksh32,333 and below, who stand to exit the income tax register under upcoming tax legislation.

Speaking on Thursday, October 22, the Treasury boss stated that the government intends to abolish the universal personal relief system entirely, replacing the longstanding credit with restructured Pay As You Earn (PAYE) tax bands that absorb entry-level earners.

The draft legislative amendments, scheduled for formal submission to Parliament next week, represent a major shift in how the Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA) assesses employment income.

"Recently you have heard us talk about some changes in PAYE bands to exempt those earning up to Ksh32,333 from tax. In fact, we want to do away with this thing called relief," Mbadi said.

![Ruto-Mbadi](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/mbadi-ruto.jpg.webp?itok=OfsdkMpG)

Alongside the PAYE overhaul, the CS confirmed that the forthcoming Treasury bill will seek to scrap stamp duty charges on land transactions valued below Ksh1 million.

### The Math Behind Section 30

Under Kenya's prevailing fiscal architecture, every formally employed taxpayer receives a standard personal tax relief of Ksh2,400 per month, totaling Ksh28,800 annually, pursuant to Section 30 of the Income Tax Act.

Employers deduct this statutory cushion directly from the gross tax computed on a worker’s monthly earnings. Under current rules, an employee whose gross tax liability amounts to Ksh5,000 sees their remittance reduced by the Ksh2,400 credit, resulting in an effective tax bill of Ksh2,600 remitted to the KRA.

The current tax structure applies a progressive band system starting at 10 percent on the first Ksh24,000 of taxable monthly income, climbing to 25 percent on the subsequent Ksh8,333, with additional tiers applied to higher brackets. For entry-level workers, the personal credit has functioned as the primary buffer lowering their effective tax liability.

### Exposure for Middle Earners

![A collage of jobseekers lining up in Nairobi (left) and the entrance at the National Treasury (right)](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/a_collage_of_jobseekers_lining_up_in_nairobi_left_and_the_entrance_at_the_national_treasury_right.jpg.webp?itok=eZvXHaVL)

By terminating the personal relief mechanism to create a zero-tax threshold at Ksh32,333, the Treasury's proposed framework fundamentally alters payroll liabilities.

While workers earning at or below the Ksh32,333 ceiling would be spared from PAYE deductions, employees earning above that threshold face a different calculation. If lawmakers repeal the Section 30 tax credit without making proportional downward adjustments to the higher tax brackets, thousands of salaried Kenyans earning above Ksh32,333 risk seeing their monthly net take-home pay contract by up to Ksh2,400.

Treasury’s push to revise the income tax schedule follows executive directions issued earlier in the week. During the 63rd Mashujaa Day celebrations at Kipchoge Keino Stadium in Uasin Gishu on Tuesday, October 20, President William Ruto confirmed the administration had adjusted its initial relief proposal upward from Ksh30,000 to Ksh32,333 per month.

“We have prepared a proposal to exempt Kenyans earning Ksh32,333 per month or less from paying PAYE income tax. For workers on modest income, every shilling saved means more money for food, transport and school expenses,” President Ruto stated.

The proposals now move to the floor of the National Assembly, where lawmakers will weigh the legal texts setting out the altered PAYE brackets against the planned repeal of the statutory personal relief.
