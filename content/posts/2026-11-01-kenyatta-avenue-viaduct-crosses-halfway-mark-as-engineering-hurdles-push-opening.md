The elevated link between Nairobi’s Central Business District and Upper Hill has crossed the 50 percent construction threshold, but structural complexities on the Ksh3 billion viaduct have formally pushed its delivery to May 2027, five months past its previous deadline.

President William Ruto announced the construction benchmark on Sunday, confirming that work on the one-kilometre elevated dual carriageway is advancing along the Kenyatta Avenue corridor. However, the timeline marks a further adjustment for a project originally expected to conclude by December 2026.

According to the Kenya Urban Roads Authority (KURA), the revised May 2027 target stems from technical hurdles encountered during the erection of the elevated deck. The agency noted that erecting the reinforced concrete piers, alongside precision engineering required to anchor heavy steel components and structural columns, demanded phased execution that slowed down earlier pace estimates.

### Stalled Groundwork and Technical Demands

The project’s climb to the halfway mark follows years of prolonged inactivity. Contractors initially broke ground on the viaduct in September 2020, but site operations froze for nearly five years following payment disputes between the state and the builders. 

Heavy machinery and crews only returned to the corridor in July 2025 after financing arrangements were restructured. Since the resumption, contractors have concentrated on casting the reinforced concrete piers that will carry the dual carriageway over the existing road grid.

Once completed, the elevated route will provide motorists an express bypass over the bottlenecked Kenyatta Avenue-Valley Road junction, channeling traffic directly into the commercial hub of Upper Hill.

### Wider Grid Realignments

The viaduct is not an isolated bypass. State planners have integrated the one-kilometre flyover into a broader reconfiguration of the western access routes serving Nairobi's core commercial district. 

The infrastructure envelope links into the Valley Road–Kenyatta Avenue/Ngong/Nyerere Interchange system, alongside the planned Upper Hill–Haile Selassie Overpass. Together, the elevated corridors are intended to strip transit traffic away from surface roundabouts that consistently choke during peak commuting hours.

For motorists enduring daily gridlock between the city centre and the corporate headquarters on Upper Hill, the crossing of the 50 percent mark confirms visible structural progress. Yet with phased column installations still underway, the full decongestion benefits remain tied to whether engineers can hold the line on the May 2027 handover.
