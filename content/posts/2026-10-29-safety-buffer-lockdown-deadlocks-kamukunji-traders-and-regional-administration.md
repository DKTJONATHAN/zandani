A nearly four-week commercial freeze triggered by a major structural failure in Eastleigh has spilled onto the streets of Kamukunji, exposing an administrative deadlock between disaster containment policies and small-business survival.

Hundreds of traders operating near the Kamukunji Business Centre marched through local streets on Thursday, October 29, demonstrating against mandatory eviction orders that have halted operations across the commercial precinct. The closures were extended to neighbouring blocks after safety agencies raised concerns that seismic shocks from a nearby building collapse may have compromised adjacent multi-storey structures.

Demonstrators carrying twigs and placards chanted through the avenues as onlookers gathered on upper floors of nearby buildings, signaling escalating frustration over an indefinite shutdown that has severed daily income for hundreds of families.

### Origin of the Commercial Freeze

The emergency restrictions stem from the October 3 catastrophic failure of a 16-storey building along 9th Street in Eastleigh, which trapped multiple victims and left dozens unaccounted for. In the immediate aftermath, multi-agency disaster teams established a wide perimeter, ordering all commercial operations directly across from the collapse zone to shut down.

![Snippets of the demos in Kamukuji over the eviction by the county government on October 29.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/kamukunji-traders-october-29.jpg.webp?itok=I0UUSrKC)

Engineers and disaster managers argued that the immense force of the 16-storey collapse presented unverified structural risks to surrounding foundations, specifically citing the multi-storey Kamukunji Business Centre. While search and recovery teams continue operating along the 9th Street corridor, the surrounding safety cordon has effectively locked out micro-enterprises that rely on high-volume daily retail turnover.

Traders on the sidelines of the Thursday procession emphasized that while disaster safety is critical, the government has offered no financial cushion, temporary relocation sites, or clear timelines for structural integrity tests.

"We want to ask the government to listen to our cry," one business owner noted during the demonstration. "When they ask us to evacuate, they should also provide more viable alternatives for us, because we depend on this business centre to put food on the table."

### Political Pushback Meets Administrative Mandate

The prolonged shutdown has forced a direct policy confrontation between local political representatives and national government security administrators.

![Aftermath of the Eastleigh building collapse in Nairobi on October 23, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/collapsed-building-eastleigh-nairobi-october-2026.jpg.webp?itok=r1ziJ8S4)

Kamukunji Member of Parliament Yusuf Hassan intervened on behalf of the affected business community, petitioning regional authorities to allow a controlled, partial reopening of unaffected sections of the market. Hassan argued that a tailored phased access plan could restore livelihoods while maintaining necessary isolation around active recovery machinery.

However, regional administrative leadership has firmly rejected any compromise on the perimeter.

Nairobi North Regional Commissioner Simon Osumba reiterated that security and safety evaluations along the 9th Street impact zone remain unchanged, stating that structural hazards to nearby buildings remain as severe as they were on the day of the disaster. Under current security directives, full access restrictions will persist until certified engineering assessments explicitly clear the surrounding built environment.

### Unresolved Timelines for Urban Traders

The standoff highlights a recurring gap in urban emergency management across Nairobi's dense commercial hubs: the absence of clear protocols for managing collateral economic disruptions during long-term disaster recoveries.

With multi-agency teams still anchored at the primary collapse site and regional administrators refusing to scale back safety cordons, traders face an indefinite lockout without designated alternative trading grounds. The standoff now rests on whether city engineers can expedite structural audits of the Kamukunji Business Centre to provide factual clarity on building safety before economic distress deepens further.
