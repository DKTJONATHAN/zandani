Armed with empty plastic jerry cans, high-decibel vuvuzelas, and branded T-shirts, civil rights activists turned Nairobi’s Central Business District into an uncomfortable public stage on Tuesday, October 20, 2026. Rather than joining official state gatherings, the demonstrators chose Mashujaa Day to launch an unscripted street protest against the Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority’s latest petroleum price increases.

The central city march targeted the government’s economic record at a moment reserved for national honor, drawing a direct line between historical struggles and present-day household distress across the capital.

### Reframing the Hero Narrative

Moving through city center streets, protesters banged empty fuel containers and blew horns, wearing printed T-shirts bearing the slogan “Reject High Fuel Prices.” For those on the streets, the national public holiday offered the ideal platform to challenge official celebrations while ordinary households struggle with compounding living expenses.

“We are not here to celebrate; we are here to cry to this government—at least hear our cries,” one demonstrator said during the march. “Those heroes they are celebrating… can’t be silent when the regime is oppressing the citizens.”

The public display directly challenged official state messaging, with demonstrators explicitly rejecting the ceremonial tone of the day to demand immediate fiscal relief from public officials.

### The EPRA Numbers Triggering the Street Action

The street mobilization followed the Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority’s (EPRA) monthly pricing review released on October 14, 2026. The new rates, which dictate petroleum costs for the October–November pricing cycle, delivered substantial increases across every major fuel category:

- **Kerosene:** Recorded the highest percentage jump, rising by Ksh 28.49 per litre—from Ksh 191.38 to Ksh 219.87.
- **Super Petrol:** Increased by Ksh 12.93 per litre, shifting the pump price from Ksh 214.03 to Ksh 226.96.
- **Diesel:** Climbed by Ksh 9.90 per litre, moving up from Ksh 217.86 to Ksh 227.76.

The steep jump in kerosene—a primary cooking and lighting fuel for low-income urban households—served as a central catalyst for the CBD demonstration, bringing economic frustration directly to the capital’s commercial hub.

### Demands for Resignation over Market Stabilization

As the procession moved through key intersections, protesters widened their criticism beyond EPRA's monthly bulletin to target executive leadership, arguing that officials unable to contain pump prices should step aside.

“Tumekuja hapa kuwaambia viongozi, kama wameshindwa kushukisha bei ya mafuta, basi waresign watoke kwa hizo viti,” another protester declared. “Kwa sababu saa hii kila mwananchi analia, wananchi wanahangaika… hatutawapigia magoti tena.”

The protest came as transport operators and small business owners across Nairobi adjust to the October 14 price shift. With diesel now standing at Ksh 227.76 per litre and petrol at Ksh 226.96, commuter minibuses and freight haulers face immediate operational cost spikes, which routinely spill over into food distribution and everyday consumer services.

By choosing Mashujaa Day for their demonstration, the civil rights groups sought to ensure that economic pressure remained central to the holiday's public discourse. Activists maintained that civic action would continue throughout the current pricing cycle as public pressure mounts on policy makers to address living costs before the next EPRA price review in mid-November.
