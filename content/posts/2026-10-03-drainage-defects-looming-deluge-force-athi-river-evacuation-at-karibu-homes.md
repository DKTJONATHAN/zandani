A seven-year progression of slow foundation sinking, compounded by rudimentary drainage errors, has forced the emergency vacation of three multi-unit apartment blocks at Athi River’s Karibu Homes estate just weeks ahead of forecast torrential seasonal rains.

The Institution of Engineers of Kenya (IEK) instructed occupants of Blocks M, N, and P—all located within the development's London Court section—to leave their premises immediately while emergency structural interventions and geotechnical testing are conducted.

While structural fractures in the blocks were first recorded in 2019, engineering inspectors determined that differential settlement—the uneven sinking of foundation footings into load-bearing soil—has widened the fissures to levels requiring full evacuation.

“Keep Blocks M, N and P vacant until repair and strengthening works are safely completed,” IEK President Harrison Kiplimo said in an October 2 directive.

### Footing Saturation and Structural Stress

![Apartments at the Karibu Homes Estate in Athi River, Machakos County.](https://i.ibb.co/yF0bgcpn/5df078ec1a19.webp)

Karibu Homes spans 500 residential units distributed across 63 low-rise apartment blocks roughly one kilometre off Mombasa Road. While the majority of the master-planned estate remains occupied, engineering assessments in London Court pinpointed chronic water mismanagement as a primary driver of the ground failure beneath the three affected blocks.

Inspectors found that roof downpipes discharged rainwater directly onto the immediate perimeter soil rather than channelling stormwater away into defined estate drainage infrastructure. The continuous saturation weakened the supporting subgrade over several hydrological cycles, destabilising the footings.

Alongside widening structural fractures through the load-bearing elements, engineers recorded extensive damp-related deterioration across the base of external walls, indicating prolonged exposure to standing surface water.

IEK has now ordered a dual-track technical audit consisting of destructive and non-destructive material testing alongside deep geotechnical boreholes to examine underlying soil strata before contractors can draft underpinning or structural reinforcement plans.

### Seasonal Window and Regulatory Gaps

![A section of apartments at the Karibu Homes Estate in Athi River, Machakos County.](https://i.ibb.co/5W93y6fG/5a63808e44b2.webp)

The evacuation comes under severe time constraints. The Kenya Meteorological Service Authority (KMSA) has projected above-average rainfall across the region between October and December under an advancing El Niño cycle.

For structures already experiencing active differential settlement, intense stormwater infiltration poses immediate risks of accelerated ground movement, prompting engineers to demand vacant possession before heavy downpours begin saturating the foundation footprint.

Beyond London Court, the engineering lobby advised estate residents to monitor adjoining blocks for auxiliary signs of foundational shifting, including jammed door frames, sticky windows, and standing water pooled against exterior walls.

The structural failure has also intensified scrutiny over building oversight in high-density developments along the Mombasa Road corridor. IEK cautioned that developer compliance with mandatory professional supervision remains inconsistent during actual execution.

"This situation demonstrates why it is very important to engage appropriately qualified and licensed professionals throughout the design, construction, inspection and completion of buildings," the lobby noted, reminding developers of their obligation to register active sites with the National Construction Authority (NCA) and maintain certified resident engineers.

Reoccupation of the vacated blocks now hinges on the results of the geotechnical borehole investigation and the developer’s capacity to install foundation underpinning before sustained rainfall saturates the Athi River soils.
