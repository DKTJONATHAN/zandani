When Caroline Karimi stepped up to claim the inaugural Top Woman in Finance in East Africa title at the 2026 CFO Awards in Nairobi, the plaque was backed by some very heavy balance sheet arithmetic.

Organised by CFO East Africa on November 26, the summit gathered regional business leaders to grade corporate stewardship. While Karimi was also nominated for the overall East Africa CFO of the Year honors, her win as the region's top female financial executive was anchored in hard operational targets rather than symbolic praise.

Behind the recognition sits a deliberate tightening of Co-op Bank Group’s financial plumbing. Under her watch as Group Director of Finance and Strategy and Chief Financial Officer, the bank established a revenue assurance unit that has successfully recovered more than Sh1 billion. Alongside that internal audit sweep, Karimi spearheaded capital and debt-raising transactions exceeding US$300 million, shoring up the group's liquidity position as it navigates a heavy multi-year expansion cycle.

Those numbers form the engine room for the bank's Good to Great Corporate Strategic Plan for 2025–2029, a roadmap targeting a balance sheet exceeding Sh1 trillion by 2029. The strategy relies heavily on the finance office to manage costs, allocate resources, and speed up digital transformation while deepening the institution's historical roots in the co-operative movement.

Karimi’s corporate footprint spans more than twenty years across multiple heavily regulated industries. She began her career in management accounting at Toyota East Africa in 2003 before moving to Safaricom in 2007 as a finance business partner. A stint at Unilever as a finance manager followed in 2012 before she permanently shifted to Co-operative Bank in December of that same year.

Within the bank, her desk shifted through cost and business change management and business performance effectiveness before she stepped into her current group director role in June 2020. Academically, she holds a Bachelor of Commerce and an MBA in Strategic Management from the University of Nairobi, alongside professional certifications as a Certified Public Accountant of Kenya (CPA-K) and a Certified Public Secretary. She is also a certified productivity coach and an alumna of the Harvard Kennedy School leadership program.

The regional win signals how executive finance roles have evolved past ledger entries and annual budget rounds into active corporate navigation. As capital markets remain sensitive to regional macroeconomic shifts, Karimi's next ledger test involves keeping Co-op Bank locked on its Sh1 trillion horizon without slipping on cost controls.
