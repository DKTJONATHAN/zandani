US-based Kenyan social media creator Bernice Saroni has pressed administrators at her son’s American school over student supervision gaps after campus security footage verified that her son, Mr Mwangangi, popularly known as Junior, was attacked and choked by a fellow student inside an unmonitored holding zone.

Saroni raised the alarm on Thursday, November 5, 2026, after Junior reached her by phone while crying and gasping for breath, recounting how he had been ambushed from behind by a sixth-grade pupil. The physical attack, according to Saroni, stemmed from an unresolvable grudge over a minor traffic cone incident nearly a week earlier.

According to Saroni, Junior had accidentally knocked over a traffic cone on school premises a week prior, prompting the other student’s father to retrieve it. Although Junior apologised immediately at the scene, the pupil allegedly held onto resentment before confronting him days later.

The altercation escalated inside the school's bus room, where the student caught Junior off guard from behind. Saroni detailed that the attacker pinned her son and cut off his airway while explicitly linking the physical violence to the prior encounter.

“While choking my baby, he told him, ‘YOU MADE MY DAD ANGRY!’” Saroni recalled, noting that only the quick intervention of Junior’s classmates prevented further harm before he was able to place an emergency call to her.

The incident exposed an operational lapse within the school’s routine monitoring setup. When Saroni immediately contacted the institution to establish how her son was assaulted while under their care, staff acknowledged that duty teachers were stationed outside the facility, leaving the children inside the bus room without direct adult oversight.

When Saroni initially demanded answers, the school principal stated she was unaware of the confrontation but committed to auditing the facility’s internal closed-circuit television (CCTV) system. The principal later called Saroni back to confirm that the security footage explicitly recorded the sixth-grade student choking Junior as described.

For Saroni, who is raising four Black sons in the United States, the official confirmation of the footage shifted the issue from an isolated peer fight into a broader critique of institutional safety and the protection of Black children in academic environments.

“WHERE WERE THE TEACHERS? WHO WAS WATCHING OUR CHILDREN? HOW SAFE ARE OUR BABIES IN SCHOOL?” Saroni questioned following the administrative briefing.

She expressed distress over the psychological toll such violence inflicts on young learners, questioning why children should encounter hostility in spaces designed for their growth and protection.

“And as a Black mother raising four Black sons in America, I cannot help but ask: Why so much hatred? Why should our children have to experience this kind of violence?” she stated, adding that no student deserves to return home traumatised from a learning environment.

Saroni, who frequently shares her family life and parenting journey with a wide online audience, maintained that the principal's verification of the video footage is only the starting point for addressing campus safety, declaring that she will not remain silent when the physical security of her children is compromised.
