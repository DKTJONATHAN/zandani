Actor and media personality Nicholas Okwach Ouma, widely known as Nick Kwach, started his Mashujaa Day reflection with a rare break from his usual backstage modesty.

“Let me make today about myself,” Kwach wrote on Instagram on Tuesday, October 20, 2026, before immediately pivoting to the statutory body behind his new title. “Okay, it is not me making it; it is the Hero Council.”

The cheeky opening set the tone for a major milestone in Kwach's career. After sixteen years spent building a footprint across Kenyan theatre, stand-up comedy, and screen production, the performer stepped onto the lawns of State Lodge in Eldoret, Uasin Gishu County, to accept a National Hero’s medal directly from President William Ruto.

The presentation took place during official Mashujaa Day activities in Eldoret, witnessed by guest of honor Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed of Ethiopia.

“I am in my 16th year as an actor and a professional artist, and on this day at the State Lodge in Eldoret I was presented a Hero’s medal by the President of the Republic of Kenya, H.E. Dr William Samoei Ruto, together with the Prime Minister of Ethiopia, H.E. Abiy Ahmed, as a National Hero,” Kwach told his followers.

For Kwach, the official decoration marks a significant transition. While mainstream celebrity coverage often focuses on short-term viral spikes, Kwach’s trajectory has been defined by long-term industry endurance—spanning live theatrical runs, sketch comedy, television appearances, and behind-the-scenes production work that keeps creative crews employed.

The National Heroes Council cited Kwach’s multi-faceted contributions across theatre, acting, comedy, and creative production. He was named among 31 honorees selected under the Arts category in the 2026 national awards framework.

The state criteria for the Arts category focuses on personalities whose creative output strengthens cultural identity, tackles social issues, preserves heritage, and provides platforms for public discourse both locally and internationally.

In his public statement, Kwach framed the presidential medal not as an individual trophy, but as a stamp of credibility for the broader fraternity of writers, directors, stagehands, and fellow actors he has worked alongside since starting out sixteen years ago.

“This recognition through the National Heroes Council validates years of determination and dedication to the craft and solidifies the need to continue creating a path,” Kwach stated, pointing to the collective sweat equity required to sustain a full-time artistic career in Nairobi and beyond.

Rather than framing the state award as a capstone to a completed career, Kwach emphasized that the Eldoret recognition serves as renewed momentum to keep expanding opportunities for younger performers trying to break into Kenya’s entertainment industry.
