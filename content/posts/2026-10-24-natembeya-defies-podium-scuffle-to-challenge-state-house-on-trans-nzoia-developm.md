Trans Nzoia Governor George Natembeya attributed an attempt by rival politicians to forcefully strip him of his microphone on Saturday to an effort to stifle scrutiny of the national government's regional development record, defying the disruption to complete a stinging critique of State House.

The standoff occurred during the burial service of veteran Cabinet minister and diplomat Burudi Nabwera, where uniformed police officers stepped in to form a protective ring around the county chief after a delegation of political figures advanced onto the dais.

### The Standoff on the Dais

The confrontation began when several political figures abruptly rose from their seats and moved toward Natembeya at the lectern, attempting to wrest the microphone from his grip. The leaders claimed the governor had breached the event's schedule, arguing his protracted address was stalling the formal interment proceedings and freezing out other dignitaries slated to speak.

![A section of leaders surrounds Governor George Natembeya at a burial in Trans Nzoia on Saturday, October 24, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/natembeya.JPG.webp?h=5e89ff6f&itok=zzUFi2lU)

Natembeya refused to yield the lectern, using the active public address system to challenge both the timing objections and the authority of the politicians trying to unseat him.

“I am the governor of Trans Nzoia. I know how the programme runs,” Natembeya told the congregation. “The programme states that the old man should be buried at 2pm, but it is currently 21 minutes past 1pm. Give me time to finish my speech.”

Uniformed officers quickly took up positions between the county boss and the opposing leaders as tempers flared, preventing the confrontation from degenerating into direct physical fighting. The resulting commotion nonetheless sparked confusion among the congregation, prompting hundreds of attendees to leave the burial grounds.

### Clash Over National Resources

Once order was restored and organizers granted him clearance to proceed, Natembeya turned his address squarely onto President William Ruto’s administration, accusing the executive of systematically deprioritizing Trans Nzoia in capital allocations while directing public funds elsewhere.

“We have rejected bias in development by the Ruto administration,” Natembeya said. “We need to tell the President the truth that despite his claims that he brings development here in Trans Nzoia, he should know that Trans Nzoia has for a long time been abandoned while other regions receive development.”

![Trans Nzoia Governor George Natembeya addressing mourners at the burial of former Minister Burudi Nabwera on Saturday, October 24, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/natembeya_0.JPG.webp?itok=YANNyohr)

The confrontation marked a sharp escalation in the governor's ongoing political positioning against the national administration, using the high-profile platform of a state funeral to voice grievances over decentralized financing and infrastructure projects.

### Honoring a Diplomatic Pioneer

The burial brought together regional leaders to mark the passing of Nabwera, who died on October 8, 2026, at his residence in Kileleshwa, Nairobi, aged 99, following multiple health complications.

Nabwera occupied a central place in post-independence public administration, serving as Kenya’s first ambassador to the United States and its inaugural Permanent Representative to the United Nations. He later transitioned to electoral politics, becoming the first Member of Parliament for Lugari Constituency upon its establishment in 1988 before his appointment to the Cabinet.

Despite the brief disruption that emptied sections of the seating pavilions, the burial ceremony resumed under heightened security, concluding with Nabwera's final rites.
