Paris has appointed Wadid Benaabou as France’s new Ambassador-designate to Kenya, replacing Arnaud Suquet who ends a four-year tour in Nairobi this Tuesday morning, September 08, 2026 EAT. Benaabou, an auditor by training, now prepares to present his diplomatic credentials to President William Ruto at State House Nairobi.

* Wadid Benaabou, previously a senior adviser at the French Court of Auditors, succeeds Arnaud Suquet as France's envoy to Kenya.
* Arnaud Suquet steps down this September 2026 after heading the embassy on Peponi Road since 2022, taking a new Africa-focused desk at the French Foreign Ministry in Paris.
* French shipping conglomerate CMA CGM committed KSh 106 billion (€700 million) to expand and run container berths at the Port of Mombasa.
* The incoming envoy must secure formal agreement and present his letters of credence to President William Ruto in Nairobi before taking full charge.

Benaabou announced his posting on Tuesday, addressing Kenyans directly in Swahili and French. "Habari Wakenya! I am honoured to begin my mission as France’s Ambassador-designate to Kenya. France and Kenya share a strong partnership, rooted in friendship and a shared ambition for the future," he stated, adding "Niko tayari!" He steps into the embassy in Westlands following years of scrutinising state expenditure at France's Court of Auditors.

Outgoing ambassador Arnaud Suquet, who also served as France’s Permanent Representative to the United Nations Office in Nairobi at Gigiri, leaves for the Quai d'Orsay to manage African affairs. In his farewell note, Suquet pointed to deep bilateral ties and friendships built during his stay in Kenya and previous postings in the Horn of Africa.

The diplomatic handover coincides with heavy French capital moving into coastal transport infrastructure. French shipping titan CMA CGM signed a KSh 106 billion agreement during the Africa Forward Summit in Nairobi, witnessed by President Ruto and French President Emmanuel Macron. The capital injection funds modernization works across container handling berths at the Port of Mombasa, expanding capacity for transit goods headed into Uganda, Rwanda, and eastern DR Congo.

Benaabou will complete formal clearance protocols through the Ministry of Foreign and Diaspora Affairs on Harambee Avenue before heading to State House. His arrival reinforces an economic focus as Paris protects its corporate footprint across Eastern Africa.

### The Nairobi read

Look past the polite Swahili pleasantries on social media. France sending an auditor to Nairobi is a deliberate calculation. Paris is no longer just managing cultural exchanges; French conglomerates have billions on the line. CMA CGM is pouring KSh 106 billion into the Port of Mombasa, Rubis runs fuel stations from Waiyaki Way to Jogoo Road, and French engineering firms want major road expansion tenders.

For a commuter stuck in traffic on Thika Road, an ambassador swap looks like routine diplomatic musical chairs. Yet the underlying shifts are tangible. State House needs deep-pocketed foreign private capital to bankroll infrastructure without blowing out public debt ceiling limits. Meanwhile, Paris needs an anchor in Anglophone East Africa as its influence in West Africa retreats. Benaabou’s real assignment is not hosting cocktail parties in Muthaiga. His job is guarding French corporate interests, tracking contract delivery, and making sure French capital keeps flowing smoothly through Kenyan corridors.
