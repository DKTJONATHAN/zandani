Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP) leader Rigathi Gachagua has staked his domestic political future on the total electoral consolidation of Mount Kenya, warning that any internal splintering of the region would guarantee President William Ruto a second term and force him into foreign exile.

The former Deputy President, speaking during an interview on Inooro TV on Sunday, November 1, revealed that he has prepared a personal exit plan to relocate to his residence in the United States if the incumbent administration retains power in the 2027 General Election. Gachagua framed his potential departure not merely as a retirement, but as a flight from anticipated political retribution that he argues will target dissenting leaders under an emboldened second-term regime.

"Ruto will try to use our people to split the region, and if I see that happening, I know that Ruto will be re-elected. And if he is re-elected, we will be in trouble, but I won't be here. I will be in America. I have a house there," Gachagua stated, explicitly linking his presence in Kenyan public life to the voting cohesion of his home base.

### An Ultimatum Built on Regional Control

![DCP leader Rigathi Gachagua and his wife, Pastor Dorcas, during a visit to their home in Dallas, Texas, in October 2026](https://i.ibb.co/60f9H81F/fc8db3e51d28.webp)

Gachagua’s relocation stance arrives days after his unpublicized return from an extensive diaspora tour across the United States, where he engaged Kenyan communities to build campaign logistics and opposition backing. During that trip, photographs surfaced of a Dallas, Texas property bearing his branded "I Love Wamunyoro" signage, formalizing months of speculation over his foreign asset base.

Yet by openly presenting an overseas retreat as his fallback position, Gachagua has elevated the stakes for Mount Kenya voters. His argument rests entirely on a zero-sum equation: regional unanimity delivers Ruto’s defeat, while any factional dissent surrenders the presidency and eliminates his local political protection.

"We cannot allow the Mt Kenya Region to be divided," he added. "I know we will defeat Ruto if we unite as the region, but if we don't, Ruto will be re-elected, and if that happens, I will board a plane and go to the U.S."

### Coalition Pushback Over Ethnic Balkanization

While Gachagua frames regional cohesion as an existential necessity, that rhetoric is actively widening cracks across the broader anti-government coalition, where several key principals openly contest his regionalist doctrine.

Jubilee Party leader Fred Matiang'i delivered a direct rebuke to Gachagua on Sunday, accusing the former deputy president of prioritizing ethnic supremacy over a viable national coalition. Matiang'i argued that attempts to organize the opposition around regional chieftaincies would alienate other voting blocs and undermine the collective effort to challenge the ruling United Democratic Alliance.

![DCP leader Rigathi Gachagua engaging with Kenyans in the U.S.](https://i.ibb.co/8DVY3tWN/7ea0d078aad8.webp)

"That pride and arrogance is what is going to split the opposition and make it difficult for the opposition to come together. We must have everyone on board," Matiang'i said, cautioning that Kenya's political direction cannot be negotiated through ethnic balkanization.

### Compounding Opposition Headwinds

The ideological friction between Gachagua and Matiang'i compounds a broader pattern of discord within the emergent opposition alliance. Just days earlier, on Friday, October 30, public hostilities flared during the launch of the Linda Mwananchi headquarters in Nairobi, where supporters allied to leader Edwin Sifuna openly booed Wiper Democratic Movement leader Kalonzo Musyoka.

Those public rebuffs have unfolded against aggressive overtures from the state. Government-aligned leaders have stepped up public appeals urging Musyoka to abandon the fractious opposition benches and cross over to the administration, arguing his seniority is unappreciated by his current partners.

Though opposition principals maintain that their overarching objective remains uncompromised—to unseat President Ruto in 2027—Gachagua's exit ultimatum underscores the fragile terms of that coordination. Rather than presenting a cohesive national front, the anti-government platform enters November navigating competing leadership styles, geographic turf wars, and the unresolved challenge of mounting a unified presidential bid.
