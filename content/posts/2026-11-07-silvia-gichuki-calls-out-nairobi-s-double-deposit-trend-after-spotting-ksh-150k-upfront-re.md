Silvia Gichuki of the Mziza Family has publicly questioned the steep cash requirements confronting Nairobi house hunters after discovering that property owners in the capital are increasingly locking tenancy agreements behind a double security deposit standard.

Reacting to the trend in a short video published across her social media accounts, the digital content creator expressed disquiet over terms that require prospective tenants to mobilize three months' worth of rental capital before receiving physical access to an apartment.

"Wheeh guys, hamkuniambia siku hizi mnalipa deposit ya miezi mbili ndo muingie kwa nyumba. Jamani!" Gichuki said, spotlighting a growing practice among urban landlords seeking higher financial guarantees from incoming occupants.

### The KSh 150,000 Entry Threshold

Under the terms highlighted in Gichuki's commentary, a tenant securing a residential apartment listed at KSh 50,000 per month is required to hand over KSh 150,000 in cleared funds prior to move-in.

The upfront structure comprises KSh 50,000 for the initial month’s rent and KSh 100,000 dedicated to a refundable two-month security deposit. While property owners traditionally utilize deposits to buffer against structural damage or unresolved utility balances when tenancies conclude, doubling the baseline requirement multiplies the immediate financial friction for movers.

For job seekers, mid-level professionals, or families relocating into or across Nairobi, raising three times the monthly lease figure creates a severe liquidity barrier even when the baseline monthly rent sits within their ongoing budget.

### Compounding Moving Expenditures

The upfront rental requirement does not exist in isolation for city residents. Gichuki noted that securing the keys represents only the primary phase of an expensive relocation process.

Before settling into a house, tenants must simultaneously absorb secondary operational costs. These include hiring logistics and transport services, settling utility connectivity deposits such as water connections, and furnishing or equipping the physical space.

For an individual earning a gross monthly income of KSh 50,000, accumulating KSh 150,000 solely for the landlord's entry requirements—before logistics and operational setup are factored in—presents a structural hurdle that narrows accessible housing stock across Nairobi’s estates.
