National security and administrative networks find themselves drawn directly into the machinery of the 2026 Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education (KCSE) examinations, as localized crises from urban absenteeism to armed border skirmishes disrupt the calendar.

While Education Cabinet Secretary Julius Ogamba was detailing operational updates during container openings in Nairobi, the Ministry faced concurrent fronts threatening the administration of the tests. In Marsabit County’s Moyale sub-county, escalating tensions from border skirmishes between Somali and Ethiopian communities forced an abrupt halt to examinations after stray bullets struck two local schools. Seven students sustained injuries, and five individuals were arrested in connection with the violence, leaving the Ministry scrambling to arrange alternative testing schedules for the 12 affected candidates.

![Several shops set on fire in Moyale as rival communities clashed on Thursday, October 22, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/wrQdn9GK/b3286bded850.webp)

At the same time, urban centers are presenting administrative hurdles of a different scale. In Nairobi alone, approximately 13 candidates have gone missing, prompting the Ministry to deploy local chiefs, assistant chiefs, and the Nyumbakumi security network to trace the absentee teenagers and encourage them to return to the examination rooms.

These logistical disruptions run parallel to a grim tally of candidate casualties reported across the country. Roteno Kongestina Achieng of Alaru Central Mixed Secondary School in Rachonyo West fell ill during her papers on Monday and died in hospital. In Turbo Sub-County, Cornelius Kiplagat Rotich of Katebe Secondary School passed away at the Moi Teaching and Referral Hospital on Tuesday after completing his morning mathematics paper. Meanwhile, independent police investigations are underway in Gem, Siaya County, following the murder of candidate Vivian Adhiambo Owiti at her family home on October 30.

![Education CS Julius Ogamba oversees the distribution of KPSEA exams in Westlands, Nairobi County on Monday, October 28, 2024.](https://i.ibb.co/03H4T78/6696bdab0bcc.webp)

Against this backdrop of administrative strain, the Ministry has sharpened its warning to exam center managers. Officials announced that every member of the examination management chain will face direct liability for any confirmed facilitation or enablement of examination malpractice.

With examinations ongoing across the remaining calendar, the Ministry faces the dual task of securing regional test centers caught in communal flashpoints while maintaining accountability protocols through local administrative channels.
