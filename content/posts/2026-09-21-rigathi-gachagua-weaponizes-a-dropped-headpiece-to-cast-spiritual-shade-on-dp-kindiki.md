Rigathi Gachagua has never been one to let a good visual blunder go to waste. Speaking to a Kenyan diaspora crowd in Kansas City, the Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP) leader turned a minor ceremonial slip-up into a major spiritual veto, claiming that a dropped traditional headpiece is proof that the ancestors have rejected his successor, Deputy President Kithure Kindiki.

The former Deputy President took his regional rivalry global during a town hall meeting at the Matt Ross Community Center on Saturday, September 19, 2026. Addressing the audience, Gachagua zeroed in on a recent cultural coronation ceremony where GEMA community elders attempted to install Kindiki as a regional leader. During the ritual, the traditional headpiece placed on Kindiki's head slipped and fell to the ground—a clumsy moment that Gachagua gleefully reframed as a sign from the spiritual world.

According to Gachagua, the mishap was not merely gravity at work; it was a deliberate rejection by the spirits of the ancestors and authentic elders of the Mt. Kenya community. He told the gathering that genuine custodians of the region's culture would never have sanctioned the ceremony in the first place, leaving "stage-managed" actors to carry out a flawed ritual.

“The elders are saying these guys think we are fools,” Gachagua told the Kansas City town hall. “You come to a meeting to tell elders we tried to divide the mountain and we have failed, and therefore the mountain cannot be divided; it should be one.”

For Gachagua, the coronation was a hypocritical display by government-aligned figures who had previously spent months trying to fracture the Mt. Kenya voting bloc. Having failed to split the region politically, he argued, they were now resorting to artificial cultural ceremonies to manufacture a legitimacy they had not earned on the ground.

By centering his attack on the fallen headpiece, the DCP leader is attempting to paint Kindiki’s leadership as not only politically weak but culturally illegitimate. He insisted that despite state-backed efforts to elevate alternative leadership figures, the electorate in Mt. Kenya remains united and sees right through the theatrical gestures.

As the battle for control of the mountain continues, Gachagua's Kansas City address makes it clear that the rivalry will not just be fought on policy or party lines. It will be waged through the interpretation of symbols, leaving Kindiki’s dropped headpiece as the latest weapon in a highly theatrical struggle for regional supremacy.
