Motorists travelling along Kenya’s main freight artery between Nairobi and western regions will wait until early December before accessing the dualled 60-kilometre section between Rironi and Naivasha, pushing back a promised late-October launch while heavy paving continues at four kilometres per week.

Transport Cabinet Secretary Davis Chirchir confirmed the adjusted timeline on Friday, October 30, during a joint site inspection with the National Assembly’s Transport Committee in Naivasha. The shift marks a departure from Chirchir’s September commitment, in which he assured drivers that four-lane operations along the Rironi-Naivasha stretch would be complete by October 30 to alleviate severe congestion along the Rift Valley escarpment.

Addressing lawmakers during the tour, Chirchir said contractors have deployed more than 90 specialized teams across the section to sustain paving speeds. The surge in site activity follows the securing of fresh bitumen stocks, resolving a supply bottleneck that had threatened to stall asphalt production during peak construction weeks.

### Partial Operational Handover at Naivasha Junction

![Roads and Transport CS Davis Chirchir alongside the National Assembly's Transport Committee inspecting the ongoing expansion of the Rironi-Mau Summit highway on Friday, October 30,](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/rironi-mau-summit-1_0.jpg.webp?itok=fJsl6Z06)

Despite pushing the operational launch into early December, the 60-kilometre road will open while significant civil engineering works remain incomplete at key junctions. The ministry has carved out active construction zones around the Naivasha Junction, where plans call for a full grade-separated interchange featuring an overpass and an underpass.

While mainline long-distance traffic will transition onto four paved lanes, construction crews will remain on site at Naivasha Junction to execute bridge foundations and structural concrete works. Contractor teams are also scheduled to advance site clearances towards Gilgil and Mai Mahiu, setting the stage for broader highway expansion work targeted for completion by June 2027.

### The Toll Model and PPP Delivery Framework

The Ksh200 billion expansion of the overall Rironi-Mau Summit corridor is being executed under a Public-Private Partnership (PPP) model. China Road and Bridge Corporation (CRBC) is managing the initial phase covering the Rironi-Naivasha stretch, while Shandong Hi-Speed Road and Bridge International Engineering is designated to execute the subsequent section from Gilgil to Mau Summit.

![Road construction and tarmac laying along the Rironi-Mau Summit highway near the Ngarariga Interchange.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/rironi-mau-summit_3.jpg.webp?itok=2-IC79QQ)

Under the overall design, the existing two-lane highway between Rironi and Naivasha expands to four lanes, while the section extending from Naivasha to Mau Summit will be widened to six lanes. Once the full infrastructure package is commissioned, the corridor will operate as a user-pay toll road managed through Intelligent Traffic Management Systems (ITMS) and modern gantry points.

Motorists will be charged Ksh8 per kilometre on the Rironi-Naivasha section, rising to Ksh8.50 per kilometre on the Naivasha-Mau Summit stretch. 

### Contractor Vetting and Immediate Benchmarks

The timeline extension coincides with heightened parliamentary review of public infrastructure performance across the country. Chirchir revealed that the Ministry of Roads and Transport has instituted tighter due diligence requirements and performance audits for firms executing state projects.

The immediate operational focus now shifts to early December, when the ministry intends to open the 60-kilometre four-lane section to peak holiday traffic while civil engineering teams initiate foundation work on the Naivasha overpass structure.
