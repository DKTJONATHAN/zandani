From the quiet heat of Texas backdrops, Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna turned his sights squarely on the executive quarters in Nairobi, bringing a fiery domestic audit straight to a Kenyan diaspora town hall in Dallas.

Addressing attendees far from the Capital's familiar political turf, the Linda Mwananchi Party leader trained his critique on proposed financial allocations earmarked for interior furnishings and building upkeep. Standing before the overseas audience, Sifuna zeroed in on what he cast as ill-timed expenditures, pegging the fresh furniture allocations at Sh24 billion with an approaching election cycle just nine months away.

“Asking for Sh24 billion now for furniture, nine months to an election that you are going to lose,” Sifuna told the gathering, framing the spend as disconnected from wider public economic pressures.

The overseas address did not stop at soft furnishings. Moving past the drawing rooms, the Nairobi lawmaker pointed his critique at the recurring architectural cycles at the House on the Hill, specifically targeting the frequency of structural refurbishments like repeated roof replacements. In Sifuna's assessment, such cyclic outlays point to deeper systemic leakages within public procurement.

“So if you look at all those things, you know that for a fact there is a lot of wastage and corruption in the country, and we need to deal with it,” he remarked to the diaspora crowd.

The cross-continental critique underscores how local political friction continues to find foreign sounding boards as political figures position themselves ahead of the 2027 polls.
