President William Ruto is set to officially commission the newly transformed Kamukunji Recreation Park in Nairobi's Shauri Moyo, where decades of political rallies and teargas are giving way to soundproof recording booths, a boxing ring, and a public performance amphitheatre.

The historic 7.27-acre complex has reached 95% completion, marking a major physical pivot for a site long recognized primarily as an epicenter of Kenya's political opposition movements and public gatherings.

While Kamukunji built its national reputation as a venue for fiery political speeches—most notably hosting the historic July 7, 1990 Saba Saba rally that defied a state ban and accelerated the push for multiparty democracy in December 1991—the revamped facility expands its role into an entertainment, sports, and creative production hub for Eastlands youth.

### Sound Booths and Performance Arenas

At the center of the site's creative infrastructure is a newly constructed community centre built to support local talent and digital learning.

The building houses a fully equipped music recording studio alongside an information technology laboratory and a public library. The dedicated studio setup is designed to offer neighborhood artists, musicians, and digital creators direct access to recording gear within their residential neighborhood.

For live creative events and showcase performances, the redeveloped park incorporates an outdoor amphitheatre designed to host public cultural shows, community gatherings, and talent exhibitions. The space is surrounded by landscaped gardens, children's play zones, and paved pedestrian walkways fitted with solar-powered lighting.

### Athletic Grounds and Combat Ring

Alongside its creative production amenities, the upgraded grounds feature expanded sports facilities targeting youth talent development across multiple disciplines.

The athletic layout includes:
* An 11-a-side standard football pitch
* A seven-a-side playing field
* Full-sized basketball courts
* A permanently built boxing ring

The integration of a boxing ring directly ties the facility to Shauri Moyo's grassroots martial arts traditions, giving local fighters a structured training location alongside field-sport athletes.

### Heritage Preservation and River Drainage

Despite the structural additions, the redevelopment maintains Kamukunji's protected status as a national heritage site. The redesign preserves the historical open-space identity of the grounds while introducing retaining walls and engineered drainage systems to control seasonal flooding.

The drainage works form part of the wider Nairobi Rivers regeneration initiative, with the Nairobi Rivers Commission identifying the Shauri Moyo site as a priority location for community-focused urban renewal.

With construction teams finalizing paved walkways, drainage channels, and studio interiors, the facility stands at 95% readiness ahead of President Ruto's official ribbon-cutting ceremony.
