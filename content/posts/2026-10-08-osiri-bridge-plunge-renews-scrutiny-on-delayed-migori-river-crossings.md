The nocturnal passage of market traders across south-western Kenya's rural river arteries turned catastrophic on Wednesday night when a lorry carrying hawkers veered off the road and plunged into River Migori at the Osiri Bridge crossing.

The crash along the Osiri-Kalangi route in Nyatike Sub-County has left multiple passengers and pedestrians feared drowned or trapped underwater, while injured survivors were pulled from the wreckage and transferred to St Joseph Mission Hospital in Migori town. As emergency teams search the waters of River Kuja, the disaster has swiftly revived community pressure over the hazardous state and slow pace of construction on the critical river crossing.

### Midnight Transit on an Unfinished Crossing

The vehicle was navigating the secondary corridor connecting Osiri to Kalangi late Wednesday, October 7, when the driver reportedly lost control on the approach to the bridge. The heavy truck crashed through the crossing boundary and dropped into the riverbed below.

![Relatives of rescuers of Migori accident victims standing outside St Joseph Mission Hospital in Migori on Wednesday, October 7, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/21Tv12dv/e9352bcde82f.webp)

Such lorries regularly serve as informal communal transports for hawkers moving stock between rural market centres after dark, carrying a dense mix of cargo and human passengers. Several occupants and pedestrians in the vicinity were caught underneath or inside the vehicle as it sank, while others were cast directly into the current.

Search and recovery teams continued combing the riverbanks into Thursday morning. Local authorities have not yet confirmed the final fatality count or the total number of individuals unaccounted for beneath the surface, with operations constrained by the depth of the water and the position of the submerged lorry.

### Uriri Lawmaker Demands Accelerated Bridge Works

While emergency responders worked downstream, the incident prompted immediate institutional demands regarding the route's physical safety. Uriri Member of Parliament Mark Nyamita, in an official message consoling bereaved families and those undergoing emergency treatment, directly linked safety on the corridor to unfinished bridge works.

Nyamita urged the national government to expedite active construction projects at the Osiri crossing over River Migori/Kuja, warning that motorists and pedestrians cannot safely negotiate the crossing under its current structural condition.

![A photo collage of a mangled wreck of a lorry involved in an accident (left) and people standing outside a hospital.](https://i.ibb.co/twBrgJ32/5262a950fae5.webp)

"I am deeply saddened by the tragic accident reported last night at Osiri Bridge along River Migori/Kuja, involving a lorry carrying hawkers and pedestrians," Nyamita stated, calling for faster delivery of a permanent, reliable span to shield local commuters from high-risk river approaches.

### Compounding Casualties on Secondary Corridors

The Nyatike river plunge comes less than a week after another devastating commercial vehicle wreck along Kenya’s highway network. On Saturday, October 3, a brake failure on a heavy lorry at Salama in Makueni County triggered a multi-car collision that claimed 17 lives and sent seven people to Sultan Hamud Sub-County Hospital.

While highway enforcement typically fixates on trunk routes like the Nairobi-Mombasa highway, crashes like the one at Osiri Bridge underscore the lethal hazards operating on rural connector roads, where uneven infrastructure and heavy commercial haulage intersect.

Official figures from the National Transport and Safety Authority (NTSA) record 9,002 road traffic fatalities nationwide between January and October 2026—a mounting casualty toll that increasingly reflects not only highway speed, but structural vulnerabilities on the rural margins where traders move their livelihoods after dark.
