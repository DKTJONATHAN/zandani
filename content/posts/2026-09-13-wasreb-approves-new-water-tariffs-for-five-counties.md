Households and businesses across Kirinyaga, Mandera, Kiambu, Homa Bay, and Nyeri will see higher utility bills starting next month after the Water Services Regulatory Board (WASREB) approved a fresh multi-year tariff review for local supply companies.

### What we know

* WASREB cleared three-year price adjustments for five regional utilities aiming to bridge operating deficits between 2026 and 2029.
* Arid Mandera absorbs the highest base charge nationwide at Sh180 per unit, exposing the structural cost of pumping in northern frontiers.
* Linked sewerage surcharges fixed at 75 percent will push aggregate sanitation costs higher for urban tenants and commercial premises.
* The approvals signal the regulator’s deliberate push toward full cost recovery, despite mounting household budget strain across the regions.

The new rates, published in a September 11 gazette notice, will anchor utility billing across the 2026/2027 to 2028/2029 financial cycles. The regulator noted that the adjustments were granted after evaluating individual utility performance, written public submissions, and operational overheads to keep regional water companies solvent.

Under the revised structure, Kirinyaga County Water and Sanitation PLC (KICOWASCO) customers using between one and six cubic metres monthly will pay Sh80 per unit, scaling up to Sh145 per unit for consumption exceeding 300 cubic metres.

In Kiambu, consumers served by Kiambu Water and Sewerage Company (KIAWASCO) will pay Sh91 per cubic metre for their initial six units, with heavy commercial and residential users paying up to Sh160 per unit above the 300-cubic-metre mark.

Mandera’s Takaba Water and Sewerage Company Limited (TAWASCO) posted the steepest domestic baseline, opening at Sh180 per unit for low-volume users and rising to Sh312 per unit for heavy consumers, even as the county administration continues to inject annual subsidies into local operations.

In Western and Mt. Kenya regions, percentage-based adjustments took precedence. Homa Bay County Water and Sanitation Company (HOMAWASCO) secured a 4.46 per cent tariff increment for the 2026/2027 billing year. Nyeri’s Tetu Aberdare Water and Sanitation Company (TEAWASCO) received approval for a 4.37 per cent increase following an audit of its operational output.

The upward shift extends directly to sanitation services, with sewerage charges across most serviced urban centres pegged at 75 per cent of a customer’s metered clean water consumption. WASREB cautioned that water service providers risk licence suspension or cancellation under the Water Act and the Public Finance Management Act if they fail to adhere strictly to the approved billing bands.

The revised tariffs take effect 30 days after the gazette publication date. The move follows similar mid-August adjustments that raised bills in Kisii, Narok, and Tharaka Nithi, while Eldoret secured downward adjustments following a Water Tribunal determination. For urban households and small businesses already navigating elevated basic utility costs, the phased rollouts leave little room for unmetered usage, making tight consumption management essential as new billing runs begin.
