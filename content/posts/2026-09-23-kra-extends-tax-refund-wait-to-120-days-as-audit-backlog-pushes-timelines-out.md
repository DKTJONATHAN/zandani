Kenyan taxpayers tracking pending disbursements face an extended liquidity wait after the Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA) quietly stretched its processing window from 90 to 120 working days.

The revised timeline emerged following mounting public inquiries from applicants whose files approached the original three-month deadline without resolution. Confirming the adjustment in official correspondence, the tax authority noted that the entire administrative lifecycle—spanning initial intake through to the final determination of whether a request is accepted or rejected—now officially occupies up to 120 working days.

![Customers being attended to at KRA's offices](https://i.ibb.co/XxqzQy2P/08829d1be1e6.webp)

While the baseline statutory expectation has expanded by 30 working days, actual disbursement speeds remain unevenly distributed. The length of time required to move funds from state accounts back to a taxpayer depends heavily on whether an individual file triggers a mandatory audit and the specific category of tax involved.

For applicants, the extended window alters cash-flow planning, particularly for businesses and individuals relying on overpayment recoveries to manage working capital. Because the 120-day limit covers both approval and rejection phases, unresolved claims can sit in administrative review for months before a taxpayer receives a definitive position from the authority.

![An undated image of the KRA entrance](https://i.ibb.co/fY3L9kYr/f4ed9a901804.webp)

The authority has yet to detail whether additional staffing or automated processing upgrades will be deployed to clear the backlog before claims hit the outer boundary of the new four-month operational cap.
