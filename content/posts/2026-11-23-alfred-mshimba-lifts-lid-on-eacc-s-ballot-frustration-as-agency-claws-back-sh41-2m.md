EACC Director of Ethics and Leadership Pascal Mweu walked into a media workshop on Monday, November 23, 2026, to address a question that routinely dogs the anti-graft agency every campaign season: why individuals flagged for massive integrity breaches still manage to get their names printed on official ballot papers.

Speaking on behalf of Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission Chief Executive Officer Abdi Mohamud, Mweu put the constitutional boundaries squarely on the record. Under Chapter Six of the Constitution, the EACC’s role during an election cycle begins and ends with background screening. The agency reviews tens of thousands of self-declaration forms and hands advisory reports to the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC). The actual power to clear or lock out any politician sits exclusively with the IEBC.

That institutional divide has created a frustrating cycle for investigators, according to EACC Commissioner Col (Rtd) Alfred Mshimba. Addressing media practitioners during the Nairobi session, Mshimba revealed that ahead of both the 2017 and 2022 General Elections, the commission submitted detailed adverse integrity dossiers naming specific aspirants deemed unfit for office. 

Every single candidate highlighted in those reports was ultimately cleared by the electoral body to contest.

### The Post-Election Catch-Up

Because advisory flags were bypassed at the ballot stage, Mshimba noted that the commission was forced to pursue those same individuals after they took office. Several leaders flagged in past cycles were subsequently arrested, prosecuted, convicted of corruption, or removed through impeachment proceedings—a sequence Mshimba acknowledged has fueled public skepticism over whether electoral integrity checks actually work.

During every major election, anti-graft officers process over 20,000 self-declaration forms submitted by hopefuls seeking public clearance. Yet without statutory authority to enforce candidate disqualification, the commission's reports function strictly as recommendations that electoral managers can choose to heed or set aside.

While ballot clearance remains outside its direct mandate, the commission is wielding substantial muscle against officials who used forged academic documents to secure appointments and salaries.

### Paper Trails and Recovered Cash

Mweu detailed the results of an ongoing crackdown targeting academic and professional certificate forgery across government institutions and elective dockets. To date, the commission has investigated 228 cases involving fraudulent paperwork. Out of those files, 134 were forwarded to the Director of Public Prosecutions with recommendations for trial.

The court proceedings have already yielded concrete financial returns. EACC has secured 45 convictions out of 50 concluded trials, successfully recovering Sh41.2 million in irregular earnings and unearned salaries drawn by individuals holding fake papers.

With early political campaigns accelerating ahead of the 2027 polls, Mweu warned that the commission will intensify its audit of academic credentials presented by aspirants, targeting both county assembly candidates and executive aspirants.

### Setting up 2027 Enforcement Desks

Beyond credential checks, the commission is positioning its field teams to monitor campaign spending and voter inducement. EACC warned public servants against participating in partisan political activities, citing explicit restrictions under the Conflict of Interest Act.

To bridge the gap between intelligence gathering and formal election challenges, the agency pledged to investigate voter bribery networks directly. Evidence gathered during campaign field operations will be compiled to support subsequent court petitions filed against winning candidates accused of electoral offenses.

To prepare the public before nominations open, the commission announced plans to launch a nationwide voter sensitization drive. The rollout will feature dedicated reporting desks designed to capture real-time evidence of ethical breaches, vote-buying schemes, and credential manipulation as campaign machinery shifts into high gear.
