The Kenya Revenue Authority has clamped down on flat-rate customs clearance for groupage cargo, introducing a mandatory exclusion list that caps restricted high-turnover goods at just 5 percent of total shipment volume.

Under guidelines published in a public notice on Thursday, November 5, the tax collector detailed 30 categories of goods barred or strictly restricted within the government's Ksh2 million consolidated cargo clearance arrangement. The policy operationalizes directives issued by President William Ruto in September, which instructed customs officials to lower the general cargo benchmark from Ksh2.5 million while walling off high-value items from simplified clearing channels.

By enforcing physical volumetric constraints measured in cubic meters (CBM) alongside strict unit-value pricing, KRA is tightening tax controls over consolidators who previously cleared mixed commercial shipments under flat rates.

### Total Exclusions vs. 5 Percent Volumetric Caps

![Small-scale traders protesting in Nairobi CBD on Friday, August 28, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/k2fzpjkd/e1b9f4a41ed3.webp)

The revised regulatory notice establishes a clear division between items completely barred from consolidated clearance and those permitted only as secondary cargo within tight volume limits.

KRA has instituted absolute exclusions for major consumer imports, removing foodstuffs, smartphones, smartwatches, televisions, used clothing (*mitumba*), used shoes, and ready-made garments from the simplified Ksh2 million framework. Importers bringing in these product lines must now process their consignments through standard individual customs entry and valuation procedures, regardless of cargo weight or total invoice value.

For a second tier of restricted items—including motorcycle and motor vehicle spare parts, industrial cables, computer accessories, mobile phone accessories, cosmetics, fabrics, carpets, household linen, and new footwear—the tax authority has introduced a strict volumetric ceiling.

Under the new rules, items listed from No. 12 to No. 30 on the exclusion list may only qualify for consolidated clearance if their aggregate physical volume does not exceed 5 percent of the total shipment volume, measured in cubic meters.

### Unit-Value Benchmarks and Declaration Rules

Beyond space limits, KRA is imposing stricter documentation burdens on consolidators attempting to clear eligible mixed shipments.

To clear restricted secondary goods under the 5 percent allowance, importers must fully disclose every item on the commercial invoice, customs entry, and master importer list, specifying exact product descriptions and quantities. KRA officers will apply mandatory unit-value benchmarks to determine the taxable customs value of each line item prior to granting final clearing approval.

The policy shift follows months of friction between revenue officials and urban small-scale traders over cargo valuation metrics. In September, following trader demonstrations over rising freight costs, President Ruto directed KRA to lower the overall consolidation threshold to Ksh2 million while tasking the authority with identifying high-value merchandise that distorts flat-rate tax collection.

By restricting electronics, wearable technology, and fast-moving apparel to standard customs channels, KRA effectively removes high-duty goods from groupage containers, requiring importers to absorb individual tariff assessments and physical verification checks before cargo release.
