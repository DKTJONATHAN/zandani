Eddie Butita is not letting creative industry ties dictate his ballot ahead of the 2027 General Election. 

Speaking in an interview on Tuesday, November 10, 2026, the comedian and content producer laid down an unusually measured political scorecard, pushing back against viral anti-incumbency rhetoric while declining to offer a blanket endorsement to his friends in Nairobi's marquee races.

While online chatter continues to ride on election catchphrases, Butita dismissed the growing sway of viral framing like *wantam* and *tutam*, arguing that internet punchlines are an unreliable metric for civic appraisal. For him, the conversation around the presidency should settle on tangible delivery rather than street buzz.

Addressing critics who have written off President William Ruto’s chances of securing a second term, Butita stated that a second administration remains an active reality on the ground. He pointed to the uncompleted Talanta Stadium in Jamhuri as the likely stage for the next presidential assumption of office.

“If Ruto wins, tutakuwa pale Talanta Stadium, tunamuapisha tena. Mtu mwenye atashinda, well and good,” Butita said, insisting that civic competition should conclude without lasting animosity. “Hakuna bad blood.”

### The Proximity Argument

Butita’s confidence in the Head of State draws heavily from his own direct exposure to executive workings. The content creator has featured prominently in several high-profile state engagements across the current term, observing presidential operations from up close.

While conceding that the administration has weathered severe public criticism and institutional blunders, Butita maintained that the public assessment should focus on infrastructure and measurable capital projects rather than absolute dismissal.

“I might be wrong, I might be right, but time will tell the truth,” he said, framing leadership evaluation as a long-term calculation that outlasts election-cycle friction.

That same pragmatic filter shaped his view on legislative contests, where Butita broke his general neutrality to throw firm support behind Lang’ata MP Felix Odiwuor, popularly known as Jalang’o. Endorsing his fellow media personality for the Nairobi Senatorial seat, Butita cited Jalang'o’s transition into constituency administration and public service drive as grounds for countywide elevation.

### Holding the Line at City Hall

Yet where friendship could have easily translated into another quick endorsement, Butita drew a firm line at City Hall.

The Nairobi gubernatorial contest has already drawn heavyweights, with incumbent Johnson Sakaja facing off against Embakasi East MP Babu Owino and former Nairobi Chief Officer Geoffrey Mosiria. Despite personal and professional rapport with the contenders, Butita declared himself entirely undecided.

Instead of aligning with an early frontrunner, the producer insisted that the capital’s gubernatorial hopefuls will have to earn their mandate strictly on paper. He urged city voters to abandon peer loyalty and partisan devotion, pointing to constitutional mandates and manifesto feasibility as the only viable benchmarks.

By declining to take an easy side in Nairobi’s fiercest municipal contest, Butita is pitching a selective political posture: standing by the President's public works record, pushing a seasoned creative colleague toward Senate, and forcing City Hall’s contenders to prove their worth on policy rather than clout.
