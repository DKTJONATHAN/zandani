Edwin Sifuna knows how to work an audience when the script goes sideways. Fresh off being turned away from an appearance before students at Jomo Kenyatta University of Agriculture and Technology, the Nairobi senator surfaced in Karen on Thursday, September 17, facing an entirely different kind of interrogation: what, exactly, is going on between him and Embakasi East MP Babu Owino?

Rather than untangle weeks of whispers that the Linda Mwananchi movement is coming apart at the seams, Sifuna pivoted to personal grooming.

Pressed directly on the health of his alliance with Babu, the senator bypassed the question entirely. Instead, he leaned into theatrical deflection, turning to the gathered JKUAT students and demanding a vibe check.

“I have said I look well. How do I look?” Sifuna asked.

The crowd delivered the requisite affirmation, giving the senator the punchline he wanted. When pressed further about murmurs that the coalition had fractured, Sifuna pointed to the faces surrounding him as living exhibits of unity. 

“Ask these people here,” he said. “Some of them have told me secretly and privately that they are in Linda Mwananchi. Do they look well? Yeah, they are well.”

It was a textbook Sifuna performance—charming, combative, and completely devoid of an answer.

Behind the quips, however, the political household he shares with Babu Owino has grown remarkably crowded and noisy. The Karen exchange arrived after days of public posturing that laid bare deep strategic clashes at the top of Linda Mwananchi.

Only two days earlier, on Tuesday, Babu called his own press conference to address the elephant in the room. The Embakasi East legislator pointed straight at the unresolved headache of picking an official party vehicle for the outfit. Early backroom talks had rotated between Babu’s political outfit and the People of Renaissance Movement, led by Saboti MP Caleb Amisi.

That fragile consensus blew open when an unnamed political figure from Western Kenya stepped forward with a brand-new party, loudly pushing for Sifuna to seize the presidential ticket. The push threw the movement’s internal hierarchy into disarray, prompting Amisi to distance himself from the joint venture and reassert his own party’s independence.

Matters had already boiled over publicly on September 13 during a Linda Mwananchi rally in Nairobi. In the aftermath of that event, Sifuna supporters pointedly accused Babu of trying to outshine the senator and carving out factional divisions. 

Babu pushed back hard on Tuesday. While dismissing claims that he was sabotaging Sifuna, and explicitly reaffirming support for Sifuna’s presidential ambitions, Babu also announced he would be pulling back from future Linda Mwananchi rallies to pour his energy into his Nairobi gubernatorial bid. To complicate the picture even further, Babu confirmed plans to formally unveil a party of his own.

Meanwhile, Amisi has pointed his finger across the county border, blaming Trans Nzoia Governor George Natembeya for escalating the hostility. Amisi accused Natembeya of adopting an aggressive stance that shoved away core founders and supporters, fueling the very rifts now threatening the collective.

Sifuna may look well on camera in Karen, but the cosmetic reassurance does little to settle the backstage maneuvering. With Babu charting an independent gubernatorial path, Amisi operating on his own terms, and party tickets multiplying by the week, the senator’s aesthetic review leaves the real partnership questions entirely unanswered.
