President William Ruto dropped the stiff diplomatic decorum just minutes before red-carpet formalities began at State House on Friday, November 27, 2026, turning sharply toward his principal assistant with a single teasing question: “What happened to El Niño?”

The unexpected corridor jab landed squarely on Deputy President Kithure Kindiki. Standing in the official lineup assembled to receive visiting Zimbabwean President Emmerson Mnangagwa, Kindiki found himself abruptly cast into the role of State House meteorologist.

As the Deputy President scrambled to improvise an explanation about the stalled downpours, Prime Cabinet Secretary Musalia Mudavadi and a row of Cabinet Secretaries broke into hearty laughter. The relaxed exchange momentarily pierced the ceremonial seriousness of an official bilateral visit, revealing the casual banter that passes between the executive team when the cameras are setting up and protocol officers are checking their watches.

### The Corridor Test

Ruto had just wrapped up the formal greetings down the receiving line when he pivoted to Kindiki. The light-hearted interrogation tapped straight into an awkward reality playing out outside the gates: despite aggressive public warnings from the meteorological desk that heavy rains would batter Kenya through the final quarter of the year, vast stretches of the country have seen largely dry skies and sluggish showers.

For Kindiki, being put on the spot was a classic display of Ruto’s informal management style—teasing his lieutenants on public matters that touch everyday urban conversation. Rather than stepping into a rehearsed policy brief, the Deputy President had to field spontaneous office teasing in front of his peers, trying to defend atmospheric conditions completely beyond his control.

### Shadows of 2023

The joke carried a deeper institutional resonance for anyone who remembered the turbulent weather politics of late 2023. Back then, conflicting statements between the executive and the Kenya Meteorological Department turned into a full-blown communications crisis.

During that episode, former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua famously summoned Kenya Meteorological Department Director David Gikungu to his Harambee House Annex office. That showdown culminated in a rare, nationally televised press conference where Gikungu publicly apologized for miscommunicating seasonal forecasts after initial projections of a severe El Niño were downplayed, only for catastrophic floods to follow.

By contrast, Friday’s scene showed a markedly different executive temperament. Instead of an angry summons over mixed messaging, Ruto turned the elusive weather into comic material, using the forecast disconnect to elicit laughs from Mudavadi and senior ministers waiting along the colonnade.

### The Missing Downpours

Ruto’s jab spoke to widespread public skepticism over the October–November–December 2026 short rains. Earlier in the year, the Kenya Meteorological Department sounded alarms over an impending deluge driven by El Niño conditions, warning residents across Nairobi, Kiambu, Murang’a, Machakos, Makueni, and Kajiado to brace for disruptive storms beginning in October.

Yet the weeks rolled on with only patchy, delayed rains, leaving ordinary Kenyans—and evidently the Commander-in-Chief—wondering where the promised deluge had vanished.

Despite the slow start, forecasters have stuck to their scientific guns. On October 16, meteorologist Jemimah Ongoma noted that El Niño conditions had firmly established themselves in the central and eastern tropical Pacific Ocean, insisting that the forecast for enhanced rainfall remained valid.

Scientists maintain that the phenomenon does not manifest as an unbroken, daily nationwide downpour, but rather produces uneven regional spikes that can arrive late and linger long after the initial forecast period. The department’s updated November bulletin continued to project that enhanced rains would spill over into early 2027.

For one brief morning on the State House veranda, however, the technical nuances of Pacific surface temperatures mattered far less than the punchline. Kindiki managed to navigate the teasing with a smile, the Cabinet enjoyed an unscripted laugh, and the team swiftly composed itself as Mnangagwa’s motorcade swept up the driveway.
