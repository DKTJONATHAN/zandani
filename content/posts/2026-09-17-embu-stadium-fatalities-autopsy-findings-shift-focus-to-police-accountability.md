Definitive medical evidence confirming that live ammunition caused two deaths during a political event in Embu has placed police crowd-control tactics and oversight mechanisms under acute scrutiny.

With forensic pathologists officially ruling the deaths as fatal gunshot trauma, the focus has abruptly shifted to the legal standard governing the use of lethal force during high-profile VIP extractions. The Independent Policing Oversight Authority (IPOA) is now facing mounting demands from human rights advocates and bereaved families to determine who authorized the firing of live rounds into crowds surrounding Nembure Stadium.

The official postmortem examinations, conducted at the Embu Level 5 Hospital mortuary by government pathologists alongside Independent Medico-Legal Unit (IMLU) observers, eliminated any lingering ambiguity surrounding how the two men died during the September 12 unrest.

According to lead pathologist Dr. Rosemary Kamau, both victims suffered high-velocity projectile wounds with through-and-through trajectories. One victim, identified as Moses, died of catastrophic head injuries inflicted by a single bullet.

“Moses had a single gunshot wound to the head, which had an entry and exit, causing brain damage with cranial fractures,” Dr. Kamau noted during the disclosure of the autopsy findings. “As a result, we concluded that the cause of death was a severe head injury secondary to a single penetrating gunshot wound to the head.”

The second victim, Mike Lewis, suffered a fatal gunshot wound to his lower right extremity. Medical examiners established that the bullet shattered his femur and ruptured major blood vessels, leading to rapid, uncontainable blood loss.

“Mike Lewis had a single gunshot wound to the right leg, which caused a compound fracture with massive surrounding hematoma,” Dr. Kamau said. “The cause of death was severe haemorrhage secondary to a compound right femur fracture, secondary to a single penetrating gunshot wound.”

Dr. Job Mwali concurred with the pathology findings. Because both rounds passed entirely through the bodies, pathologists recovered no bullet fragments from either victim, leaving ballistic matching reliant on scene recovery and official firearm auditing.

The fatal encounters unfolded as security personnel attempted to clear an emergency evacuation route for Deputy President Kithure Kindiki, who was attending a United Democratic Alliance (UDA) mobilization function for polling center officials. Clashes erupted after a section of residents was turned away at the gates, causing tempers to flare along the stadium perimeter. As the security detail escorted the Deputy President away from the rising commotion, police discharged both tear gas canisters and live ammunition.

The confirmation of gunshot fatalities sharply elevates the legal stakes for the commanding officers responsible for the operational perimeter that afternoon. Under national policing guidelines, the discharge of live ammunition against civilians is strictly limited to instances of direct, imminent threats to life, raising urgent questions about whether defensive clearance operations at political rallies cross statutory boundaries.

As formal police investigations continue, civil rights organizations and the families of the deceased have urged IPOA to fast-track its independent probe. The families are demanding full disclosure of the officers assigned to the security detail at Nembure Stadium and seeking explicit guarantees that those who fired the fatal rounds will face criminal proceedings.
