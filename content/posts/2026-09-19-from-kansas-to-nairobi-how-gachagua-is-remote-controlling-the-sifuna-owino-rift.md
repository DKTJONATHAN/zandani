Miles away from the political heat of Nairobi, former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua found himself playing distant peacemaker, picking up the phone from Kansas in the United States to quell an escalating digital and ground war between allies of Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna and Embakasi East MP Babu Owino.

The friction within the Linda Mwananchi movement centers on competing visions for the coalition's electoral strategy and its choice of political vehicle heading into the 2027 General Election. Supporters of both lawmakers had taken their disagreements online, trading barbs and public insults that threatened to fracture the nascent opposition alliance following a major rally in Nairobi.

“I know there is a small challenge back home with Linda Mwananchi, between supporters of Edwin Sifuna and Babu Owino, trading barbs and insulting each other. But I placed a call to Sifuna from here and had a chat with him. I did the same with Babu,” Gachagua disclosed during a diaspora media engagement.

Beyond the personality clashes between the two vocal legislators, Gachagua pointed a finger at the state, claiming that President William Ruto’s administration and the National Intelligence Service (NIS) have poured resources into destabilizing the movement. He alleged that state operatives were deployed to sow discord among key leaders specifically to neutralize the political momentum generated by their recent public gatherings.

To keep the coalition from unraveling while he remains abroad, Gachagua revealed he has tapped his party’s Secretary-General, Nyandarua Senator John Methu. Methu has been instructed to coordinate joint dialogue sessions between Sifuna and Owino to bridge the widening gap and establish a unified front before campaign realignments harden further.
