The University of Nairobi is banking on its Ksh220 billion property and land holdings to pull itself out of technical insolvency, even as lawmakers demand proof that paper assets can resolve immediate operational debts totaling Ksh20 billion.

Appearing before the National Assembly Public Investments Committee on Governance and Education, the university’s top executive team faced intense scrutiny over how one of East Africa’s largest real estate holders allowed statutory debts, pension defaults, and accumulated deficits to paralyze its balance sheet.

Committee chairman Dick Maungu, the Luanda MP, directed the university’s management to deliver a detailed operational turnaround plan by December 1, 2026, showing verifiable cash-flow schedules rather than broad proposals.

### The Cash Flow Disconnect

The parliamentary intervention follows an examination of audit findings tabled by Auditor-General Nancy Gathungu. The audit established that the university posted a standalone deficit of Ksh2.2 billion, widening its accumulated red ink to Ksh8.8 billion.

![The University of Natobi VC and DVC (left) before the National Assembly Public Investments Committee on Governance and Education on Thursday, October 22.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/uon-vc-dvc-the-national-assembly-public-investments-committee-on-governance-and-education-october.jpg.webp?itok=76tuPUWo)

More critical to daily operations is an unremitted debt load of Ksh10.6 billion tied directly to statutory payables, including payroll taxes and pension contributions withheld from employees but not forwarded to the relevant schemes.

Deputy Vice-Chancellor Josiah Aduda formally acknowledged to the committee that the audited figures placed the premier institution in a condition of technical insolvency. The administration defended its viability by highlighting that its Ksh220 billion asset register could generate liquid cash if monetized through long-term commercial leases, partnerships, and expanded property management.

Lawmakers, however, pressed Vice-Chancellor Ayub Gitau to explain why revenue-generating internal units have continued to post structural losses instead of buffering the core academic budget.

### Accountability Beyond the Land Bank

The committee questioned whether austerity measures and internal efficiency pledges could bridge the immediate fiscal shortfall without a radical restructuring of overhead costs.

"The committee would continue examining the Auditor-General's reports for the remaining financial years and would make recommendations after considering the issues raised," Maungu stated during the session, insisting that the December 1 deadline must outline precise timelines for liquidating arrears.

The scrutiny on Nairobi mirrors an institutional funding impasse across Kenya's public university tier, where collective liabilities have breached Ksh100 billion in pending supplier bills, payroll deductions, and tax obligations.

![A photo of the University of Nairobi Main Campus](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/uon_1_0.jpg.webp?itok=30vcymEw)

Within that deficit pool:

* Egerton University carries the largest individual exposure at Ksh25.5 billion;
* Kenyatta University owes creditors and statutory agencies approximately Ksh12.8 billion;
* Moi University maintains an outstanding burden of Ksh10.4 billion;
* The wider higher education sector faces aggregate liabilities exceeding Ksh205 billion when factoring in funding shortfalls at the Higher Education Loans Board (HELB).

### Policy Promises vs. Statutory Reality

President William Ruto’s administration has repeatedly stated an intention to clear historical university debt within two to three years through revamped student financing formulas, potential tax penalty waivers, and incentives for commercial research. Yet the institutional mechanics on campus remain constrained by heavy payroll costs and constrained exchequer releases.

For the University of Nairobi, commercialising pieces of its prime real estate across the capital represents the only self-directed revenue lever large enough to counter a Ksh20 billion drag without relying solely on state bailouts.

The National Assembly committee will resume its review following the December 1 submission, when UoN management must present concrete financial schedules showing how its property revenues will begin servicing the Ksh10.6 billion tax and pension backlog.
