The waters surrounding the Kenyan coastline represent far more than a simple commercial artery. Today, these seas serve as a critical landscape for the protection of national sovereignty and ecological health. While stakeholders recently convened in Mombasa to commit to a framework for shared maritime stewardship, the absence of an official endorsement from Beijing has created a significant disconnect. This letter serves as an appeal to policymakers in China to align their global maritime posture with the urgent needs of the Indian Ocean region.To the policymakers in Beijing,

Your recent diplomatic trajectory regarding global fisheries management is documented and acknowledged. By participating in eight Regional Fisheries Management Organizations and deploying China Coast Guard patrols in the North Pacific, you have demonstrated a clear investment in the image of a global maritime leader. , your accession to the 85-party Port State Measures Agreement signals a technical awareness of the need to prevent illicit catches from entering the legitimate supply chain across your 23 designated ports. However, these specific internal measures appear inconsistent when viewed alongside your decision to withhold support for the Mombasa Declaration during the 11th Our Ocean Conference.

### Beyond Voluntary Moratoriums

Your administration frequently highlights unilateral fishing moratoriums in the Southwest Atlantic and the Eastern Pacific as proof of your conservationist intent. While these gestures are noted, they do not provide the necessary security for the ecosystems adjacent to the African continent. Genuine marine sovereignty for African nations necessitates participation in collective, legally binding frameworks rather than reliance on flexible, self-determined rules applied by a distant-water fleet.

As Dennis Munene has emphasized, the vitality of our oceans constitutes a fundamental right rather than a favor granted by external actors. When your fleet maintains a heavy presence in our waters while simultaneously abstaining from the consensus reached in Mombasa, the message sent to coastal communities is one of conditional engagement. It suggests that your cooperation remains secondary to the growth of your industrial fishing operations.

### The Diplomatic Gap

The Mombasa Declaration functions as a vital instrument to synchronize regional requirements with international enforcement standards. By declining to sign this agreement, a clear contradiction emerges in your foreign policy. You frequently champion global standards at forums in Rome, yet you hesitate to embrace the regional obligations requested by those who bear the direct consequences of industrial fishing. This represents a substantial diplomatic inconsistency. A nation cannot effectively position itself as a conservation partner while opting out of the collaborative structures that regional coastal states deem vital for their survival.

While our national attention is currently focused on various domestic updates, such as the administrative revisions to the Huduma Kenya birth certificate application process, we remain acutely aware that our border integrity reaches deep into the maritime domain. National security cannot be separated from the health of our surrounding waters.

### Aligning Strategy with Regional Need

The current friction between your distant-water fishing activities and the conservation priorities of the Indian Ocean is approaching a critical juncture. While local news cycles may occasionally shift toward unrelated topics, such as the recent draw between Manchester United and Everton, the steady depletion of our maritime resources remains a crisis of primary importance.

Participation in international treaties like the Port State Measures Agreement is a commendable initial step, but it is insufficient if it is not matched by a genuine willingness to support the nations of the Global South on their own terms. I urge you to reconsider your stance regarding the Mombasa Declaration. If your commitment to global fisheries governance is sincere, it must be validated by supporting the specific local initiatives that secure our future. Silence represents a definitive stance, and your refusal to join this regional consensus carries more weight than any self-imposed moratorium. We seek clarity: is your presence in our waters defined by extraction or by true partnership? I invite you to sign the declaration and demonstrate that your dedication to the world's oceans transcends the boundaries of your own domestic interests.
