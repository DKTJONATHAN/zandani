Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale has laid bare the clinical timeline behind Kenya’s first confirmed Ebola fatality, confirming that specialized laboratory analyses by state virologists verified the presence of the rare Bundibugyo strain shortly before the patient succumbed at The Nairobi Hospital.

Addressing the media, Duale disclosed that the deceased, a Kenyan national who had spent the last seven years residing in the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC), passed away on Monday night at approximately 11:30 PM while undergoing aggressive supportive medical intervention in an emergency isolation facility.

### The Cross-Border Journey

According to official health ministry records presented by the Cabinet Secretary, the patient had been unwell for nearly a month while still in the DRC, seeking medical attention at several regional clinics before attempting the journey back home. 

On October 3, 2026, the individual traveled overland across the DRC border into Kampala, Uganda. From Entebbe, he boarded Jambojet flight 8523 to Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (JKIA) in Nairobi. Duale confirmed that the traveler cleared routine public health screening stations upon landing before being met by a relative in a private vehicle and taken directly to The Nairobi Hospital for urgent medical care.

### Emergency Isolation and Laboratory Detection

Clinical teams at The Nairobi Hospital immediately recognized the severity of the case upon arrival. Triage personnel moved the patient into a specialized emergency room isolation ward after noting high-risk viral hemorrhagic symptoms.

Medical reports indicated the patient was experiencing extreme fever, chills, profound muscle fatigue, sore throat, acute pain when swallowing, and sub-dermal bleeding around injection sites. 

Given the extensive travel history through endemic territory in the DRC and the specific clinical profile, doctors immediately drew samples for advanced molecular testing. Two separate institutions—the National Virology Reference Laboratory under the National Public Health Institute and the Kenya Medical Research Institute (KEMRI)—processed the specimens, both returning positive results for the Ebola Bundibugyo virus disease (BDBV).

### Containment and Active Surveillance

Duale reiterated that while the death marks Kenya’s first documented case of this specific viral hemorrhagic strain, emergency public health protocols were triggered immediately to halt potential local transmission.

Surveillance teams have established direct monitoring for 28 primary contacts identified during the patient's immediate hospital transfer and family interaction. Concurrently, contact-tracing units are working through passenger manifests to locate 23 travelers and four crew members aboard the regional flight from Kampala.

The Ministry of Health has instructed county surveillance networks to maintain heightened vigilance for patients displaying severe unexplained fevers combined with bleeding symptoms as active monitoring continues.
