A sudden Ksh8 billion upward adjustment by the National Treasury has broken a four-week paralysis in Kenya’s public university sector, forcing the state to abandon its hardline funding cap to secure an agreement with striking academic staff.

After insisting for weeks that state coffers could not stretch beyond Ksh12 billion, government negotiators capitulated on Thursday, presenting a Ksh20 billion financial package that received verbal acceptance from union leadership representing workers across 35 public universities.

The breakthrough came after a series of collapsed bargaining sessions. As recently as Monday, October 26, an inter-ministerial committee put forward a revised counter-offer of Ksh11.96 billion—a figure slightly below the previous Ksh12 billion mark that unions rejected out of hand. Union negotiators pointed out that the Ksh12 billion proposal amounted to a meager 3 per cent basic salary increase, far short of the framework envisioned in the 2025-2029 Collective Bargaining Agreements (CBAs).

![A meeting involving key Cabinet officials from the Education, Public Service, and the National Treasury on Wednesday, October 28](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/oganba-treasury-meeting-october-28.jpg.webp?itok=y7dHqmt6)

By Thursday, the Treasury’s financial posture shifted dramatically, raising the offer to Ksh20 billion and clearing the primary hurdle to ending the walkout that began on October 2.

"Today we have agreed on a figure we have negotiated with the government, not a figure imposed on the unions," said Constantine Wesonga, Secretary-General of the University Academic Staff Union (UASU), shortly after emerging from the closed-door sessions.

The deal was hashed out in high-stakes talks involving Education Cabinet Secretary Julius Ogamba, National Treasury Cabinet Secretary John Mbadi, Internal Security Principal Secretary Raymond Omollo, university vice-chancellors, and representatives from UASU, the Kenya Universities Staff Union (KUSU), and KUDHEIHA.

![The University of Nairobi students protesting along Waiyaki Way on Wednesday, October 21, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/uon-students-protest.jpg.webp?itok=xW5iZ1Jo)

The state's fiscal pivot followed escalating pressure on multiple fronts. Beyond the complete disruption of lecture schedules for hundreds of thousands of students across 35 institutions, anger over the standoff erupted onto the streets. University of Nairobi students staged repeated demonstrations during the week, blocking Waiyaki Way twice and disrupting traffic along the capital's central transport corridor.

Attention now shifts to the technical implementation of the package. On Friday, October 30, technical committees from the unions and the ministry will convene to translate the Ksh20 billion global figure into specific payroll allocations for individual staff cadres across public campuses. Once the breakdown is validated, union officials and government delegates are scheduled to sign the binding agreement later the same day.

With formal execution targeted for Friday, union leaders have instructed students and academic staff to prepare for an immediate return to campus operations. Classes across all public universities are set to officially resume on Monday, November 2, ending a month-long academic freeze.
