A 25-year claim over a 2.7-acre school playground in Kikuyu has collapsed after the Environment and Land Court at Thika ruled that defunct local authorities possessed no statutory authority to extinguish public utility reservations and transfer them into private hands.

In a judgment delivered by the court and detailed in filings reviewed on Monday, October 26, the court nullified the title deed held by a church women’s guild for the parcel designated as L.R. No. KARAI/KARAI/350. The bench held that the defunct County Council of Kiambu acted outside the law when it excised the property in 2001, effectively restoring an educational reservation originally created nearly seven decades ago.

The litigation was mounted jointly by the Law Society of Kenya (LSK) and the Boards of Management of Kanyiha, Lusigetti, and Njumbi Primary Schools, with the Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission (EACC) joining proceedings as an interested party to present archival land records.

### Paper trail voided

![The 2.7 Acre land in question recovered by EACC in Kikuyu, Kiambu County.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/2.7-acre-kikuyu-land-eacc.jpg.webp?itok=nZfhBnE-)

The dispute centered on whether a local authority could lawfully reallocate land set aside under colonial-era planning frameworks for common educational infrastructure. Court records confirmed that the parcel had been formally earmarked as a joint school playing field by the County Council of Kiambu in 1957.

When the parcel was transferred to the church guild in 2001, the transaction omitted the statutory administrative steps required for alienating public property. Evidentiary submissions brought by the anti-graft agency demonstrated that the allocation lacked a letter of offer, contained no record of formal written acceptance, bore no proof of payment of rates or rent, and showed no financial consideration paid to the local authority.

Finding those omissions fatal to the title, the court determined that the council held the land strictly in custodial trust for the community and could not legally convey it to a third party.

“Consequently, this Court finds that L.R. No. KARAI/KARAI/350 was public utility land set aside and reserved as a school playing field, held in trust by the defunct County Council of Kiambu for the public benefit and the students of the neighboring schools,” the court held.

### Enforcement through mandamus

Rather than stopping at title cancellation, the judgment deploys an Order of Mandamus to prevent bureaucratic delay in securing the site. The court ordered both the Chief Land Registrar and the National Land Commission (NLC) to formally register the 2.7 acres as public school land.

Under the court’s decree, the property must be registered as held in trust specifically for Kanyiha, Lusigetti, and Njumbi Primary Schools, under the joint oversight of their respective Boards of Management and the Ministry of Education.

To prevent further physical encroachment on the grounds—which serve a combined student body of more than 1,300 pupils across the three institutions—the court issued a permanent injunction barring the previous titleholders, their officers, or agents from fencing, building upon, or transferring any portion of the field.

The ruling places an immediate administrative burden on the Land Registrar and the NLC to expunge the 2001 registry folios and produce a clean public vesting deed reflecting the three schools' custodial rights.
