A minor clerical error in a government Gazette notice has forced former nominated Senator Millicent Omanga into a swift public clarification, highlighting her delicate task of brand-policing as she pivots away from the ruling coalition.

The confusion stems from an August 26, 2026, gazette publication authorized by Interior Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen. The notice listed the new Board of Visitors for the Shikusa Borstal Institution under Section 20 (1) of the Borstal Institutions Act, appointing a "Millicent Kerubo Omanga" to a three-year term starting August 14, 2026.

As speculation mounted online that the prominent politician had quietly accepted a low-profile correctional facility board seat, Omanga moved quickly to distance herself from the appointment.

"My full name is Millicent Nyaboke Omanga... and to the gossipers: sorry," she posted on social media on Saturday, September 19, pointing out the discrepancy between her actual middle name and the "Kerubo" listed in the state document.

![Nominated Senator Millicent Omanga taking the oath of office at Parliament buildings on August 31, 2017.](https://i.ibb.co/kVDPBQjm/4414f245de6e.webp)

The speed and directness of her response signal more than just a correction of official paperwork. For Omanga, who defected from the United Democratic Alliance (UDA) to the Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP) earlier this year, maintaining a clear separation from the Kenya Kwanza administration's mid-level appointments is critical to her political survival.

Having declared her intention to run for the Nairobi County Woman Representative seat in the 2027 General Election, any perceived acceptance of a minor parastatal role could complicate her strategy to run as a fresh alternative to the status quo.

Omanga's relationship with state appointments has been historically volatile, marked by short-lived roles and public rejections. In March 2023, she was appointed Chief Administrative Secretary (CAS) in the Ministry of Interior, a position she held until July of that year when the High Court declared the entire cadre of appointments unconstitutional.

![Omanga Ruto](https://i.ibb.co/Bmmg1Kv/4b80e74632b6.webp)

By October 2024, President William Ruto named her to the newly established Nairobi Rivers Commission. Omanga publicly declined the offer, stating she would instead support the city’s environmental restoration in a private capacity.

A brief tenure on the Board of Directors of Kenya Shipyards Limited followed in January 2025, but the government revoked that appointment in April 2026, just months before her political defection to the DCP.

Her career in public administration began much earlier, when she served as a director at the Kenya Electricity Generating Company (KenGen) from 2013 to 2017. Following a failed bid for the Nairobi Woman Representative seat in 2017 under the Jubilee Party, she was nominated to the Senate, serving until 2022.

By drawing a sharp line between "Kerubo" and "Nyaboke," Omanga has effectively shut down talk of a quiet return to the government fold, ensuring her focus remains entirely on her 2027 campaign under her new party banner.
