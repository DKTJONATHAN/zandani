A breakdown in wholesale price compliance across Kenya’s fuel distribution chain has escalated into an operational revolt, with independent petroleum stations directed to immediately halt all product loading from suppliers demanding rates above state-mandated limits.

The purchasing boycott, ordered in a joint directive issued Tuesday, October 27, by the Petroleum Outlets Association of Kenya (POAK) and the Kenya Independent Petroleum Dealers Association (KIPEDA), targets major Oil Marketing Companies (OMCs) accused of illegally bypassing statutory price ceilings set by the Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority (EPRA).

According to the dealer lobbies, primary importers and large marketing firms have systematically inflated bulk depot prices since October 15, when EPRA introduced its current monthly tariff cycle. The practice has effectively wiped out the trading margin between bulk collection points and station forecourts, leaving smaller retailers facing severe cash burn on every litre dispensed.

### The Anatomy of the Margin Squeeze

Under the pricing schedule gazetted by EPRA for the October 15 to November 14 window, wholesale rates in Nairobi were strictly capped at Ksh211.80 per litre for Super Petrol, Ksh212.60 for Automotive Diesel, and Ksh204.79 for Kerosene.

![Eng. Edward Kinyua, Petroleum and Gas Director at EPRA speaking at a media roundtable](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/eng-edward-kinyua-petroleum-and-gas-director-at-epra-speaking-at-a-media-roundtable-696x456.jpg.webp?itok=spQ0Vi5s)

Maximum allowable retail pump charges were fixed at Ksh226.96, Ksh227.76, and Ksh219.87 respectively—creating a gross operating spread of roughly Ksh15.16 per litre to cover retail transport, depot handling, station overheads, and merchant profit.

Instead, the associations report that bulk suppliers have been billing dealers well beyond the Ksh211.80 and Ksh212.60 wholesale thresholds. Because independent operators cannot legally adjust their pump prices above EPRA’s retail ceiling without risking regulatory penalties, absorbing the overcharge leaves them carrying direct operating losses.

“Independent stations nationwide are facing critical product stock-outs,” the associations stated in their joint notice, warning that operators are caught between sustaining ruinous operating deficits and shutting down their pumps entirely.

### Regulatory Vacuum After Subsidy Removal

The pricing friction comes in the wake of the government’s formal withdrawal of the fuel price stabilisation mechanism. The rollback of that cushion instantly drove up pump costs during the mid-October review, adding Ksh12.93 per litre to petrol, Ksh9.90 to diesel, and a steep Ksh28.49 to kerosene.

While EPRA Director General Edward Mwirigi Kinyua defended the move as necessary to conserve stabilization balances for future price shocks, the sudden escalation in landing costs appears to have strained discipline at the wholesale distribution level. With OMCs seeking to insulate their cash flows against volatility, price increases have been passed aggressively down the supply chain into the dealer network.

Despite formal petitions submitted to EPRA and the State Department for Petroleum over the past fortnight, the lobby groups maintain that regulators have failed to sanction offending marketers or enforce the published depot benchmarks.

### Looming Stock-Outs in Rural and Suburban Hubs

The stand-off carries immediate supply consequences, particularly beyond Nairobi and major logistical arteries. Independent fuel dealers control a significant portion of retail outlets in rural, peri-urban, and agricultural hubs, where multinational marketers maintain minimal retail infrastructure.

With dealer associations instructing members countrywide to boycott loading depots charging non-compliant rates, localised stock-outs of petrol and diesel are set to emerge as forecourt holding tanks run dry. 

Whether the dispute triggers broader supply paralysis now depends on how swiftly EPRA and the Ministry of Energy move to inspect loading depot billing logs and enforce adherence to the gazetted wholesale limits before existing station inventories are fully exhausted.
