Kenyans have until Tuesday evening to formally shape the legal framework that will lock petroleum Value Added Tax at 8 per cent, after the National Assembly initiated an accelerated five-day public consultation window that straddles a weekend.

A notice issued on Friday, October 9, by National Assembly Clerk David Njoroge gave motorists, commercial hauliers, and manufacturers just over 100 hours to submit written memoranda on the Tax Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026. The legislation serves as the formal statutory vehicle to anchor fuel tax relief that was rolled out administratively earlier this year.

Sponsored by National Assembly Majority Leader Kimani Ichung’wah, the proposed law amends two key fiscal statutes: the Value Added Tax Act and the Excise Duty Act. Alongside the fuel tax clause, the draft legislation overhauls the taxation of building materials by changing how excise duty on tiles is determined, shifting the calculation basis from total weight to surface area.

### Codifying Emergency Interventions

![A man fueling a car at a petrol station](https://i.ibb.co/JWS36bv3/2f64ee1fedb4.webp)

The legislative push marks a crucial transition from reactive executive market buffers to permanent parliamentary statute. In April 2026, escalating military conflict involving Iran destabilised international crude shipping routes and caused an immediate spike in landed import costs.

During the April pricing cycle, domestic petrol prices surged by 16 per cent while diesel climbed by 24 per cent. To avert a broader cost-of-living shock, the government trimmed fuel VAT from the standard 16 per cent down to 13 per cent, before slicing it further to 8 per cent on April 16.

Data from the Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority (EPRA) showed that lowering the tax rate to 8 per cent extracted Ksh9.37 per litre from the retail price of super petrol and Ksh10.21 per litre from diesel. To backstop the market through the peak of the shock, President William Ruto subsequently committed Ksh28 billion in combined tax relief and state price stabilisation across the April–May and May–June pricing periods.

While consumers felt the immediate benefit at retail pumps, the adjustment existed on an executive and regulatory footing. The Tax Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026, is designed to give that 8 per cent rate a permanent statutory anchor within the VAT Act.

### The Tuesday Cutoff

Despite the nationwide implications of energy costs on food distribution, public transport fares, and manufacturing overheads, the consultation clock is running on a compressed timetable.

Stakeholders wishing to endorse, challenge, or amend the provisions must send their formal memoranda via email to `cna@parliament.go.ke` or hand-deliver physical copies to the Office of the Clerk at the Main Parliament Building in Nairobi before 5:00 pm on Tuesday, October 13, 2026.

Once the deadline passes, the relevant departmental committee will review the submissions and compile a public participation report for consideration during the second reading on the floor of the House. The proposed adjustments remain proposals and cannot take effect until lawmakers pass the bill and it receives presidential assent.

Even if the statutory rate is cemented at 8 per cent, retail pump charges will remain tied to EPRA’s monthly pricing formulas, where landed product costs, pipeline transport tariffs, and foreign exchange fluctuations dictate the final figure paid at the nozzle.
