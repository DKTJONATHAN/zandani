The political friction between President William Ruto and his predecessor Uhuru Kenyatta surfaced on opposing regional stages on Friday, September 18, as Kenyatta used a public ceremony in Murang'a County to mock state allegations that he is covertly funding opposition activities.

Speaking during a burial service in Gikuu, Kiharu Sub-County, the retired president told attendees he had deliberately chosen to limit unscripted remarks to prevent political misinterpretation, explicitly referencing the "sponsor" tag frequently leveled against him by administration allies.

"And usually I speak off-cuff, and these days life is becoming too dangerous for me," Kenyatta said. "So I have decided before the media goes to misinterpret my words. Thanks so much, that is all from the sponsor."

### Rhetorical Escalation in Sindo

![Uhuru Ruto](https://i.ibb.co/1jn8Mk9/83c793d1b3e8.webp)

Kenyatta's sarcastic deflection came less than 24 hours after President Ruto publicly renewed attacks on his former boss during a development tour in Sindo, Homa Bay County, on Thursday, September 17. Addressing a public gathering, Ruto claimed Kenyatta was bankrolling prominent opposition figures, specifically naming Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna, to destabilize government initiatives.

During the Homa Bay visit, Ruto characterized opposition leaders as puppets acting on instructions from a bitter political backer who had failed during his own tenure.

"They are clueless because they have only been sent by their sponsor, who is bitter seeing us succeed where he failed," Ruto stated in Sindo. "We have fixed the economy he destroyed."

### The Broken Political Pact

![Former President Uhuru Kenyatta, during a past engagement.](https://i.ibb.co/6J4M7CpL/e0e028b3cce1.webp)

Beyond allegations of financial backing, Ruto revisited the personal and political alliance that carried the Jubilee coalition through multiple elections, explicitly reminding voters in Nyanza of an unfulfilled power-sharing arrangement.

According to the President, the original arrangement had stipulated a decade-long presidency for Kenyatta followed by a similar 10-year term for himself—a covenant Ruto claims was abandoned during the 2022 general election cycle.

"I stood with you, helped you, and worked for you. When time came for you to help me, you did not go as we had agreed," Ruto said, referring to Kenyatta's decision to back alternative candidates. "We had agreed 10 for you and 10 for me, but you did not keep the promise."

While Ruto conceded that Kenyatta maintained the democratic right to choose his preferred political path, the exchange underlines how deep-seated grievances from the 2022 transition continue to dictate public engagements for both leaders across the country.
