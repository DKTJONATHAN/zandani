Former Interior Cabinet Secretary Dr. Fred Matiang’i is turning his sights back on his former colleagues at Harambee House, challenging President William Ruto to move past political sparring and use executive authority to open a full inquiry into the River Yala bodies.

Speaking during a radio interview in Mombasa, the Jubilee Party leader took direct aim at recent remarks from President Ruto. The Head of State had criticized the former CS for lecturing the current administration on accountability, pointing to the disturbing discovery of bodies dumped in River Yala during Matiang’i’s tenure.

Rather than dodging the historical security record of the Uhuru Kenyatta administration, Matiang’i shifted the focus directly to the composition of the National Security Council (NSC). He highlighted the fact that Ruto served as Deputy President throughout those years, sitting alongside him and receiving the exact same high-level intelligence briefings.

“We served alongside him on the National Security Council for all those years, and now he is the President,” Matiang’i noted during the broadcast. “How can he ask questions about the people who were killed and dumped in the Yala River instead of taking action himself?”

The NSC brings together the country's topmost leadership—including the President, Deputy President, Cabinet Secretaries for Defence, Interior, Finance, and Foreign Affairs, alongside the Attorney General and security agency heads. Matiang’i argued that because the body handles routine, sensitive security updates, claiming ignorance of the operational climate during that era holds no administrative weight.

With his declared intention to challenge Ruto for the presidency in 2027, the former Interior boss maintained that the current Commander-in-Chief holds the sole constitutional levers required to institute formal inquiries and act on unresolved historical security questions. 

The exchange underscores how the security apparatus and unresolved human rights cases from past administrations are rapidly solidifying as central flashpoints in the run-up to the 2027 general election.
