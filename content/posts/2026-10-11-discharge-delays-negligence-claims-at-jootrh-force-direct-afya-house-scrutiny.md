A wave of public distress over systemic clinical bottlenecks and unaddressed patient deaths at Jaramogi Oginga Odinga Teaching and Referral Hospital (JOOTRH) has prompted the national government to intervene directly in the administration of Western Kenya’s primary referral facility.

Medical Services Principal Secretary Ouma Oluga arrived in Kisumu on Saturday, October 10, convening an extraordinary meeting with hospital medical and administrative staff. The intervention followed days of mounting public outrage on social media platforms, where former patients and bereaved families shared detailed accounts of fatal negligence, administrative indifference, and punitive waiting times.

Addressing the facility’s workforce, Oluga made it clear that the national executive had taken note of the community's outcry, signaling that Afya House would no longer treat frontline operational failures as localized county disputes.

"It's both in the value of the President's agenda that this hospital functions properly," Oluga said. "We are also not blind to some complaints that are there on social media. Just so the staff knows that we are still at the helm of leadership and we are watching the responses in the community."

### The Anatomy of Clinical Delays

![Undated image of the Jaramogi Oginga Odinga Teaching and Referral Hospital (JOOTRH) in Kisumu](https://i.ibb.co/7xBHTyC4/c135fd570651.webp)

The ministry's direct escalation was accelerated by specific public grievances detailing severe administrative inertia inside the wards. Among the core complaints was the hospital’s protracted discharge system. One patient’s account documented how clearing the ward upon recovery routinely consumed an entire business day under optimal conditions, with the procedure often extending much longer during routine ward operations.

Other accounts alleged catastrophic clinical lapses, with relatives recounting how patients deteriorated and died while navigating unresponsive hospital bureaucracy. The volume and specificity of the public testimonies created an accountability crisis that the hospital’s executive leadership could neither contain internally nor deflect.

Prior to Oluga’s arrival, JOOTRH management had initiated its own internal inquiry into the allegations, confirming that administrators had opened direct contact with an individual complainant who brought forward specific allegations regarding service breakdowns.

### Mandatory Retraining and Cycle of Care

Rather than treating the issue as isolated misconduct, the ministry’s response centers on restructuring the hospital's operational culture. Oluga announced that staff across the facility will be subjected to mandatory transformational retraining designed to repair breakdown points across the hospital’s entire patient journey.

"For the staff, we are going to do a lot. We are going to do transformational training and look at how we work together in the cycle care," Oluga stated, pushing the facility's clinical leads to scrutinize how standard procedures translate into actual patient experiences.

![PS Oluga during the discussion of the Kenya-US health partnership on November 3, 2025.](https://i.ibb.co/8nmTLwCW/5f8526347635.webp)

The Principal Secretary challenged the hospital to conduct an unsparing audit of its daily clinical decisions, arguing that institutional excellence cannot be claimed if routine interactions produce trauma for vulnerable families.

"We have to look deeply and reflect on ourselves to see the accuracy of our actions and the experiences those actions produce for the people and the community," he noted.

### Operational Pressures at Western Kenya's Hub

As the largest teaching and referral institution in the region, JOOTRH handles complex emergency and specialist caseloads drawn from Kisumu and neighboring lake basin counties. That extensive regional reliance magnifies any internal systemic delay, turning discharge queues and ward triage sluggishness into immediate public health hazards.

While Oluga extended assurances that the Ministry of Health would actively protect patient rights and guarantee dignified service delivery, the immediate test will center on how swiftly JOOTRH finalizes its internal probe into the specific negligence claims.

For the patients who rely on the Kisumu referral facility, the measure of Saturday’s high-level ministry visit will not rest on administrative directives, but on whether the promised cycle-of-care reforms eliminate the dangerous waiting times that brought Afya House to its doorstep.
