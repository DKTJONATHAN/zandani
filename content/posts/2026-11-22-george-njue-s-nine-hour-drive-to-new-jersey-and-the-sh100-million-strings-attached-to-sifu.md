Driving nine hours straight across American highways to reach a New Jersey hotel room is not the usual way voters interact with Kenyan politics, but for George Njue, the distance was secondary to securing a direct commitment from Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna.

Speaking before a gathering of supporters in the US, the Embu native made it clear that the diaspora community was prepared to back Sifuna’s 2027 presidential ambitions through the Linda Mwananchi Movement with a Sh100 million war chest. Yet, rather than offering a blank check or relying on political euphoria, Njue’s pitch came attached with distinct operational demands.

“Engage us, come with a structure on how we can channel our money to Linda Mwananchi. We can give you Sh100 million,” Njue told the room, pressing the senator to establish a transparent and accountable mechanism for receiving and managing overseas funds before any formal mobilization begins.

The gathering, which drew enthusiastic backing from attendees asked whether they were willing to fund the campaign, also shifted quickly from conventional political financing into concrete economic proposals. Njue outlined that the diaspora’s financial involvement should not end at the ballot box or campaign rallies, suggesting instead a structured Public-Private Partnership (PPP) framework should Sifuna ascend to State House.

According to the North Carolina-based supporter, overseas investors are eyeing collaborative ventures designed to deliver tangible infrastructure at home, specifically targeting public works such as dams and regional irrigation projects to lift local livelihoods.

For now, the multimillion-shilling promise rests entirely on whether the Linda Mwananchi Movement can build the administrative infrastructure required to satisfy donors who expect clear channels and policy partnerships in return for their backing.
