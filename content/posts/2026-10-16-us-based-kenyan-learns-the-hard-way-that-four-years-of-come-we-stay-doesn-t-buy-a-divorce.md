For a Kenyan man living stateside, trying to officially divorce a partner he never actually married turned into an expensive legal miscalculation at the Nakuru magistrate’s court.

Identified in court documents only by his initials BMT, the petitioner thought a formal courtroom decree was the proper way to cleanly sever ties with his former partner, MWM, after their four-year domestic arrangement hit the rocks. Having relocated to the United States in search of greener pastures while maintaining his base in Nakuru, BMT marched to court seeking a legal exit on grounds of cruelty.

His core grievance? He claimed he was left holding the financial bag, shouldering the entire burden of household expenses while MWM allegedly refused to contribute a single shilling. To add salt to injury, he told the court that after they separated in 2024, his former partner had already moved on to a new romance. He wanted the bench to legally presume a marriage existed so he could dissolve it.

Senior Principal Magistrate Aloyce Ndege was unimpressed by the paperwork and the narrative. On Tuesday, October 13, 2026, Magistrate Ndege threw out the petition entirely, pointing out a fundamental flaw in the man’s logic: you cannot dissolve a union that never legally existed in the first place.

“I find a period of four years not that long, and given the evidence adduced by the petitioner herein, I find no quality in the short period that the parties herein cohabited to make me presume a marriage,” Magistrate Ndege ruled.

The court noted a glaring lack of basic documentation, starting with the absolute absence of a marriage certificate. , MWM chose not to enter an appearance or file any defense after being served, leaving the matter to proceed purely on BMT’s oral testimony.

Under Kenyan law, recognized unions fall strictly into specific categories—Christian, civil, customary, Hindu, or Islamic. While the legal framework does allow courts to presume a marriage in select circumstances based on long-term cohabitation, public presentation as husband and wife, and mutual intent, a simple 'come-we-stay' setup does not automatically cross the threshold.

Rather than granting him the clean legal break he flew back or instructed lawyers to chase, the magistrate left the petitioner legally single in the eyes of the law, while stinging him with the costs of the aborted lawsuit.
