Political analyst and media commentator Mutahi Ngunyi took to social media on Sunday, October 4, 2026, to offer an unvarnished reading of former President Uhuru Kenyatta’s recent high-profile engagement in Kirinyaga County, framing it as the opening gambit in a high-stakes campaign to reclaim political dominance in Mt Kenya.

Writing on X, Ngunyi asserted that Kenyatta’s visible public reappearance in the central region was far from coincidental, describing it as a calculated and deliberate move to dismantle the regional supremacy currently held by Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua.

According to Ngunyi, the optics and messaging that unfolded in Kirinyaga point directly to a long-game strategy designed not just to alter the political calculus ahead of the 2027 General Election, but to position Kenyatta as the central kingmaker shaping regional alliances extending into 2032.

### Reading the Kirinyaga Chessboard

Ngunyi’s commentary singled out Kirinyaga County as the geographic flashpoint where Kenyatta chose to demonstrate his enduring regional appeal. In Ngunyi’s assessment, the former head of state’s public reception provided verifiable proof of an active effort to rebuild grassroots leverage across the vote-rich Mt Kenya bloc.

"According to Ngunyi, the developments in Kirinyaga point to a broader strategy by Uhuru to challenge Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua’s influence over the Mt Kenya region," the commentary outlined, emphasizing that the former president is directly testing the waters of regional loyalty.

The timing of Ngunyi’s analysis comes as Deputy President Gachagua continues his own sustained push to consolidate undisputed control over Mt Kenya’s political machinery, establishing himself as the primary negotiator for the region ahead of future electoral cycles.

### The Battle for Regional Supremacy

Ngunyi’s public intervention brings the quiet power struggle between Kenyatta and Gachagua back into sharp national focus. Since leaving office, Kenyatta’s public political interventions have been selective, making any direct engagement in regional matters a major talking point across the country's political commentary landscape.

By spotlighting the Kirinyaga outing, Ngunyi argued that any sustained resurgence by Kenyatta would fundamentally disrupt the prevailing balance of power. A revitalized Kenyatta faction within the mountain could split regional allegiances, creating immediate friction for Kenya Kwanza's mobilization plans and opening up room for shifting national coalitions beyond 2027.

As political figures across both divides digest the fallout from the Kirinyaga gathering, Ngunyi’s Sunday intervention underscores that the contest for Mt Kenya’s political soul remains openly contested, with the battle lines between the former president and his successor drawing fresh scrutiny with every public appearance.
