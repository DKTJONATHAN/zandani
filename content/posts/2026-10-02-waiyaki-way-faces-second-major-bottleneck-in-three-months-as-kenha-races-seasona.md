Inbound commuters entering Nairobi from the western suburbs face another severe contraction on their primary transit corridor, as road authorities launch a month-long partial shutdown on Waiyaki Way less than ten weeks after clearing a similar bottleneck on the same road.

The Kenya National Highways Authority (KeNHA) confirmed that the Mombasa-bound carriageway between Villa Rosa Kempinski and the Museum Hill Interchange will operate with reduced capacity from Thursday, October 8, through Saturday, November 7, 2026. The 30-day intervention on the A8 trunk route is designated for structural highway maintenance.

During the closure, motorists headed into the central business district will be restricted to the remaining operational lanes on the affected stretch, with KeNHA urging drivers to divert through secondary connector routes where possible.

### The Rush Against the Calendar

![An image of the traffic snarl-up along Waiyaki Way on September 9, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/dwMv6h0F/34612a3bb01d.webp)

The timing of the maintenance places heavy civil works directly against Nairobi's seasonal calendar. KeNHA and City Hall, under Governor Johnson Sakaja, have stepped up multi-agency infrastructure and drainage overhauls across the capital to cushion road networks before the onset of heavy rains expected in late October.

By executing resurfacing and drainage corrections on the inbound lanes now, the highway agency is attempting to arrest roadbed degradation before storm runoffs hit. However, compressing four weeks of maintenance into this specific window concentrates logistical pain along Westlands’ busiest commercial strip.

The intervention is not isolated. Along Mombasa Road at the Cabanas Eastern Bypass off-ramp, KeNHA has already sealed off sections of the highway until October 22 to build a reinforced box drain, leaving both ends of the city's central transit spine throttled by simultaneous civil works.

### Compounding Friction on the A8

![museum hill expressway](https://i.ibb.co/9mGHrkqY/169ed47c6ce7.webp)

For motorists using Waiyaki Way, the upcoming restriction compounds months of interrupted flow. Between July and August, KeNHA closed inbound lanes along the adjacent stretch from the Global Trade Centre (GTC) towers to Villa Rosa Kempinski for similar maintenance. 

Even where main lanes have remained open, surrounding transit capacity along the corridor has operated under friction. Ongoing service lane widenings, overpass structural refurbishments, and continuous pedestrian walkway builds have kept sections of the road perimeter barricaded or narrowed.

KeNHA stated that dedicated traffic marshals alongside national police officers will be stationed between the Kempinski entrance and the Museum Hill ramps to sequence merging streams and enforce lane discipline.

Whether those measures can prevent daily spillover into Westlands roundabout and Chiromo Road will become clear on October 8, when morning peak flows meet the barricades at Kempinski.
