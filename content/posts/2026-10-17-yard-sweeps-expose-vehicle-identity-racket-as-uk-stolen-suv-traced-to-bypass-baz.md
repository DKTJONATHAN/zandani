Commercial car bazaars and roadside display yards across Kenya are facing renewed scrutiny after a coordinated inter-agency dragnet laid bare the tactical maneuvers syndicates use to launder stolen foreign and domestic vehicles into open retail lots.

The week-long operation, coordinated under the banner Operation Carback II, moved beyond routine highway checks to target the point of retail sale. Investigators zeroed in on vehicle identity scrubbing, unearthing instances where high-end machinery and imported luxury sports utility vehicles were placed directly onto sales floors with fabricated or cloned legal identities.

At the centre of the cross-border inquiry is a grey Land Rover Discovery impounded at a motor vehicle yard along Nairobi’s Northern Bypass. When detectives queried the vehicle's manufacturing details through the INTERPOL global database, the records tied the vehicle directly to an active theft report filed in the United Kingdom.

Rather than operating solely in shadowy transit routes, the syndicate had placed the British-sourced Land Rover onto an active commercial lot in Nairobi, exposing how easily illicit cross-border cargo merges with legitimate dealership inventories.

### Swapped Tags and Grounded Chassis

![detectives-cars.jpg](https://i.ibb.co/fYWLW275/2e4b5ca78e90.webp)

The recovery of the Land Rover represented only one tier of the identity-masking techniques uncovered during the multi-agency sweep. Local vehicle cloning operations were documented operating in tandem with transnational networks.

In Thika West, detectives intercepted a white Mercedes-Benz Kompressor displaying registration marks that legally belonged to an orange Suzuki station wagon. The registration swap—a common tactic used to bypass automated road cameras and rudimentary logbook spot-checks—allowed the premium saloon to operate on documentation stripped from an entirely different vehicle class.

Further east in Embakasi, enforcement officers tracked down heavy industrial machinery subjected to physical identity erasure. Detectives impounded a forklift whose accompanying documentation described it as a crawler tractor. A closer forensic check revealed that its chassis identification number had been completely ground down, blocking on-the-spot verification and prompting a dedicated investigation by DCI Embakasi detectives.

### The Logistics Behind the Sweep

Pulling off the dragnet required breaking institutional silos between border revenue collectors and field police units. The operation integrated technical officers from the Directorate of Criminal Investigations, INTERPOL, the National Transport and Safety Authority (NTSA), the Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA), the Kenya Bureau of Standards (KEBS), the Office of the Director of Public Prosecutions (ODPP), and regular and administration police wings.

![dci-vehicle.jpg](https://i.ibb.co/7JL9Jv5N/50a21cd1dc54.webp)

By uniting KRA’s tax entry rolls, NTSA’s registration ledgers, and INTERPOL’s Stolen Motor Vehicle repository, teams mounted an extensive verification drive:

* **260,000+** digital records screened in real time against international databases.
* **1,800+** vehicles physically inspected on-site by multi-agency examiners.
* **7 regional belts** covered simultaneously, spanning Nairobi, Central, Eastern, Rift Valley, Coast, Western, and Nyanza.

Field units did not confine inspections to vehicle dealerships. Teams moved into commercial showrooms, open-air bazaars, mechanics' garages, auto spare-parts outlets, and high-frequency transit checkpoints along major regional transport arteries.

### Accountability in the Sales Chain

The findings place commercial yard operators and import agents under direct legal pressure. While transnational cartels handle the logistical transit of luxury cars from overseas container ports, the presence of stolen units on public bypass yards shifts the investigative burden onto domestic intermediaries.

With the Land Rover impounded at DCI headquarters along Kiambu Road and forensic examinations underway on altered chassis numbers in Embakasi, state prosecutors are focusing on identifying the clearing agents, yard owners, and registry insiders who facilitated the issuance of local plates to foreign-stolen metal. The next phase of the crackdown is expected to determine whether dealership proprietors will face direct fraud and handling charges for housing undocumented stock.
