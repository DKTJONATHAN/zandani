The Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission (EACC) confirmed on Monday that the Milimani Anti-Corruption Court has convicted a former Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) employee for using a forged university certificate to climb the agency's internal pay structure.

The former Assistant Elections Officer was found guilty of uttering a false document after presenting a fabricated Moi University Bachelor of Business Management (Finance and Banking) degree. In a ruling delivered on September 17, 2026, the court ordered the convict to pay an Ksh 80,000 fine or serve eight months in prison.

Investigators established that the forged academic credential was submitted deliberately to support an application for internal advancement. The fraudulent document successfully secured the officer an elevation from Grade 8 to Grade 7 within the electoral body before the discrepancy triggered criminal proceedings.

### Promotion Fraud on the Public Payroll

The case underscores how credential forgery in statutory bodies extends beyond initial recruitment into mid-career promotions, allowing staff to secure higher salary bands and supervisory positions without prerequisite qualifications.

EACC noted that the conviction provides a necessary deterrent against widespread academic fraud across public agencies. In recent years, forged Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education (KCSE) certificates and falsified university degrees have siphoned millions of shillings from the exchequer through unearned salaries, allowances, and elevated pension obligations attached to higher job groups.

### Automated Checks to Close Institutional Loopholes

The conviction comes as the Public Service Commission (PSC) and the Kenya National Examination Council (KNEC) advance plans with the Ministry of Education to deploy integrated, automated verification platforms. The unified system aims to cross-reference academic credentials against primary institutional registries before approving recruitment or processing grade promotions.

While state agencies push to close legacy verification blind spots, administrative desks across parastatals and commissions face ongoing audits to root out established staff whose career progressions were built on unverified paperwork.
