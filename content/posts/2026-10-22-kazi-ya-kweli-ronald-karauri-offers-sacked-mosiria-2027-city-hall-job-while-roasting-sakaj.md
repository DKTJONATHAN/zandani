Kasarani Member of Parliament and SportPesa chief executive Ronald Karauri has never been one to let high-profile drama slip past without a well-timed flex. Within hours of Geoffrey Mosiria being unceremoniously pushed out of City Hall, the lawmaker stepped in with an advance employment contract of his own making.

Addressing the dismissal on Wednesday, October 21, 2026, Karauri told the former Chief Officer for Public Participation, Citizen Engagement, and Customer Service not to lose sleep over Governor Johnson Sakaja’s administrative purge. If the Kasarani MP takes the Nairobi governor’s mansion come the August 2027 General Election, Mosiria will already have a desk waiting for him.

“Mosiria, usijali bro, 2027 nikiingia City Hall kama Governor wa Nairobi, nitakuandikia kazi,” Karauri announced, framing his prospective gubernatorial bid around the ouster. “Kazi ya kweli, sio ile ya kukaa ofisi bila responsibilities since 2023.”

### The Punchline Behind the Dismissal

Karauri did not stop at dangling future executive patronage. He zeroed in on the sheer bureaucratic irony of firing a top county official whose primary duty was, by definition, public engagement.

“Imagine being the Chief Officer wa Public Participation, then unafutwa kazi kwa ku-participate,” Karauri remarked, turning the county shake-up into a one-liner.

To make sense of the backroom manoeuvres that precipitated Mosiria’s exit, the lawmaker reached for an old adage on pretexts and sacrificial lambs. He pointed to the manufactured nature of administrative fallout when leadership wants an official out of the room.

“Fisi akitaka kula watoto wake, kwanza huwasingizia wananuka mbuzi,” Karauri noted, taking direct aim at the rationale floated for Mosiria’s abrupt removal.

Karauri’s intervention also laid bare a long-simmering grievance regarding Mosiria's actual authority at City Hall. By describing the promised 2027 portfolio as "kazi ya kweli," Karauri directly backed claims that Mosiria had spent the past three years effectively sidelined from meaningful administrative duties despite holding a senior docket.

### The Legal Reality at City Hall

While Karauri was busy sketching out shadow cabinet roles for 2027, the formal paperwork sealing Mosiria’s departure moved forward under statutory procedure. 

The restructuring was executed through county notice NCC/CS/003/2026, referencing Sections 44(3)(a), 45(5), and 64 of the County Governments Act, 2012. Those provisions grant the county executive wide-ranging discretion to deploy, reorganize, or dismiss chief officers at will.

To fill the administrative vacuum left by Mosiria’s sudden exit, the county leadership appointed Zipporah Njeri Mwangi to steer the Public Participation, Citizen Engagement, and Customer Service portfolio in an acting capacity. Mwangi, who substantive role remains Chief Officer for City Culture, Arts, and Tourism, now absorbs both dockets simultaneously as City Hall rearranges its front desk.

Whether Karauri formally mounts a run for Nairobi’s top seat remains a question for the 2027 ballot, but his swift defense of Mosiria has turned a standard county reshuffle into an early political audition.
