Information, Communications, and the Digital Economy Cabinet Secretary William Kabogo Gitau has handed John Kieru Wambui a fresh three-year lease on the Kenya Institute of Mass Communication Council, opting for institutional continuity at the country’s oldest broadcaster and media talent incubator.

The reappointment, formalised through the Kenya Gazette published on Friday, October 9, 2026, secures Wambui’s seat on the council through late 2029. It positions him at the administrative center of an institution responsible for training a significant share of Kenya’s television producers, newsroom talent, radio presenters, and digital media technicians.

Kabogo’s endorsement came as part of a sweeping cycle of state corporation renewals and board appointments executed across several ministries, blending seasoned public personalities with targeted administrative changes.

### Mbadi Keeps Veteran Figures at NG-CDF

Over at the National Treasury, Cabinet Secretary John Mbadi moved to protect political experience within key funding agencies. Mbadi reappointed former Kisumu Town West Member of Parliament Olago Aluoch as the non-executive chairperson of the National Government Constituencies Development Fund (NG-CDF) Board for a three-year period.

Aluoch, a veteran lawyer and long-serving legislator, will continue steering the multi-billion-shilling constituency kitty alongside former nominated senator Masitsa Naomi Shiyonga, whose membership on the same board was renewed for three years.

Mbadi also extended his boardroom adjustments beyond constituency oversight. The Treasury boss appointed Kevin Owango Onyango and union doctor Dennis Miskellah as trustees on the Local Authorities Provident Fund Board, filling key oversight roles for municipal retirement funds. On the trade desk, Mbadi renewed Gerald Okoko’s directorship on the Kenya Trade Network Agency board for another three-year cycle.

### Duale, Wandayi, and Kagwe Move on Strategic Boards

Other ministries similarly focused on maintaining stability across specialized regulatory and scientific agencies.

Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale appointed Luka Chemwolo to the Kenya Medical Research Institute (KEMRI) Council for a three-year term, reinforcing oversight at the state’s primary research organization. 

In the energy docket, Cabinet Secretary Opiyo Wandayi extended Jane Masai’s seat on the Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority (EPRA) board. The extension, which officially takes effect on October 14, 2026, keeps Masai inside the country’s high-stakes fuel and power pricing boardroom for another three years.

Agriculture and Livestock Development CS Mutahi Kagwe renewed John Karuru Mwangi’s directorship at the Kenya Animal Genetics Resource Centre for three years, while Environment, Climate Change, and Forestry CS Deborah Mlongo Barasa granted fresh tenures to two forestry officials. Barasa retained Jane Simita Munka on the Kenya Forest Service board from October 14 and extended Metett Kibet Robert’s seat on the Kenya Forestry Research Institute board, effective October 9.

### State House Renewals and Alice Wahome's Revocation

At the executive level, President William Ruto confirmed leadership continuity across major water and conservation portfolios. Ruto reappointed Job Chirchir as non-executive chairperson of the Water Services Regulatory Board for three years, effective October 14. He similarly renewed Titus Kipkoech Korir’s chairmanship at the Kenya Forest Service for another three years from the same date.

Ruto also reaffirmed four members of the NEPAD/APRM Kenya Governing Council—Francis Habara Moroto, Habon Billow Farah, Joseph Kimutai Boit, and Rusiana Makasi Mitau—granting each an additional three-year term.

While most dockets leaned on renewals, Lands, Public Works, Housing, and Urban Development CS Alice Wahome delivered the lone outright boardroom dismissal in the gazetted cohort. Wahome appointed Mugendi Geoffrey Moses as non-executive chairperson of the Physical Planners Registration Board for a three-year term, formally revoking the appointment of Peris Chepchirchir Mang’ira to make room for the new chair.
