Intensified roadside inspections along Kenya’s major transit corridors have triggered an unexpected enforcement vulnerability: fraudsters deploying counterfeit traffic violation notices to extort on-the-spot payments from unsuspecting drivers.

The National Transport and Safety Authority issued an urgent advisory on Friday, October 30, warning motorists against fraudulent offence slips and fake payment receipts circulating across the road network. The counterfeit documents replicate official branding, combining the agency’s insignia with handwritten entries of alleged infractions to demand instant cash transfers from drivers on the spot.

The racket capitalises on anxiety generated by the high-visibility multi-agency highway crackdown launched following a spike in fatal collisions. While transport regulators did not directly tie the scam to active enforcement deployments, drivers report being accosted with demands for immediate payment shortly after encountering checkpoint corridors.

### Exploiting the Paperwork Void

![A fake traffic offences notice flagged by NTSA](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/ntsa-notice.jpg.webp?itok=xMoU02_H)

The scheme operates in the ambiguous space between legitimate statutory enforcement and digital compliance. Under the Traffic Act, designated police and transport enforcement officers retain the legal power to physically serve motorists with formal notices of intended prosecution or traffic infraction citations.

However, legitimate enforcement protocols require verified documentation issued through prescribed statutory channels. The counterfeit slips bypass institutional systems entirely, presenting motorists with fabricated charge sheets that insist on immediate mobile or cash settlements rather than standard court appearances or verified government payment gateways.

“Watch out for fake traffic offence notices and payment receipts,” the Authority said in its public advisory, cautioning drivers to inspect physical paperwork served on the road and verify all payment demands through official platforms.

### High-Tempo Checkpoints Create Cover

The emergence of the paper fraud coincides directly with the aggressive rollout of the *Msimu Bila Ajali Usalama Barabarani* campaign, an inter-agency initiative uniting police and transport enforcement officers for continuous day and night inspections.

State enforcement teams moved late-evening and night-time inspections to the center of the operation on Thursday, October 29, identifying after-dark transit hours as the most hazardous window on key trunk routes. The operation specifically targets motorists operating under the influence of alcohol, drivers without valid road licences, expired inspection certificates, and vehicles lacking statutory insurance coverage.

![NTSA](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/untitled-design-6_0.jpg.webp?itok=RguxJ0Qk)

That wide enforcement net has left many drivers uncertain about the exact boundaries of field citations. The rapid succession of highway checkpoints provides rogue actors with plausible cover to impersonate regulatory checks, brandishing forged papers at intimidated motorists eager to avoid vehicle impoundment or overnight custody.

### Repeated Exploitation of Regulatory Charges

Friday’s advisory marks the second time in two months that regulators have had to publicly counter syndicates monetising motorist confusion. In late September, the Authority issued a similar alert over fraudulent driving licence price schedules shared widely on digital channels, which steered applicants toward bogus middlemen promising expedited document clearance.

While the agency maintains that legitimate physical notices remain legally binding when served by authorized personnel, it has yet to publish a definitive checklist or instant digital verification system to help motorists distinguish authentic statutory summonses from fraudulent paper slips during roadside confrontations.

For drivers navigating the current enforcement blitz, the burden of vigilance remains on field verification—demanding that any officer issuing a citation demonstrate official credentials and direct all monetary settlement exclusively through designated national e-Citizen payment billings rather than private numbers or paper receipts.
