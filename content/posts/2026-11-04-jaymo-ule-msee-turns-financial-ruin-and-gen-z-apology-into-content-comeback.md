Wilson Muirani Gathoni is trading on the raw currency of his own downfall. Better known to digital audiences as Jaymo Ule Msee, the Kenyan content creator has stepped forward with a public mea culpa directed squarely at Gen Z, coupling an apology for past political alignments with an unvarnished confession of severe financial distress.

In a detailed statement released to his followers, Gathoni pulled back the curtain on the quiet isolation that follows commercial miscalculation and political overreach. While acknowledging that he lost his footing while pursuing political ambitions away from the cameras, he pinpointed specific remarks made on June 25, 2025, as the flashpoint where he alienated the youthful audience that built his brand.

“To Gen Z: I sincerely apologise for my insensitive remarks on 25 June 2025. I failed to give your pain and concerns the respect they deserved,” Gathoni stated, taking personal ownership of his vocabulary and decisions rather than deflecting blame onto the political arena itself.

Yet the most striking disclosure in the creator's address was not the political reckoning, but the economic reality sheet. Admitting that he is “super broke,” Gathoni detailed the psychological weight of being recognized on the street by fans while privately calculating how to survive the silence of an empty ledger.

Breaking from traditional celebrity decorum, Gathoni published his M-Pesa details for well-wishers willing to back his recovery financially, while noting that audience engagement—such as sharing and watching his digital output—remains a vital alternative form of support.

Rather than retreating from the digital space, however, Gathoni is immediately monetising the fallout. He announced a 20-episode streaming project titled *Inside the Life of a Prodigal Creator*, designed to  the exact mechanics of losing money, ego, and influence outside the studio lights.

The series is scheduled to premiere on Monday, November 9, 2026, promising an unvarnished look at the uncomfortable truths of survival after the applause stops.
