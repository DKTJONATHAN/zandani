Thirteen months after a fierce public backlash over comments regarding the death of Raila Odinga forced his resignation from the Council of Governors executive, Nyeri Governor Mutahi Kahiga has secured a prominent regional platform, taking over as chairperson of the East African County and Local Governments Association (EACLGA).

The two-year tenure, running from 2026 to 2028, was confirmed in Nairobi during the ninth East Africa Local Governments Forum Ministerial Meeting. The transition marks the end of Uganda’s stewardship of the bloc, which had held the seat since the 2024 Kampala Summit.

Kahiga takes over from Wilson Sanya, the former Mayor of Koboko Municipality in Uganda and president of the Urban Authorities Association of Uganda (UAAU). While the Nyeri County Government celebrated the appointment as a landmark achievement, Kahiga did not take the seat in person during the Nairobi ministerial gathering; Trans Nzoia Deputy Governor Philomena Bineah stood in as his representative to negotiate the transition.

### Backlash to Regional Helm

![Officials attend the 9th East Africa Local Governments Forum Ministerial Meeting in Nairobi, November 4, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/Y7VTXM5m/05402b98333e.webp)

The move marks a quiet institutional rehabilitation for the two-term governor. In October 2025, Kahiga resigned under immense political pressure from his post as Vice Chairperson of Kenya’s Council of Governors (CoG)—the statutory organ through which Kenyan county administrations plug into EACLGA. 

His departure followed widespread condemnation across the political divide over insensitive public remarks concerning the passing of the veteran opposition leader. Now, through Kenya’s scheduled turn at the rotating leadership of the East African Community’s sub-national framework, Kahiga inherits an institutional desk covering Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda, and Burundi.

### The Decentralization Mandate

The regional body represents local, municipal, and devolved authorities seeking leverage against centralized national executives across the bloc. At the opening of the forum, Prime Cabinet Secretary Musalia Mudavadi laid out Kenya's five core strategic targets for the two-year term, with sub-national financial independence placed at the top.

![Mutahi Kahiga](https://i.ibb.co/wZbSLzpC/aaefe4aed6e4.webp)

Mudavadi indicated that Kenya’s tenure would focus on anchoring local governments more firmly within the East African Community organs, deepening decentralisation frameworks, expanding public participation—particularly targeting women and youth—and standardising administrative training across member jurisdictions.

Local authorities across the region share chronic structural vulnerabilities, notably delays in revenue disbursements from central treasuries, constrained municipal borrowing powers, and uneven statutory protections. Bineah, speaking on Kahiga’s behalf during the ministerial sessions, argued that cross-border partnerships between border counties and municipal councils would be critical to making devolution functional across the EAC trade corridors.

### Five-Year Strategy on the Table

Kenya’s assumption of the chair coincides with the expiration of the association's previous planning cycle. The ministerial assembly is tasked with finalizing the Nairobi 2026 Declaration alongside the EACLGA corporate strategy spanning 2026 to 2031.

That strategic document will define how the association lobbies national parliaments for ring-fenced local government revenues and coordinates cross-border municipal infrastructure over the next five years. For Kahiga, the immediate test will be translating a diplomatic portfolio negotiated by proxy into an active regional consensus before the Nairobi declarations are presented to the EAC Council of Ministers.
