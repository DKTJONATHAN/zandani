Motorists navigating the Westlands corridor toward the Central Business District face a significant traffic bottleneck this weekend as the MOJA Expressway Company initiates maintenance on the elevated overpass deck.

Beginning Saturday, September 19, at 8:00 pm, the section of the expressway stretching from the James Gichuru Junction toward the city center will be closed to all traffic. The closure is scheduled to remain in effect until Monday, September 21, at 12:00 pm.

For thousands of commuters, this shift represents more than a simple detour; it forces a sudden influx of expressway traffic onto the ground-level lanes of Waiyaki Way. While the expressway’s Westlands entrance is slated to remain operational, the diversion of CBD-bound traffic to the lower road threatens to replicate the gridlock seen earlier this month.

On September 9, a similar closure at the ABC Place-Red Hill Road junction resulted in extensive delays, with motorists reporting long, stagnant queues that paralyzed movement along Waiyaki Way. That incident served as a stark reminder of the limited capacity of the ground-level arterial to absorb displaced high-speed traffic during peak or transition hours.

MOJA Expressway has advised drivers to build extra time into their weekend schedules to compensate for the anticipated congestion. The company has confirmed that traffic marshals will be deployed to manage the flow, though they have not provided specific measures to prevent the overflow onto the lower road that characterized the September 9 disruption.

While the elevated deck remains off-limits, the expressway operator noted that normal operations for motorists exiting the facility toward the overpass are expected to resume by 5:00 pm on Saturday.

As the weekend progresses, the primary challenge for traffic management will be the intersection of the expressway’s operational Westlands entrance and the diverted flow of vehicles navigating the ground-level construction zone. Drivers are encouraged to monitor real-time traffic updates, as the effectiveness of the diversion plan remains untested under the full pressure of Nairobi’s weekend transit patterns.
