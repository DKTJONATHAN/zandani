Geological constraints have knocked Kenya out of Dangote Cement’s medium-term manufacturing expansion, forcing the continent’s largest producer to anchor its East African supply strategy in neighboring markets.

Addressing investors during the company’s Capital Markets Day in London, Chief Executive Officer Arvind Pathak stated that Kenya does not feature in the group’s immediate growth pipeline, which targets scaling overall corporate capacity to 80 million tonnes through a 25-million-tonne buildout.

The exclusion hinges on raw material feasibility. While the multi-billion-dollar manufacturer has secured extensive high-grade limestone reserves across its active markets, exploratory assessments in Kenya failed to yield deposits that meet the required thresholds for volume, material purity, and market-adjacent logistics.

Without a viable local extraction base to feed a large-scale grinding and clinker operation, the group has frozen plans for a domestic manufacturing facility, choosing instead to supply the regional corridor through its established production hubs in Ethiopia and Tanzania.

![Dangote](https://i.ibb.co/RGRBWQsT/f65074676390.webp)

### Cross-Border Supply Nodes Take Priority

Rather than pouring greenfield capital into local cement production, Dangote is doubling down on existing East African operational assets that already hold secured mineral rights.

In Ethiopia, the company is pushing higher output from its West Shewa facility in Mugher. The plant, which currently operates at its maximum rated design capacity of 2.5 million tonnes annually, is slated for structural expansion to absorb demand across northern East Africa.

Concurrently, the group is executing a low-cost operational optimization project at its Ksh65 billion ($500 million) flagship plant in Mtwara, Tanzania. Operating at 85 to 90 percent of its three-million-tonne annual capacity, the Mtwara facility is expected to deliver a 16 percent boost in production volume once the efficiency upgrades are complete.

![An image of the Dangote Petroleum Refinery in Nigeria.](https://i.ibb.co/DH05FvcX/0bb234eae16c.webp)

This cross-border posture allows the group to serve Kenyan market demand through regional trade flows rather than localized heavy industry, reserving its next wave of new market entries for southern African territories including Zimbabwe and Botswana.

### Energy Footprint Retains Kenya Core

While raw material quality has stalled Dangote’s heavy manufacturing footprint in the country, it has not altered the group’s broader capital deployment into Kenyan energy infrastructure.

The cement decision runs parallel to the executive rollout of Dangote’s downstream energy interests in the region. The group is preparing to formally break ground on its proposed Ksh2.59 trillion ($20 billion) oil refinery and petrochemical complex in Lamu on September 30, positioning Kenya as the principal off-take and distribution market for its broader energy strategy.
