A legislative push to anchor professional quotas in Kenya's diplomatic missions has opened a debate over how tightly Parliament should restrict executive discretion in ambassadorial postings.

The Foreign Service (Amendment) Bill, 2026, sponsored by Mwingi West MP Charles Ngusya, proposes capping non-career political appointees at 30 per cent while reserving up to 70 per cent of nominations for career foreign service officers. The draft law also seeks to codify a legal definition for career diplomats by introducing a mandatory requirement of at least 10 years of service within the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

However, stakeholder sessions before the National Assembly Departmental Committee on Defence, Intelligence and Foreign Relations have exposed key drafting friction points that professional bodies warn could undermine the bill's intent.

### The Battle Over Quotas and Floors

![Members of the National Assembly's Departmental Committee on Defence, Intelligence and Foreign Relations hold a session to consider stakeholder views, November 3, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/kVyzgxPS/8ce2378416a3.webp)

During public review sessions, diplomatic interest groups cautioned that the current legislative draft leaves room for executive workaround. The International Relations Society of Kenya (IRSK) pointed out that phrasing career representation as "not more than seventy per cent" creates a statutory ceiling rather than a protected floor.

IRSK urged lawmakers to adjust the text so that 70 per cent operates as a mandatory minimum baseline for career diplomats, preventing future administrations from leaning on the 30 per cent non-career cap to underfill career slots.

Questions were also raised regarding how the 10-year experience benchmark will be audited in practice. IRSK asked the committee to clarify whether secondments to international organizations, inter-ministerial transfers, or assignments outside ministry headquarters count toward the decade-long operational threshold.

### Experience Thresholds and Rank Filters

![parliament](https://i.ibb.co/pvWZtCfD/f6ffa71e4b17.webp)

Taking a more restrictive stance, the Association of Former and Retired Ambassadors recommended raising the entry bar higher than the sponsor's draft.

The retired diplomats submitted that the baseline experience requirement should be elevated from 10 to 15 years of continuous ministry service. To prevent junior appointments from skipping operational tiers, the association also urged the committee to restrict nominations to officers who have attained the seniority rank of Director or Minister Counsellor.

Under current practice, the law requires a general balance between career diplomats and political appointees while observing gender, regional, and background diversity. The proposed bill maintains the constitutional sequence where the President fields nominees for high commissioner and ambassadorial roles subject to National Assembly vetting.

The Departmental Committee on Defence, Intelligence and Foreign Relations is compiling stakeholder submissions to formulate its committee amendments ahead of the bill's second reading in the National Assembly.
