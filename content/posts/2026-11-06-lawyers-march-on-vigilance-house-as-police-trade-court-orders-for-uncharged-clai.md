The standoff between Kenya’s legal fraternity and top state security organs moved to the streets on Friday morning, after police leadership failed to comply with a judicial ultimatum to produce missing Nairobi advocate Abshir Ahmedsadak Abdi.

Led by Law Society of Kenya (LSK) President Charles Kanjama, hundreds of advocates, human rights defenders, and civil society actors marched from the Milimani Law Courts along Kenyatta Avenue toward Vigilance House, the national police headquarters. The demonstration follows the lapse of a High Court production directive that security agencies have yet to satisfy.

Abdi was abducted in the early hours of Sunday, November 1, along Muchai Drive in Nairobi. Closed-circuit television footage recovered from the scene captured armed men chasing the advocate before forcibly bundling him into an awaiting vehicle. 

### The Collapsed Habeas Corpus Deadline

![Lawyers march from Milimani Law Courts towards Vigilance House during protests over the abduction of advocate Abshir Abdi, November 6, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/h1c10HQN/b2ad64aaa4fa.webp)

The march on police headquarters represents an escalation from the courtroom to direct administrative confrontation. Two days earlier, on Wednesday, November 4, High Court Justice Alexander Muteti issued an explicit order directing designated state officials to present Abdi before the court at 2:30 p.m.—alive or dead—and formally account for his disappearance.

The habeas corpus proceedings directly targeted the apex of Kenya’s security establishment, naming Inspector General of Police Douglas Kanja, Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) boss Amin Mohammed, National Intelligence Service (NIS) Director-General Noordin Haji, and Interior Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen as respondents.

Justice Muteti also demanded that the state table any arrest warrants or detention documentation that would provide statutory cover for Abdi’s custody. No such warrants were delivered to the court.

### Allegations in Place of Compliance

Rather than producing the advocate or tabling lawful detention records, police officers appearing before the court introduced claims that Abdi was under investigation for potential involvement in an extortion ring.

Addressing demonstrators before the march left Milimani, Kanjama called out the state's pivot from constitutional accountability to character disparagement, arguing that uncharged suspicions cannot override basic protections against enforced disappearance.

![A collage showing LSK President Charles Kanjama addressing demonstrators and marching alongside Vocal Africa CEO Hussein Khalid during protests, November 6, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/dsTx5Cgz/0ccd1ae72817.webp)

“We are here today to express our rage and anger, as well as our commitment and determination to leave no stone unturned and continue engaging until our colleague is safely back with us and the culture of abductions comes to an end,” Kanjama told the gathering.

The LSK maintained that even if a citizen is suspected of criminal conduct, statutory procedure requires formal arrest, prompt arraignment, and access to legal counsel, none of which occurred along Muchai Drive.

### DCI Committee and Unanswered Questions

The DCI has since confirmed the formation of a special inquiry committee to examine the circumstances surrounding Abdi’s abduction. However, the move has done little to quell friction between the bar and the National Police Service, with advocates viewing the internal panel as an evasion of clear judicial directives.

With the state's security command now cornered between contempt proceedings at Milimani and sustained demonstrations outside Vigilance House, the focus shifts to whether Inspector General Kanja and the DCI will formally present findings to Justice Muteti's court or disclose the advocate's whereabouts.
