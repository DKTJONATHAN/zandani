The proposed 10,000-seater indoor arena in Nairobi represents more than a new venue for concerts and basketball; it serves as a critical test for the commercial viability of the Nairobi Railway City urban renewal plan. Backed by a $294.5 million (Ksh38.2 billion) investment from the Zaria Group-co-founded by Dallas Mavericks President Masai Ujiri-and the US-based Helios Sports & Entertainment Group, the project moves the needle on how Nairobi leverages its central public land.

Unlike the state-backed Talanta Sports City currently under construction, this development relies on a long-term lease agreement with Kenya Railways. By securing space within the Railway City precinct, the developers are positioning the arena as the anchor for a broader mixed-use entertainment district. This model mirrors the group’s operations in Rwanda, where they have invested in similar facilities like Zaria Court Kigali and the management of the BK Arena.

For the city, the stakes extend beyond the event calendar. The developers have projected the creation of 3,500 construction jobs and 1,500 permanent roles upon completion. , they estimate that the facility’s operations could trigger up to 25,000 temporary positions annually across the hospitality, retail, and live production sectors. These figures suggest that the project’s success will be measured by its ability to integrate into the city’s existing service economy rather than just its ability to fill seats.

This investment highlights a deliberate strategy by the Zaria-Helios partnership to build a network of sports and entertainment infrastructure across major African markets, with Lagos and Johannesburg identified as future targets. By choosing Nairobi, the developers are entering a space where the government is simultaneously pouring capital into large-scale sports facilities. The challenge for this private-led venture will be navigating the transition of the Railway City from a conceptual master plan into a functioning, revenue-generating urban hub.

While the arena is designed to host everything from international music tours to high-level conferences, its ultimate impact will depend on the surrounding infrastructure of the Railway City. As Kenya Railways prepares to hand over the site, the project remains one of the most significant private-sector commitments to Nairobi’s urban core in recent years.
