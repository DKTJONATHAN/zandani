Dr. Raymond Omollo has drawn a firm line against executive misconduct inside lake region fishermen cooperatives, warning officials against treating member savings as personal bank accounts.

The Principal Secretary for Internal Security and National Administration stated that protecting hard-earned deposits from mismanagement is essential if small-scale lake operators are to secure predictable capital for gear and boat equipment. Speaking on local cooperative operations, Omollo emphasized that financial indiscipline at the executive level directly starves fishermen of the liquidity required to sustain their daily operations.

### Correcting the Cooperative Audit Scope

During his address, Omollo addressed figures previously raised by an official named Sylvester, who had identified one or two troubled cooperatives in the area. The PS clarified that administrative assessments actually point to four distinct cooperatives requiring immediate governance interventions.

To address systemic weaknesses, Omollo proposed pulling leadership teams from all four entities into a unified training framework focused on accounting standards, board oversight, and statutory compliance.

"There is also the matter of cooperatives, and Sylvester mentioned one or two, though there are actually four," Omollo noted. "I want us to come together and conduct proper training for your leaders so that when you deposit your hard-earned money into the cooperative, it does not become personal funds for officials to use as they please."

### Building a Reliable Revolving Pool for Fishing Gear

The core of the proposed reform centers on re-establishing trust in the cooperative revolving fund model. Under the structure outlined by the PS, sound leadership ensures member contributions create a self-sustaining pool of low-cost capital specifically earmarked for operational gear.

Fishermen often face steep out-of-pocket expenses for nets, outboard engines, boat repairs, and safety apparatus. Without transparent cooperative financing, many are forced to seek high-interest credit or delay seasonal lake trips.

Omollo explained that when management safeguards member deposits, the revolving system functions continuously: a member borrows from the pooled savings to buy gear, returns to active fishing, repays the principal with nominal interest, and replenishes the capital pool for the next borrower.

"That way, whenever you need capital to buy fishing gear for our lake, your savings are safely there—topped up through the revolving pool contributed by fellow members—allowing you to equip yourself, repay the loan, and give other members the exact same opportunity to borrow and move their livelihoods forward," Omollo said.

### Institutional Oversight and Local Impact

The intervention by the National Administration underlines growing concern over how financial mismanagement at the grass-roots level undermines local economic security. By targeting leadership accountability, the State Department aims to transform these four cooperatives from fragile, dispute-ridden groups into reliable micro-finance hubs.

For lake fishermen, the success of the joint training initiative will be measured by whether their daily deposits remain intact and accessible when gear purchases cannot wait. The upcoming leadership training sessions are expected to establish new operational protocols, financial reporting guidelines, and member oversight mechanisms across all four designated cooperatives.
