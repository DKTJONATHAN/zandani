State meteorologists at the Kenya Meteorological Department are refusing to back down under scorching heat, doubling down on their high-stakes forecast of enhanced El Niño downpours across more than 40 counties despite a prolonged dry spell that has left many questioning the skies.

Addressing growing public skepticism over delayed rainfall, weather authorities maintained that current hot and dry conditions do not signal a retreat from their seasonal outlook. The government had previously placed a 99 percent probability on above-normal rainfall for the October–December short rains season, projecting heavy downpours to persist through January and February 2027 in several regions.

Weather forecasters stressed that the timing, distribution, and intensity of the rains will vary by region rather than hitting the country simultaneously. Western Kenya and the Lake Victoria Basin—including Kisumu, Kakamega, and Busia—were identified as early onset zones, with wet conditions projected to extend into early 2027.

For Nairobi and the central highlands, covering Kiambu, Murang’a, Nyeri, Kirinyaga, Nyandarua, Laikipia, Meru, Embu, and Tharaka-Nithi, the anticipated onset window was set between the second and third weeks of October, with rains expected to run into January.

Meteorologists explained that the persistent dry spell stems from complex interactions between global sea surface temperatures and regional atmospheric drivers like the Indian Ocean Dipole. Authorities explicitly warned against mistaking isolated light showers for the formal start of the rainy season, clarifying that El Niño’s overall strength does not dictate localized timing in a linear fashion.

While the promised deluge is expected to boost agricultural output, fill depleted water reservoirs, and restore grazing lands for pastoral communities, officials cautioned that the shift will come with serious hazards. Beyond flash floods, landslides, and infrastructure destruction, the Ministry of Health has already issued advisories to all 47 counties to prepare for potential outbreaks of waterborne diseases, including cholera and typhoid, alongside vector-borne illnesses linked to stagnant floodwaters.

Meanwhile, southeastern counties including Machakos, Kitui, Makueni, and Kajiado, the coastal strip of Mombasa, Kwale, Kilifi, and Lamu, and the northeastern region spanning Marsabit, Mandera, Wajir, Garissa, and Isiolo remain forecasted for rains through October, with coastal and northeastern seasons concluding by December. County weather offices continue to advise residents in flood-prone areas to monitor local alerts and refrain from navigating flooded roads or swollen rivers.
