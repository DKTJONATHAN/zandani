Phelix Odiwuor is not letting his former party secretary-general enjoy the American diaspora circuit in peace. 

The Lang’ata lawmaker, who made a lucrative career out of radio punchlines before entering the National Assembly, turned his timeline into an impromptu party summons, deploying his familiar comedic sign-off to puncture Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna’s overseas political tour.

“Nani… Toka America Rudi kwa chama!! Wachana na hao wanakudanganya!… Aloooo,” Jalang’o posted directly to his public Facebook page.

The message, translated from sheng and colloquial Swahili, delivers a blunt order wrapped in media-host theatrics: *“You… Get out of America and come back to the party! Stop dealing with those who are misleading you!… Aloooo.”*

### The Irony of the Table-Turning

For followers of Nairobi’s political theatre, the exchange carries a rich layer of irony. Sifuna spent years as the fiery gatekeeper of Orange House, enforcing party loyalty with sharp administrative decrees. Jalang’o, conversely, spent substantial stretches of the current parliamentary term navigating the cold fringes of the party after choosing early cooperation with President William Ruto’s administration.

Now, the roles have completely flipped. Sifuna is out in the political cold, stripped of his influential post as secretary-general in June 2026 and ousted from his Senate leadership position. Meanwhile, Jalang'o is comfortable enough on the inside to playfully summon his former disciplinarian back to the dinner table.

The public teasing catches Sifuna in the middle of an intensive cross-country roadshow across the United States. Having departed Nairobi on November 20, the senator is actively engaging Kenyan diaspora chapters to raise funds and build momentum for an ambitious 2027 presidential run.

### Chasing Power Across the Atlantic

Sifuna’s push across the Atlantic forms the logistical core of his bid under the Linda Mwananchi banner. Following the death of veteran opposition figure Raila Odinga in October 2025, ODM fractured over how closely it should intertwine its fortunes with the ruling United Democratic Alliance (UDA).

Sifuna broke ranks with the party brass, arguing that an embrace of the Kenya Kwanza executive would swallow the outfit's opposition credentials whole. After being removed from his party perch, Sifuna and his inner circle adopted The Equitable Party (TEP) as their ballot vehicle, setting in motion a rebranding drive to transform it into the Linda Mwananchi Movement.

By September, the formation formally tapped Sifuna as its designated presidential candidate to contest the 2027 general election. Yet the breakaway camp has struggled to maintain unified ranks. Embakasi East MP Babu Owino has steered toward The Mwananchi Party, while Siaya Governor James Orengo has directed his political allegiance toward the Liberal Democratic Party (LDP).

### A Divided Orange House

Jalang’o’s online banter comes against the backdrop of fraught boardroom haggling back home. ODM and UDA have been examining a structured pre-election coalition agreement, but the arrangement has triggered open friction.

One camp within ODM is demanding a signed, airtight pact guaranteeing specific cabinet slots and up to 50 percent of government positions before endorsing Ruto's re-election campaign. A rival internal faction insists the party should back the head of state without tabling excessive terms that could alienate voters or derail executive cooperation.

Across the aisle, UDA hardliners have pushed back against the prospect of handing half the government to their former rivals, questioning whether the current ODM formation holds the electoral numbers to justify such leverage.

Through it all, Jalang’o is treating the ideological split with the lightness of an afternoon drive-time show. Sifuna has not publicly answered the Facebook tease, keeping his social accounts focused on town halls and town-to-town town meetings across American states. His overseas diaspora tour is scheduled to wrap up on December 7, when the senator is expected to land back in Nairobi and face the very party machinery Jalang’o wants him to rejoin.
