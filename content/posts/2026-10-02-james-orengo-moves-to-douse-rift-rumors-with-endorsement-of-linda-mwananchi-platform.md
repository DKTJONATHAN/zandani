Siaya Governor James Orengo has stepped forward to clear the air on his political alignment, throwing his weight behind the Linda Mwananchi Movement just days after missing the retreat that positioned Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna as its preferred leader.

Taking to social media on October 1, the veteran lawyer publicly backed the push for a single opposition flagbearer in the 2027 General Election, seizing on a landmark legal breakthrough to signal unity within the anti-government coalition.

“We laud the Linda Mwananchi success story and remain steadfast. Aluta continua!” Orengo posted, placing a high-profile endorsement behind the movement at a pivotal moment.

Orengo’s statement addresses weeks of mounting speculation regarding a possible fallout within the opposition ranks. Whispers of division flared after Orengo failed to show up for the movement’s strategic retreat, where delegates picked Sifuna to anchor their political agenda. The absence was compounded by reports suggesting the Siaya Governor was quietly laying groundwork for his own independent 2027 presidential bid.

By publicly locking arms with the platform and endorsing a unified flagbearer strategy, Orengo has moved to shut down narrative threads pointing to fragmentation ahead of the upcoming electoral cycle.

His public pivot followed a favorable ruling by the Political Parties Disputes Tribunal (PPDT), which cleared the legal hurdle blocking the movement's registration. A bench chaired by Innocent Muganda set aside an earlier decision by the Office of the Registrar of Political Parties (ORPP) that had rejected the movement's name reservation.

The ORPP had previously blocked the reservation of “Linda Mwananchi Movement” and its acronym “LLM,” citing potential confusion with another entity. However, the Muganda-led tribunal found that the competing name reservation had legally expired under regulatory timelines, leaving no valid basis to deny the application.

The tribunal issued a direct order directing the registrar to process and complete the movement's application within seven days.

With legal recognition now within reach and heavyweights like Orengo lining up to support a single candidate model, the Linda Mwananchi Movement enters its next operational phase with both legal backing and solid political reinforcement. All eyes now shift to the registrar's office as the seven-day processing deadline ticks down.
