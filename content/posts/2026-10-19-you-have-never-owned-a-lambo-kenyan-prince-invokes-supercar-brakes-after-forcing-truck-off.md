Raymond Omosa, the self-styled forex trader and content creator known online as Kenyan Prince, is refusing to back down after posting footage of a near-miss head-on confrontation on a Kenyan highway, arguing that public critics lack the technical background to evaluate high-performance vehicle engineering.

The viral clip, published directly across Omosa’s social media accounts, depicts his luxury SUV pulling out of a stationary queue along a two-lane road and accelerating directly down the opposing lane. The maneuver brought the vehicle into the immediate path of an oncoming Mercedes Actros heavy-duty commercial truck, leaving no braking margin on the shared bitumen.

Disaster was averted not by the SUV’s stopping power, but by the rapid intervention of the commercial driver. Facing an imminent head-on collision, the operator of the Actros swerved the heavy truck completely off the main carriageway and onto the dirt shoulder, opening a path for Omosa’s oncoming vehicle to clear the line.

When widespread commentary poured in condemning the high-risk pass, Omosa rejected any suggestion of recklessness, framing the encounter as a lesson in automotive capability rather than a traffic violation.

“You have never owned a Lambo; you don’t know the power it has or its ability to brake,” Omosa stated in response to the online backlash. “I am the one who posted the video; let me tell the story.”

Omosa insisted that public outrage was rooted in ignorance regarding advanced automotive safety features and high-end braking mechanics, claiming the vehicle's engineering gave him full control over the maneuver.

However, road safety advocates and online commentators quickly pointed out the flaw in his bravado. Observers noted that regardless of an SUV’s acceleration profile or factory braking systems, high-speed head-on passes in an occupied oncoming lane rely entirely on the evasive reflexes of other motorists. Commentary across social platforms largely praised the truck driver’s fast steering for preventing a fatal highway collision.

The incident highlights a continuing trend of digital personalities using public infrastructure to stage high-visibility motoring content. Despite intense scrutiny over distance judgment and traffic compliance, Omosa maintains that his vehicle ownership experience gives him sole authority to interpret the close call.
