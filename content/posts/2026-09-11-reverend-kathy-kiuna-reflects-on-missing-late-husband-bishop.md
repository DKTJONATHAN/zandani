NAIROBI - Reverend Kathy Kiuna, co-founder of Jubilee Christian Church (JCC), has publicly addressed her ongoing process of grieving her late husband, Bishop Allan Kiuna, while contrasting the spiritual foundation of their three-decade marriage with contemporary relationship trends.

**What we know:**
* Bishop Allan Kiuna passed away on July 9, 2024, at the age of 57 following a battle with cancer.
* The couple was married in 1993 and established JCC in Nairobi in 1994, growing it into one of Kenya’s largest ministries.
* Reverend Kiuna stated that the longevity of their union was rooted in spiritual commitment rather than external factors.
* The preacher cited current societal tendencies toward prioritizing fame, wealth, and social media validation as contributors to instability in modern marriages.

In a statement shared via social media, the preacher reflected on the early years of their marriage, noting that faith served as the primary anchor during periods of personal and professional hardship. She emphasized that while financial success and public perception are often prioritized in modern culture, they lack the capacity to sustain unions through periods of adversity.

"There was a time when all we had was this; us and God," Reverend Kiuna wrote. "We are in a generation where marriages are founded on fame, looks, and wealth and social likes but no presence of God."

Regarding her current status as a widow, Reverend Kiuna acknowledged the difficulty of the transition while attributing her resilience to her religious practice. She noted that while she continues to experience personal loss, her faith remains the primary source of stability in her daily life.

"I miss my husband terribly, but the same God that was with us is with me," she stated. "He has proven to me that His love for me is greater than how my husband could ever love me."

Reverend Kiuna remains the lead administrator of JCC, a ministry that has maintained a significant presence in the Kenyan religious landscape since its inception 32 years ago.
