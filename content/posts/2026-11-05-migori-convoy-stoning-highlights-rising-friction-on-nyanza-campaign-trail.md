Tensions along the Nyanza campaign trail spilled into direct violence on Thursday after stone-throwing crowds targeted a high-profile opposition motorcade in Migori County, exposing deep political friction as non-aligned leaders challenge established regional strongholds.

The procession was carrying Democratic Party (DP) leader Justin Muturi, Wiper Party leader Kalonzo Musyoka, and Democratic Action Party of Kenya (DAP-K) leader Eugene Wamalwa during a scheduled joint campaign tour of the region. Images from the scene confirmed structural damage to several vehicles, with side and rear windows shattered by thrown projectiles.

### Accusations Target Broad-Based Allies

![A collage of the United Opposition convoy in Migori County on November 5.](https://i.ibb.co/tP1jwGb7/8e4362158408.webp)

The violent disruption prompted immediate political retaliation from the convoy's principals. Muturi, a former Speaker of the National Assembly, publicly condemned the attack and directed blame at politicians associated with the broad-based government framework, specifically pointing to elements within the Orange Democratic Movement (ODM).

Muturi alleged that political figures allied with the broad-based administration sponsored individuals to violently disrupt the opposition gathering, framing the incident as an effort to block political competition in the region.

"We will not be silenced or shaken," Muturi stated, asserting that the team would continue its tour despite security risks.

![A collage of the United Opposition convoy in Migori County on November 5.](https://i.ibb.co/0p9QryDM/60f49d46ede7.webp)

### Testing Regional Dominance

The joint Nyanza tour by Musyoka, Muturi, and Wamalwa represents a deliberate push by the united opposition front to test public sentiment and build grassroots structures outside their primary electoral bases. However, the pushback in Migori highlights the volatility facing outside political alliances operating in counties long anchored by ODM dominance.

The realignment of key ODM leaders into broad-based governance structures has reshaped local political dynamics, creating heightened hostility toward rival coalitions attempting to conduct public rallies in the region.

By Thursday evening, security agencies in Migori had not officially disclosed any arrests or named prime suspects linked to the stoning incident. Opposition organizers indicated that their planned itinerary across the Nyanza region would continue under reviewed security measures.
