University students grappling with unplanned living expenses from disrupted academic calendars will wait until at least January 2027 before receiving their next state upkeep allocations, following a directive issued by the Higher Education Loans Board.

In guidance released on November 2, the agency stated that funds for the second semester of the 2026/2027 academic cycle remain strictly tied to the formal start of the new term. The announcement simultaneously closes the door on calls for early interim relief and quashes confusion surrounding second-term administrative paperwork.

“Semester 2 HELB funds will only be disbursed once the semester begins, around January. You do not need to make another application. HELB application is done once per academic year,” the board confirmed in an official advisory.

### Industrial Action Extends Living Costs

The confirmed January timeline introduces practical budget pressures on campuses where semesters have been prolonged by recent nationwide lecturer strikes. While tuition fees remain covered on institutional ledgers, the physical disruption forced students to extend residential tenancies and cover weeks of unbudgeted meals, daily transport, and utility payments while academic schedules dragged out.

Because loan disbursements are calculated around standard term durations, the calendar stretch has left many learners with depleted personal reserves weeks before the current semester formally concludes. HELB’s insistence on a single annual application prevents any administrative intervention to pull forward semester two allocations, requiring students to self-fund the remaining gap through the end of the year.

![A collage of Embu University (left) and the University of Nairobi (right)](https://i.ibb.co/60tQMG6z/afb93ec60b57.webp)

### Lingering First-Semester Releases

The second-semester waiting period arrives as the state financier only recently concluded releasing delayed first-semester installments. Although first-year beneficiaries were scheduled to access their opening allocations from August 15, bureaucratic processing saw supplementary batches trickling into student bank accounts through late October.

HELB has directed all continuing beneficiaries to verify that their linked bank and personal account records remain active and error-free to prevent automated rejections when the mass January payment run begins. Students have also been advised to track payment notifications through the agency’s online portal rather than attempting to register new application profiles.

For university households already managing the financial toll of stretched campus terms, the board's rigid schedule shifts the entire burden of bridging November and December costs back onto family pockets.
