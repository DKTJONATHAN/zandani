Cassypool Capon has offered a public apology to his audience for allowing high-stakes political advocacy to ruin his entertainment career, delivering a rare admission of brand destruction that highlights the commercial perils of creator political alignment in Kenya.

Speaking during an interview on Sunday, November 1, 2026, the outspoken digital personality acknowledged that his outspoken backing of President William Ruto systematically eroded his fan base, cancelled commercial opportunities, and triggered severe personal distress. Yet, even as he admitted to ruining his own standing, Cassypool introduced a striking caveat: given the same choice, he would make the exact same political decision.

“I do regret supporting President William Ruto. I do regret it because it cost me my brand. I killed my brand,” Cassypool said during the sit-down.

His retreat comes after years of public friction with audiences who had built his platform before he pivoted into aggressive political commentary. Addressing followers who felt alienated by his stance, the influencer asked for forgiveness for allowing his political positions to disrupt the relationship with his audience.

“To all those people I hurt because of the political stance I took, I just want to apologise to them. I’m asking for your forgiveness. I did wrong and took a political stand that hurt my fans,” he said.

The fallout from his political posturing extended far beyond dropped follower counts and lost corporate partnerships. Cassypool disclosed that the relentless stream of online hostility severely damaged his mental health, pushing him into clinical depression and leading him to contemplate taking his own life.

According to the entertainer, adjusting to constant public hostility proved difficult after years of enjoying widespread applause. The digital backlash also spilled over into his home life. He revealed that his wife became overwhelmed by the scale of the online abuse, eventually urging him to step away from public commentary altogether. Cassypool added that he remains deeply concerned about his children, who are old enough to navigate social media platforms and read targeted attacks directed at their father.

### A History of High-Stakes Advocacy

Cassypool’s political entanglements began ahead of the 2022 General Election. After initially announcing a presidential bid, he withdrew from the race to back then-Deputy President William Ruto. By 2024, he publicly referred to Ruto as his political boss, abandoning his own political ambitions to champion the administration's policy platform.

His position hardened during the intense public debate over the Finance Bill 2024. As widespread public resistance mounted, Cassypool emerged as one of the few high-profile digital commentators willing to defend the government’s fiscal proposals, arguing publicly that citizens needed to endure tough economic measures before conditions would improve.

That uncompromising posture frequently brought him into direct confrontation with the broader entertainment industry. In August 2025, Cassypool publicly clashed with musician Bien-Aimé Baraza after the singer led crowds in a ‘Ruto Must Go’ chant during a live performance—an act Cassypool condemned as disrespectful to the Head of State.

### The Creator Political Paradox

Cassypool’s admissions illustrate the distinct risk digital creators face when attempting to monetise general audience engagement while taking partisan political stands. While corporate brand endorsements rely on broad cultural appeal and positive sentiment, political advocacy in Kenya's current climate carries sharp polarization.

What makes Cassypool’s admission unusual in creator apology culture is his refusal to recant the underlying political allegiance. Despite spelling out the exact timeline of his professional collapse—from lost revenue and ruined credibility to severe mental health struggles and family strain—he maintained throughout the interview that he would still align with President Ruto if given another opportunity.

For an influencer whose platform was built on public visibility, the resulting reality remains stark: an apology issued to fans for killing a brand, coupled with an unyielding political stance that guarantees the brand remains dead.
