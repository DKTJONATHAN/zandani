Local businesses and corporate employers face tighter compliance scrutiny as the Kenya Revenue Authority enforces a 9 per cent benchmark rate for employer-provided loans and shareholder advances through December 2026.

The adjustment, which ticks upward from the 8 per cent threshold applied during the previous quarter, governs both Fringe Benefit Tax and deemed interest calculations under the Income Tax Act. Under the statutory framework, Fringe Benefit Tax is triggered when companies extend below-market loans to personnel, taxing the financial advantage gained by the employee. Deemed interest similarly applies when firms issue interest-free advances to shareholders or associated parties.

![Kenya Revenue Authority building at Times Towers](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/kra-headquarters_0.jpg.webp?itok=RHNkHMyr)

Beyond the base interest rate hike, the tax authority confirmed that a 15 per cent withholding tax remains active for deemed interest obligations throughout October, November, and December. Employers are bound by strict statutory timelines, required to deduct the applicable sums and remit them directly to the Commissioner within five working days of the payroll cycle.

Crucially, the regulatory burden extends beyond active employment terms. Under existing tax rules, if a loan's repayment tenure stretches past the formal termination date of an employee, the 9 per cent calculation mandate continues to apply, requiring payroll administrators to maintain tracking mechanisms well past exit dates.

![KRA](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/kra%20desk.jpg.webp?itok=qevlCpIp)

The upward revision forms part of the taxman's routine quarterly market adjustments intended to mirror prevailing economic conditions. However, the authority has not disclosed the specific macroeconomic indicators that drove the one-percentage-point increase from the third quarter.

Affected entities must adjust their accounting software and payroll computations immediately to avoid penalties ahead of the upcoming remittance cycles.
