Kenya's security agencies are shifting their approach to political violence ahead of the 2027 General Election, turning focus away from street-level clashes to hit the financial backers who place organized groups on their personal payrolls.

Under a new coordinated operational framework, the Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) has begun issuing summonses to profiled politicians suspected of funding violent disruptions, paired with plans to bar identified sponsors from appearing on the ballot.

### Targeting the Payroll

The tactical pivot follows intelligence profiling conducted across regional security committees, which identified specific political figures funding militia-for-hire groups to intimidate opponents at public gatherings.

DCI Nyanza Region Coordinator George Matonya confirmed that regional detectives have drawn up lists of sponsors and will start questioning them directly on their financial connections to recent attacks.

"We have been able to profile the known politicians who are connected to goonism. And as the regional security committee, we have agreed that we should summon employers of the goons and those who have them on their payroll," Matonya said.

Where detectives establish sufficient financial and administrative links, the DCI plans to institute criminal charges against the politicians rather than limiting prosecutions to hired foot soldiers.

### The IEBC Disqualification Mechanism

Beyond criminal court filings, the National Police Service (NPS) is linking its investigative findings directly to electoral eligibility. The agency is coordinating with the National Cohesion and Integration Commission (NCIC) and the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) to implement candidate disqualifications.

This administrative avenue builds on commitments made during the security sector's electoral planning rollout on August 25, 2026, where police leadership outlined a framework to submit offender registries directly to the electoral commission.

Deputy Inspector General of Police Gilbert Masengeli noted that police recommendations to the IEBC will focus on candidate suitability under integrity and security thresholds.

"We will recommend to the IEBC those individuals who are not fit to run for office based on their track record of promoting or perpetrating violence in Kenya," Masengeli stated.

By leveraging electoral laws alongside the Criminal Procedure Code, security agencies aim to create a double jeopardy for sponsors: active court prosecution and formal exclusion from contestation before party primaries kick off.

### High-Stakes Escalation

The institutional push arrives following a series of violent disruptions at high-profile political events. The urgency was underscored by a September 12 incident in Embu during a rally for Deputy President Kithure Kindiki.

That clash resulted in one fatality, multiple injuries, and extensive property damage, including the destruction of six vehicles—some belonging to journalists covering the event. Police officers engaged armed attackers for hours before restoring order.

Similar disruptions across various regions have prompted regional security desks to treat political violence as organized economic crime rather than spontaneous public disturbance.

### What Comes Next

The immediate test of the new strategy rests on the speed and legal strength of the upcoming DCI summonses. Detectives are expected to present initial evidentiary dockets to the Director of Public Prosecutions (DPP) while simultaneously submitting candidate risk profiles to the IEBC ahead of the 2027 election clearance window.
