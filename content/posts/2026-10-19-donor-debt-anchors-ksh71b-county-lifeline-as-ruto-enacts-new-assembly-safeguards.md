External loans and bilateral grants will finance nearly three-quarters of the newly approved Ksh71.09 billion conditional funding package heading to Kenya's 47 counties, anchoring devolved capital projects heavily to donor balance sheets.

President William Ruto assented to the County Governments Additional Allocations Bill, 2026, on Monday at the Eldoret State Lodge, turning the Senate-passed financial framework into enforceable law for the 2026/27 financial year alongside sweeping protections against arbitrary purges within county assemblies.

While the headline figure unlocks critical financing for devolved units, the statutory breakdown reveals a sharp divergence between domestic exchequer commitments and foreign development liabilities.

### The Arithmetic Behind the Allocation

Out of the total Ksh71.09 billion unlocked under the new law, the national government's own share of revenue accounts for Ksh16.46 billion. The balance of Ksh53.82 billion—representing roughly 76 percent of the entire package—consists of loans and negotiated grants provided by multilateral and bilateral development partners.

![President William Ruto assents to two tax Bills, the Tax Laws Amendment Bill 2026 and the Kenya Revenue Authority Bill 2026, at the Eldoret State Lodge on October 16, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/TqYL8D4m/72648344880a.webp)

The legislation, introduced to Parliament by Mandera Senator Ali Roba, formally establishes the required statutory conduit to channel conditional transfers straight into county revenue funds. Without this annual legal instrument, counties are barred under public finance regulations from accessing partner-supported funding lines.

The capital is designated for ring-fenced sector interventions, with the conditional rules restricting drawdowns to health service delivery, agricultural programmes, affordable housing construction, urban infrastructure upgrades, and climate change mitigation projects. By keeping these transfers conditional, the law prevents governors from redirecting the donor-backed millions into recurrent operational overheads.

### Firewalls Around Assembly Staff

Alongside the multi-billion-shilling financing law, Ruto signed the County Assembly Services (Amendment) Bill, 2023, introducing tight procedural barriers designed to halt summary dismissals of top legislative staff across the counties.

County assemblies have frequently functioned as political battlegrounds where ward representatives and presiding speakers routinely suspended clerks or reconstituted administrative boards during local power struggles. The newly signed amendments strip local leadership of that summary authority.

Under the statutory procedure, any petition seeking the removal of a County Assembly Service Board member must now be lodged with the Clerk and formally transmitted to the assembly floor by the Speaker within seven days. The petition is then committed to an 11-member select committee, which is bound by a strict 10-day timeline to investigate the grounds.

The law formally guarantees targeted board members the right to legal counsel during the committee hearings, closing an administrative loophole previously used to conduct expedited ousters.

### Clerk Suspensions and Officer Protections

Similar statutory thresholds have been imposed on disciplinary actions directed at county assembly clerks. Appointing authorities are now legally required to furnish a clerk with detailed, written reasons prior to any proposed suspension.

The legislation institutes a mandatory seven-day window for the affected clerk to submit a formal response, followed by an oral disciplinary hearing before any punitive sanction can take effect.

Beyond boardroom personnel, the amendment shields career officers seconded from county assemblies to other public bodies. The law guarantees continuous protection of their basic pay, established allowances, and institutional benefits throughout their external assignments, while explicitly ring-fencing their right to return to substantive assembly posts once secondment tenures lapse.

With both statutes now enacted, the immediate focus shifts to the National Treasury, which must publish the disbursement schedule dictating how rapidly the Ksh53.82 billion in donor tranches and Ksh16.46 billion in state allocations move into county bank accounts.
