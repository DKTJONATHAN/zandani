The National Treasury has reopened talks with a major United States investment bank to engineer a Ksh129.7 billion debt swap, accelerating an aggressive move away from costly local borrowing as debt-servicing obligations approach record highs.

The transaction, first reported by Bloomberg, resurrects a stalled liability-management plan and is expected to target one of Kenya’s outstanding commercial Eurobonds. Under the arrangement, existing obligations would be retired and replaced with more favorable repayment terms, allowing fiscal managers to divert the resulting interest savings toward public spending priorities.

The proposed swap forms the cornerstone of a broader Ksh700 billion external financing basket laid out for the financial year ending June 2027. Rather than leaning on conventional dollar-denominated commercial debt alone, Treasury’s blueprint spreads exposure across non-traditional sovereign debt instruments. 

Alongside the Ksh129.7 billion swap, the pipeline allocates Ksh38.9 billion ($300 million) to a Chinese panda bond, Ksh64.8 billion ($500 million) to an Islamic sukuk, and Ksh105.7 billion ($815 million) to a benchmark Eurobond issue.

### Reworking the Maturity Wall

The pivot toward bespoke swaps reflects an institutional effort to loosen the grip of domestic debt maturities. High domestic interest rates have constrained the exchequer, forcing public debt managers to design alternative structures that lengthen repayment profiles without exhausting local bank liquidity.

Talks of structured debt swaps gathered momentum in early 2024, when Treasury confronted the maturity of a $2 billion Eurobond issued in 2014. While that hurdle was cleared through a mix of market buybacks and multilateral credit, the government subsequently formalized swaps within its broader debt-management policy.

By September 2025, fiscal authorities had embedded the framework directly into national planning documents, detailing negotiations for a $1 billion debt-for-food security transaction involving the World Food Programme (WFP). It remains unconfirmed whether the transaction currently being shaped by the US banking giant directly integrates the WFP structure, but financial planners are evaluating provisions that would earmark interest savings for state-backed food distribution initiatives.

### Mounting Repayment Obligations

The liability restructuring unfolds against an unyielding debt calendar. Official projections indicate that debt-servicing expenditures will demand roughly Ksh2.31 trillion in the 2026/2027 financial year alone, driven by heavy clusters of maturing domestic paper and interest on bilateral credit.

Neither the National Treasury nor the transaction advisers have publicly commented on the terms, coupon structures, or completion dates under review.

With annual service costs expected to hold above the Ksh2 trillion mark over the medium term, the progress of the Ksh129.7 billion swap will serve as an early test of whether international institutional desks are willing to offer Kenya viable interest concessions without steep structural covenants.
