England manager Thomas Tuchel drew attention with a number of major omissions when he named his 26-player squad for the 2026 FIFA World Cup on May 22, 2026. Among the most notable players left out were Manchester City forward Phil Foden and Chelsea attacker Cole Palmer.

The selection also did not include Real Madrid defender Trent Alexander-Arnold or Manchester United pair Harry Maguire and Luke Shaw. Maguire had already indicated that he would miss the tournament, describing his reaction to the decision as shock and disappointment.

### Tuchel puts the team ahead of individual reputation

Tuchel said his aim was to build the best team rather than simply collect the 26 most talented individuals. He stressed that players needed to understand their roles, commit to the group and contribute to the team's overall spirit.

The decision involving Foden and Palmer was particularly striking because both had previously been central figures in England's plans. Foden had earned major individual recognition in English football, while Palmer had made a significant impact at the 2024 European Championship, including scoring in the final against Spain.

Their club seasons, however, had been less consistent. Foden had found regular starts harder to secure at Manchester City, while Palmer had also experienced a difficult run of form. Tuchel's choices suggested that current readiness and tactical fit carried considerable weight in the final selection.

### Established names remain in the squad

England retained key attacking and midfield options including Harry Kane, Jude Bellingham, Bukayo Saka and Declan Rice. Ivan Toney was a notable inclusion after limited international appearances since moving to Saudi Arabia, while Kobbie Mainoo earned selection following a stronger second half of the season at Manchester United.

The squad also included Arsenal's Eberechi Eze and Noni Madueke, both heading to their first World Cup, and Marcus Rashford after rebuilding his form at Barcelona.

The omissions sparked debate over whether Tuchel had made a calculated move towards a more balanced group or left out players capable of changing a match. His stated position was clear: tournament success would depend on collective commitment rather than reputation alone.
