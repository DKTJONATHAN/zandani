Jeremiah Kioni has stepped up to the broadcast desk to deliver a blunt reality check to his fellow opposition figures: trading insults with President William Ruto is not landing blows, it is handing him free fuel.

Speaking in an interview on Radio Generation, the Jubilee Deputy Party Leader dismantled the opposition’s go-to podium playbook, warning that personal vitriol and performative grievances ahead of the 2027 General Election have completely backfired against an opponent who has built a career out of weaponising bad press.

For Kioni, the former Ndaragwa lawmaker, the current opposition appetite for fiery press conferences and social media shade misses the fundamental psychological armor of the man sitting in State House.

### The Currency of Bad Press

Rather than chipping away at the President’s standing, Kioni argued, the steady stream of abuse coming from podiums and microphones has simply reinforced Ruto's political insulation.

“There is no amount of insulting William that is going to help you win the votes,” Kioni stated during the radio sit-down. “He’s already immune and he has demonstrated it from day one… You must be very careful when you are dealing with him. Because it could be his currency. It could be his strategy to win against us.”

Kioni pointed directly to the way Ruto navigates the bruising world of Kenyan digital commentary. In an arena where politicians routinely rush to the Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) or file civil suits at the first hint of reputational damage, Kioni noted that Ruto has conspicuously bypassed the courts and the cells when dealing with his loudest online critics.

“Unlike many of us, he has never sued anybody for defamation,” Kioni remarked. “He’s never taken a blogger to the police station. You know the way bloggers are flogged to police station because they have inserted you in the social media. He’s never.”

That calculated restraint, Kioni insisted, should have served as an early warning to the United Opposition that crude takedowns do not rattle the Head of State—they feed his narrative of enduring hostility without blinking.

### Dropping the ‘Mole’ Melodrama

The former legislator took equal aim at the internal theatrics plaguing anti-government camps, particularly the habit of calling urgent press briefings simply to trade accusations of betrayal and point fingers at supposed fifth columnists.

Instead of pitching convincing alternatives to the public, Kioni argued, opposition leaders frequently reduce their platform appearances to gossipy witch-hunts, looking for internal scapegoats while doing zero spadework to recruit undecided voters.

“You will hear that the best news that somebody can give when he calls a press conference is to say so and so is a mole,” Kioni observed. “How does that help you get more votes? How does that advance your agenda of fighting President William Ruto?”

In Kioni’s assessment, the obsessive hunt for political turncoats and the endless parade of name-calling have crowded out substantive policy arguments. The result is a cycle of low-stakes theater that generates quick headlines but leaves ordinary Kenyans with little reason to switch allegiances.

### A Clean Break from Noise

Kioni’s candid intervention lands as the broader opposition coalition continues to wrestle with its identity, coalition pacts, and unified strategy for taking on the ruling Kenya Kwanza machinery in 2027.

Rather than running the same tired script of character assassination and press-briefing paranoia, Kioni challenged his colleagues to rethink their entire public posture before the window to convince voters firmly shuts.

“Can we, as leaders in the opposition, concentrate more on what are the issues that we should be saying and doing that will help us win the government?” Kioni asked. “And concentrate less on the issues that help the incumbent become more dominant.”

With the 2027 campaign cycle already gathering pace behind closed doors, Kioni’s warning leaves the opposition staring at an uncomfortable mirror: either swap cheap vitriol for concrete policy, or watch William Ruto ride their own insults all the way to a second term.
