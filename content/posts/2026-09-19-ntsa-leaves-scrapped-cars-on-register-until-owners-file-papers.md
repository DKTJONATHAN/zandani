The National Transport and Safety Authority (NTSA) has confirmed that it relies entirely on motorists to report wrecked or decommissioned cars before scrubbing them from state books, an administrative approach that leaves police chasing ghost records while offering a paperwork buffer to cross-border car-cloning syndicates.

The regulatory position, clarified by the agency on Friday, September 18, places the entire burden of database accuracy on the public. Despite automated crash tracking across police commands, NTSA maintains that written-off metal will remain active in the national register indefinitely unless an owner files for formal cancellation.

"This is done through the deregistration process, which is initiated by the owner," the authority stated in response to public inquiries over why crash write-offs remain listed as roadworthy assets.

### The Six-Million Blind Spot

![Kenyans lining up inside the National Transport and Safety Authority (NTSA) office in Nairobi in 2024.](https://i.ibb.co/vCymRLJS/ccfe73a1975b.webp)

The admission codifies an operational bottleneck that the agency’s leadership has acknowledged cripples basic transport planning. With Kenya’s motor registry holding more than 6 million files, the government cannot verify how many of those machines still run on tarmac.

NTSA Director General Nashon Kondiwa underscored the scale of the blind spot during an interview on NTV in June 2026, conceding that the historical lack of automated cleanups leaves state agencies managing a register disconnected from reality.

“We do not know the number of vehicles on our roads, because since we started registering vehicles, we do not deregister them; they die, they use them to make other metals,” Kondiwa noted.

That institutional gap was laid bare during a 2022 internal data-cleaning audit, which uncovered widespread inconsistencies and incomplete vehicle histories. Because owners facing salvage losses rarely prioritize state filing fees and bureaucratic clearances to scrap a wrecked shell, inactive profiles sit undisturbed in government servers for decades.

### Cover for Cloned Metal

While the voluntary reporting model spares NTSA the operational workload of field verification, the agency admits the lag directly aids motor vehicle smuggling networks along regional borders.

![NTSA officers assisting drivers with free motor vehicle inspection services.](https://i.ibb.co/whWfvGxS/fb89852e5399.webp)

Kondiwa revealed that syndicates capitalize on dormant registration entries by lifting plates and logbook profiles from vehicles that have ceased to exist. They then paste those active identities onto undocumented imports sourced from neighboring countries.

“What happens is that other people come with vehicles from neighboring countries, pick number plates from the damaged vehicles and put them on the unregistered vehicles,” Kondiwa said, detailing how ghost files become operational shields during highway traffic stops.

### Digital Safeguards

To plug vulnerabilities in vehicle identification, the authority deployed an electronic logbook system on June 10, designed to tie ownership data directly to real-time traffic policing, insurance records, and commercial verification.

Yet the digital overhaul remains tethered to the original structural weakness: the new eLogbook can only track active digital transactions, leaving millions of historical, untransferred wrecks preserved on paper until an owner steps forward to retire the metal.
