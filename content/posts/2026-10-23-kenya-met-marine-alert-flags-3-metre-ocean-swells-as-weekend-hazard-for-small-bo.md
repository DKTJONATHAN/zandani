Small-scale fishers and commercial boat operators along Kenya’s coastline face sharply narrowed safety margins this weekend, as the Kenya Meteorological Department projects Indian Ocean wave heights to surge up to three metres.

The department’s seven-day marine bulletin, released on Friday, October 23, warned that an intensification of surface winds will generate rough to very rough conditions across exposed nearshore and deep-sea waters through next week.

Wind velocities ranging between two and 12 metres per second are expected to dominate coastal waters, driven by shifting east-south-easterly to south-south-westerly air currents. The agency cautioned that the combination of accelerating winds and elevated swells presents direct navigational hazards to non-industrial maritime craft.

“Small boats and other vessels operating in exposed waters should take appropriate safety precautions and closely monitor official marine weather updates,” the advisory stated, noting that mariners and beachgoers must exercise extreme vigilance in areas encountering peak chop.

### Saturday Swell Peak

![An image showing strong winds along the Kenyan coast, forcing palm trees to bend.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/high-wind-warning-1000x500_0.jpg.webp?itok=dx4Rc_8y)

The marine data outlines a rapid escalation in offshore turbulence heading into the weekend.

On Friday, October 23, baseline sea swells were recorded between 0.3 and two metres. Forecasters expect conditions to deteriorate on Saturday, October 24, when wave heights are modelled to rise to between 0.5 and three metres—the highest projected threshold of the current cycle.

The rough conditions are scheduled to taper only slightly as the week progresses. Waves on Sunday, October 25, are projected to range from 0.5 to 2.5 metres, before settling into a corridor of 0.25 to two metres on Monday, October 26.

For the artisanal fishing sector, which relies predominantly on low-freeboard wooden dhows and open fiberglass skiffs lacking advanced navigation or stabilization gear, sustained waves above two metres routinely disrupt operations, elevate capsize risks near reef channels, and complicate harbor re-entry.

### Regional Basin Pressures

![Photo of Diani Beach in Mombasa County captured on October 2019](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/diani_beach.jpg.webp?itok=q1gY4-Bn)

The meteorological service indicated that the atmospheric disturbance extends well beyond Kenyan territorial waters. Satellite mapping attached to the marine advisory shows an expansive pressure system delivering comparable sea turbulence and high-velocity wind fields along the shorelines of southern Somalia and northern Tanzania.

Compounding the maritime instability, forecasters are actively monitoring a tropical storm system over the wider Indian Ocean basin.

While the agency stressed that any precipitation trigger for mainland Kenya remains contingent on the storm’s eventual track and landfall coordinates, the system's offshore circulation is already influencing prevailing regional wind vectors and surface chop.

The marine warning unfolds against a stark atmospheric divergence across the mainland. While coastal waters experience volatile sea states and escalating wind stress, extensive parts of the hinterland continue to record dry, sunny weather.

That contrast has defied earlier public expectations of an immediate onset of October El Niño-related rainfall, leaving agricultural communities waiting for sustained showers even as maritime sectors contend with dangerous water conditions.

With winds projected to fluctuate day to day across the seven-day forecast window, maritime safety authorities and county landing site committees along the coast are relying on daily marine bulletins to determine whether local sea-going clearances should be temporarily restricted.
