Existing family trusts holding land, commercial real estate, and corporate stock across Kenya face a strict two-year deadline to disclose their underlying control structures following the enactment of a new trust law.

Assented to by President William Ruto on September 8, the Trust Administration Act, 2026 establishes a consolidated legal framework for the creation, registration, incorporation, administration, and dissolution of trusts in Kenya. While the law provides families with a formalized mechanism to reduce succession disputes over high-value property, it simultaneously eliminates legal opacity by mandating full transparency over who controls and benefits from trust assets.

Under the statutory framework, trustees must maintain complete records of beneficial ownership and file them directly with the Registrar. These disclosures must identify the settlor, active trustees, designated beneficiaries, and any individuals exercising effective ultimate control over the trust.

For existing incorporated trusts, the legislation imposes a strict 24-month compliance window from its date of commencement to submit updated beneficial ownership registers to state authorities.

### Limits on Statutory Asset Protection

While trusts are widely used as holding vehicles to shield agricultural land, rental portfolios, and private enterprise shares from length probate disputes, legal protection under the new regime is not automatic.

The Act establishes that transferring property into a trust does not inherently insulate assets from legal claims, creditor enforcement, or succession challenges. Protection depends on how the trust was originally established, whether the asset transfer was legally executed, and how trustees carry out their daily administration duties.

When property is legitimately transferred to a trust prior to estate proceedings, it is managed according to the founder's explicit terms rather than falling into the deceased individual's personal estate.

### Governance Overhaul for Trustees

The mandatory disclosure provisions will require corporate trustees, family wealth managers, and legal representatives to carry out immediate administrative reviews. Trust officers will need to audit existing trust deeds, verify beneficiary rosters, and align governance arrangements before submitting documentation to the Registrar.

Failure to align existing structures with the new legal standards within the 24-month transitional period leaves incorporated trusts exposed to regulatory non-compliance, even as families seek to rely on them for long-term estate management.
