Kiharu Member of Parliament Ndindi Nyoro has launched a scathing critique of the national government, linking the persistent insecurity in Isiolo County to broader failures in administrative accountability. Speaking during a tour of Isiolo town, Nyoro condemned the recent attack in Gotu-which claimed the lives of two police officers, a National Police Reservist, and two civilians-as a definitive failure of the state to uphold its constitutional mandate to protect its citizens.

The lawmaker’s visit follows a wave of unrest in the region, where residents recently blocked sections of the Isiolo-Moyale Highway to protest the escalating violence. Local leaders, including Chari Ward MCA Mejja Golicha, underscored the economic impact of this instability, noting that the rampant theft of livestock has systematically impoverished pastoralist families who rely on cattle as their primary economic lifeline.

Beyond the immediate security concerns, Nyoro leveled serious allegations against elements within the government, claiming that high-ranking officials are complicit in the illegal cattle rustling trade. He argued that this internal involvement has fueled the cycle of banditry, leaving communities vulnerable while the government remains inactive. To support those affected by the Gotu tragedy, Nyoro donated 200,000 shillings to the victims' families.

Shifting his focus to national economic policy, Nyoro took aim at Treasury Cabinet Secretary John Mbadi regarding the government’s intent to divest its stake in Safaricom. He insisted that the National Treasury must respect the High Court ruling that nullified the proposed sale, urging the administration to abandon any plans to appeal the decision. Nyoro maintained that keeping these assets within the public domain is essential for maintaining economic stability and upholding the rule of law.

The Kiharu MP also addressed the political climate ahead of the 2027 General Election, calling on President William Ruto to cease political hostilities against retired President Uhuru Kenyatta. Nyoro argued that the administration should prioritize addressing the severe economic hardships facing Kenyans rather than engaging in feuds with the former head of state. He cautioned that the President’s political performance should be measured against current public discontent rather than past leadership.

Reflecting on the future of the political landscape, Nyoro indicated that opposition factions are working to consolidate their efforts into a united front. He promised that this coalition would present a realistic development agenda focused on managing Kenya’s debt burden and restoring national security.

The pressure on the government continues to mount as local leaders, including Governor Abdi Guyo, Senator Fatuma Dullo, Woman Representative Mumina Bonaya, and KANU chairman Gideon Moi, join the chorus of demands for urgent intervention. As the administration faces these dual challenges of regional insecurity and judicial pushback on asset liquidation, the demand for a more transparent and responsive governance model remains at the forefront of the national discourse.
