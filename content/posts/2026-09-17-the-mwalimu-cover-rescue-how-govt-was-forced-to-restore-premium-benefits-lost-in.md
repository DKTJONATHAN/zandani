The transition of Kenya's public school teachers from their long-running private medical cover to the state-run Social Health Authority (SHA) has hit an administrative reset. 

![Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale chairs a consultative meeting with teachers' union representatives and Social Health Authority officials on September 17, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/duale-teachers-september.jpg.webp?itok=FZtGqaLH)

Faced with mounting union pressure and widespread reports of educators being turned away from major hospitals, the Ministry of Health has agreed to restructure the Mwalimu Comprehensive Medical Cover to restore high-end services that disappeared when the state took over. 

The adjustments, brokered in a consultative meeting on Thursday between Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale, the Kenya Union of Post Primary Education Teachers (KUPPET), and the Teachers Service Commission (TSC), represent a significant concession. When the government migrated public servants to SHA, teachers lost the seamless access to specialized treatments they previously held under the privately managed Minet Kenya scheme.

The newly negotiated terms specifically target premium and specialized therapies that have been out of reach for teachers since the transition. Under the revised plan, SHA will now guarantee coverage for in vitro fertilization (IVF) services, oncology treatment, chronic disease management, monthly prescriptions, specialized care, and last-expense services—benefits that were standard under the old Minet cover but became highly contested under the new state architecture.

To address the immediate deficit in cancer care, Duale announced that SHA has contracted 122 haemato-oncology facilities out of its 7,031 active provider network. The ministry has also pledged to fast-track domestic capacity for highly specialized procedures, including bone marrow transplants, to reduce reliance on overseas referrals.

### The Friction of Centralization

This policy reversal highlights the friction of merging specialized public sector schemes into a standardized national health insurance framework. Since the rollout of SHA, teachers across the country have reported a sharp reduction in their net medical benefits. Many have been forced to pay cash for routine prescriptions or turn to expensive private facilities when SHA's electronic system failed to authenticate their premium eligibility.

Under the old Minet Kenya framework, teachers enjoyed a comprehensive tier of private care. The transition to SHA effectively downgraded their access, leading to systemic friction where premier private hospitals turned teachers away due to integration failures and unresolved administrative debts under the new public fund.

To curb administrative failures at the facility level, the joint committee agreed on a stricter oversight framework. The government will now generate monthly implementation reports to track how hospitals handle teachers' claims. , SHA has warned of prospective punitive actions against contracted private and public hospitals that refuse to honor the Mwalimu Comprehensive Medical Cover terms or turn patients away during emergencies.

### Documenting the New Terms

To simplify the transition for beneficiaries, the ministry is drafting a dedicated SHA benefits guide specifically for teachers. This document aims to clarify exactly what entitlements have been preserved from the old scheme and how they can be claimed under the Public Officers Medical Scheme Fund (POMSF).

Additionally, both parties have agreed to establish a rapid-response channel to prioritize medical incidents raised by union members, attempting to bypass the slow bureaucratic channels of the standard SHA claim system.

While the administrative corrections offer a roadmap to restore specialized care, the success of the intervention hinges on whether SHA can successfully resolve its system integration issues with large private hospitals, which have historically formed the backbone of teachers' preferred healthcare options.
