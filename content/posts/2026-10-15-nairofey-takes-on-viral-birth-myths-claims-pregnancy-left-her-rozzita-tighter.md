Content creator Nairofey has waded into digital discourse surrounding motherhood, taking direct aim at online narratives that depict natural childbirth as permanently altering a woman's body.

Taking to her Instagram Stories, the personality called out a specific video circulating online, which she argued propagated false assumptions about postpartum anatomy. Nairofey stated that her personal experience following delivery ran entirely counter to the claims being shared across social feeds.

“I saw a video that was misleading, and it’s crazy to think that there are women or men out there that assume women who give birth naturally, their ‘rozzita’ is as wide as a sufuria,” Nairofey told her followers, before offering her own assessment. “I don’t know about you, but after giving birth, my ‘rozzita’ actually became tighter than it was before.”

Elaborating on her stance, the creator pointed to the natural elasticity of human tissue, arguing that the body possesses an innate capability to recover after stretching during labor.

“Your vagina is elastic. It has the ability to expand and contract, and your body knows what to do once you push the baby. Trust me, it goes back,” she noted, adding that recovery outcomes can vary depending on individual birth experiences.

Nairofey’s remarks arrive as public figures, including content creators like Dem Wa Facebook and media personality Risper Faith, increasingly share personal details regarding their experiences with delivery, including decisions surrounding Caesarean sections. These disclosures have frequently reignited online conversations touching on intimate physical changes and how they influence modern choices around childbirth.

While personal accounts vary widely across social media, medical consensus notes that postpartum healing depends on multiple factors—including hormonal shifts, tissue elasticity, and potential complications during delivery. Healthcare professionals frequently recommend professional guidance, such as pelvic floor physiotherapy, for individuals managing postpartum discomfort or muscle weakness following childbirth.
