When inDrive founder and CEO Arsen Tomsky stepped up at MIP Africa in Cape Town to announce the fourth edition of the Alternativa Film Festival, the headline figure was bound to turn heads: $120,000 (approximately KSh 15.5 million) in prize money, with dedicated categories for African directors.

Yet behind the cash injection lies a calculated push away from standard festival red carpets and toward the unglamorous plumbing of international film distribution. For Tomsky, throwing money at awards misses the point if independent creators across the Global South cannot get their work past Western gatekeepers.

"Exceptional filmmaking can come from anywhere, but access to international audiences is still hindered by a deeply unequal system," Tomsky said, framing the initiative as a direct challenge to industry imbalances.

![Lost Land - Nativa Award - AFF 2026](https://i.ibb.co/ZRx0D1L2/80a3d3aec38d.webp)

To tackle that bottleneck, the festival's organizers are leaning heavily on Alternativa Distribution, a non-profit model rolled out earlier in the year. Unlike traditional sales agents who take projects on commission after production debt has already choked a film's momentum, this distribution arm offers filmmakers an upfront minimum guarantee for non-theatrical rights. It also covers the heavy lifting of subtitling and dubbing, clawing back costs only after distribution begins, with remaining revenues reinvested into local industries.

This distribution-first mindset dictates how films are vetted. The festival's catalogue—featuring past selections like Nepal's *Elephants in the Fog*, Malaysia's *Lost Land*, Afghanistan's *No Good Men*, and Tunisia's *Four Daughters*—serves as a pipeline for the distribution network, meaning films submitted for the 2027 competition carry the bonus potential of securing international platform placement.

On the ground, the 2027 edition will structure its KSh 15.5 million kitty across seven award categories, carving out three exclusive slots for African storytellers. These include a $20,000 Focus Award and two $10,000 Short Film Awards, while African directors remain eligible to compete for the remaining open categories. , directors whose features make the official competition will have their travel and accommodation expenses fully covered to attend the awards ceremony.

![Arsen Tomsky, Founder and CEO of inDrive](https://i.ibb.co/r2FXtXcb/20341d6e4c9c.webp)

Festival head Liza Surganova noted that the move to Africa capitalizes on the momentum of previous stops. The festival operates as a nomadic event, moving from Almaty, Kazakhstan in 2023 to Yogyakarta, Indonesia in 2024, before hitting a peak turnout of 11,000 attendees across 1,831 submissions from 55 countries in Medellín, Colombia earlier in 2026.

That nomadic trail has already started yielding practical cross-border collaborations. Malaysian producer Elise Shick, whose film *Lost Land* picked up the Nativa Award in Medellín, revealed that relationships forged during the festival have sparked a nine-country co-production project currently eyeing a premiere at the upcoming African edition.

Submissions for the 2027 festival are open for fiction, documentary, animation, and hybrid projects completed after January 1, 2026. While feature films are open to creators across Africa, Asia, and Latin America, short film submissions are strictly reserved for African filmmakers.

Creators have until March 1, 2027, to submit their projects free of charge via the official Alternativa Film Festival website, ahead of the festival's late 2027 rollout in an African host city yet to be named.
