A push by the Ministry of Industry for local millers to manufacture strawberry- and vanilla-flavoured ugali for overseas export has met an immediate operational reality check: processors say Kenya’s state grain silos are empty while the country prepares to import 25 million bags of maize to avert a domestic shortfall.

The policy proposal, delivered by Industry Principal Secretary Juma Mukwana during the Kenya Millers Fortification Index Awards in Nairobi on Saturday, October 31, challenged processors to break away from traditional unflavoured flour. Mukwana argued that creating standalone, flavoured variants is the only pathway for Kenya’s primary staple to cross oceans and attract non-traditional consumer bases abroad, while preparing for a domestic population projected to reach 70 million by 2050.

Yet for the processors tasked with executing this commercial pivot, the immediate hurdle is not consumer palate research, but raw grain availability.

Responding directly to the state challenge at the same forum, Cereals Millers Association of Kenya Chairman Beju Shah pointed out that while manufacturers remain open to commercial experimentation, the state has failed to maintain the buffer stocks required to stabilise daily milling operations.

![Industry Principal Secretary Juma Mukwana during a past event.](https://i.ibb.co/60QX5br5/01b10aafd239.webp)

Shah turned the policy spotlight back onto the National Cereals and Produce Board (NCPB), disclosing that millers currently have no domestic backstop to draw from as local supplies tighten.

“National Cereal and Produce Board should store enough maize so that when there is a shortage we can buy from the depots, and like now they don't have,” Shah stated, linking industry innovation to baseline food security.

The millers' warning lands against the backdrop of an acute production deficit triggered by poor domestic harvests. With local farms unable to meet daily household consumption, the government has been forced to look across borders, preparing import pipelines for up to 25 million 90-kilogram bags from Tanzania and wider regional markets to plug the structural shortfall.

Mukwana’s thesis assumes that value addition can decouple the milling sector from standard household meal tables. Addressing factory heads, he argued that shifting demographics require aggressive product engineering.

![Supermarket attendants restocking maize flour on their shelves.](https://i.ibb.co/FLQTDSMF/24fbe837d291.webp)

“As we navigate this landscape, we have to find out, because you are the people who are feeding; how are you going to feed 70 million in 2050?” Mukwana said. “We want ugali that is standalone. Whether it's strawberry, whether it's vanilla, maybe that is the only way you will get this ugali to cross the oceans to other places.”

While fortified and flavoured flours exist nichely in global health food and breakfast segments, Kenyan commercial millers remain almost entirely configured around high-volume, thin-margin white maize processing for unflavoured meal.

Operating in a market where basic unshifted and sifted meal prices dictate household cost-of-living indices, industry players contend that investing capital expenditure into offshore consumer testing remains unviable while primary raw materials must be brought in by truck from neighbouring territories.

The immediate contest for millers over the coming quarters will not be testing offshore consumer receptivity to sweetened maize cakes, but securing freight allocations and regional clearance for millions of bags of regular grain before domestic wholesale prices react to dwindling local harvests.
