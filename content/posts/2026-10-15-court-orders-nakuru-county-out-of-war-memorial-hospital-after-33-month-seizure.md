The Environment and Land Court in Nyandarua has ordered the Nakuru County Government to immediately vacate Nakuru War Memorial Hospital and pull its security personnel from the compound, formally dismantling a county-led administrative takeover that paralyzed the medical facility for nearly three years.

In a definitive ruling on the prolonged property conflict, the court affirmed that the private healthcare provider holds lawful ownership of the prime 25-acre parcel in Nakuru’s upmarket Milimani Estate. The bench explicitly barred county authorities from trespassing on the tract or interfering in any manner with the hospital's administration, effectively restoring the property to its private trustees.

### The Anatomy of an Executive Seizure

The judicial rebuke terminates an enforcement standoff that began on January 23, 2024, when devolved authorities raided and seized control of the institution. County officials had alleged that the hospital’s management secured an unlawful 50-year lease extension over the land, which has housed the facility across multiple generations.

![A photo of kanjos and residents standing outside the War Memorial Hospital in Nakuru County](https://i.ibb.co/Q75h6fT9/7f7a77d4fdf5.webp)

Rather than resolving the tenure question through administrative dialogue while keeping medical operations insulated, county enforcement officers took physical control of the grounds. That intervention brought routine clinical operations to a halt, cutting off specialized healthcare access for thousands of Nakuru residents who relied on the institution to ease pressure on overstretched public referral facilities.

Management moved to the Environment and Land Court seeking protection against state eviction, petitioning the bench to affirm the legitimacy of its title deeds and restrain executive officers from meddling with daily hospital functions.

### Registry Maneuvers Crumble Under Scrutiny

Throughout the trial, the county administration mounted an aggressive defense aimed at nullifying the facility’s land registration. County legal teams argued that the underlying title was tainted by procedural defects and warranted outright revocation.

The state’s justification hinged heavily on administrative decisions made within the lands registry. In January 2026, former Nakuru Land Registrar Eric Nyamu testified before the bench that he had unilaterally cancelled the hospital’s lease title after uncovering an existing commercial bank charge on the property folio.

That registry intervention received formal backing from the national government in July, when Senior State Counsel Prisca Adomeyon, representing the Attorney General’s office, maintained that the former registrar acted in good faith despite procedural contentions surrounding the 50-year lease extension. 

![Nakuru Governor Susan Kihika, during an empowerment programme in Nakuru County on August 3, 2025.](https://i.ibb.co/4RrckB0f/a42c1830322f.webp)

By ruling in favor of the hospital, the Environment and Land Court discarded those arguments, confirming that the grounds cited by devolved authorities and the land registry did not legally extinguish the hospital’s proprietary claim over the Milimani land.

### The Cost of a Shuttered Ward

The verdict leaves devolved authorities facing the logistical and legal imperative of immediate compliance, while shining an uncomfortable light on the steep public cost of executive overreach. 

For 33 months, wards, surgical suites, and specialized clinics at the Milimani property remained deserted under armed guard. What the county initially framed as a decisive recovery of public assets evolved into a prolonged judicial loss that suppressed private healthcare capital and stripped the regional health network of a core partner.

With the land reverting entirely to the hospital's trustees, attention shifts to whether county security detachments will withdraw without procedural friction, and how swiftly the facility can rehabilitate infrastructure left dormant since the January 2024 raid.
