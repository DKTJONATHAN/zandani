Hassan Joho, the flamboyant former Mombasa Governor once known for his high-octane verbal wars against William Ruto, has officially rewritten his political playbook. Standing in Taita Taveta County, the newly minted Cabinet Secretary for Mining and Blue Economy traded his historic rebel persona for the language of a transactional alliance, declaring that the Coast’s relationship with the presidency is now based on a strict currency of mutual exchange.

"Because you have respected us, we will respect you in return; because you have lifted us up, we will lift you up," Joho said, addressing the President directly during a public address in Mwatate.

The declaration marks a dramatic brand shift for Joho, whose political identity was largely forged through defying the national executive. Now serving as an executive insider in Ruto’s broad-based cabinet, the "Sultan" of Coast politics is framing regional development not as a political favor, but as a reciprocal deal that has successfully dismantled decades of marginalization narratives.

To back his pitch, Joho pointed to a highly specific, geographic comparison of development projects to show that the Coast is no longer being left behind in favor of traditional political strongholds. 

"The Coast is enlightened today," Joho argued, mapping out the administration's nationwide infrastructure footprint. "We see that when you build a stadium in Elgeyo Marakwet, you also build one in Voi, and when you construct a market in Kisumu, you build one in Kilifi."

By pairing a stadium in the President's Rift Valley backyard with one in Voi, and a market in western Kenya with one in Kilifi, Joho sought to validate his transition from a key opposition figurehead into an executive insider. He insisted that this visible parity has restored the self-worth of a region that historically felt excluded from the national cake.

"Mr. President, you have honored the people of the Coast region, raised our dignity, and restored our self-worth," Joho added, speaking with the same theatrical confidence that once made him a dominant voice in the Orange Democratic Movement (ODM).

The former governor argued that this mutual recognition has fundamentally changed how Coast residents perceive the national government. Rather than viewing the state through a lens of neglect, Joho asserted that the region now sees itself as an equal partner in the country's development agenda, setting a new tone for how local leaders negotiate with State House.

Beyond national projects, the Cabinet Secretary focused heavily on local dynamics, assuring the President of unified political backing in the very areas where opposition politics once ran deep. He explicitly pledged that the residents of Mwatate and the wider Taita Taveta County remain firmly aligned with Ruto's leadership.

"I want to assure you that here in Taita Taveta and across Mwatate, our community is fully united behind your leadership," Joho added.

As politicians across the country begin quiet alignment maneuvers ahead of the 2027 General Election, Joho's calculated rhetoric in Taita Taveta serves as a clear signal of his personal and regional political realignment. By framing infrastructure projects as a direct exchange for regional respect and political loyalty, the former governor is betting his political capital on a partnership that was once deemed highly unlikely.
