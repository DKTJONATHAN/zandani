Motorists and cross-border hauliers navigating Kenya’s busiest freight corridor face a dual operational timeline over the next six months, following a government push to bring forward the completion of the 175-kilometre Rironi–Mau Summit Highway to April 2027.

While the presidency trimmed two months off the headline June 2027 commissioning target on Sunday, the immediate physical test of the project arrives much earlier: an October 30, 2026 deadline to finish dualling the notorious escarpment stretch between Rironi and Naivasha.

Speaking during a church service at St. Mark’s Catholic Church in Likuyani Constituency, Kakamega County, on Sunday, October 11, President William Ruto positioned the revised April 2027 deadline as the operational handover for Phase One of the Northern Corridor upgrade, directly tying its handover to the rollout of the next cross-border stretch into Western Kenya.

“Phase One will reach Mau Summit. I will open the road in April 2027. That is when we will begin the construction of the Mau Summit–Malaba highway,” Ruto told the congregation, confirming that civil works along the 175-kilometre route would alternate between four and six lanes depending on terrain and traffic density.

### Unblocking the Escarpment Bottleneck

Before the wider trunk road reaches Mau Summit, the Ministry of Roads and Transport has anchored its construction schedule to opening partial sections to live traffic before the close of 2026.

According to Roads and Transport Cabinet Secretary Davis Chirchir, contractor resources have been concentrated on eliminating the chronic gridlocks that define the initial climb out of the capital.

“By October 30 this year, from Rironi to Naivasha, we will have completed the dualling, and people will be able to pass smoothly. There will be no more traffic on that route,” Chirchir stated.

Once the Rironi–Naivasha section is fully dualled, engineering teams are scheduled to push the expansion outward through Gilgil, cutting toward Nakuru City and terminating at Mau Summit. 

### Lane Capacity and the Revenue Model

The decision to widen the corridor into variable four-lane and six-lane sections serves an explicit fiscal design alongside traffic relief. Ruto noted that the expanded capacity is calculated to ease transit times while generating state revenue, pointing to planned commercial operations along the artery that links the Port of Mombasa to Uganda, Rwanda, and eastern Democratic Republic of Congo.

Managing the sheer freight volume passing between the coastal port and landlocked hinterlands has historically tested Kenya's single-carriageway bottlenecks. The state is banking on the expanded lanes to create a high-capacity tolling footprint capable of supporting subsequent infrastructure borrowing.

### AIIB Grant Secures Western Extension

The April 2027 deadline operates as the formal trigger for the Northern Corridor’s western leg. The national government has already secured preparatory grant financing from the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB) to conduct preliminary technical studies for the second phase between Mau Summit and the Malaba border post.

According to Ruto, the multilateral grant covers feasibility work required to take the highway past Eldoret and straight into the border hubs connecting Kenya to Uganda and the wider Great Lakes trade ecosystem.

With preliminary studies underway for the western corridor, the transport ministry’s immediate accountability milestone rests on the tarmac between Rironi and Naivasha, where road users will judge the state's compressed timelines on October 30.
