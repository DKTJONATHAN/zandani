Esther Arunga has closed the bureaucratic chapter on the surname that irrevocably upended her public life. Sixteen years after trading her status as Kenya’s premier television news anchor for a tumultuous marriage to Quincy Timberlake, the former screen personality has quietly signed away his name at the Registry of Documents in Nairobi.

The administrative clean break was finalized through Gazette Notice No. 16203, published on October 9, 2026. The instrument—drafted by Nairobi law firm LAK Advocates—records a deed poll executed on October 2, 2026, wherein she formally detached herself from the hyphenated and marital moniker she carried across international borders and high-profile criminal trials.

Under the terms of the notice, Arunga took the formal step to discard every legal iteration of her married identity.

“Esther Adongo Arunga… formerly known as Esther Adongo Timberlake, formally and absolutely renounced and abandoned the use of her former name Esther Adongo Timberlake and in lieu thereof assumed and adopted the name Esther Adongo Arunga, for all purposes,” the notice states.

### The Split After Sixteen Years

The move marks the end of a relationship that began under intense media glare in March 2010. At the peak of her media career, Arunga’s sudden union with Timberlake stunned Kenyan viewers and set off a chain of events that saw the couple abandon the local broadcast landscape altogether.

The pair subsequently relocated to Australia, slipping away from the local celebrity circuit only to reemerge years later at the center of an unthinkable family catastrophe. In June 2014, their three-year-old son, Sinclair Timberlake, died at the couple's home in Kallangur, north of Brisbane.

The tragedy drew Australian police into the household and launched an agonizing multi-year judicial saga that played out across Brisbane courtrooms.

In 2019, Arunga faced the bench and pleaded guilty to being an accessory after the fact to manslaughter, acknowledging that she had initially provided misleading information to investigators regarding the circumstances surrounding her son's fatal injuries. The court handed her a 10-month custodial sentence, which she served entirely on parole.

### Timberlake in Detention, Arunga on Paper

Her estranged husband faced heavier criminal scrutiny. In September 2023, Quincy Timberlake entered a guilty plea to manslaughter over the child's death, drawing an 11-year prison sentence.

By mid-2026, Timberlake completed the custodial portion of his Australian prison term. Instead of securing freedom, he was transferred straight into Australian immigration detention, where he remains held.

While Timberlake remains tied down by immigration authorities abroad, Arunga’s deed poll signals an administrative effort to rebuild an identity untethered to his legal estate. By reverting to Esther Adongo Arunga, she restores the name that first established her reputation in Kenyan newsrooms before the dramatic controversies of 2010 took hold.

Intriguingly, the Gazette filing anchors Arunga’s legal representation to a domestic address—specifically listing P.O. Box 22568-00100 in Nairobi. The filing does not disclose whether Arunga has physically returned to Kenyan soil or remains overseas while instructing counsel remotely.

What the gazetted record does confirm is that as far as the Kenyan state and the Registry of Documents are concerned, the Timberlake chapter has been formally excised from her papers.
