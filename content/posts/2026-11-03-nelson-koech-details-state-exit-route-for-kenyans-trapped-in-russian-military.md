Belgut MP Nelson Koech has spotlighted a significant security and diplomatic intervention, commending President William Ruto for stepping in to halt the recruitment of young Kenyans into the Russian armed forces following reports of casualties and captures along the Ukrainian battlefield.

Speaking on the growing human toll linked to foreign military enlisting, the lawmaker detailed how recruitment schemes had brought grief to households across the country, leaving some families mourning slain relatives while others struggle with the knowledge that their family members are currently held as prisoners of war in Ukraine.

“One thing that we must laud the President for doing is the stoppage of enlisting young Kenyans in the Russian military, where people have died, families are mourning and some Kenyans have become prisoners of war in Ukraine,” Koech stated.

Beyond stopping fresh recruitment, Koech revealed that state action has extended to building a formal retrieval pipeline for citizens who had already been dispatched to foreign military ranks or active war corridors.

According to the legislator, the government set up a dedicated mechanism designed to handle foreign contacts, facilitate safe travel routes, and secure the repatriation of affected Kenyans to bring them back home to their families.

“There was also a mechanism established to have Kenyans already recruited into the Russian military sent back home safely and reunited with their families,” Koech added.

The lawmaker's remarks highlight the urgent state response triggered by shadow recruitment channels targeting Kenyan youth for foreign combat contracts. By combining an immediate ban on active recruitment drives with a specialized diplomatic return pathway, the administration aims to cut off foreign military hiring networks while working to secure the return of surviving recruits and captured personnel.
