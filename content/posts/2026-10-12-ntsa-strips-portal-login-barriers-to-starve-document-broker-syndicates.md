The National Transport and Safety Authority has stripped user registration requirements from its document verification system, opening direct online tracking for uncollected driving licences, vehicle logbooks, and reflective plates in a bid to dismantle the intermediary syndicates operating outside its service centres.

Under the public rollout announced on Monday, October 12, motorists are no longer required to create or navigate password-protected portal accounts to verify if their statutory documents have been processed. The open lookup architecture requires only a national identity card number to audit smart driving licence status, while motor vehicle and motorcycle registration numbers instantly pull up processing records for logbooks and newly minted reflective plates.

By publishing collection readiness directly to an open database, the transport regulator is targeting the primary leverage point used by informal fixers: the informational vacuum that forces motorists to make speculative trips to regional centres or pay illicit facilitation fees to confirm document progress.

### Targeting the Middleman Economy

For years, backlogs in the transition to third-generation reflective number plates and digital driving permits created a thriving underground market around major licensing hubs. Middlemen frequently charge vehicle owners fees ranging from several hundred to thousands of shillings under the guise of tracking applications, locating physical files, or expediting counter retrieval.

![Kenyans lining up inside the National Transport and Safety Authority (NTSA) office in Nairobi in 2024.](https://i.ibb.co/W4DyM8d8/bbe964d04c50.webp)

NTSA explicitly addressed the dynamic on Monday, cautioning the motoring public that status verification carries no statutory fee and requires no third-party intervention.

“Checking the status of your smart driving licence and number plates is free; do not deal with brokers,” the authority said in its directive.

Beyond verifying whether a document has cleared the production line, the platform matches the verified record to the specific collection point where the physical card or metal plate is held. The routing is designed to eliminate speculative counter visits across different regional offices, ensuring applicants travel only to stations holding their verified consignments.

### Digital Dispute Loop

To manage historical delays that previously pushed motorists into the hands of station fixers, the system integrates a public grievance logging module. Applicants experiencing unexplained delays or data discrepancies can submit formal dispute tickets directly through the open portal.

Under this loop, resolution updates bypass physical desks entirely, routing automated settlement notices to the specific email address entered during ticket submission.

![A photo collage of a driving licence (left) and a reflective number plate.](https://i.ibb.co/0Vrvvpsk/f900290389d4.webp)

“Feedback to clients upon resolution of complaints or concerns is sent through the email address provided by the client at the point of submission via the portal,” the authority noted.

### Accelerated Counter Timelines

The zero-barrier lookup tool forms part of a wider administrative overhaul rolled out over the past week aimed at clearing physical footfall at licensing bureaus. 

On Thursday, October 8, the authority announced a compressed frontline counter model designed to issue ready driving licences in under two minutes upon a client's arrival at designated collection desks. That efficiency target relied on applicants presenting themselves only after their records cleared central processing—a precondition the new public lookup tool is built to operationalise.

Simultaneously, the agency has deployed real-time SMS tracking across vehicle ownership transfer pipelines. The automated text alerts ping registered applicant phone numbers at each sequential phase of transfer approval, closing the information gap between buyer submission and logbook generation.

Whether the removal of portal logins will successfully clear historical piles of uncollected metal plates and smart chips now rests on public adoption. While motorists can pinpoint their documents in seconds from a smartphone, the physical burden of collection still requires thousands of vehicle owners to physically present themselves at county centres that have spent months managing severe administrative backlogs.
