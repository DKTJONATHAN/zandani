A heavily congested September itinerary has forced the National Treasury into a tight operational corridor, leaving lawmakers and tax officials with a compressed window to clear mandatory public participation before submitting proposed Pay As You Earn (PAYE) revisions to Parliament.

Treasury Cabinet Secretary John Mbadi disclosed that public vetting on the Bill seeking to overhaul tax bands will kick off during the first week of October. The delay stems from a packed diplomatic and travel calendar through September, which left insufficient breathing room to clear statutory requirements without risking legal challenges in court.

![National Treasury Building](https://i.ibb.co/21pHWyV4/eed0484dac17.webp)

“I will do the public participation first,” Mbadi stated. “Kenya has become what it is because of the Constitution. If I bring them without public participation, someone might go to court to stop it.”

The proposed framework aims to alter relief structures for low- and middle-income earners. Under the current proposals, employees drawing up to Ksh30,000 monthly would be exempted entirely from PAYE, while those earning between Ksh30,000 and Ksh50,000 would face a reduced rate of 25 per cent.

To absorb these reforms alongside broader macroeconomic policy, the Treasury has mapped out a seven-day public engagement programme. Treasury Principal Secretary Bonface Makokha announced that the finance and economic week will run from October 12 to October 18, coinciding with national Mashujaa Day celebrations.

![Treasury Principal Secretary Boniface Makokha during senior management preparatory meeting at the Treasury building on Thursday, September 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/DPgPTXk1/589d5fea50e7.webp)

Speaking after a senior management preparatory session at the Treasury Building, Makokha noted that the program will run in tandem with the Ministry of Interior and host county authorities. Beyond payroll adjustments, the seven-day forum is slated to cover revenue mobilisation, public debt management, pension frameworks, and private capital inclusion under the Bottom-Up Economic Transformation Agenda.

With public forums scheduled to open in early October, the upcoming weeks will test whether the Treasury can balance mandatory constitutional compliance against its fast-tracked legislative timetable before the proposed tax brackets reach the floor of the House.
