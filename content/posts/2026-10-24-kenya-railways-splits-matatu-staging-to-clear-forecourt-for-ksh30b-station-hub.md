Kenya Railways has begun decoupling matatu parking from active passenger discharge around Nairobi Central Railway Station, shifting vehicle holding operations to Green Park terminus to clear the station apron for construction crews.

The operational shift compresses live public service vehicle access at the front of the station down to two designated running lanes and eight passenger drop-off points clustered around the Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) station perimeter. Matatus that have long parked and waited for passengers directly on the station’s forecourt will no longer be permitted to dwell there.

Instead, Green Park will act as a holding reservoir, absorbing idle vehicles while only active drop-offs are channelled through the restricted station approach.

### Clearing the Forecourt

![The revamped Green Park Terminus in Nairobi CBD.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/the-green-park-terminus.jpg.webp?itok=LXt5jqmR)

The reorganisation follows a Friday planning session convened by Nairobi Railway City Project Manager Milly Kiziili, who briefed matatu operators on the operational boundaries required to hand the site over to contractors.

The core challenge facing Kenya Railways is securing physical site possession without triggering gridlock across the southern rim of the Central Business District. The agency confirmed that while curbside activity will be strictly streamlined, commuter continuity remains mandatory during the transition.

By segregating vehicle holding from passenger discharge, the corporation intends to empty the central station frontage so groundwork can begin. The cleared zone is slated to become the primary interchange anchor for the broader 438-acre Nairobi Railway City, bringing Meter Gauge Rail, the Standard Gauge Railway link, BRT infrastructure, and dedicated non-motorised transit corridors into a unified hub.

### Safety Works on the Ground

To keep the narrower two-lane transit corridor functioning safely through construction phases, Kenya Railways outlined immediate civil adjustments on the access routes.

![An artist's impression of the proposed Nairobi Railways City](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/New%20railway%20city.jpg.webp?itok=5TPY0F8g)

The works include resurfacing access roads, applying high-visibility lane markings, and putting up protective pedestrian barriers to keep foot traffic separated from turning matatus. Stormwater drainage along the BRT approach will also be retrofitted to prevent seasonal pooling from choking the eight drop-off bays, alongside floodlight installations to maintain round-the-clock visibility for early-morning and late-evening commuters.

### The Ksh30B Redevelopment Scope

The site handover clears the path for the Ksh30 billion Nairobi Railway City development, a major capital undertaking financed entirely by the United Kingdom government through a bilateral Public-Private Partnership framework.

The wider scheme covers 438 acres of prime railway land stretching across the southern edge of Nairobi's commercial centre, with the historic station forecourt serving as the development's primary multi-modal nerve centre.

With the initial operator briefings concluded, enforcement of the eight-point drop-off rule and the redirection of holding fleets into Green Park mark the first physical steps toward contractor mobilisation on the terminal grounds.
