The Kenya Ports Authority (KPA) has launched an investigation into its safety and incident management procedures following an operational accident involving a gantry crane at the Port of Mombasa.

The incident, which occurred at approximately 3:40 p.m. on Thursday, September 17, 2026, involved a Rubber-Tyred Gantry (RTG) crane and prompted an immediate response from the port's emergency teams.

While the operator of the truck tractor involved was not injured, the RTG crane operator sustained minor soft-tissue injuries to his legs. He was taken to a hospital for medical assessment and has since been discharged.

KPA issued a clarification after video footage began circulating on social media showing several stacked shipping containers that had toppled over at the terminal, with port personnel gathering around the scene. The authority's official statement did not detail the exact sequence of events that led to the collapse.

"KPA confirms that an operational incident involving an RTG occurred and was immediately attended to by the Authority’s safety and emergency response teams in accordance with established procedures," the statement read.

Port officials urged the public to refrain from sharing unverified reports about the accident to prevent unnecessary alarm, directing stakeholders to official KPA communication channels for updates.

The ongoing investigation aims to determine the cause of the accident. KPA stated that a review of its safety and incident management procedures is underway, and any necessary corrective measures will be implemented once the findings are finalized.

The accident follows a fatal incident at the port months earlier, when a cargo rigger died at Berth 14 after heavy pipes rolled over him during offloading operations from the vessel MV Hin Hong Bao Shi.
