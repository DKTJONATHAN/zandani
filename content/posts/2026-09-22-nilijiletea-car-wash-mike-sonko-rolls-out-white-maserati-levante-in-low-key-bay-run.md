Mike Sonko has never believed in keeping high-end machinery behind tinted compound gates. True to form, the former Nairobi governor took an Italian performance badge straight to an ordinary washing bay, leaning against a pristine white Maserati Levante SUV to casually narrate his own addiction to horsepower.

“Sijui ni kwanini I’m in love with cars hivi,” Sonko remarked in a video clip shared across his official social media channels. “Hii ndio Maseratti nilijiletea car wash manenos.”

Filmed on September 18, the short clip captured the former county boss treating a vehicle that sits among Modena’s premier SUV exports like an everyday neighborhood runabout. The white Levante marks an intriguing pivot in Sonko’s established motoring taste, trading the aggressive, boxy silhouettes of his customary heavy-duty cruisers for the sleek, low-slung lines of high-end Italian engineering.

### The Anatomy of an Unapologetic Garage

For close to two decades, Sonko’s motor vehicles have functioned as an extension of his public brand—loud, heavily customized, and deployed specifically to draw eyes. 

While the Levante introduces Italian flair to the mix, it joins a garage historically dominated by oversized American muscle and Japanese off-road stalwarts. In July 2019, Sonko created a spectacle at Parliament Buildings when he arrived for a Senate appearance flanked by twin 2020 Cadillac Escalades—one finished in black, the other in white. Both full-size American SUVs carried massive 6.2-litre V8 powertrains matched to 10-speed automatic gearboxes, with base market valuations averaging KSh8 million per unit before bespoke options.

Alongside the Escalades sits a 2018 Lexus LX570, a heavy-duty Japanese luxury flagship powered by a 5.7-litre V8 engine that pumps out 383 horsepower. Built with 8.9 inches of ground clearance and five distinct terrain modes, the LX570 is engineered for harsh landscapes, commanding upwards of KSh15 million depending on options.

His fleet has also featured his heavily photographed Toyota Land Cruiser 200 Series models, distinguished on city streets by their custom gold wrap finishes. The 2.7-tonne Land Cruisers, driven by 4.5-litre V8 engines, carried valuations near KSh14 million for late-cycle production years, with specialized paintwork and wraps alone reportedly costing upwards of KSh200,000 to apply.

### From Convertibles to Heavy Utility

The Maserati’s arrival adds another distinct leisure cruiser to a stable that has rotated through multiple styling eras. For open-air city drives, Sonko previously showcased a 2018 Mercedes-Benz E-Class Cabriolet. Powered by a 3.0-litre bi-turbo V6 engine capable of 329 horsepower and routed through a nine-speed automatic transmission, the cabriolet’s soft-top roof operates at the tap of a button, typically carrying an entry price point around KSh6.7 million.

His garage has also housed a 2018 Nissan Patrol boasting a 5.6-litre V8 engine, rolling on 18-inch alloys and fitted with headrest-mounted media monitors for backseat passengers. Over the years, the lineup has expanded to include Mercedes-Benz G-Wagons, a Porsche Cayenne, an all-electric BMW i7 executive sedan, an imposing Hummer H2, a Toyota Crown Athlete, and various iterations of the Range Rover Sport and Vogue.

Sonko’s tendency to treat his vehicular paperwork as public theater is well established. In May 2019, during a live television studio appearance, he hauled out a stack of more than 100 motor vehicle logbooks to refute claims surrounding his personal assets. At the time, he stated that the bulk of those registered units had been acquired for and assigned to the Sonko Rescue Team, his non-profit philanthropic outfit operating ambulances, hearses, and utility vans across Nairobi’s informal settlements.

By pulling a Maserati Levante into a public bay and filming the soap suds himself, Sonko offered a reminder that even when his garage choices lean toward refined Italian motoring, his playbook for showing them off remains firmly grounded in the street.
