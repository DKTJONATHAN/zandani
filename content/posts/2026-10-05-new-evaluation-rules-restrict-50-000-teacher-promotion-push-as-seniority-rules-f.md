Teachers aiming to secure a share of an expanded 50,000 promotion pool in the 2026–27 financial year will face an evaluation framework that systematically dismantles decades of seniority-based advancement.

Speaking on Monday, October 5, at Kasarani during World Teachers' Day celebrations, President William Ruto directed the Teachers Service Commission to scale up its promotion targets from an initially advertised 34,016 slots to 50,000. While the state has committed to allocating additional resources beyond the initial Ksh2 billion earmarked for the exercise, the pathway to those higher grades relies on a fundamentally transformed structural gate.

![President William Ruto, Education CS Julius Ogamba (left) at Kasarani during World Teachers' Day on October 5.](https://i.ibb.co/t0BL2vR/32c2c003bae3.webp)

Under guidelines deployed for the current cycle, the commission has sidelined the traditional metrics of age, accumulated experience, and length of service. Instead, advancement hinges on structured career progression and standardized assessments. Educators must complete a mandatory three-year tenure in their current job grade to qualify, retaining the core eligibility floor established in the previous fiscal cycle.

Alongside the academic and tenure hurdles, the updated framework enforces strict behavioral compliance. Regulation 77 of the Code of Regulations for Teachers (2015) operates as a hard administrative barrier, barring any educator who has recently faced disciplinary verdicts or administrative warnings from advancing through the promotion pipeline.

The policy adjustments run parallel to a restructuring of the Junior Secondary School workforce. President Ruto announced that over 20,000 contract JSS teachers who have served past the two-year threshold will transition to permanent and pensionable terms starting January 2027.

![An image of teachers during the 2026 World Teachers Day Celebrations on October 5, with an insert of President William Ruto](https://i.ibb.co/C3Ct9TJt/e50ae857558d.webp)

Meanwhile, the broader administrative architecture governing teacher mobility remains subject to final clearance. Consultations between the TSC and teachers' unions have concluded, leaving the revised Career Progression Guidelines pending formal approval by the Salaries and Remuneration Commission.

The SRC review process is slated for completion within the next two weeks, determining whether the new assessment and promotion matrix will fully clear before the expanded selection exercise commences.
