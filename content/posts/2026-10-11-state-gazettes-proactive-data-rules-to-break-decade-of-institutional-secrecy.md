According to a joint policy gazetted by the Ministry of Information, Communications and the Digital Economy alongside the Commission on Administrative Justice (CAJ), citizens, civic watchdogs, and good-faith whistleblowers stand as the direct beneficiaries of a sweeping administrative overhaul designed to end routine stonewalling across public offices.

The regulatory instrument, published in a Kenya Gazette notice dated October 9, 2026, directly confronts persistent operational bottlenecks that have hobbled the Access to Information Act of 2016 for a decade. Under the newly promulgated framework, state institutions and designated private bodies must invert standard bureaucratic practice: instead of sitting on public records until compelled by formal citizen petitions, agencies are now statutorily required to proactively publish public-interest records as a routine administrative duty.

### Dismantling Institutional Secrecy

![President William Ruto chairing a cabinet meeting on Friday, October 9, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/NdRBxg13/d8ffc0b8b589.webp)

In its formal policy justification, the government offered an unvarnished audit of its own administrative failings over the past decade. The state conceded that implementation of the 2016 law had stalled amid poor records management, endemic bureaucratic delays, inconsistent disclosure criteria, chronic underfunding, and an entrenched "culture of secrecy" across ministries, departments, and state agencies.

To curb arbitrary refusals, the framework eliminates discretionary gatekeeping. Accounting officers are mandated to establish public procedures for receiving, tracking, and resolving information requests within firm administrative deadlines. Where requests are denied or restricted, public bodies must issue written statements explaining the legal basis for refusal and explicitly notify the applicant of their statutory right to appeal.

The policy directs that ordinary data access will be provided free of charge, barring reasonable and verifiable administrative fees associated with physical reproduction or specialised dispatch. Requesters are entitled to receive records through diverse formats, including physical document copies, electronic files, direct in-person registry inspection, or structured analytical summaries.

### County Mandates and Whistleblower Shield

![Kenyans witnessing the swearing-in ceremony of Kithure Kindiki as the deputy president at KICC on Novemebr 1, 2024.](https://i.ibb.co/KjnrR56N/332e3715d4f8.webp)

A critical operational front established under the directive sits within devolved units. Kenya's 47 county governments must now establish dedicated information disclosure desks, appoint formal focal points, and sponsor local access-to-information legislation through county assemblies to eliminate devolved opacity in procurement and project administration.

To institutionalise compliance, the framework introduces specific workplace protections for public servants and civic insiders who disclose wrongdoing. Public and qualifying private entities are required to build internal reporting mechanisms that actively shield good-faith whistleblowers from institutional victimisation, career retaliation, or disciplinary action when exposing administrative malpractice.

Enforcement and appellate arbitration remain anchored within the Commission on Administrative Justice, which retains oversight powers to review adverse ministerial determinations, audit departmental compliance ledgers, and adjudicate violations of citizens' constitutional rights to public records.

The policy now moves into frontline execution, leaving county executives and ministry accounting officers facing the immediate task of digitising paper registries and drafting local bills to meet the CAJ's compliance timeline.
