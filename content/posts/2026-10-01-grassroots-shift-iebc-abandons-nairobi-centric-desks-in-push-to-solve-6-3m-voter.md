The Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) is dismantling its centralized, Nairobi-based inclusion desks, shifting coordination to the 47 counties in an operational bid to rescue its ambitious voter-registration targets ten months before the 2027 General Election.

The structural pivot, announced on Thursday, October 1, invites state and non-state organizations representing youth, women, persons with disabilities (PWDs), and ethnic minorities to register with county electoral offices. The commission has set a strict deadline of Friday, October 30, 2026, at 5:00 PM for groups wishing to partner in these newly localized inclusion committees.

![Press IEBC](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/press%20iebc.jpeg.webp?itok=42uQykM8)

At the heart of this administrative decentralization is a stark mathematical reality. The IEBC is aiming for a historic register of at least 28 million voters for the 2027 polls. However, the national database currently stands at 22.1 million. To close the remaining 6.3 million voter deficit, the commission must venture deep into marginalized, rural, and informal settlements where traditional national campaigns rarely register a lasting impact.

Previously, the IEBC managed minority and marginalized group inclusion from its national headquarters. During the 2022 general election cycle, for instance, the commission established a national-level Women Coordinating Committee on Elections. While that national structure brought together high-profile public and non-state organizations, it struggled to translate policy into registered voters at the grassroots level. A similar centralized model governed disability and youth advocacy.

By shifting coordination to the counties, the IEBC hopes to build localized frameworks that can navigate regional dynamics. However, the commission is introducing strict compliance checks to prevent the onboarding of inactive groups. Organizations seeking to join these county-level committees must undergo rigorous vetting, requiring them to submit formal registration certificates, Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA) PIN certificates, clear categories of operation, contact details of designated representatives, and verified physical addresses within their counties of operation.

![IEBC Chairperson](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/iebc%20chairperson.jpg.webp?itok=g6BzL3dO)

This operational overhaul represents the first major structural shake-up under the current bench of IEBC commissioners. Having taken office in July 2025, the 2027 polls will be their maiden general election. With only ten months remaining on the clock, the pressure to demonstrate administrative competence and avoid the logistical bottlenecks of past elections is intense.

While the commission has executed continuous voter registration campaigns in recent months, the decentralization of inclusion committees marks a transition from mass marketing to targeted, group-specific mobilization at the constituency and county level.

As the October 30 registration deadline approaches, county election managers will face the immediate task of vetting hundreds of community-based organizations. Whether these newly decentralized committees can quickly mobilize the required millions of unregistered Kenyans remains the critical question of the 2027 pre-election cycle.
