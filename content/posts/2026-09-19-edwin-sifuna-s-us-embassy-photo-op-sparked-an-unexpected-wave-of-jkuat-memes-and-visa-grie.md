Just days after being blocked from addressing students at Jomo Kenyatta University of Agriculture and Technology (JKUAT), Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna found a much warmer reception at the residence of United States Chargé d’Affaires Susan Burns. 

But while the senator was busy showcasing his high-level diplomatic access, Kenyans on social media quickly hijacked the photo-op, turning his polished photoshoot into a chaotic mix of campus memes and passionate protests over American visa policies.

Sifuna shared photos of the meeting on X on Thursday, September 18, 2026. Dressed in a sharp blue suit and tie, the senator was pictured in deep conversation with Burns and her diplomatic team, with the American flag standing prominently in the background. 

"Happening….meeting @USEmbassyKenya Charge d’Affaires Susan Burns and her team at her residence in Nairobi," Sifuna posted, keeping the official agenda strictly under wraps. 

For many Kenyans online, the stark transition from a chaotic university gate to a serene diplomatic residence was too good to ignore. 

"Someone stops you from entering JKUAT, they catch you at the US Embassy. This life no balance!!!" joked one user, pointing to the irony of Sifuna’s turbulent week.

But the commentary quickly shifted from lighthearted campus banter to real-world frustrations. Rather than debating Sifuna's 2027 political prospects, several Kenyans used his comment section to lobby the US diplomat directly over the embassy's highly controversial visa application process.

"Salimia huyo Susan and tell her that I am saying, US iache kuibia Wakenya pesa hapo kwa embassy who genuinely want to go and study by denying them visas," wrote another user, echoing a widespread local frustration regarding non-refundable visa fees and high rejection rates for Kenyan students.

Of course, Sifuna's staunch political supporters saw the meeting as proof of his readiness for higher office. Pointing to the diplomatic backdrop, some declared that Sifuna was already conducting "bilateral talks" ahead of the 2027 General Election. One supporter, @Osoro20Fred, wrote: "Kenya-US relations secured under Linda Mwananchi government. We are not playing."

Despite the online chatter, neither Sifuna nor the US Embassy has confirmed whether the discussions had anything to do with his rumored presidential ambitions.

This high-profile meeting is part of an ongoing charm offensive by the Nairobi Senator, who has been aggressively networking with foreign missions in Nairobi. Just two days prior, on September 16, Sifuna met with Dutch Ambassador to Kenya Henk Jan Bakker at his residence. He has also held similar closed-door engagements with EU Ambassador Henriette Geiger and former French Ambassador Arnaud Suquet.

For the US Embassy, the meeting comes during a prolonged transitional period. The US has not had a Senate-confirmed ambassador in Nairobi since Meg Whitman resigned in November 2024. Susan Burns, who assumed the role of Chargé d’Affaires in August 2025, has been leading the embassy's engagements on bilateral justice, health, and economic cooperation.

While Sifuna continues to collect photo-ops with Nairobi's diplomatic elite, the actual substance of his conversations remains a mystery—leaving ordinary Kenyans to use his comment section as a digital suggestion box for their visa grievances and political punchlines.
