Two hundred and twenty-seven flood hotspots spread across Nairobi’s 17 sub-counties have been placed under intense monitoring as national and county security teams finalize contingency plans for the upcoming short rains.

Speaking in Karen during a multi-agency disaster preparedness session, Deputy President Kithure Kindiki outlined the spatial blueprint mapped out to handle potential displacement when heavy downpours begin between the second and third weeks of October. Low-lying residential areas and informal settlements form the core of the risk register, specifically Mukuru, Pipeline, Imara Daima, Kware, Kibera, Nyayo Highrise, Nairobi West, Madaraka, Lang’ata, and sections of Embakasi.

To accommodate households that may be forced out of their dwellings by rising waters and blocked drainage systems, authorities have designated 27 primary shelters and drop-off points. These holding facilities rely primarily on public schools and civic infrastructure, where food supplies, non-food items, and medical kits are currently being assembled.

“We have identified possible evacuation areas and temporary holding grounds,” Kindiki stated, noting that residents in high-risk sectors will be issued timely alerts and formal relocation orders if weather projections materialize into direct threats against safety and property.

The coordinated intervention draws on the Nairobi City County Government alongside national agencies tasked with urban drainage, road infrastructure, and energy networks. Field engineers from the power sector have concurrently initiated structural inspections along major transmission corridors to mitigate storm-induced outages and protect grid installations.

Beyond urban drainage and shelter logistics, the public health sector has activated response protocols across 18 high-risk counties. Plans include the distribution of 10 million treated mosquito nets alongside proposed monthly national sanitation campaigns aimed at curbing waterborne and vector-borne disease outbreaks once the rains set in.
