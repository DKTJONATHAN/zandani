Suba North MP Millie Odhiambo has thrown her weight behind President William Ruto’s decentralized development agenda, publicly commending the administration's execution of the ‘Usawa ni Sawa’ initiative.

Odhiambo’s endorsement centers on the President's stated policy that public resources and infrastructure projects must reach all parts of the country without regard to political affiliation, tribal lines, or geographical background. Her remarks draw attention to a persistent fault line in Kenyan politics: the historical concentration of development projects in select regions.

The Head of State has increasingly anchored his public engagements around the ‘Usawa ni Sawa’ framework, presenting it as a direct counter to discriminatory resource distribution. In pushing the narrative, the administration has repeatedly pointed to specific investments spanning road networks, rural electrification, market construction, educational facilities, and healthcare access as tangible markers of its equity goals.

Odhiambo’s public backing underscores the shifting dynamics of cross-aisle policy commentary, moving away from routine political antagonism to evaluate infrastructural delivery on its operational footprint. Her statements highlight the growing legislative focus on ensuring that marginalized or historically sidelined constituencies secure a predictable share of national development budgets.

While regional balance remains a volatile and heavily scrutinized subject within public debate, Odhiambo’s explicit recognition of the administration's equity push adds a notable voice to the ongoing conversation over how national resources are deployed across Kenya’s 47 counties.
