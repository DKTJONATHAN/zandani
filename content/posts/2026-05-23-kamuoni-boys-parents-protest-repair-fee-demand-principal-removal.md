Parents at Kamuoni Boys High School in Makueni County protested against a compulsory KSh20,000 restoration contribution and called for the transfer or removal of the school's principal in May 2026.

The proposed levy followed serious damage during student unrest reported to have begun on May 12. The school administration and its Board of Management put the repair bill at approximately KSh23 million. Reports described damage to a dormitory, furniture, office windows, the kitchen and other facilities. The school was closed while the damage was assessed and plans for reopening were considered.

### Parents question the restoration levy

Parents challenged the amount being requested from each family and said they had not received a sufficiently clear explanation of how the money would be spent. They questioned whether the proposed contributions could cover the full repair estimate and called for a detailed account of the costs before being asked to pay.

The protest also brought wider complaints about conditions at the school. Parents raised concerns about students' living conditions, additional charges, academic performance and discipline. Some argued that these unresolved issues had contributed to tensions between learners and school management.

### Calls for leadership changes and dialogue

Alongside their opposition to the fee, parents demanded a change in leadership, saying new management was needed to rebuild confidence and restore calm. The school's closure added pressure on families waiting for clarity about when learning would resume.

Leaders in Makueni County appealed for dialogue between parents, school officials and other stakeholders. The protests came amid wider disruption in the county, where local leaders said several schools had experienced strikes after the reopening of the second term.

The dispute placed two immediate questions before the school community: how repairs would be funded transparently, and what steps would address the grievances that parents said had built up over time.
