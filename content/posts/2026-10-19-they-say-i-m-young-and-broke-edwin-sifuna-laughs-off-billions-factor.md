Edwin Sifuna knows exactly what is being whispered behind his back across the city's political lounges: he is too young, too raw, and far too light in the pocket to mount a credible bid for State House.

Taking the pulpit before a Sunday congregation in Donholm, the Nairobi Senator met those murmurs head-on. Rather than dancing around his lack of traditional oligarchic patronage, Sifuna leaned straight into the chatter, turning the widespread gossip about his modest campaign chest and junior political mileage into the centerpiece of an audacious 2027 presidential declaration.

“I am receiving a lot of pressure from Kenyans to vie for the presidency. People are asking, ‘Huyu Sifuna mnaita Rais, how will it happen?’ They say I am young and have no money,” Sifuna told the congregation. “But I know God’s guidance will lead me. I will be the first one-term senator to become president.”

### Betting on a Ten-Month Startup

The declaration marks a daring break from Kenya’s conventional political playbook, where a presidential run typically requires decades of national name recognition, vast corporate networks, and deep personal war chests. Sifuna is attempting to shatter that barrier in a single leap, wagering that his sharp podium delivery and grassroots popularity can bypass the traditional financial toll booths.

His bid arrives through a newly minted political outfit. With his controversial exit from the Orange Democratic Movement (ODM) now settled fact, Sifuna revealed that his team has officially moved to secure an independent launchpad. The Office of the Registrar of Political Parties is currently processing paperwork to rebrand the Equitable Party into the Linda Mwananchi Movement, positioning Sifuna at the helm of an entirely fresh outfit.

Sifuna acknowledged the steep arithmetic of building a nationwide machine from scratch, setting an ambitious clock for his political startup.

“I was forcefully removed from ODM, but God is still making a way for me. I am now the party leader of Linda Mwananchi, and we are ready to face them,” Sifuna declared. “Kuna muujiza tutafanya 2027—it is possible to make Linda Mwananchi popular across the country in less than 10 months and win the presidency.”

### Babu’s Podium Pressure

Sharing the same church service in Donholm, Embakasi East MP Babu Owino added fuel to the opposition's fluid reshuffle. While Sifuna planted his flag on an independent presidential ticket, Babu turned the heat on the wider anti-government camp, warning that fragmented egos would simply guarantee President William Ruto an easy walkover.

“We urge the entire opposition, from Gachagua to Kalonzo to Matiang’i, to unite so that Kenyans can have a new beginning in 2027,” Babu urged, highlighting the delicate dance underway between younger urban lawmakers and the veteran figures maneuvering for State House dominance.

Yet for Sifuna, the challenge remains intensely personal. His transformation from a high-decibel party spokesman into an outright principal tests whether digital-era charisma and oratorical swagger can survive the crushing logistical machinery of a national campaign.

### Battling the State House Machine

Beyond defending his youthful credentials, Sifuna used the Donholm appearance to sound the alarm on what he characterized as a squeeze play by the state. He pointed to recent administrative sanctions against regional opposition leaders as proof that the political battleground is already hardening.

Specifically, Sifuna protested the withdrawal of official security details for Siaya Governor James Orengo and Trans Nzoia Governor George Natembeya, linking the state crackdowns to recent public rifts over regional projects.

“They are using every tactic to frustrate us,” Sifuna said. “The Siaya governor has lost his security. It is God who will protect Orengo, just as they are doing to Natembeya. We will not kneel before that Pharaoh.”

With the Registrar of Political Parties concluding the paperwork on Linda Mwananchi’s rebranding, Sifuna’s gamble moves from Sunday pulpit rhetoric into the cold reality of national party architecture. Whether a first-term senator without an established billionaire backer can actually alter the 2027 math will be tested the moment his ten-month countdown formally begins.
