In a bid to bypass fragmented transport policing, the National Transport and Safety Authority (NTSA) is petitioning the national government to formally classify road traffic crashes as a national disaster under the newly operationalized National Disaster Risk Management Act.

The push follows a Sunday head-on collision between a public service vehicle and a commercial truck at Kanga, near Mtito Andei along the Nairobi-Mombasa Highway. The crash killed 24 people, with Red Cross Kenya and local emergency responders warning the death toll could rise as critically injured passengers undergo emergency treatment. Preliminary findings by crash investigators point to improper overtaking along the corridor.

While fatal collisions routinely prompt enforcement drives, NTSA’s petition seeks to invoke the statutory provisions of the National Disaster Risk Management Act, which commenced in June 2026. Under current administrative structures, road safety responsibilities are divided between transport regulators, the National Police Service (NPS), county health departments, and infrastructure agencies. 

Reclassifying traffic crashes as a national disaster would legally transition oversight to central coordinating bodies, specifically the National Disaster Operations Centre (NDOC) and the National Disaster Risk Management Authority (NDRMA).

### Multi-Agency Mandates and Emergency Funds

NTSA argues that annual figures exceeding 5,000 fatalities and roughly 20,000 reported crashes have pushed road safety past a routine enforcement issue into a national security and economic crisis. The authority maintains that local healthcare networks and county-level emergency infrastructure are routinely overwhelmed by casualty surges along major transit corridors.

Under the 2026 Act, a formal disaster assessment evaluates whether an ongoing crisis exceeds county-level operational capacity. If declared, the classification establishes legally binding casualty-reduction targets and forces joint accountability across multiple authorities:

* **National Police Service:** Mandated multi-agency traffic enforcement protocols tied to central operational oversight.
* **Healthcare Providers and Counties:** Direct resource allocation to scale up roadside trauma care and regional emergency response.
* **Road Agencies:** Mandatory structural and blackspot interventions funded through emergency infrastructure disbursements.

### The Path to Executive Declaration

Transitioning road safety into a national disaster requires formal assessment pipelines. NDRMA and NDOC must first evaluate the scale of casualties and systemic disruption before multi-agency technical teams, Cabinet Secretaries, and county leadership submit formal recommendations to the Cabinet.

Should the executive act on the recommendation, the President holds constitutional authority under Article 132(4)(d) to declare a formal state of emergency or national disaster. Such a step would mark the first major national disaster classification since September 2021, when the government declared severe drought across arid and semi-arid counties a national emergency to deploy emergency food assistance to 2.5 million people.

For now, joint NTSA and police crash investigation teams remain on the ground at Kanga to complete forensic mapping of the Sunday morning collision, while regional health facilities continue treating survivors.
