Nameless stepped away from the playback speakers over the weekend with an assessment rooted entirely in process: Bien-Aimé Baraza’s transition to a solo powerhouse is being driven by pure studio endurance rather than casual industry momentum.

Addressing his peer during an exclusive Nairobi listening session for Bien’s forthcoming solo album, *Alusa Continua*, the veteran pop pioneer framed the 17-track project as the natural dividend of unyielding groundwork.

"You are a trailblazer... I know guys are going to be blown away with what you have," Nameless told Bien. "I just can't wait to see how the world is ready for you, bro. Continue, bro, I am extremely proud of you. You put in the work. Let's come and enjoy ourselves."

### The Anatomy of Studio Obsession

That emphasis on structural preparation was echoed directly by singer Wahu Kagwi, who isolated Bien’s forensic approach to songwriting as his defining professional trait. Rather than relying solely on vocal intuition, Wahu noted that Bien operates with an exhaustive research regimen designed to position Kenyan pop on international stages.

"One thing about Bien is he gets obsessed with his art," Wahu said during the session. "He's obsessed about getting better. He researches a lot."

![Nameless // Mpasho (Instagram)](https://i.ibb.co/s9Gx4Lc2/931b7d41861b.webp)

The resulting 17-track body of work reflects that deliberate international scope. The project pairs Bien with American rap star 2 Chainz and Nigerian Afrobeats vocalist Oxlade, while retaining a firm East African foundation through collaborations with Tanzanian heavyweight Alikiba and Kenyan lyricist Femi One.

Broadcaster and musician Talia Oyando captured the sentiment in the room plainly, labelling Bien a "national treasure" whose current creative arc carries significant weight for the wider local scene.

### Crafting the Ensemble Behind *Alusa Continua*

For the creatives who helped assemble the audio, the listening party offered validation for months of intense studio governance. Bensoul, who handled writing, production, and broader creative direction across key portions of the LP, pointed to Bien’s habit of marshalling diverse talents under a shared, forward-looking vision.

"I think the consistency and the resilience and always like looking ahead for the future in terms of, taking care of other people and bringing them together," Bensoul noted, reflecting on the collaborative architecture underpinning the tracklist.

Fellow artist Charisma linked that creative posture to the sheer survival skills required to maintain longevity in Nairobi’s demanding music ecosystem.

![Talia Oyando and Wahu // Mpasho (Instagram)](https://i.ibb.co/HThJcsrt/1f9702d4b61f.webp)

"The one thing that I've learned... I'd say two things that I've learned so much from him," Charisma said. "Perseverance and discipline. Being in this journey is not easy."

That sentiment carried into specific song construction. Recording artist Coster Ojwang, who secured a guest slot on the album, revealed that their partnership materialized only after waiting for the right creative fit.

"Ilitoka tu muda coz kuna spiritually the songs you want to have me on," Ojwang said, describing a natural alignment once the production tone matched his style.

### Sauti Sol Fraternity Endorsement

The weekend gathering also served as a moment of continuity for Sauti Sol’s brotherhood. Bandmate Savara Mudigi was on hand to back Bien’s solo ambitions, linking the playback to his own current creative releases.

"My latest single is about celebrating, so it’s a very good time to be alive, the stars have aligned so I’m dedicating it to *Alusa Continua*," Savara said. "Bien is my brother forever, so I’m celebrating him with my latest single."

With peer scrutiny cleared and the 17-song sequence fully vetted by industry veterans, Bien’s operational focus shifts squarely to the international market as *Alusa Continua* heads toward global streaming platforms.
