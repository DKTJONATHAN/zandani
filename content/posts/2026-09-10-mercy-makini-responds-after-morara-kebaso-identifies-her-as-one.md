NAIROBI - Mercy Makini, the estranged wife of activist and politician Morara Kebaso, has formally disassociated herself from his public declarations of polygamy, citing a history of domestic violence as the primary cause for their separation.

The statement, issued by Makini on Tuesday, September 10, 2026, serves as a direct rebuttal to recent comments made by Kebaso, in which he identified her as one of his two wives. Makini, who shares three children with the activist, asserted that she exited the marriage on March 2, 2026, and is no longer a party to his personal arrangements.

“As for this talk about polygamy, please stop creating the impression that I am still part of your life. Polygamy is your business, not mine. I left. I moved on. I am living my life,” Makini stated.

In her response, Makini detailed the circumstances surrounding her departure, explicitly alleging that she was a victim of physical assault during the marriage, including incidents occurring while she was pregnant. She stated that the decision to leave was a proactive measure to ensure her safety, noting, “Society only appreciates a woman when they walk out of a marriage dead; I chose to walk out alive.”

The public clarification follows ongoing speculation regarding Kebaso’s personal life, fueled by images circulating on social media of the politician in the company of a woman identified as Pauline, his second wife.

Makini indicated that she had opted for silence regarding the dissolution of their union to maintain privacy, but stated she felt compelled to speak out because Kebaso continued to reference her in public discourse.

As of Thursday, September 10, 2026, Kebaso has not issued a formal response to the specific allegations of domestic violence raised by his estranged wife. The matter remains a subject of significant public interest.
