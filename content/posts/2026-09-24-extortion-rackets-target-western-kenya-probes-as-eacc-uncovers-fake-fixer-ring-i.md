Active anti-corruption investigations in regional hubs are increasingly being shadowed by parallel extortion rings, where fraudsters convert the threat of state prosecution into lucrative private shakedowns.

The mechanism relies on subject anxiety: individuals already undergoing scrutiny by the Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission (EACC) are targeted by self-styled fixers offering to bury files in exchange for cash.

That operational model collapsed for two suspects in Bungoma after commission detectives intercepted a Ksh30,000 down payment—part of a Ksh100,000 demand made to a person under active EACC investigation. The suspects had claimed to hold direct internal connections capable of terminating the ongoing probe.

Following their detention, the two were processed at the EACC Western Regional Office before being booked at Bungoma Central Police Station, where they remain in custody pending formal charges.

### A Recurring Regional Playbook

![eacc-arrested.jpg](https://i.ibb.co/k2s4vQG5/320fd7799c2d.webp)

The Bungoma bust is not an isolated shakedown. According to the anti-graft agency, the suspects belong to a wider group operating across Western Kenya that specializes in impersonating commission detectives or selling non-existent influence over official files.

The scheme mirrors past operations uncovered by state investigators in neighboring counties, where fake fixers built elaborate facades to mirror legitimate law enforcement activity.

In August 2025, EACC intelligence surveillance in the South Nyanza region led to the arrest of seven individuals operating a similar impersonation network. During that raid, detectives seized physical tools of fake authority, including fabricated EACC job cards, official letterheads, counterfeit rubber stamps, and altered case documents used to convince targets that their prosecutions were imminent unless paid off.

Earlier that same year, in January 2025, detectives arrested two other individuals who had posed as senior EACC executives, similarly promising to abort active corruption inquiries in exchange for direct cash payouts.

### Exploiting the Inquiry Gap

![A photo of a woman in handcuffs](https://i.ibb.co/bgtKnLkz/21250498a423.webp)

The persistence of these operations highlights how third-party extortionists target subjects who are often reluctant to report shakedowns out of fear of drawing further attention to their underlying inquiries.

By claiming they can alter case outcomes, impersonators exploit victims at their most vulnerable point in the investigative cycle, extracting cash without any capacity to influence the legal outcome.

The EACC has reiterated that its investigation protocols are closed to third-party intervention and that no officer or external party possesses the statutory authority to drop charges or alter files in exchange for money.

### Next Steps in Bungoma

Detectives at the Western Regional Office are expanding their inquiry to establish whether the Bungoma suspects acted as independent fraudsters or operated in concert with additional peripheral associates across the region.

Investigators are also verifying whether the group possessed physical forged materials similar to those recovered in Nyanza or relied solely on verbal claims of internal access to execute the shakedown.
