The contested commercial future of Kwale County’s high-value mineral reserves has surged to the center of Kenya's political arena, as opposition figures challenge the government's engagements with American investors during President William Ruto’s visit to the United States.

At the heart of the dispute are the unexploited deposits at Mrima Hill, a site rich in niobium and rare earth elements that Kenya is currently opening to international developers. Addressing Kenyans living in the diaspora on Saturday, September 19, Democracy for Citizens Party (DCP) leader Rigathi Gachagua alleged that President Ruto’s attendance at the 81st United Nations General Assembly in New York serves as cover to execute an agreement transferring rights to the Kwale deposits to a United States firm.

![Mrima Hill Kwale County](https://i.ibb.co/QvhqC0gw/98987aa42cce.webp)

Gachagua claimed the proposed deal involves critical mineral assets valued at Ksh 8.2 trillion, alleging that preliminary terms were signed in Nairobi four weeks ago through an intermediary before President Ruto traveled to finalize the arrangement.

"William Ruto is in the air on his way to New York for the United Nations General Assembly, UNGA," Gachagua said during the virtual diaspora session. "But that is not his mission. That is just a camouflage. His real mission in the United States is to come and seal a deal with Americans on how niobium will be exploited from Kenya."

The allegations arrive at a moment of heightened diplomatic and commercial maneuvering around East Africa's critical minerals. Washington has actively signaled its intent to secure supply chains for transition metals, with US Assistant Secretary of State for African Affairs Frank Garcia confirming on September 9 that American firms were seeking direct partnerships in Kenya to process minerals locally rather than exporting raw ores.

![A collage of the Democracy for Citizens Party (DCP) leader Rigathi Gachagua (left), President William Ruto (right), and the United States President Donald Trump (Insert).](https://i.ibb.co/NgVtZ0QF/d047242a8f50.webp)

Kenya’s Ministry of Mining has been reviewing competitive tenders from global firms bidding for the development rights to the Mrima Hill site, where vast quantities of niobium—a key component in high-strength steel alloys, superconducting magnets, and advanced energy storage—remain undeveloped.

The political broadside against the pending Kwale negotiations marks an escalation in Gachagua's attacks on the administration's management of strategic natural resources. Days earlier, the former Deputy President publicly accused President Ruto of maintaining personal commercial motives behind state pressure that forced a foreign commercial operator out of the Lake Magadi soda ash operations.

Neither State House nor the Ministry of Mining has issued a formal response to the Ksh 8.2 trillion figure or the claims of a pre-signed proxy agreement. As President Ruto arrives in New York for high-level UN General Assembly side meetings and bilateral economic sessions, the administration faces growing pressure to outline the precise regulatory framework governing foreign concessions for the country's most valuable mineral assets.
