Overnight flash floods that paralyzed movement along Nairobi’s primary transport arteries are set to meet continuous weather stress, with severe rain episodes forecast to return across four of the next five days.

According to an alert issued by the Kenya Meteorological Service Authority, moderate to heavy rainfall will persist across the country from Saturday, October 31 through Wednesday, November 4. The forecast hits an urban road network already struggling to clear waterlogged channels along major entry and exit routes serving the capital.

### Commuter Corridors Under Pressure

The immediate impact of the downpours materialized early Saturday as surface runoff submerged sections of Waiyaki Way, Thika Road, Kiambu Road, and Mombasa Road. Transit between the Nairobi Central Business District and neighboring residential hubs in Kiambu, Kajiado, and Machakos counties faced heavy delays following hours of overnight rain.

![Motorists using a flooded highway during cold and rainy weather in Nairobi](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/rains_1.jpg.webp?itok=gejNPgyy)

The weatherman projects that Nairobi will experience moderate afternoon showers on Saturday, Sunday, Tuesday, and Wednesday, leaving only a brief pause early next week before precipitation resumes. Surrounding counties face a similar cycle, with Kiambu expecting rain on Saturday afternoon, Sunday, Tuesday, and Wednesday, while Machakos and Makueni trace an identical four-day pattern.

### Five Days of Continuous Thunderstorms in Western Hubs

While the capital faces episodic afternoon downpours, a more relentless weather pattern is forming across Western Kenya and the Rift Valley. Meteorologists warn that 17 counties in these regions will experience afternoon showers accompanied by thunderstorms on all five days of the forecast window.

This continuous storm activity covers major agricultural and transport hubs including Nakuru, Kericho, Bomet, Kakamega, Kisii, Narok, Laikipia, Samburu, Uasin Gishu, Nandi, Baringo, Vihiga, Bungoma, Busia, Kisumu, Homa Bay, and Migori.

![A photo of motorists using the Thika Superhighway during cold and rainy weather](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/a_photo_of_motorists_using_the_thika_superhighway_during_cold_and_rainy_weather.jpg.webp?itok=871QlqTz)

Along the coastline, rainfall will follow an alternating schedule. Mombasa and Lamu are slated for downpours on Saturday and Wednesday, whereas Kwale, Kilifi, and Taita Taveta will see showers on Saturday, Sunday, Tuesday, and Wednesday. Tana River is set to receive rainfall on Saturday, Tuesday, and Wednesday.

In Central Kenya, Nyeri expects showers on Saturday and Tuesday, while Murang’a will record rainfall on Saturday and Wednesday.

### Readiness Test for Short Rains

The five-day outlook arrives during the onset of the October-December short rains season, a period already marked by heightened preparedness warnings regarding anticipated El Niño conditions. 

With municipal drainage systems in Nairobi and adjacent counties already overflowing from the first heavy downpours, public safety and road management agencies face an immediate test to clear drainage bottlenecks before the predicted afternoon showers hit saturated ground through Wednesday.
