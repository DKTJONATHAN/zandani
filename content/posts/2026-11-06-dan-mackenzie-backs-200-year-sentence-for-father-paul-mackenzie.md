Dan Mackenzie has publicly aligned with the judicial system against his own father, backing the High Court's decision to hand down a 200-year prison sentence to controversial former televangelist and cult leader Paul Nthenge Mackenzie. The ruling, delivered by High Court Judge Justice Diana Kavedza in Mombasa on Friday, November 6, 2026, stems from the forced starvation and murder of 190 children in the Shakahola forest—a tragedy that claimed Dan’s own younger brother.

The Mombasa judgment dismantled the remaining public profile of the former broadcast preacher, sentencing Mackenzie to two consecutive 100-year terms specifically for murder by forced starvation of minor victims. Alongside the former televangelist, 28 accomplices were sentenced to prison terms spanning between 10 and 160 years, following their convictions last month. Defense attorney Lawrence Obonyo confirmed immediate plans to challenge the conviction and sentences in the Court of Appeal.

Dan Mackenzie’s stance highlights the deep personal fractures caused by his father’s transition from a televised ministry to an apocalyptic forest movement near Malindi. Justice Kavedza detailed how Mackenzie weaponized his charismatic broadcast platform to recruit vulnerable families, instructing followers to starve themselves and their children under the premise of reaching heaven before the end of the world. For Dan, whose younger brother perished inside the forest complex, the judicial resolution marks a definitive public break from his father despite the permanent personal loss.

The evidentiary record presented in court laid bare the brutal mechanics of the Shakahola operation. Testimony established that children were subjected to watching their siblings die of hunger over prolonged periods. Followers who attempted to break their fast or obtain food were systematically beaten, bound, and confined within designated "fasting bays." Subsequent forensic exhumations uncovered emaciated remains in shallow graves, latrines, and evidence of individuals buried while still alive.

The personal toll described by Dan Mackenzie echoed throughout the courtroom, where other surviving family members recounted total household devastation. Stephen Mwiti, a victim who lost six children after his wife joined Mackenzie's movement, noted that no legal outcome could restore what was destroyed. The initial discovery of the tragedy in April 2023 yielded 448 bodies recovered by search teams, according to figures documented by the Kenya National Commission on Human Rights (KNCHR). Additional charges involving 52 victims located in an adjacent village bring the total suspected death toll close to 500.

Beyond the immediate criminal penalties, the court's findings exposed long-standing systemic lapses by state authorities. Justice Kavedza noted that state and security agencies failed to intervene despite clear warning signs dating back to 2017, when investigators first examined Mackenzie’s broadcast and physical premises prior to his 2019 relocation into the Shakahola forest. Rights organizations, led by the Kenya Human Rights Commission (KHRC), continue to demand formal accountability regarding local administrative and intelligence officials who overlooked early red flags.

With defense counsel Obonyo preparing appellate filings, the case now shifts toward legal challenges in higher courts. However, Dan Mackenzie's open support for his father's dual-century sentence seals an absolute familial separation, cementing Paul Mackenzie's transition from a televised preacher to one of the country's most heavily sentenced convicts.
