The High Court’s Anti-Corruption and Economic Crimes Division has granted the Assets Recovery Agency immediate custodial control over commercial revenues and physical assets held by six individuals, moving beyond standard ownership caveats to intercept cash flows before civil forfeiture proceedings begin.

The conservatory directions, issued across three separate matters under the Proceeds of Crime and Anti-Money Laundering Act (POCAMLA), target prime real estate, passenger vehicles, and freight logistics equipment suspected of being illicitly acquired.

Under the terms approved by Justice Benjamin Musyoki, state recovery efforts will actively intercept business yields rather than solely freezing titles. In a matter touching on real estate in Nyeri County, the court ruled that all rental collections, accrued profits, and financial proceeds derived from two plots in Aguthi/Gatitu and a third parcel in Mweiga/Kamatongu must be diverted straight into an agency-controlled escrow account.

Justice Musyoki barred the registered owners from accessing any commercial proceeds arising from the properties, establishing a state financial hold pending the formal hearing and conclusion of the forfeiture petition.

### Strict Physical Surrender Timelines

The court paired the fiscal interdiction with stringent operational ultimatums, demanding the physical handover of documentation and transport assets within seven calendar days of legal service.

For the Nyeri properties, the respondents must surrender original title deeds and land ownership instruments to the agency's custody. The same directive binds the owner of an associated Toyota Axio, who was ordered to relinquish the vehicle itself, its registration logbook, and its ignition keys directly to state investigators.

A parallel file before the same division imposed an identical seven-day compliance mechanism on passenger vehicles. The preservation order froze five motor vehicles: two Mercedes-Benz units, a Toyota Corolla Axio, a Nissan Dayz, and a Mazda CX-5. The bench issued an explicit directive instructing the surrender of the Mazda CX-5 and its original registration logbook within a week, providing the agency with automatic authority to seize the vehicle should the respondent fail to comply.

### Commercial Freight Assets Seized

In a third recovery action, the bench broadened the enforcement net to commercial cross-border operations. Justice Rose Ougo granted preservation orders over a heavy-duty Scania truck, a haulage trailer, and a shipping container tied to a clearing and forwarding enterprise.

Justice Ougo’s ruling placed the commercial transport assets directly under the agency's physical control, empowering state investigators to seize and secure the freight equipment from private operations.

All three rulings, initially issued between October 2 and October 16, were formally promulgated on Friday, October 23, across three statutory Kenya Gazette notices.

To lock down the asset base, the High Court instructed the Registrar of Lands and the National Transport and Safety Authority to lodge formal restrictions against the registered folios and vehicle logbooks, closing off any secondary transfers or encumbrances while the agency prepares its final forfeiture applications.
