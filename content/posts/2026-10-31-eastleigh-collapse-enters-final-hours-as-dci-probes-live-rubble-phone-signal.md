A scheduled Sunday wind-down of recovery operations at the collapsed 18-storey tower on Yusuf Haji Avenue has run directly into an unresolved forensic puzzle, as detectives examine live cellular connections originating from beneath the debris nine days after the structural failure.

Multi-agency emergency responders had designated Sunday, November 1—the tenth consecutive day of excavation—as their target date to conclude search-and-recovery efforts at the Eastleigh disaster zone. Yet the operational timetable is now shadowed by evidence referred to the Directorate of Criminal Investigations, following confirmed cellular activity on an alternate telephone line registered to Auerilia Wanjiku, one of 16 people still officially unaccounted for under the concrete wreckage.

### Repeated Connections and Ambient Machinery

According to Wanjiku’s mother, Jude Rose Wanjiru, relatives attempting to trace the missing woman bypassed her primary mobile line, which was unresponsive, and dialled her second number. That line connected repeatedly.

![Emergency responders at the scene of the Eastleigh building collapse in Nairobi on October 23, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/kenya-red-cross-eastleigh-nairobi-october-2026.jpg.webp?itok=R--aHccl)

When relatives dialled the number on Saturday morning from locations well outside Eastleigh, the calls were picked up on the other end. While the recipient remained completely silent and unable to respond to voice prompts, the line carried distinct, audible audio: the mechanical rumble of earthmovers and heavy plant equipment clearing concrete in real time.

“When we called, it was received; however, it was like she could not hear us, but we could clearly hear the noise from her surroundings,” Wanjiru said, recounting the repeated attempts that have defined the family’s daily presence at the cordoned site since October 23.

The audio feedback, carrying the clear cadence of ongoing site clearing while relatives listened from a distance, prompted site commanders to escalate the cellular log directly to the DCI. Detectives are now attempting to determine the precise geographic origin of the transmission, verifying whether the device is responding to automated touch pressure, physical disturbance from heavy plant machinery, or deliberate human engagement within a structural void.

### The Ten-Day Window and 16 Missing

The technological trace arrives at a critical juncture for the multi-agency operation, which brings together military personnel, local disaster units, and humanitarian responders. Nine days of shifting heavy slabs along Yusuf Haji Avenue have exacted a severe toll on families who have kept vigil along the security perimeters, particularly following the earlier recovery of a dismembered female victim.

![rescue-collapsed-building-emergency.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/rescue-collapsed-building-emergency.jpg.webp?itok=RVmcyH3m)

With 16 individuals still listed as missing, the logistical reality of urban search-and-rescue protocols dictates that operations cannot continue indefinitely. Emergency teams have systematically cleared the bulk of the collapsed building's front profile and are now concentrating their remaining heavy machinery exclusively on the structure's rear perimeter.

Field commanders believe that section represents the most probable pocket where trapped occupants might remain intact, either in survival voids or buried beneath the sheer weight of collapsed upper floors.

### Resolving the Signal Before the Deadline

The referral of telecommunications data to the DCI introduces an urgent technical obligation into the operation's final scheduled hours. Tracking cellular pings to a specific cubic quadrant within tens of thousands of tonnes of compacted masonry requires rapid coordination between mobile network service providers, cyber investigators, and excavation engineers.

As heavy excavators continue turning over concrete slabs at the rear of the property heading into Sunday's shift, search teams must balance their planned demobilisation against the unresolved question of whose hand—or what mechanical shift—is picking up Wanjiku’s phone.
