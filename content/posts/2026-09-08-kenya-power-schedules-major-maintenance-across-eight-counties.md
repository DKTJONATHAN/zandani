Kenya Power has scheduled extensive network maintenance for Tuesday, September 8, 2026, forcing power outages across Nairobi, Uasin Gishu, Migori, Kisii, Homa Bay, Nyeri, Tharaka Nithi, and the Coast region. Residents and business owners in affected areas must prepare for service interruptions lasting up to eight hours as crews conduct infrastructure upgrades.

Nairobi bears the brunt of the maintenance today. Loresho, specifically around the University Farm and Mandela Hostels, loses power from 9:00 a.m. until 5:00 p.m. Waithaka residents face a similar blackout affecting Naivasha Road, Ndwaru Road, and Dagoretti High School. Across the city in Donholm, commerce stalls for establishments near Mima Centre, Equity Bank, and Savannah Road, with supply expected to return at 5:00 p.m.

Moving to the Rift Valley, Uasin Gishu County faces disruptions in Kokwet, Chebororwo, and surrounding villages. In the Nyanza and Western blocks, Migori County residents in Kakrao and Warisia will be without electricity from 8:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. Further south, the connection covering Gamba and God Ber across Kisii and Homa Bay experiences a shorter outage, running from 9:00 a.m. to 2:00 p.m.

Nyeri County faces the most granular impact. The maintenance covers massive swaths of the town including Kimathi Estate, the Nyeri National Polytechnic, and the KRA offices. Additional work hits Baraka Sasini, Mahiga, and Rubate Market. In all these locations, businesses dependent on a constant grid supply-from coffee factories to local tea collection centers-must rely on backup generators or halt operations entirely until the 5:00 p.m. restoration mark.

Coastal operations are not spared. Kilifi County loses power in Kanamai and Bomani, affecting local clinics and housing estates. Mombasa County’s Miritini area is also on the list, with the entire estate and industrial sites like Doshi Miritini facing an eight-hour silence from the grid.

### Between the lines

The recurring nature of these "planned" interruptions remains the ultimate test of patience for the Kenyan entrepreneur. While Kenya Power frames these outages as essential network maintenance, the economic cost of a workday lost in places like Donholm or Nyeri town is rarely factored into the utility’s public notices. For the kiosk owner near a coffee factory in Mahiga or the data center staff near Waiyaki Way, a simple notice is poor consolation for the loss of cold-chain integrity or disrupted digital workflows.

If you are stuck in traffic near the Westlands exit or crawling toward Kasarani, look at the streetlights. When the grid fluctuates, the small businesses you pass-the salons, the welding shops, the butcheries-are the ones absorbing the cost of every silent hour. It is a quiet, expensive tax on productivity. We are told to make arrangements, but for many, the only arrangement is waiting for the hum of the transformer to return. Kenya Power operates as a monopoly, and today, that monopoly dictates the rhythm of the nation’s heartbeat. Businesses are left standing in the dark, wondering when the infrastructure will finally match the modern ambitions of a country that cannot afford to switch off.
