Fresh from a temporary pause in active public engagements due to medical recovery, veteran politician Oburu Oginga has returned to the political arena with a sharp message for external players attempting to backseat-drive Orange Democratic Movement affairs.

The ODM party leader issued a blunt statement cautioning non-party members to stay out of ongoing high-level negotiations with President William Ruto’s United Democratic Alliance. Oburu made it clear that the opposition outfit requires no unsolicited coaching on how to conduct its political business.

Public debate had recently swirled around whether the backroom engagements between ODM and UDA should eventually result in a formal, written power-sharing blueprint. Oburu pointedly noted that these discussions gained momentum precisely while he was away from active political duties.

Addressing the commentary directly, Oburu emphasized that coalition negotiations are serious institutional undertakings rather than public entertainment or media spectacles. He asserted that the party possesses the necessary internal machinery and seasoned leadership to navigate the talks without outside interference.

Beyond managing external opinions, the ODM leader drew a firm line on the substance of any prospective deal. He reiterated that full implementation of the party's 10-point agenda remains a rigid, non-negotiable prerequisite for any final agreement with the ruling administration.

Oburu signed off his address with a word of gratitude to party faithful and well-wishers who stood by him during his health recovery, noting that his physical progress remains steady as he steps back into the political fray.
