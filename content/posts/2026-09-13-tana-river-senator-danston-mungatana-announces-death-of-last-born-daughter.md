Tana River Senator Danson Mungatana and his wife, Deputy Government Spokesperson Mwanaisha Chidzuga, are in mourning following the death of their eight-year-old daughter, Amal Mghoi Mungatana, who succumbed to a long-term illness at Gertrude’s Children’s Hospital in Muthaiga, Nairobi, on Sunday morning.

### What we know

* A prominent political household has been hit by personal tragedy, pausing official state and legislative business for both key public figures.
* The eight-year-old’s protracted medical struggle had quietly engaged family resources across international and local specialist clinics for nearly her entire life.
* Political delegations and community leaders are pivoting to Tana Delta this week as Ngao village prepares for an emotionally heavy Friday send-off.

Amal, who was born in 2018, had endured persistent medical challenges from infancy. Her parents confirmed that the family had sought care through several specialized regimens, flying her abroad at various intervals in search of lasting interventions before her admission to the pediatric facility in Muthaiga.

In a joint statement released on Sunday afternoon, the family confirmed the loss and formally notified constituents and colleagues across government.

"It is with profound sorrow that the family of Hon. Senator Dr. Danson Mungatana and his wife, Hon. Mwanaisha Chidzuga, Deputy Government Spokesperson, announce the passing of their last-born daughter, Amal Mghoi Mungatana," the statement read. "Amal went to be with the Lord on Sunday 13th September 2026, while undergoing treatment at Gertrude’s Children’s Hospital, Muthaiga."

Following the announcement, the family relocated to their ancestral home in Ngao Village, within Tana River County, where mourning rites and funeral arrangements have begun. The burial has been slated for Friday, September 18.

Public figures, legislators, and senior administration officials have spent the day conveying condolences to the couple, who have both requested privacy and prayer as they make final arrangements to lay their youngest child to rest.

Behind the protocol and public posture that define their offices, the couple now confronts the devastating reality of pediatric loss-a private, grueling battle that ended quietly in an intensive care ward, far removed from the glare of national statecraft.
