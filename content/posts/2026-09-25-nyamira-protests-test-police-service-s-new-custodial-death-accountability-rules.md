Barely weeks after the National Police Service unveiled a stricter framework for handling fatalities behind bars, a custodial death in Naromoru has triggered open defiance in Nyamira County, exposing the widening trust gap between institutional policy and street-level reality.

Residents of Iranya in Kitutu Masaba Constituency took to the streets following the death of 24-year-old Dennis Oremo. Arrested by officers from Naromoru Police Station over allegations that he had assaulted his employer while working on a farm, Oremo never made it to a courtroom. Instead, police informed his relatives that he was found hanging inside his cell, using a strip of mattress covering left in the room.

![A collage of protests in Iranya, Nyamira County, over the death of 24-year-old Dennis Oremo, with an inset of his mother, Mary Moraa.](https://i.ibb.co/d05kZhFW/16c6e95080b0.webp)

The family has flatly rejected that account, raising immediate questions as to why a suspect was neither charged nor safeguarded while detained. Ruth Moraa described her son as a quiet, cooperative young man who had no history of conflict. For the family, the explanation given by officers compounds the shock of the loss with the crushing financial and emotional weight of arranging a funeral for a breadwinner who went to work and ended up in a mortuary.

“It seems that even people in police custody have no security,” said Angley Nyangena, an uncle to the deceased, calling on authorities to lay out the unvarnished facts of what transpired within the station walls.

![Residents of Iranya in Kitutu Masaba Constituency, Nyamira County, have held protests demanding justice over the death of a 24-year-old man](https://i.ibb.co/6J73H7jG/1c140cf8195e.webp)

The confrontation places immediate pressure on the leadership of Inspector General Douglas Kanja and Interior Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen, alongside the Independent Policing Oversight Authority (IPOA). Under guidelines rolled out by the NPS in August, any fatality occurring within police custody is meant to trigger an immediate, impartial investigation, with every officer who was on duty at the time designated as a person of interest.

Yet for communities watching from Nyamira, the written promises of institutional reform mean little without transparent, verifiable accountability on the ground. As the family appeals for high-level intervention, the central test remains whether the newly mandated internal protocols will yield independent answers or simply collide with the wall of silence that has historically surrounded deaths behind locked cell doors.
