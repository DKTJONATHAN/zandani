Sarah Mtalii is redefining what matters in her personal life, making it clear that accumulating wealth without knowing how to enjoy it is no longer on her agenda.

Speaking on Massawe Japanni’s mid-morning show on Radio Jambo, the Bonfire Adventures co-founder laid out the specific emotional and lifestyle standards she expects in any future romantic partner, months after her divorce from business partner Simon Kabu was officially finalized.

While Mtalii was quick to clarify that she is not actively searching for a partner, she noted that her outlook on companionship has evolved to prioritize experiential living over mere financial drive.

### The Shift Beyond Wealth Accumulation

Describing herself as someone who loves to “eat life with a big spoon,” Mtalii explained that ambition and commercial success hold little appeal if a partner cannot unplug and share memorable moments.

“They don’t know how to create memories. So for me, I want such kind of a person,” Mtalii said during the broadcast, stressing that the ability to step back and savor life experiences now outweighs balance-sheet figures.

When pressed on personal preferences, Mtalii noted an affinity for tall, dark, and handsome Black American men, referencing a close friend who works as a doctor. Even so, she insisted that physical traits remain secondary to character.

“I love them. I have a friend who is a Black American, a doctor, and he’s very kind-hearted,” she said, underscoring kindness, unwavering honesty, and loyalty as her core requirements.

“I am the most honest, loyal person you can ever meet,” she added, reiterating that her focus remains grounded in her personal and professional growth.

### Drawing the Line on Platonic Travel

The conversation also tackled persistent public chatter regarding a male companion frequently spotted by her side during public outings and trips.

Mtalii firmly dismissed speculation of an ongoing romantic affair, defining the individual as a strictly platonic confidant who provides emotional grounding and sound advice.

“He’s my best friend. One of those friends when I’m around with, I feel peaceful. I feel so good, but not in that way that people imagine,” she told Japanni.

She noted that their mutual comfort allows them to travel together seamlessly while maintaining appropriate personal boundaries, including taking separate rooms. Mtalii described his role in her inner circle as that of an informal counselor and sounding board who understands her character without romantic entanglement.

### Maintaining Business Ties Post-Split

The candid exchange marks one of Mtalii’s most expansive personal reflections since her high-profile separation from Simon Kabu earlier this year.

The pair built Bonfire Adventures from the ground up over a decade of marriage, establishing one of the region’s most visible tour operations. Kabu confirmed on February 18 that their divorce proceedings had concluded, following a period in which Mtalii had acknowledged the heavy emotional toll of the marital split.

Despite the legal end of their marriage, both entrepreneurs remain actively involved in the day-to-day stewardship of Bonfire Adventures, balancing their joint corporate interests while forging separate personal paths.
