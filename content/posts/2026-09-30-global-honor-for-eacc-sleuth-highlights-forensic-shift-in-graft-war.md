The fight against public-sector corruption in Kenya is increasingly being fought through complex ledger audits and financial mapping rather than traditional dragnet enforcement—a technical transition brought into sharp focus by international professional recognition.

Christine Ombaka, a forensic investigator with the Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission (EACC), has been named the recipient of the 2026 Association of Chartered Certified Accountants (ACCA) Global Public Sector Advocacy Award. The honor specifically recognizes her technical contributions to forensic accounting, corruption investigations, and asset tracing, alongside her work in developing internal risk-management and investigation systems inside government bodies.

Beyond investigative casework, the ACCA judging panel cited Ombaka’s role in shaping policy discourse on ethics, governance, and institutional accountability, as well as her work mentoring emerging professionals across employers, students, and professional networks.

### The Forensic Frontline

![An image of Ms Christine Ombaka, an Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission’s (EACC) Forensic Investigator, receiving the prestigious Association of Chartered Certified Accountants (](https://i.ibb.co/Hs1KMhc/db00c895810c.webp)

For anti-graft bodies, the evidentiary bar in court increasingly hinges on an investigator's ability to unpick layered bank accounts, shell entities, and disguised asset transfers. Forensic accounting provides the evidentiary architecture required to freeze illicit gains before funds vanish from domestic jurisdiction.

Speaking on behalf of EACC Chief Executive Officer Abdi Mohamud during the ceremony, Director of Field Services and Coordination Jackson Mue noted that the global honor reflects an institutional emphasis on embedding specialized professional skills into field operations.

“Christine’s achievement demonstrates the value of professional expertise and commitment in advancing the Commission’s work,” Mohamud stated in remarks delivered at the event. “We remain committed to supporting the professional development of our officers so they can continue to excel in their areas of expertise and make a meaningful contribution to the fight against corruption.”

The commission maintains that deploying qualified accountants directly into investigative teams has become essential to matching the sophisticated methods employed by syndicates diverting state resources.

### Professional Standards in Public Practice

George Njar, Head of the ACCA Eastern Africa Chapter, characterized the global recognition as evidence that specialized professional training is reshaping governance standards across the region's public service. Njar pointed to Ombaka's work as a demonstration of how accounting standards serve as a practical bulwark against financial leakage when applied inside statutory enforcement bodies.

Accepting the award, Ombaka credited the commission’s investigative environment for enabling technical officers to carry out complex financial reviews, dedicating the international honor to the agency's collective operations.

The recognition places renewed attention on the internal capacity of public integrity agencies, where the ability to sustain convictions and claw back public resources now rests squarely on the rigor of forensic trail reconstruction.
