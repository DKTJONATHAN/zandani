Deputy President Kithure Kindiki has taken direct aim at infrastructure vandals while attempting to calm rising public anxiety over anticipated El Niño heavy rains, signaling that deliberate damage to Kenya’s power transmission lines will now be treated as a national security threat.

Speaking from Karen, Nairobi County, on Thursday, September 24, 2026, Kindiki issued a public reassurance regarding meteorological projections that indicate heightened rainfall beginning between the second and third weeks of October. Alongside his assurances of state readiness, the Deputy President focused sharply on operational vulnerabilities threatening public utilities, pointing to a rising pattern of vandalism, theft, and active sabotage along critical power transmission corridors.

To prevent utility failures during severe weather, Kindiki confirmed that the executive branch will formally escalate power line security to the National Security Council. The move comes as Kenya Power and the State Department for Energy coordinate with county governments and local communities to clear wayleaves and minimize storm-induced blackouts.

### Security Escalation and Infrastructure Risks

Addressing the nation from Karen, Kindiki urged residents across the country and in the capital to refrain from panic as weather tracking models pinpoint the mid-October start window. He stated that multi-agency teams are actively deploying across mapped flood zones to shield communities and critical installations.

“We are prepared. There is still work ongoing,” Kindiki said during the address. “We want to ask the people of Kenya and the people of Nairobi not to panic. We will also be giving them regular communication so that nothing catches us by surprise.”

The central focus of his security warning targets power distribution networks, where physical damage or theft could jeopardize emergency response efforts and public safety during flash flooding. By taking the issue directly to the National Security Council, the executive intends to secure high-voltage transmission lines while field crews clear foliage along distribution pathways.

### Nairobi Dredging and ASAL Hotspots

Beyond power infrastructure, Kindiki disclosed that financial bottlenecks previously threatening to delay emergency operations have been cleared, allowing full-scale preparation to move forward.

In Nairobi, daily flood mitigation efforts are underway across known trouble spots. Teams from the Nairobi County Green Army alongside workers from the national Climate Works program are conducting daily dredging of major rivers and clearing drainage blockages in high-density areas.

Outside the capital, specialized disaster response operations are targeting vulnerable low-lying regions within Arid and Semi-Arid Lands (ASALs). Emergency planners are prioritizing flood-prone locations such as El Wak and the lower Tana River basin, where seasonal overflow routinely cuts off ground transport.

### Dignity in Displacement

Kindiki also outlined contingency plans for populations living in high-risk river basins, confirming that temporary holding centers equipped with food, medical provisions, and basic necessities are stocked and ready should rising water levels force evacuations.

He emphasized that any necessary displacement will follow clear guidelines and early notification to prevent chaotic moves during heavy downpours.

“Any evacuation or relocation will be done humanely, will be communicated in good time, and will be done in a manner that gives dignity to the affected people,” Kindiki stated, calling on residents in designated flood basins to cooperate with disaster response personnel as the mid-October timeline approaches.
