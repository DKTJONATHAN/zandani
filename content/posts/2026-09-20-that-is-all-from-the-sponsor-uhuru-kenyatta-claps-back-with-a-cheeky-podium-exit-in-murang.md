Former President Uhuru Kenyatta has found a novel way to manage the political crosshairs trained on his retirement: taking the government's sharpest insults and turning them into his closing punchlines.

Taking the microphone on Friday, September 18, 2026, during a burial service in Gikuu village, Kiharu Sub-County, Murang’a County, the retired Head of State offered an unusually brief appearance. Instead of laying out a detailed political rebuttal to recent broadsides from the Kenya Kwanza administration, Uhuru delivered a dry, tongue-in-cheek exit line that instantly flipped the script on his accusers.

Addressing the mourners with deliberate irony, Uhuru announced that the era of him offering freewheeling, off-the-cuff political lectures was effectively on pause because speaking his mind in public had turned into a hazardous occupation.

“And usually I speak off-cuff, and these days life is becoming too dangerous for me. So I have decided before the media goes to misinterpret my words—thanks so much, that is all from the sponsor,” Uhuru said, before handing back the microphone.

### Embracing the Punchline

The quip was a direct, satirical nod to the label slapped on him barely 24 hours earlier by his successor, President William Ruto. During a development tour in Sindo, Homa Bay County, on Thursday, September 17, Ruto escalated his rhetorical campaign against Uhuru, openly accusing him of acting as the hidden financier behind high-profile opposition agitation.

In Sindo, President Ruto had singled out Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna, alleging that the former president was channeling funds to vocal critics out of frustration over the current administration's economic moves and infrastructure agenda. The narrative of Uhuru acting as an unseen political benefactor—or "sponsor"—has been a recurring motif from the ruling coalition, designed to frame opposition criticism as purchased outrage rather than organic dissent.

Yet rather than issuing an indignant legal denial or deploying a spokesperson to issue a formal rebuttal, Uhuru chose the village podium in Murang’a to own the moniker with performative self-deprecation.

### The Art of the Micro-Speech

Uhuru’s calculated brevity in Gikuu reflects a growing pattern in his post-presidency appearances. By acknowledging that "life is becoming too dangerous" for unscripted remarks, he managed to mock the intense state surveillance and scrutiny placed on his movements while dodging the trap of fueling a multi-day political cycle with extended political arguments.

The dry sign-off—“that is all from the sponsor”—allowed Uhuru to acknowledge the Homa Bay broadsides without dignifying the specifics of Ruto's financial claims regarding Senator Sifuna. By walking away immediately after delivering the line, he left the audience laughing and denied his political rivals fresh substantive material to parse over the weekend.

With political temperatures rising ahead of upcoming alignments, the Kiharu appearance demonstrates that while Uhuru may be keeping his formal speeches to a minimum, he remains keenly tuned in to the insults directed at him—and more than willing to turn them into public theater.
