Members of Parliament have ordered the Kenya Universities and Colleges Central Placement Service to submit a list of higher learning institutions withholding KSh500 million in placement fees. CEO Mercy Wahome faced the National Assembly Education Committee in Parliament Buildings, Nairobi, over unremitted funds collected from thousands of government-sponsored students.

**What we know**
* KUCCPS CEO Mercy Wahome appeared before the National Assembly Education Committee in Nairobi to answer audit questions.
* Universities, colleges, and TVET institutions owe KUCCPS KSh500 million in unremitted KSh1,500 placement fees per student.
* Administrators claim students never reported to campus to avoid remitting placement cash, yet claim government capitation for those same learners.
* Total public university debt across Kenya has surpassed KSh100 billion, led by Egerton University at KSh25.5 billion.

Wahome admitted to the committee that several vice-chancellors and college principals pocket the mandatory KSh1,500 fee paid by applicants but fail to forward the money to the placement agency. Students pay KSh500 while still in secondary school and top up KSh1,000 during their first course revision. Non-school candidates pay the whole KSh1,500 upfront.

When KUCCPS sends invoices to collect its dues, university administrators claim the listed students never showed up on campus. Yet those same institutions turn around and submit the same student names to the Universities Fund to secure government capitation and scholarship billions. Lawmakers warned that doctoring enrollment registers to play both sides borders on criminal misinformation.

The audit hits hard because Kenya public universities are choking on KSh100 billion in debt. Egerton University in Njoro, Nakuru County, tops the list with KSh25.5 billion in unpaid bills and unremitted statutory deductions. The University of Nairobi on Waiyaki Way owes KSh17 billion, Kenyatta University along Thika Road owes KSh12.8 billion, and Moi University in Eldoret owes KSh10.4 billion.

Lawmakers gave the placement agency a short deadline to produce individual breakdowns showing how much each university owes, how long the arrears have been sitting on balance sheets, and why enforcement officers failed to collect the revenue for years.

### The Nairobi read

Vice-chancellors are running high-stakes shell games with public cash. A parent squeezes out KSh1,500 via Mobile Money from a kiosk in Kasarani or Kibera, trusting it secures a degree path for their child. That cash goes straight into a university bank account, disappears into pending bills, and the placement body gets ghosted. When the same university claims capitation for a missing student, that is not an accounting oversight. That is deliberate book-cooking.

While commuters sit in traffic past Kenyatta University or the UoN main campus, the real story is that higher education management in Kenya is operating on borrowed time. The institutions are broke, owing over KSh100 billion to suppliers, taxmen, and pension funds. But stealing from placement fees to patch hole-ridden budgets leaves students caught in the middle. Parliament must move past parliamentary tongue-lashings. Freeze the accounts of defaulting vice-chancellors, force remittance at the source, and publish the names of those playing with young Kenyans' futures.
