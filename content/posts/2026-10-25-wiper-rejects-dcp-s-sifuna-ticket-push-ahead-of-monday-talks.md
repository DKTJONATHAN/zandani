Kalonzo Musyoka’s Wiper Patriotic Front has rejected an attempt by the Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP) to unilaterally settle the opposition’s 2027 presidential ticket, repudiating claims that negotiations are underway to draft the former Vice President as running mate to Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna.

The pushback follows public declarations by DCP Deputy Party Leader Cleophas Malala, who stated that his party had already settled on Sifuna—the Linda Mwananchi Movement (LMM) leader—to face President William Ruto, and intended to meet Kalonzo on Monday to formalise the arrangement.

Wiper spokesperson Ndegwa Njiru dismissed the planned engagement as an unsanctioned political manoeuvre designed to sow discord rather than forge an institutional alliance.

### Pushback on Unilateral Ticket Decisions

Speaking on behalf of Wiper, Njiru instructed the public and political observers to disregard Malala's overtures, arguing that the former Kakamega senator possessed no mandate to structure the opposition's candidate hierarchy.

![collage of Wiper Leader Kalonzo Musyoka and Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna at separate events](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/kalonzo-sifuna.jpg.webp?itok=VV78IrOS)

"I urge the media to treat this statement with the contempt it deserves. The remarks attributed to Senator Malala can only be characterised as the work of a playwright rather than a serious political actor," Njiru said. "Malala appears intent on driving a divisive knife through the heart of the opposition."

The public rebuke exposes the fragile state of consensus-building among anti-Ruto factions. Malala had announced the Monday meeting on Saturday in Kakamega during a welcoming ceremony for Kakamega DCP Youth leader Lewis Ndumbi, asserting that DCP's top brass had already made up their minds.

"You saw Rigathi Gachagua and Sifuna shake hands recently; we will also talk to Kalonzo on Monday so that he can back Sifuna. Gachagua and I have already agreed to back him for the seat," Malala said, presenting the lineup as a settled compact awaiting Kalonzo’s accession.

### The Shadow Over Consensus Mechanics

While Kalonzo had earlier signalled personal openness to deputising Sifuna, Wiper's institutional reaction demonstrates that formal negotiations have neither endorsed that configuration nor accepted DCP's pace.

![DCP Deputy Party Leader Cleophas Malala during an event in Kakamega County on Octiber 24, 2026](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/malala_4.jpg.webp?itok=OtNcZvAG)

The standoff underscores a recurring structural vulnerability in the opposition’s push for a single presidential ticket: the absence of an agreed candidate-selection protocol. Instead of formal deliberations, individual principals and their deputies have resorted to unilateral public announcements to box rivals into subordinate roles.

The scramble for the flagbearer role has also unleashed open accusations of internal sabotage across the coalescing parties. Rather than debating coalition platforms, principals and party executives are trading warnings that rival factions are covertly coordinating with the state to fragment the anti-Ruto vote.

DCP Secretary General John Methu recently escalated that friction, directly accusing Kiharu MP Ndindi Nyoro and Jubilee Party Leader Fred Matiang'i of collaborating with President Ruto to derail the joint ticket initiative from within.

### Looming Deadline Amid Solo Threats

Former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua has maintained that the opposition front remains coherent, insisting that a single presidential candidate will be unveiled in due course. Yet that posture conflicts with warnings from several coalition principals, who have threatened to mount independent presidential campaigns if the ticket negotiations bypass their candidacies.

With Wiper formally disowning Malala's overture, the proposed Monday dialogue between DCP and Kalonzo faces immediate procedural resistance. Until the principals agree on whether a ticket will be determined through delegate consensus, polling, or backroom horse-trading, premature endorsements such as DCP’s are likely to deepen mistrust among the very leaders attempting to forge a united front.
