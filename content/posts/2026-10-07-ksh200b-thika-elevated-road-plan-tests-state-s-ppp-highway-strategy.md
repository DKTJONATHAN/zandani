Financing a multi-billion shilling second layer of asphalt above an already crowded highway has thrown the state’s reliance on Public-Private Partnerships into sharp relief.

Preliminary estimates place the proposed 60-kilometre Nairobi-Thika Expressway between Ksh150 billion and Ksh200 billion. The ambitious project, which kicked off with design and feasibility studies announced by Deputy President Kithure Kindiki, will run from Thika town to Museum Hill in Nairobi, tying directly into the existing Nairobi Expressway.

![Traffic Disruption in Thika Road](https://i.ibb.co/3267H3X/a9371c83994a.webp)

The engineering concept calls for an elevated structure hovering above the current Thika Superhighway—itself a landmark infrastructure investment completed over a decade ago. According to government timelines, the feasibility study must determine critical parameters including engineering constraints, traffic capacity, environmental impacts, land acquisition needs, and exact construction costs.

This capital-intensive undertaking arrives while the treasury is already heavily leveraged across other major transport arteries. Kindiki noted that the Rironi-Mau Summit highway alone has absorbed a Ksh200 billion commitment, with its initial sections from Rironi to Naivasha and Mai Mahiu slated for completion by December. Concurrently, contracts have been awarded to dual sections of Kiambu Road from Muthaiga.

![Nairobi Expressway](https://i.ibb.co/MyrGX4J5/68179ee20656.webp)

For a transport sector heavily reliant on private capital partnerships to bridge public budget deficits, stacking multi-billion shilling mega-projects creates acute fiscal exposure. While the PPP model shifts initial construction burdens away from direct exchequer allocations, it locks motorists into long-term tolling arrangements to recoup private investments.

The upcoming feasibility findings will determine whether the projected price tag holds firm and how engineers plan to navigate land requirements and traffic management along one of the country's busiest economic corridors without bringing daily commerce to a complete halt.
