Public servants attached to the Ministry of Education must report physically to ministerial headquarters this week or lose their pay, under emergency screening terms that offer no dispensations for officers away on approved leave or recuperating in hospital.

The unbending directive, communicated to heads of directorates through an internal memo dated Friday, October 16, converts Jogoo House into a physical muster station for three separate days—Monday, October 19, Wednesday, October 21, and Thursday, October 22. It marks the first operational escalation of an executive purge meant to halt a documented Ksh6.2 billion payroll haemorrhage across central government.

Under the verification rules issued by the ministry, every personnel record must be validated against a living, documented individual on the ground floor of Jogoo House. Workers are required to produce their original national identity cards, Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA) personal identification numbers, and original letters covering both their initial appointment and current posting.

### Screening at the Door

![Public Service Commission House](https://i.ibb.co/BHh0Dh8h/0f900313acd3.webp)

The internal memo eliminates traditional bureaucratic exemptions. Any employee who fails to clear the physical verification table faces immediate administrative removal from the ministerial payroll.

"All employees are required to present themselves for the exercise, including those on leave and those in hospitals, with those failing to undergo the process risking their salaries being stopped," the circular instructed directorate heads, tasking them with ensuring absolute compliance among all personnel under their direct supervision.

The stringent posture follows an extensive payroll examination carried out across state departments and devolved units by the State Department for Public Service and Human Capital. That inquiry assessed 53 state departments and red-flagged 12 for severe payroll anomalies, totaling Ksh6.2 billion in questionable disbursements.

The scale of the leakage prompted State House intervention during a Cabinet meeting on June 30, where the executive ordered the Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) to open criminal proceedings into human resource desks across ministries, extra-ministerial agencies, and state corporations.

![DCI headquarters off Kiambu Road, Nairobi](https://i.ibb.co/yBMgJSSD/17cfa9a304ba.webp)

### Criminal Dragnet Expands

The Jogoo House verification operates parallel to an expanding forensic probe by detectives. In July, the DCI and the Public Service ministry initiated technical reviews aimed at tracing personal employment numbers used in payroll processing, mapping illicit remuneration flows, and confiscating funds siphon off through institutional schemes.

Initial findings by investigators detailed elaborate manipulations within personnel rolls, including files where compensation packages were routinely assigned to minors, unborn children, and entirely fictitious identities.

Accountability measures have already disrupted senior administrative ranks in other key departments. Across the Ministry of Health, the Kenya Prisons Service, and the Kenya Police Service, 84 human resource managers were ordered to step aside for 30 working days to prevent interference with ongoing evidentiary audits.

With the Education docket opening the physical muster phase, the screening net is expected to widen across the remaining 11 flagged departments, testing whether physical verification can dismantle entrenched payroll rackets without shutting out legitimate workers caught on sickbeds or remote duty stations.
