The Central Bank of Kenya’s statutory tolerance ceiling is suddenly under direct strain. 

Fresh figures published Friday by the Kenya National Bureau of Statistics (KNBS) show headline inflation climbed to 7.3 per cent in October, leaving monetary authorities with an uncomfortably narrow 0.2 percentage-point cushion beneath their mandated 7.5 per cent upper limit. 

The acceleration from September's 6.8 per cent marks the culmination of an aggressive eight-month inflationary climb. After bottoming out at 4.3 per cent in February, the Consumer Price Index tracked upward to 5.6 per cent in April, touched 6.7 per cent in May, and hovered in the mid-sixes through the third quarter before breaking sharply upward this month.

### The 17.1% Transport Shock

![OTC Bus Station](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/transport.jpg.webp?itok=nqOELpZy)

The primary engine behind this latest push was not a seasonal harvest failure, but a sharp re-pricing across the transit corridor. Transport costs jumped 17.1 per cent year-on-year, representing the single largest price escalation across all tracked expenditure categories.

That surge reflects immediate pass-through effects from higher pump prices. During October, national average retail prices for super petrol advanced from Ksh214.95 to Ksh227.85 per litre, while diesel rose to Ksh228.77 from Ksh219.04. Illuminating kerosene recorded a 14.7 per cent jump within the month, climbing from Ksh192.56 to Ksh220.88 per litre.

Commuters felt the impact within days. On suburban feeder routes such as Nairobi's Westlands-to-Kangemi corridor, standard matatu fares climbed 25 per cent, moving from Ksh40 to Ksh50 per trip as operators adjusted to the fuel reality.

### Core Inflation Begins to Drift

The broader risk for economic managers lies in how volatile fuel costs are beginning to seep into underlying prices. KNBS data shows non-core inflation jumped from 14.0 per cent in September to 15.1 per cent in October. More critically, core inflation—which strips out volatile food and energy items—drifted upward from 4.0 per cent to 4.3 per cent, signaling that generalized price pressures are cementing.

Food and non-alcoholic beverages, which hold substantial weight in consumer baskets, expanded at an annual pace of 10.2 per cent. A standard 500ml packet of processed milk increased to Ksh64.13 from Ksh61.23, while loose fresh milk moved from Ksh77.54 to Ksh80.58 per litre. Cooking oil edged higher to Ksh361.04 per litre.

![Photo of Supermarket Shelves In Kenya](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/supermarket_shelf_in_kenya_0.jpg.webp?itok=on5ujOoM)

Dry goods and market produce followed a parallel pattern. Irish potatoes rose to Ksh125.06 per kilogram from Ksh121.98, cabbages reached Ksh82.38, and beef with bones ticked up to Ksh789.29 per kilogram. Two-kilogram packets of wheat flour crept to Ksh184.88, while dry beans climbed to Ksh185.75.

Household energy provided almost no offsetting relief. A 13-kilogram refilled liquefied petroleum gas cylinder averaged Ksh3,429.61, up slightly from Ksh3,419.24. Even isolated decreases were negligible: domestic electricity for a 200-unit monthly bracket eased by just four shillings to Ksh5,529.66, while sugar dropped modestly from Ksh165.79 to Ksh163.84 per kilogram.

### Tightening the Policy Corridor

Combined, the three expenditure divisions of food, transport, and household energy now command over 57 per cent of total household spending weight. With all three showing simultaneous upward momentum over the past year—housing, water, and fuel rising 3.7 per cent alongside double-digit leaps in transit and food—the space for organic price cooling has thinned.

For policymakers on Central Bank Road, the October return effectively ends the comfort of the middle-band target. With inflation having advanced by three full percentage points since the first quarter and pump hikes fully established across supply chains, the central bank heads into November with virtually no mathematical room left before an official breach of its 7.5 per cent threshold.
