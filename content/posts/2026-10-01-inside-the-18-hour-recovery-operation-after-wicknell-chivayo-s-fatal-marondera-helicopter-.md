Recovery teams in Marondera spent nearly a grueling 18 hours combing a fractured 100-metre debris field before the remains of Wicknell Chivayo, his wife Lucy, and four others could be cleared from the wreckage of a Bell 430 helicopter.

The aircraft went down at approximately 5 pm local time as it traveled toward Harare, carrying four passengers and two crew members. The sheer velocity and severity of the impact left human remains scattered across a wide expanse of rural terrain, forcing emergency and security personnel to execute a meticulous, slow-paced grid search before any transport of the victims could begin.

Family members confirmed the deaths of the high-profile Zimbabwean businessman and his spouse following the evening tragedy, while government representatives verified that no one on board survived the impact.

Chivayo, a fixture in Zimbabwean commercial circles known for high-level enterprise and political proximity, had his life abruptly cut short mid-flight, shifting the public focus from his corporate dealings to the grim logistics of the crash site.

As the remains were finally cleared after nearly a full day on-site, aviation and law enforcement authorities confirmed that no preliminary findings or definitive causes for the descent had been established. Investigators are expected to review flight logs, weather conditions, and mechanical systems of the Bell 430 to determine what triggered the fatal afternoon disaster.
