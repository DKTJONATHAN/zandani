Groundwork on a Ksh50 million pilgrimage shrine atop Baringo County’s second-highest ridge has run into fierce community resistance after planners commenced site operations without filing a mandatory Environmental Impact Assessment report with the National Environment Management Authority (NEMA).

Residents of Ewalel Chapchap Ward took to the slopes of Morop Hill on Wednesday, October 28, demanding an immediate freeze on all activity tied to the tourism infrastructure project. Demonstrators revealed that clearing and preparation works moved ahead despite a total absence of public participation forums and statutory environmental oversight.

The early ground operations directly threaten the hydrology of the 2,720-metre ridge. Approximately 15 natural springs emerge from Morop Hill's upper slopes, providing the primary gravity-fed water supply for thousands of households across the surrounding valley settlements. 

### Bypassing Statutory Checks

![Morop Hills residents in Baringo County protesting over the proposed construction of a Ksh50 million pilgrimage shrine on October 28.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/morop-hills-residents-october-28-2.jpg.webp?itok=KZwrKfHi)

Local administrative records indicate that the community sought formal intervention nearly two months ago. Residents petitioned Baringo Governor Benjamin Cheboi’s office on September 3, raising alarms over quiet attempts to introduce heavy civil construction into an unshielded water catchment.

Rather than pausing site assessments to address the petition, county authorities maintained silence until setting an official public participation session for October 29—a day after preliminary ground preparations had already started. The county executive has not offered a public explanation for why works began ahead of both the statutory EIA submission and the civic forum.

The proposed development blueprint features a towering monument cross measuring between 15 and 30 metres, alongside a dedicated prayer chapel, visitor walkways, and accompanying commercial tourism infrastructure. 

Located 10 kilometres west of Kabarnet Town, Morop Hill forms the primary southern backbone of the Tugen Hills, trailing only Saimo Hill in elevation. Its vantage point overlooks Lake Baringo, Lake Bogoria, and Lake Kamnarok, making its steep slopes a prime target for religious tourism expansion at the expense of its protective tree cover.

![Morop Hills, Baringo County.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/morop-hills.jpg.webp?itok=sQBlP_cy)

### Cultural and Water Security at Stake

Beyond its function as a vital regional watershed, the hill serves as a protected historical landmark for the local community. The site houses Kikojo Falls—a historic cultural shrine where the Tugen community performed rainmaking ceremonies—and contains preserved grounds marking inland slave trade routes.

Local elders maintain that converting the summit into an intensive tourism hub compromises irreplaceable socio-cultural heritage and destabilizes the shallow aquifers feeding downslope agricultural belts.

The dispute mirrors growing legal scrutiny over public infrastructure initiated on culturally and environmentally protected parcels across Kenya. In April 2026, the Environment and Land Court in Thika suspended the Githunguri affordable housing development after petitioners proved that the designated 58-acre parcel encompassed gazetted national monuments, Mau Mau war graves, and colonial-era execution sites.

Community representatives on Morop Hill have signaled they may pursue similar judicial relief should the county executive attempt to ratify pre-existing site clearings during the October 29 public meeting. For now, residents insist that no machinery will be permitted past the ridge line until an independent environmental audit is commissioned and formal clearances are granted.
