Social Health Authority (SHA) Chief Executive Officer Dr. Mercy Mwangangi has drawn a hard operational line for Kenya's healthcare providers, granting a 14-day grace period to complete their 2026–2029 contracts while making it clear that leniency has an immediate expiration date.

In an official directive issued on October 1, 2026, Mwangangi confirmed that medical facilities whose previous contracts expired on September 30 will be permitted to continue offering services under their existing terms. However, the temporary lifeline comes with an unyielding boundary: the window closes permanently on October 14, 2026, with zero further extensions on the table.

To lock in that temporary coverage and maintain unbroken patient care, facility administrators face a tight clock. SHA has required healthcare operators to log into its e-contracting portal, sign, and return the temporary extension agreement within three days. State-backed funding for medical procedures will remain active only for facilities that complete this explicit digital acceptance within the designated timeframe.

For facilities choosing not to sign, Mwangangi’s directive outlines an immediate exit strategy. Operators opting out of the 2026–2029 cycle are required to coordinate directly with SHA county managers to organize the orderly transfer of ongoing patients to fully compliant, contracted hospitals.

To clear administrative bottlenecks across the country, SHA is deploying dedicated operational support. Starting Monday, October 5, 2026, the authority will roll out specialized “HAKIKA” contracting clinics across all county offices to help healthcare operators resolve paperwork glitches and finalize documentation.

More than 10,000 healthcare providers have already entered the authority's 2026–2029 contracting pipeline at various stages. With the HAKIKA clinics opening on October 5 and the final October 14 cliff-edge approaching, the burden now shifts to health facility owners to meet the three-day digital sign-off mandate or initiate patient transfers.
