The confirmation of Safari Rally Kenya on the 2027 World Rally Championship (WRC) calendar has set off a high-stakes financial countdown for local organizers, who must now deliver the global motorsport event without a single cent of direct taxpayer funding.

While President William Ruto celebrated the Fédération Internationale de l’Automobile’s (FIA) decision to keep Kenya on the 15-round global circuit, the announcement shifts immediate pressure onto a newly restructured secretariat tasked with keeping the multimillion-shilling event afloat through corporate sponsorships alone.

Under the official 2027 calendar confirmed by the FIA on Thursday, October 8, the Safari Rally will run as the fifth round of the championship, scheduled for March 18 to 21. It retains its iconic venue, routing international drivers across the plains and valleys of Naivasha. Kenya joins an elite list of 15 host nations spanning five continents, alongside seasoned circuits such as Monaco, Sweden, Finland, and Japan, as well as newer additions like Paraguay and Saudi Arabia.

![Finnish rally driver Esapekka Lappi during the shakedown at the WRC Safari Rally Championship 2026, Thursday, March 12, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/35dtQRs5/77cebb350e3b.webp)

Kenya’s inclusion ends lingering uncertainty over the event's long-term future. Since returning to the WRC stage in 2021 after a 19-year absence, the rally’s place had only been contractually guaranteed through the 2026 season under its initial bilateral agreement.

However, the 2027 edition will mark a radical departure from the financing models of the past five years. During the closing ceremony of the 2026 competition in March, President Ruto announced that the state would completely pull back its financial machinery from the event starting in 2027.

This withdrawal of public utility funding is part of a broader government strategy to redirect sports spending. According to the state, the heavy capital outlays previously used to subsidize and promote the prestigious rally will instead be channeled to support school sports and other grassroots sporting activities across the country.

![Ruto WRC](https://i.ibb.co/93t86t1B/5029c8307620.webp)

The responsibility of navigating this unprecedented financial pivot falls squarely on veteran Kenyan rally driver Carl Tundo, who was appointed Chief Executive Officer of the WRC Safari Rally Project in June 2026 by Sports Cabinet Secretary Salim Mvurya. Tundo, a multiple Safari Rally winner who understands the immense logistical and financial demands of the terrain, must now build a private-sector coalition capable of absorbing the millions previously guaranteed by the National Treasury.

While previous editions enjoyed direct state promotion and fiscal cushions, the 2027 event will have to prove its independent commercial viability to corporate brands. Beyond the physical challenge of the Naivasha dirt, the true test of the 2027 Safari Rally will be fought in corporate boardrooms.

With the March 2027 dates now locked, Tundo and his team have less than six months to secure the corporate partnerships required to fund safety, logistics, and international broadcasting standards demanded by the FIA. The success of this corporate push will determine whether the Safari Rally can maintain its world-class stature or if the withdrawal of state funds will compromise the quality of one of Africa’s most celebrated sporting spectacles.
