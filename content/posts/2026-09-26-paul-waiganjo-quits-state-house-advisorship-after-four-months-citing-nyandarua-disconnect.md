Barely four months after stepping inside the corridors of power at State House Nairobi, gospel musician-turned-politician Paul Waiganjo has made a sharp about-turn, walking away from his post as President William Ruto’s adviser on development matters.

Waiganjo announced his resignation during a press briefing in Nairobi on Friday, September 25, 2026, revealing that he formally tendered his exit at 3 p.m. He argued that he could not in good conscience hold a mandate he felt unable to execute meaningfully.

“Because I refuse to hold a mandate I cannot meaningfully execute, I have made the firm, principled, and good faith decision to tender my resignation,” Waiganjo said, marking a swift conclusion to an appointment that began on May 28 and was later highlighted during a church service in Ol Kalou by Tourism Cabinet Secretary Rebecca Miano.

The artist-turned-politician, whose political trajectory previously involved the Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP) and the Ol Kalou parliamentary contest before aligning with the ruling United Democratic Alliance (UDA), said his time in office exposed a stark chasm between official titles and conditions on the ground. 

Before making his decision, Waiganjo stated that he spent time consulting local clergy, professionals, entrepreneurs, and young people in his home county of Nyandarua. Those talks, he noted, revealed a painful mismatch between the ambitious vision he held for the region and the actual framework of his advisory role.

He pointed to pressing local struggles, questioning how he could continue drawing a salary from a position while local hospitals faced critical resource shortages, school dropout rates spiked due to delayed capitation, and hardworking citizens grappled with heavy statutory deductions.

“A profound disconnect existed between my state role and the pressing struggles on the ground,” Waiganjo said, adding that remaining in the role under such circumstances would constitute a fundamental betrayal of his oath.

When he initially accepted the appointment, Waiganjo had hoped to influence development areas such as school construction, hospital upgrades, borehole drilling, street lighting, and job creation. However, consultations in Nyandarua continually surfaced persistent hurdles, including inadequate roads, unreliable electricity, and post-harvest agricultural losses.

Waiganjo did not immediately declare a specific elective seat for the upcoming 2027 polls, though his remarks signalled an ongoing focus on Nyandarua County and potential future interest in the Ol Kalou parliamentary race or another local position. He also used the briefing to register his opposition to a second term for President Ruto, asserting that political shifts in the Mt Kenya region would be determined through voter registration and the ballot box.
