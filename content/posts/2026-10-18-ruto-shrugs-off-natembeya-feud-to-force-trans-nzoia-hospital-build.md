William Ruto has drawn a hard line against Governor George Natembeya, declaring that personal bad blood and political hostility at the county executive level will not block his administration’s health infrastructure push in Trans Nzoia.

Addressing the simmering turf war between State House and the county leadership, the President made it clear he has no intention of seeking permission or negotiating truce terms before deploying national resources to the county’s medical network.

“Hospitals will be built even if the governor has a problem,” Ruto stated, brushing aside the political standoff that has characterised relations between the two leaders.

### Bypassing County Executive Resistance

The blunt public dismissal brings the friction between Ruto and Natembeya out of backroom speculation and onto the public record. While devolved governance structures typically require tight coordination between governors and the national executive on major capital projects, Ruto signalled that public health facilities will proceed regardless of local executive posturing.

The Head of State argued that the proposed Trans Nzoia facility is meant to serve ordinary residents whose day-to-day medical needs cannot be treated as bargaining chips or collateral damage in protracted political rivalries. By directly confronting Natembeya’s stance, Ruto positioned the project as an executive priority designed to stand above personal disputes.

The standoff in Trans Nzoia is not an isolated undertaking. It anchors a wider regional healthcare rollout currently moving across Western Kenya and the North Rift, where the national government is pushing specialised health facilities into spaces traditionally reliant on overburdened referral networks.

### The Wider Regional Health Grid

According to the President, the Trans Nzoia project links directly to parallel facilities currently taking shape in Kapsabet, Kakamega, and Bungoma. The regional push follows the recent commissioning of another critical healthcare site in Kitale, setting up an infrastructure corridor meant to ease pressure on major national referral institutions.

Beyond the western belt, Ruto detailed an active pipeline of 10 hospital construction projects underway across varied geographic blocs, citing active works in Bomet, Embu, and Kwale. 

The central logic of the capital spending, Ruto noted, centres on eliminating the punishing logistical costs faced by patients seeking advanced care. For decades, families outside major cities have had to finance long-distance travel and emergency transit simply to reach specialised diagnosis and treatment centres. 

Developing fully equipped, specialised centres within local borders is designed to eliminate that journey, ensuring families are spared the financial strain of seeking routine or critical care outside their home counties.

### Expanding Beyond 2026

The current construction footprint marks the opening phase of a multi-year institutional expansion. Ruto announced that the national government has scheduled ground-breaking on an additional 15 hospitals starting in 2027.

That multi-year timeline signals that the administration’s strategy of planting major national installations inside devolved territories will continue into the next election cycle, setting up further potential friction with independent-minded county chiefs.

For Trans Nzoia, however, the immediate reality is an executive green light issued directly over the head of Governor Natembeya. While political temperatures between the county boss and State House remain unresolved, the national government has signalled that contractors will move forward on site, establishing the hospital as an immovable ground reality.
