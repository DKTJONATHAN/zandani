Busia Senator Okiya Omtatah is not stepping into the ring against Edwin Sifuna’s nascent political outfit, despite what a trending social media graphic wants the internet to believe.

The veteran activist and legislator moved swiftly to shut down a widely circulated digital card that claimed he was preparing to dismantle the Linda Mwananchi Movement in court. Omtatah dismissed the artwork as cheap digital bait concocted by online operatives seeking traffic at the expense of substantive political decorum.

“This is clearly misinformation, seemingly intended to generate clicks and online engagement,” Omtatah stated, pulling the rug out from under the circulating rumour.

The fake card, timestamped Saturday, October 31, attempted to mimic mainstream news alerts. It quoted the senator allegedly brushing off the movement’s debut as an irrelevance and issuing a direct threat to pull the plug on its existence through litigation.

“The Linda Mwananchi Movement launch is inconsequential in Kenya’s political landscape. I will challenge its legality in the coming weeks,” the text on the disputed graphic declared.

Yet for anyone familiar with Omtatah’s actual public track record, the card carried an immediate giveaway long before the senator issued his denial. The anonymous creators botched the lawmaker’s identity entirely, plastering the quote across an attribution to an invented “Okiya Omutatah.”

Beyond laughing off the typographical blunder, Omtatah drew a sharp line between his well-known courtroom crusades and petty political mudslinging. For a lawmaker whose career has been defined by dense constitutional petitions, public interest litigation, and meticulous legal filings, being painted as a reckless political sniper did not sit well.

“I prefer to engage on issues with facts, reason and mutual respect,” Omtatah noted. “I do not trade in insults or descend into unnecessary exchanges. That is simply not my style.”

The fabricated attack landed right in the middle of a high-stakes transition for Sifuna’s political vehicle. Formerly registered as The Equitable Party (TEP), Linda Mwananchi has spent recent weeks rebranding and positioning itself as an alternative platform ahead of the 2027 General Election.

The outfit unveiled its proposed top hierarchy and secretariat between October 30 and October 31, setting off intense chatter across the political divide. Under the newly announced structure, Sifuna stepped up as the party leader-designate, backed by Vihiga Senator Godfrey Osotsi as the proposed national chairperson.

The roster also pulled in prominent household names looking to recalibrate their political fortunes. Among those named to the executive lineup were former Meru Governor Kawira Mwangaza, former Law Society of Kenya president Faith Odhiambo, Mavoko MP Patrick Makau, and former Rarieda MP Caroli Omondi.

With high-profile figures boarding the movement, online spaces quickly turned into a battleground of competing narratives, making Omtatah an attractive target for digital puppeteers. His reputation as Kenya’s premier legal disruptor often lends instant credibility to any claim of an impending court challenge.

By publicly calling out the fraud, Omtatah effectively neutralised attempts to frame him as an early spoiler to Sifuna’s grand rollout. While the senator remains a fixture in the corridors of justice, he made it abundantly clear that any legal battle bearing his name will be drafted by his own hand—properly spelled, grounded in statute, and argued before a judge rather than peddled on digital posters.
