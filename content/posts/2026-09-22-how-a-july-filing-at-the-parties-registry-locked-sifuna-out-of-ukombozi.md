A paper race at the Office of the Registrar of Political Parties (ORPP) has decisively settled the first territorial clash of Kenya’s emerging opposition realignments, giving Kalonzo Musyoka’s coalition legal custody of the "Ukombozi" brand while shutting down rival efforts by allies of Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna.

While politicians traded public accusations over who first coined the political slogan, regulatory records show that bureaucratic timing—rather than campaign trail rhetoric—closed the door on Sifuna's camp.

In a formal rejection letter dated September 8, 2026, the Registrar of Political Parties declined an application to rebrand the existing United Democratic Party (UDP) into the Ukombozi People's Party (UPP). The regulator determined that the proposed name was deceptively similar to a prior reservation: an application for "The Ukombozi Alliance" (TUA) formally lodged on July 20, 2026.

That single administrative reservation cleared the runway for Kalonzo to announce on Monday the full conversion of the Azimio La Umoja Coalition into The Ukombozi Alliance, flanked by Democratic Action Party-Kenya (DAP-K) leader Eugene Wamalwa.

### The Battle of the 2025 Filing Dates

With the regulatory registry closed against them, the two factions have retreated into conflicting accounts of chronological primacy dating back to early 2025.

Wiper Patriotic Front spokesperson Ndegwa Njiru moved to defend Kalonzo against claims of brand hijacking, arguing that the former Vice President had already anchored his political posture on the term while Sifuna was still bound to the Orange Democratic Movement’s institutional machinery.

"Records show that as early as April 29, 2025, while Sifuna was still ODM Secretary General and the broad-based government MoU was being signed, Kalonzo had been using the phrase 'Komboa Kenya'," Njiru said. He maintained that "Ukombozi" was simply drafted as a natural noun form from that linguistic foundation, adding that coalition partners had submitted their formal rebranding paperwork well ahead of any competitor.

Wamalwa corroborated that defense, stating that the opposition coalition had filed its identity paperwork before the label became linked to Sifuna’s circle.

![Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna walks with students from JKUAT Karen campus, September 17, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/2zbVRwR/22b9ba799f1a.webp)

Sources close to Sifuna, however, dispute that version of events. They contend that their faction took concrete steps to take over and rename the UDP vehicle to Ukombozi in March 2025—roughly a month before Kalonzo's cited public remarks.

### Realignment Pressures Outside the Broad-Based Fold

The dispute highlights the friction among opposition politicians attempting to construct clean, unencumbered political vehicles ahead of the next election cycle.

Ever since sections of ODM entered into a broad-based governing framework with the Kenya Kwanza administration, non-aligned figures and dissident opposition voices have scrambled to corner evocative liberation messaging. The term *ukombozi*—Swahili for deliverance or liberation—has become prime political real estate for actors seeking to distinguish themselves from both the state and its newly incorporated partners.

Yet the ORPP's decision proves that historical rhetoric offers zero legal protection in party registration. Because Sifuna’s camp failed to secure a verified hold on the moniker before the July 20 docket entry, their planned vehicle remains administratively grounded under its original UDP identity, leaving Kalonzo and Wamalwa in unchallenged control of the brand inside official gazettes.
