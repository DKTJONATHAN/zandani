According to the State Department for Roads, landlocked East African economies and local haulage operators are poised to reap significant logistics savings after Kenya obtained a multi-billion-shilling grant from the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank to kickstart preparations for the Mau Summit-Malaba Highway.

### What we know

* Treasury is increasingly leaning on Beijing-backed multilateral lenders like AIIB to de-risk ambitious transit projects before bringing private concessionaires to the table.
* Extending high-capacity dual carriageways toward Malaba secures Mombasa Port's competitive edge against rival Tanzanian transit corridors servicing Uganda and the DRC.
* KeNHA's push to include tolling models signals a permanent policy shift toward user-financed infrastructure as national debt ceilings constrain direct budgetary spending.
* Government strategists are bundling western highway connectivity with capacity upgrades at JKIA to present a unified multimodal investment package to Asian financiers.

The funding commitment was finalized during a high-level bilateral meeting between senior officials from the National Treasury, the State Department for Roads, and AIIB executives at the bank’s headquarters in Beijing on Wednesday.

“Kenya appreciates AIIB’s support through a grant agreement for project preparation for the Mau Summit-Malaba Road Development Project, an important section of the Northern Corridor and a critical regional trade route,” the State Department for Roads stated.

Confirming the deal following a media engagement in Kisumu earlier in the week, President William Ruto stated that the Asian fund had provided an initial Ksh300 million to execute comprehensive feasibility work across the strategic western transit routes.

“We have already discussed with the Asian Infrastructure Fund, and it has given us the first 300M to do the feasibility study to extend that road from Mau Summit-Kisumu-Malaba and the other leg Nakuru-Eldoret-Malaba,” President Ruto stated.

The Ksh130 billion highway project completed its preliminary pre-feasibility assessment in May, clearing the runway for a full-scale feasibility phase scheduled between July and September. Under the guidance of the National Treasury and the Kenya National Highways Authority (KeNHA), the upcoming competitive procurement process will evaluate private sector participation and user-pay tolling mechanisms to guarantee long-term maintenance and cost recovery.

The proposed dual carriageway will extend from Mau Summit through Eldoret to the Malaba border post, complementing the planned Nairobi-Mau Summit dualing project. Once complete, the expanded artery will eliminate persistent bottlenecks along the Northern Corridor, accelerating transit times for freight moving between the Port of Mombasa, Uganda, and the eastern Democratic Republic of Congo.

Beyond the highway corridor, state negotiators in Beijing are leveraging the newly broadened partnership to secure financial backing for aviation infrastructure.

“The Government is also engaging AIIB on the expansion and modernisation of Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (JKIA), including the proposed construction of a second runway, to enhance capacity and strengthen Kenya’s position as a regional aviation hub,” added the State Department for Roads.

The dual focus on highway capacity and airport expansion underscores Nairobi’s broader strategy to use multilateral grant funding to de-risk complex capital projects before inviting private investors through public-private partnership (PPP) frameworks. By securing AIIB's backing for pre-investment studies, the government aims to shorten project lead times while keeping direct sovereign debt exposure to a minimum.
