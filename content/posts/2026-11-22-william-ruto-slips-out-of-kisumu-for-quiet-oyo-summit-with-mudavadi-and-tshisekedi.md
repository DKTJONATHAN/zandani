President William Ruto wrapped up his tour of Western Kenya on Saturday evening, walked onto the tarmac at Kisumu International Airport, and took off for the Republic of Congo without the customary pre-travel advisory from State House Nairobi.

Rather than returning to the capital, the Head of State slipped out of the country accompanied by Prime Cabinet Secretary and Foreign and Diaspora Affairs Cabinet Secretary Musalia Mudavadi. By Saturday night, the Kenyan delegation had touched down in Oyo, a northern town in the Republic of Congo, answering a discreet summons from veteran President Denis Sassou-N’Guesso.

The quiet flight path underscored the delicate nature of the mission. While communication channels in Nairobi remained mute on his international movement, the Congolese presidency confirmed his presence on Sunday, November 22, 2026. The unpublicised trip brings Ruto into the same room as Democratic Republic of Congo President Félix Tshisekedi, marking an attempt to clear two years of political frost between Nairobi and Kinshasa.

### A Four-Way Table in Oyo

The gathering convened by Sassou-N’Guesso was not framed as a routine bilateral handshake. According to Congolese authorities and regional media dispatches, the Oyo mini-summit assembled four regional heads to  the deteriorating security climate in eastern DRC and the friction it has ignited across the East African Community.

Sharing the table alongside Ruto, Sassou-N’Guesso, and Tshisekedi was Togolese Prime Minister Faure Gnassingbé, establishing an expansive mediation corridor between Central and West African leadership. 

For Ruto, bringing Mudavadi directly along signaled that Kenya’s foreign policy apparatus was fully loaded for the encounter. Mudavadi has overseen diplomatic firefighting over the Great Lakes file, yet Saturday’s departure bypassed the typical media briefings that usually herald presidential foreign engagements.

### The Kinshasa Fallout

The core friction dragging Nairobi into Congolese crosshairs traces back to decisions made on Kenyan soil. Kinshasa’s diplomatic establishment has long accused Nairobi of providing a platform to Congolese dissidents and armed actors.

The rift opened sharply in December 2023, when high-end Nairobi venues hosted the launch of the Congo River Alliance (CRA)—a political and military umbrella grouping that openly aligned itself with the M23 rebel movement operating in eastern DRC. Authorities under Tshisekedi treated the launch as an act of diplomatic hostility from an East African partner.

Tensions flared again in October 2025 when Kenyan security permitted a major political gathering organized around former DRC President Joseph Kabila. Kinshasa interpreted the opposition conclave as further proof that Kenya was hedging against Tshisekedi’s administration, prompting frosty exchanges between both capitals.

### Defending the Neutrality Playbook

Ruto has repeatedly countered Congolese displeasure by defending Kenya’s traditional posture as a regional peace facilitator. The Kenyan leader has insisted that sustainable peace in the eastern DRC requires an open door for all players, advocating direct and inclusive dialogue between Tshisekedi’s government and the M23 insurgents.

Kinshasa has consistently rejected that posture, refusing to grant sovereign legitimacy to armed rebel factions and treating any regional platform that entertains them as an unfriendly party.

By traveling directly from Kisumu to Sassou-N’Guesso’s political stronghold in Oyo, Ruto opted for closed-door Central African mediation over megaphone diplomacy. Whether the face-to-face sit-down with Tshisekedi in northern Congo eases the two-year chill now rests on the outcome of the four-party discussions underway behind Oyo’s guarded doors.
