The United States is restructuring its foreign commercial strategy in East Africa, positioning American firms as technology vendors and processing partners rather than direct mine operators in Kenya’s critical minerals sector.

Speaking at a Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS) panel on Friday, September 18, U.S. Assistant Secretary of State for African Affairs Frank Garcia outlined Washington’s framework for commercial diplomacy, citing direct discussions held with President William Ruto during a visit to Nairobi the previous week.

According to Garcia, the strategy leverages 10 to 15 years of domestic research and development in extraction and refining systems. Instead of purchasing or operating physical concessions, American involvement will center on licensing and deploying specialized processing technologies to host nations.

“The United States made a decision early on that we weren’t necessarily going to be the miners, but we were going to be the technology leaders in going after the minerals,” Garcia said.

![Assistant Secretary of State for African Affairs Frank Garcia.](https://i.ibb.co/qLR9r1pg/bd6171ee69df.webp)

The deployment strategy targets a long-standing vulnerability in African mining economies: the export of unrefined raw ores that leaves the bulk of economic value in overseas processing hubs. By supplying specialized equipment capable of refining minerals near the point of extraction, Washington aims to satisfy local demands for value addition while building secure supply networks for critical raw materials.

Among the systems being pitched is multi-element recovery technology, which allows processing plants to extract multiple secondary minerals from a single ore stream during initial processing. Garcia noted that a U.S. firm is currently competing in a commercial tender in an unnamed African nation to deploy the multi-recovery system, though he did not specify whether similar formal bids have been submitted in Kenya.

For Kenya, where the government has increasingly tightened controls on raw mineral exports to stimulate domestic industrial processing, the U.S. proposal aligns with state priorities to establish local refining facilities. Discussions regarding a bilateral critical minerals framework between Nairobi and Washington are ongoing, with technical teams reviewing how joint processing projects will be funded and structured.
