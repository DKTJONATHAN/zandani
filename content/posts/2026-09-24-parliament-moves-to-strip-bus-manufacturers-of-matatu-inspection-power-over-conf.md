A legislative push in the Senate aims to break the Kenya Association of Bus Manufacturers' monopoly over public service vehicle body evaluations, citing an inherent conflict of interest in letting an industry group police its own technical standards.

The National Transport and Safety Authority (Amendment) Bill, 2026, currently before the Senate Standing Committee on Roads and Transportation, seeks to overhaul how matatus and other public service vehicles (PSVs) undergo safety conformity checks. Under the current framework, conformity assessments have rested with the industry's manufacturing association—a structural overlap that Parliament argues compromises objective oversight.

![NTSA officers assisting drivers with free motor vehicle inspection services](https://i.ibb.co/yFPHSBDL/e830c6d95f56.webp)

To correct this, the proposed legislation empowers the NTSA to register and license independent conformity assessors. The draft rules strictly bar any licensed assessor from holding design, manufacturing, supply, installation, purchase, ownership, or maintenance ties to the vehicles they inspect. 

Beyond reshaping who holds the inspection mandate, the Bill establishes a structured compliance timeline. NTSA will be tasked with prescribing the criteria for conformity checks and enforcing a mandatory three-year inspection cycle for all PSVs. , the authority will conduct periodic audits of the licensed assessors themselves to ensure ongoing compliance with statutory standards, filling a regulatory vacuum where no dedicated legal framework previously governed body approval processes.

![Senate sitting](https://i.ibb.co/YF9PBVgK/47bb26b9dc47.webp)

The Senate committee has opened a 14-day public participation window for motorists, matatu operators, and industry stakeholders to review the text. Written submissions must reach the Clerk of the Senate at Parliament Buildings in Nairobi or be submitted via email by 5 p.m. on Thursday, October 8, 2026, ahead of further legislative stages.
