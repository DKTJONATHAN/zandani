The Kenya Revenue Authority’s (KRA) announcement of a massive online auction in Mombasa has exposed a deep, multi-year logistical logjam at the coast. While public attention often focuses on the high-end luxury vehicles up for grabs, the official listing reveals a starker operational reality: some of the imported cargo has been sitting in Mombasa’s customs yards for up to a decade.

According to a Gazette notice dated September 18, 2026, the tax authority is putting hundreds of overstayed consignments under the hammer. The move leverages Section 42 of the East African Community Customs Management Act, 2004, which allows the state to treat goods as abandoned and auction them if they are not cleared within a prescribed period.

Importers have been handed a strict 30-day ultimatum from the date of the notice to settle their outstanding customs dues and retrieve their property. If they fail to do so, the goods will be sold to the highest bidder during an online auction scheduled for November 2 to 6, 2026.

### The Decade-Long Storage Lag

![Cars for auction at the Port of Mombasa](https://i.ibb.co/svj1PHtJ/8f52e1f0fe02.webp)

While the auction list features modern high-end SUVs—including Toyota Land Cruisers, Range Rovers, Mercedes-Benz GLE450s, and Audi Q7s—the customs data reveals that many of these assets have long since ceased to be active imports. 

Several of the listed vehicles were discharged at Mombasa's Container Freight Stations (CFS) as far back as 2016, 2017, and 2020. This means they have accumulated years of storage fees and experienced significant physical depreciation while trapped in legal, financial, or administrative limbo. 

These older, stagnant imports sit alongside newer shipments brought in between 2023 and 2025. The presence of decade-old vehicles highlights a systemic challenge for Mombasa's port logistics: the extreme lag in disposing of dead capital, which continues to occupy premium holding space at key freight facilities.

### Beyond Luxury Cars: An Industrial Clearance

The upcoming auction is not merely a car sale but a sweeping clearance of industrial and commercial cargo. The sheer variety of items listed under Cluster B2 at the Mombasa CFSs points to a broad failure by commercial importers to complete the clearance cycle.

Among the heavy-duty machinery facing liquidation are:
* Backhoe loaders, tractors, and agricultural equipment.
* Transmission line tower parts and greenhouse spare parts.
* Raw industrial chemicals, including separate consignments of methanol and ethanol.
* Heavy construction materials, including bronze reflective glass, stainless steel, and glazed tiles.

![Vehicles at an auction](https://i.ibb.co/FLM1ZQhR/97a5e0826d96.webp)

The backlog also extends to consumer goods and perishable food items that have long outlived their intended market cycles, such as Thai white rice, spaghetti, canned tuna, and brown sugar.

### The Cost of Abandonment

Under East African trade laws, cargo is typically sent to customs warehouses when importers face disputes over valuation, fail to secure import permits, or find themselves unable to pay the steep duties and accrued port storage charges. In many cases, when the accumulated demurrage and storage fees exceed the open-market value of the cargo, importers simply walk away.

By forcing a hard 30-day deadline, the KRA is attempting to free up vital physical space at the Mombasa port corridor and recover billions of shillings in unrealized tax revenues.

Prospective buyers will be allowed to inspect the sprawling inventory at designated Container Freight Stations on October 29 and 30, 2026, ahead of the five-day virtual bidding process.
