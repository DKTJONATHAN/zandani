Long before gym routines and scale milestones became public talking points, Eve Mungai found herself breaking down during an interview after being relentlessly targeted for her naturally slim frame.

Taking to Instagram, the prominent creator flashed back to a period when her weight hovered around 43kg, recalling how public commentary crossed the line into active cyberbullying. “Gosh, hapa kgs zilikuwa zimekatalia 45, yooh, you guys bullied me hadi nikalia kwa hii interview,” she shared, recounting the emotional toll of internet commentary that dissected her appearance when she was at her thinnest.

That vulnerability offers a stark look at the double-edged sword of digital visibility. For creators whose personal lives and physical transformations play out entirely online, shifting body weight rarely brings relief from unsolicited opinions. 

Months after that low-weight phase, Eve took on a structured fitness journey starting October 6, documenting her efforts to build strength. By December 2025, her weight had climbed to 62kg—a physical peak she embraced at the time, noting that she was the heaviest she had ever been and felt comfortable in her skin.

Yet, entering a heavier phase did little to silence the peanut gallery. Instead of drawing sympathy, her physical transformation triggered a completely inverted wave of criticism from followers demanding she revert to her previous slim silhouette.

Faced with a public that felt entitled to dictate her appearance from both ends of the scale, Eve drew a firm boundary in January 2026. Rejecting the notion that her audience held veto power over her physique, she made it clear that her physical presentation belonged to her alone. 

“You do not get to choose what version of me you like; you will prefer whatever body I decide to have because this is my world, not yours,” she stated at the time.

That friction highlights the unique pressure faced by digital personalities, where transitioning from one physical state to another simply replaces old criticisms with new ones, leaving creators to navigate a continuous cycle of public evaluation.
