Lang’ata MP Phelix Odiwuor Jalang’o stepped off the campaign trail and into high-level policy talks on Tuesday, joining Kasarani lawmaker Ronald Karauri and Interior Principal Secretary Dr. Raymond Omollo to spearhead urban disability interventions across Nairobi.

The consultative engagement, convened under the state-backed *Usawa ni Sawa* initiative, brought the two high-profile city legislators into direct dialogue with grassroots coordinators representing persons with disabilities (PWDs) drawn from across the capital’s sub-counties.

For Jalang’o, who made the high-visibility transition from mainstream comedy and broadcast radio to elective office, the session placed his legislative focus squarely on Nairobi’s community welfare systems. Alongside Karauri—the former aviation pilot and corporate executive now steering Kasarani constituency—the duo formed a key legislative contingent flanking the Interior Ministry’s administrative chief.

### Dismantling Capital Barriers

During the working session, Dr. Omollo underscored that genuine inclusivity across the urban landscape demands eliminating the systemic and physical hurdles that lock citizens with disabilities out of economic progress.

"Genuine inclusivity requires dismantling barriers so that every citizen—including persons living with disabilities—can actively participate in and benefit from the country’s socio-economic progress," Dr. Omollo affirmed.

The Principal Secretary pointed to the *Usawa ni Sawa* framework as an institutional vehicle aimed at restoring dignity, ensuring equitable access to ongoing state welfare programs, and securing fair representation for marginalized communities across Nairobi and beyond.

### Direct Line to Constituency Leadership

The forum provided a rare direct bridge between grassroots disability organizers and the lawmakers presiding over two of Nairobi's most populous jurisdictions. Coordinators representing community units tabled pressing local priorities, focusing on the practical challenges facing PWD residents in accessing municipal infrastructure, social services, and administrative support.

By pulling Lang’ata and Kasarani leadership into the same room as the national security and interior docket, the talks aimed to align local constituency development interventions with broader government policy.

The meeting closed with commitments between Dr. Omollo, the two Nairobi MPs, and the coordinator network to build structured collaboration channels, ensuring that practical interventions directly strengthen support systems across the capital's grassroots.
