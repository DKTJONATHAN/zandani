According to the Kenya Revenue Authority, illicit manufacturers in the North Rift are turning to extreme architectural concealment, forcing revenue agents to dismantle physical barricades and search below ground level to safeguard legitimate business operators and state revenue.

The agency reported on Wednesday that a joint enforcement team from the North Rift region and officers from Baringo Central Police Station breached a fortified residential compound in Baringo County, discovering a subterranean vault hidden under a bathroom floor.

“Accessing the homestead proved challenging, as the compound had more than six gates, apparently designed to restrict access and frustrate enforcement efforts,” the tax authority said in a statement. “The hidden chamber demonstrates the increasingly sophisticated measures employed by illicit traders to evade detection and conceal prohibited goods.”

The operation yielded nine 30-litre jerricans containing suspected ethanol, 40 sachets of 200ml packaging containing the same substance, and six kilograms of uninspected tobacco. While the physical cargo carried an estimated market value of Ksh226,292, the revenue loss prevented by the interception stood at Ksh170,212 in unremitted taxes. The principal suspect managed to flee the compound during the raid and remains at large as multi-agency teams pursue his arrest.

The Baringo discovery highlights a broader structural challenge confronting revenue protection units across rural outposts. Rather than relying on covert transit routes or remote bush hideouts, contraband handlers are heavily investing in structural modifications to private residences. Multi-tiered gate networks serve as early warning buffers against surprise inspections, buying time for handlers to evade apprehension while their inventory remains tucked beneath functional household rooms.

KRA underscored that these enforcement operations are crucial for protecting fully compliant manufacturers from unfair competition while shielding consumers from the unregulated health hazards inherent in uninspected alcohol and tobacco.

“Such operations also help promote compliance with the law and ensure a level playing field for businesses that operate within the legal framework,” the authority stated, noting that targeted crackdowns on clandestine supply networks will continue across the region.
