Acting head of the Kenya Meteorological Service Authority Edward Muriuki has pushed back against oversimplified weather assumptions, warning that the newly commenced October–December rainfall season is driven by complex climate interactions rather than a single atmospheric headline.

With precipitation already recorded across 24 counties, Muriuki is directing residents and farmers to bypass generic national summaries and consult local county meteorological offices for precise micro-forecasts.

"County-specific weekly and monthly forecasts are available from the offices of respective county directors of Meteorological Services," Muriuki noted, emphasizing that both the timing and volume of rainfall will vary significantly depending on local topography and regional climate drivers.

### The Staggered Onset Calendar

Rainfall has already established its footprint across the Western, Rift Valley, Nyanza, and Coastal belts. Counties currently recording ongoing precipitation include Nandi, Kakamega, Vihiga, Bungoma, Siaya, Busia, Baringo, Nakuru, Trans Nzoia, Uasin Gishu, Elgeyo Marakwet, West Pokot, Kisii, Nyamira, Kericho, Bomet, Kisumu, Homa Bay, Migori, and Narok.

Along the coastline, wet conditions have taken hold in Mombasa, Kwale, Lamu, Kilifi, and select sections of the Tana Delta.

For the northern and eastern regions, the arrival of the rains follows a distinct multi-stage rollout:

* **First to second week of October:** Marsabit, Mandera, Wajir, Garissa, and Isiolo are slated for seasonal onset.
* **Mid-October windows:** Nairobi, Kiambu, Murang’a, Kirinyaga, Nyeri, Nyandarua, Laikipia, Embu, Meru, Tharaka Nithi, Machakos, Kitui, and Makueni—alongside Kajiado, Taita Taveta, and inland Tana River—are expected to transition into active rainfall.
* **Late October onset:** Turkana and Samburu will mark the final regional arrival during the third or fourth week of the month.

### Deconstructing the Climate Drivers

Addressing widespread speculation around extreme weather, Muriuki clarified that while global systems are shifting, public chatter often misattributes every downpour solely to El Niño.

Data supported by the World Meteorological Organisation indicates that a positive Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD)—a phenomenon measured by sea-surface temperature differentials between the western and eastern tropical Indian Ocean—is expected to develop alongside strengthening El Niño conditions.

"Rainfall is influenced by several climate systems, including El Niño and the Indian Ocean Dipole," Muriuki stated, cautioning against viewing any single driver in isolation.

### High-Volume Zones and Operational Hazards

Despite the broader forecast predicting above-average precipitation across much of the country, regional delivery will be uneven. Turkana and Samburu are projected to record poor to fair total rainfall despite late onset dates.

Conversely, heavy downpours and above-normal accumulation are anticipated across Kwale, Mombasa, Kilifi, Lamu, Mandera, Taita Taveta, Tana River, Garissa, and Wajir. Inland high-volume zones include Makueni, Kajiado, Machakos, Nairobi, Kiambu, Murang’a, Kirinyaga, Embu, Tharaka Nithi, Meru, Isiolo, Nyandarua, Nyeri, Marsabit, Laikipia, Nakuru, and Narok.

While improved soil moisture offers immediate relief for pasture regeneration and land preparation, the Met authority warned of logistical and safety challenges. Flood-prone lowlands and riverine settlements face elevated risks of inundation and landslides, while agricultural zones contend with potential crop diseases, waterlogging, and transport bottlenecks.

### Divergent Season Cutoffs

Temperatures nationwide are expected to remain warmer than average throughout the season, but the close of the rainfall window will diverge sharply by geography.

For northern counties—including Marsabit, Mandera, Wajir, Garissa, and Isiolo—the rainy period is projected to wrap up early, concluding during the first or second week of December.

In contrast, central, western, and eastern counties are set for an extended wet stretch, with precipitation expected to spill over into January.
