Customs brokers, transit shed handlers, and bonded warehouse operators have until October 31 to clear a stringent multi-agency compliance audit or risk losing their operating rights before the 2027 trade calendar begins.

Under three operational notices issued by the Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA), businesses facilitating cross-border cargo must lodge their renewal applications through the Integrated Customs Management System (iCMS) two full months before their active permits lapse on December 31.

Rather than treating the process as a pro-forma administrative rollover, the tax authority is using the window to cross-reference corporate debts, personal director tax liabilities, and outstanding customs disputes before sanctioning approvals.

### The Paperwork Filter

![An Image of a cargo clearance officer supervising clearance at Mombasa port](https://i.ibb.co/qLTvDFNr/1a3347cc7823.webp)

For bonded warehouses, transit godowns, and Manufacture Under Bond (MUB) facilities, the barrier to entry has been tethered directly to historical compliance. Operators must present their current 2026 licenses alongside valid CB6 security bonds, certified CR12 ownership records from the company registry, and audited accounts for the 2025 financial year.

The review extends beyond the entity itself. KRA is requiring valid Tax Compliance Certificates (TCC) for both the business and individual directors, proof of premises ownership or lease agreements, and a duly filed Form C18. Crucially, the authority warned that renewal remains conditional on having zero unsettled transactions or unresolved disputes across any customs department.

Customs agents face parallel scrutiny. Clearing firms must file their renewals through Form C20 on the iCMS portal, backed by their CR12 or CR13 forms, corporate tax certificates, and verified bond and debt clearance documents.

They are also required to upload their expiring C21 licenses alongside an active clearance certificate from the Kenya International Freight and Warehousing Association (KIFWA), binding their regulatory standing to professional body accreditation.

### High Stakes for Transit Sheds

The financial bar rises higher for transit shed operators, who are applying for three-year operating permits running through 2029. Beyond matching the requirements for bonded warehouses—including gazette operating notices, director-level tax clearance, and 2025 audited statements—successful transit shed applicants must remit a Ksh 1.3 million ($10,000) statutory license fee.

![Senior government officials, led by the Head of Public Service, Felix Koskei, pay a visit to the KRA offices in Nairobi.](https://i.ibb.co/4gFnTRdK/9cb032b204ed.webp)

KRA has explicitly cautioned that satisfying the document checklist does not guarantee an operational permit. Applications will be subjected to internal vetting, leaving non-compliant or contested operators exposed to exclusion.

### Pressure on the Import Pipeline

The early October deadline places immediate pressure on the primary conduits of Kenya's import economy. Clearing agents and bonded facilities serve as the direct intermediaries for more than 59,000 active domestic importers operating across regional distribution, manufacturing, and general retail.

Those businesses route over 2.09 million commercial shipments annually through national entry points, heavily anchored by inbound cargo from China, the United Arab Emirates, and Saudi Arabia.

With KRA shutting the renewal window eight weeks ahead of the new year, logistics operators unable to regularize disputed entries, reconcile security bonds, or settle director tax records risk being locked out of the clearance manifest when the January transition takes effect.
