Detectives from the Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission (EACC) on Friday pounced on a Narok County surveyor inside her office, arresting her as she pocketed a Ksh50,000 cash instalment to mark boundary beacons on private land.

### What we know
* The suspect, a county surveyor stationed in Narok, was arrested inside county offices on Friday, September 11, 2026.
* She allegedly demanded a Ksh100,000 bribe from a citizen to establish beacons between two parcels of land in Nairagie Enkare.
* Graft-busters staged an entrapment operation and caught her receiving an initial down payment of Ksh50,000.
* She was taken to the EACC South Rift Regional Office in Nakuru for statement recording ahead of formal charges.

The operation unfolded after a frustrated landowner approached anti-graft investigators, reporting that the public officer had refused to render routine survey services without an upfront payout. The complainant had sought official assistance to resolve an adjoining boundary line and identify missing boundary pegs for two parcels situated in the Nairagie Enkare area.

Instead of processing the request through established administrative channels, the surveyor allegedly slapped the applicant with an illicit Ksh100,000 fee. EACC sleuths laid a trap at the county government headquarters, catching the officer red-handed as the initial Ksh50,000 changed hands.

The Narok arrest caps a rapid-fire, 72-hour sweep by anti-graft officers across the Rift Valley, highlighting systemic rent-seeking in frontline public offices:

* **Thursday (Kipkelion):** Two Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) officers were arrested after demanding Ksh500,000 to drop a murder investigation. EACC detectives arrested them after they accepted a down payment of Ksh120,000.
* **Wednesday (Eldoret):** A Magistrate’s Court assistant was apprehended while pocketing Ksh20,000, part of an alleged Ksh50,000 bribe demanded to fast-track an injunction halting an imminent land auction.
* **Wednesday (Nakuru):** A process server at the Nakuru Law Courts Civil Registry was booked for extracting bribes from litigants in exchange for assigning favourable mention dates. The officer was processed and granted Ksh5,000 cash bail.

On the ground across the South Rift, land boundary disputes remain explosive, often tying up family estates and smallholder farms in bureaucratic red tape. For ordinary landowners, the surveyor's tape measure routinely comes at an extortionate premium. The latest arrests expose how essential public registries-from land mapping desks to court registries and police stations-continue to be weaponised by civil servants running toll stations against the very citizens they are salaried to serve.
