Kenya’s energy regulator faces mounting demands to open its internal pricing worksheets to external scrutiny, after transport lobbies called on Parliament, the Auditor-General, and the Competition Authority of Kenya (CAK) to audit the mechanics behind the October–November pump price review.

The push, mounted Wednesday by the Motorists Association of Kenya (MAK), shifts public anger beyond standard pump-price frustration into a formal challenge of the state's petroleum supply architecture. The lobby has demanded an independent audit of the Government-to-Government (G-to-G) petroleum importation scheme, full disclosure of the Petroleum Development Levy Fund’s cash position, and itemised proof that bilateral fuel deals have reduced consumer costs.

At the center of the dispute is a stark pricing anomaly in the latest review cycle. EPRA increased pump prices across the board, but kerosene absorbed an increase of Ksh28.49 per litre—nearly three times the Ksh9.90 rise slapped on diesel and more than double the Ksh12.93 adjustment on super petrol.

That disparity came despite kerosene recording the lowest percentage increase in landed import costs during the review window, prompting MAK to challenge the regulatory formula that translated cheaper port delivery into the heaviest retail shock.

![EPRA Director General Edward Mwirigi Kinyua during the EPRA event on October 14.](https://i.ibb.co/mVdjDKgD/db1ff7b9ad71.webp)

### The Landed-Cost Disparity

According to the motorists lobby, EPRA's reliance on higher kerosene pricing as a policy lever to curb fuel adulteration penalises low-income households reliant on lighting and cooking fuel, substituting retail price inflation for effective border and supply-chain policing.

The association wants EPRA to publish line-item calculations showing how individual cost centers—including freight, marine insurance, distribution margins, local tax bands, and price stabilisation interventions—were weighted to produce the Ksh28.49 jump.

"An explanation is not scientific merely because it contains figures," the association stated in its Wednesday dispatch. "It becomes scientific when its assumptions, methodology and calculations can be independently examined and reproduced."

![An image of Matatus in the busy streets of Nairobi, with a fuel pump with fuel drop on the side.](https://i.ibb.co/prNPF2H4/cdc3ee6bfb93.webp)

Beyond domestic retail formulas, the lobby is pressing for an independent assessment of the government's flagship G-to-G petroleum import framework. The state had promoted the government-backed arrangement as a buffer against foreign exchange volatility and supplier premiums, but the association wants commercial proof: verifiable spreadsheets covering international benchmark pricing, financing overheads, and exchange rates applied during procurement.

### Accountability at the Pump

The lobby has also targeted the opacity surrounding the Petroleum Development Levy Fund. While the fund was established in part to provide cash reserves that cushion consumers during global oil shocks, motorists are demanding full disclosure of its current account balances, collections, and actual stabilisation disbursements.

Should the state decline to open the pricing worksheets and review the current cycle's tax structures, the lobby warned it will consult members and transport sector operators to coordinate public protests across the country, reviving the friction seen during anti-fuel price street demonstrations in May.

For now, the pricing controversy rests with state oversight bodies. Parliament and the Auditor-General have been formally urged to scrutinise whether EPRA's pricing tools and the state's procurement contracts are functioning in the public interest, or shielding structural inefficiencies behind administrative gazette notices.
