Consumer evaluation data from the 2026 Superbrands East Africa study highlights a distinct performance profile for digital mobility platforms in Kenya: while app distinction and brand perception score near the top of the market, day-to-day operational consistency remains the most sensitive index for gig-economy services.

In the national brand survey conducted by research firm Kantar Insights EA across 112 categories, ride-hailing operator Bolt placed eighth overall, recording a composite Superbrands score of 88.786. The company registered a brand awareness mark of 78 percent among surveyed Kenyan consumers, securing a position inside a top-ten ranking otherwise anchored by long-established telecommunications networks, device manufacturers, and retail chains.

### Metric Disparity in Platform Scoring

An examination of the survey criteria reveals how consumers differentiate between app usability and service execution. In the category breakdown, respondents awarded Bolt a 9.14 score for distinction and 9.08 for overall quality.

![Dimmy Kanyankole, Bolt Senior General Manager for East Africa, speaking during the launch of the Gig Economy Report, March 19, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/DfR09j19/6de94f9dead0.webp)

However, the platform’s reliability score stood lower at 8.79. The gap reflects the ongoing operational friction inherent to platform-based transport—where service delivery relies on distributed driver networks, real-world traffic conditions, and surge dynamics, unlike fixed product manufacturers or digital software services.

The 2026 top-ten list features M-PESA, Safaricom, Samsung, WhatsApp, Google, Naivas, Dettol, Bolt, Kenya Airways, and YouTube. Bolt is the sole pure-play ride-hailing and logistics application to make the upper bracket.

### Consumer Trust and Commercial Weight

The survey metrics underline the commercial stakes tied to brand endorsement in the regional market. According to Superbrands East Africa, 86 percent of surveyed Kenyan consumers reported they are more likely to buy products or services bearing the official Superbrands seal. , 84 percent indicated the mark elevates overall confidence in a company, while 85 percent noted it would prompt them to consider trying a new service.

![An undated Bolt taxi while making errands.](https://i.ibb.co/7dzWTWWW/b5dfdb6391a2.webp)

"The findings show that quality, reliability, and distinction remain at the centre of purchasing decisions," said Jawad Jaffer, Project Director and Associate Publisher at Superbrands East Africa.

For platform operators, maintaining consumer confidence requires balancing customer-facing software standards with driver and courier partner retention. Bolt management pointed to driver support as a core requirement for maintaining its market standing.

"Being named among Kenya's Top 10 strongest brands is a significant milestone for Bolt and a reflection of the trust customers have placed in our platform," said Dimmy Kanyankole, Senior General Manager for Rides in East Africa at Bolt. "This recognition reinforces our commitment to providing reliable, quality services while continuously improving how we serve riders and support the driver and courier partners who make our platform possible."

As digital platforms continue to solidify their place in urban transit infrastructure, the practical challenge for ride-hailing firms lies in closing the distance between high customer awareness and consistent ground-level reliability during peak operational demand.
