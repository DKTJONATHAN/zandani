Comedian and digital creator YY has bypassed the usual routine of blaming club administrators and federations for the meager pay across Kenya’s domestic leagues, turning the spotlight squarely onto local fans who stay home on matchdays.

Taking to his social media platforms on Tuesday, November 10, 2026, the entertainer laid out a commercial breakdown of local sports, arguing that domestic footballers remain impoverished because spectators refuse to fund the product they criticize.

“We are the reason why Kenyan footballers cannot get paid well,” YY said. “As much as football is a sport, football is also a business—you get paid based on the revenue you generate.”

### The Kasarani Turnstile Math

To substantiate his point, the comedian broke down the latent gate potential of Kenya’s largest sporting venue, the 60,000-seat Kasarani Stadium. 

Under YY’s matchday projection, a sold-out arena charging a flat rate of Ksh 1,000 per ticket yields Ksh 60 million from a single fixture. Expanding that model across a consistent league schedule, he argued that three fully packed high-stakes fixtures in a month could easily generate tens of millions, setting a trajectory where an active, paying fan culture could unlock up to Ksh 1 billion monthly across league fixtures.

For YY, the absence of those gate receipts creates a compounding deficit that starves clubs at the foundational level, leaving them incapable of offering competitive player contracts or retaining top-tier talent within the domestic borders.

### The Commercial Chain Reaction

The comic insisted that match attendance is not an isolated revenue stream, but the catalyst required to unlock high-value commercial partnerships. Corporate brands, he noted, measure marketing investments against physical foot traffic and spectator attention rather than goodwill.

“Sponsors will not come if there are no people,” YY explained, emphasizing that corporate partners inevitably pull back when domestic fixtures are played against backdrops of bare concrete terraces.

He argued that television executives follow the same consumer metrics. Consistent crowd turnouts naturally create a vibrant broadcast spectacle, which in turn incentivizes television networks to purchase broadcasting rights. Without the visual energy of packed stadiums, the value of those broadcast packages remains depressed, denying clubs the residual broadcast distributions that sustain professional teams globally.

### The Teargas Dilemma

While demanding accountability from the public, YY stopped short of treating fan absenteeism as simple apathy, acknowledging the safety concerns that have historically alienated families and casual spectators from local matches. 

The comedian pointed directly to the recurring threat of stadium hooliganism, recalling instances where peaceful supporters found themselves caught in volatile crowd disturbances and forced to endure teargas.

He noted that until sports authorities deliver secure, orderly matchday environments, casual fans will hesitate to trade their screens for stadium seats. Concluding his breakdown, YY called on venue administrators to treat spectator safety as an operational priority, while maintaining that fans who desire better compensation for local athletes must ultimately be willing to buy tickets.

“If we want footballers to earn well, we need to start attending these matches,” he said.
