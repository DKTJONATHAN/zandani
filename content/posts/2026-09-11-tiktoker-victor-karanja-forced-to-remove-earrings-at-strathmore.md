NAIROBI - Content creator Victor Karanja was denied entry to the Strathmore University campus on Friday morning after security personnel mandated the removal of his earrings, an incident that has triggered a sharp divide regarding dress codes in Kenyan higher education.

**What we know:**
* Victor Karanja was barred from entering the university premises by gate security due to his visible ear jewelry.
* The content creator publicly contested the move, noting that his attire has been accepted in corporate and NGO environments previously.
* Online discourse has framed the incident as a clash between evolving creative professional standards and rigid academic institutional regulations.
* Social media reactions remain split, with critics labeling the university’s policy as archaic, while others defend institutional autonomy.

The encounter, documented and shared by Karanja, serves as the latest flashpoint in the ongoing debate over personal branding versus institutional decorum. Supporters of the creator argue that traditional institutions are failing to keep pace with the modern creative economy, where personal expression is largely decoupled from professional competency.

Conversely, defenders of the institution point to the university's internal policy-often cited as a hallmark of its professional grooming standards-as a binding contract for all visitors and students.

While the university administration has not issued a formal statement, the viral nature of the footage has forced a broader conversation on whether local institutions are isolating themselves from a younger, more expressive generation of Kenyans. As it stands, the debate reflects the widening gap between traditional gatekeeping in private education and the fluid, digital-first culture of Nairobi’s rising creative class.
