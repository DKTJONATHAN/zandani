Kenya's core operational budget was left running on thin margins between July and September, as loan servicing absorbed all but Ksh141.3 billion of the taxes collected during the opening quarter of the 2026/2027 financial year.

According to the National Treasury’s cash disbursement report for the quarter ending September 30, the government directed approximately Ksh490.5 billion toward debt obligations against net tax collections of Ksh631.8 billion. The payout meant that for every Ksh10 remitted by taxpayers, roughly Ksh7.80 bypassed public service delivery and capital projects to settle domestic and foreign creditors.

The first-quarter outturn closely tracks the fiscal ceiling set in the approved budget estimates, which allocated Ksh2.316 trillion to public debt service against an annual tax revenue target of Ksh2.859 trillion—an anticipated yearly absorption rate of about 81 per cent. However, the heavy concentration of maturities in the early months leaves little liquidity for ministerial recurrent expenditure, county transfers, and infrastructure outlays.

### The Ksh13.1 Trillion Balance Sheet

![The National Treasury Building](https://i.ibb.co/zWf71qD7/39a3fbe9c921.webp)

The repayment pressure reflects the scale of liabilities accumulated on the national balance sheet. Treasury’s Public Debt Bulletin for June 2026 positioned total public debt at Ksh13.12 trillion, translating to 70.44 per cent of gross domestic product.

Domestic borrowing constitutes the majority of that portfolio at Ksh7.327 trillion (55.8 per cent), maintaining intense domestic debt rollover requirements through the Central Bank of Kenya's weekly and monthly bond auctions. External commitments make up the remaining Ksh5.794 trillion (44.2 per cent).

Multilateral institutions remain the principal overseas claimants, accounting for 54.9 per cent of foreign obligations. The World Bank’s International Development Association leads with Ksh1.77 trillion, followed by the African Development Bank and African Development Fund at Ksh565.29 billion, and the International Monetary Fund at Ksh452.12 billion.

Commercial lenders represent 26.8 per cent of external loans, led by Ksh1.36 trillion tied up in sovereign Eurobonds and Ksh177.23 billion due to commercial banks. Bilateral credit accounts for 17.1 per cent, where China holds the largest sovereign claim at Ksh619.82 billion, followed by France at Ksh100.08 billion and Japan at Ksh91.20 billion.

### Looming 2027 Interest Surge

While rating agency Moody’s upgraded Kenya’s credit profile from Caa1 to B3 with a stable outlook in January 2026, its broader sub-Saharan debt assessment published this month points to structural spending rigidities across the region.

![An undated image of the KRA entrance](https://i.ibb.co/Gfyt8tLc/92e58088298f.webp)

Moody’s projects that Kenya will allocate approximately 35 per cent of all government revenue solely to interest payments in 2027. That proportion places Kenya alongside Zambia among the nations committing the highest shares of state income to interest overheads rather than principal reduction or basic public administration.

Public pushback against aggressive tax collection measures has narrowed the administration's options for raising domestic revenue rapidly enough to dilute debt service ratios.

### The Liability Management Pivot

To prevent debt servicing from continually starving line ministries of operating capital, the Treasury has initiated discussions over a proposed Ksh129.7 billion debt swap structured through a United States investment bank. The transaction is designed to refinance costlier existing debt on extended maturities and softer interest terms.

The prospective arrangement forms the initial tranche of a larger Ksh700 billion external financing and liability-management blueprint intended for execution before June 2027. Treasury officials are prioritizing these refinancing mechanisms to smooth out large upcoming debt maturities, avoiding sudden cash crunches as the remainder of the fiscal year unfolds.
