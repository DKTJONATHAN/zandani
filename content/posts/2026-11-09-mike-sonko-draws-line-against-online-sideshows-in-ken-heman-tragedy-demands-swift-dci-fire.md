Mike Sonko has stepped firmly into the public grief surrounding content creator Kennedy Njau, issuing an uncompromising warning against turning the deaths of two young sisters into an arena for online squabbling and personal grudges.

Taking to his platforms to address the devastating fire that claimed the lives of Njau’s daughters, Angel and Tehila, the former Nairobi governor moved to build a protective wall around the bereaved family. Njau, widely known online as Ken Heman, has spent recent days grappling with the catastrophic blaze that engulfed a residential apartment in Gachie, leaving his household shattered and triggering widespread demands for answers across the digital creator community.

Rather than allowing the tragedy to devolve into the sort of performative bickering that frequently infects high-profile online spaces, Sonko drew a sharp boundary around the narrative. He stressed that the loss of the two young girls is far too grave to be trivialized by public point-scoring or recycled interpersonal feuds.

“This is not about settling personal scores or engaging in public condemnation,” Sonko made clear. “It is about two innocent children whose lives were tragically cut short and a family that deserves truth, justice and closure.”

### Backing Ken Heman’s Pursuit of Truth

Sonko’s intervention comes at a critical juncture for Njau, who has found himself navigating unfathomable grief under the unforgiving glare of public attention. The former governor confirmed that he had made a personal commitment to walk alongside the creator throughout the ordeal, guaranteeing that the father would not have to wage the campaign for truth in isolation.

Describing the loss as an unbearable void that no parent should ever have to confront, Sonko underlined that his public positioning was engineered with a single objective: getting concrete answers regarding what unfolded inside the Gachie building. He insisted that public sympathy must be backed by tangible support, shielding the family from external distractions while keeping the spotlight squarely on the memory of Angel and Tehila.

For Njau, the solidarity brings high-profile legal and public leverage to a case that has left local residents and followers unsettled, with basic questions remaining unanswered about how the blaze started and why the young girls could not be brought to safety in time.

### Direct Challenge to the DCI

Beyond offering personal solidarity to the grieving father, Sonko escalated the matter directly to the country's top criminal investigators. The former county chief issued a public call to Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) boss Mohamed Amin, urging the agency to take command of the matter and run an expedited, impartial inquiry.

Sonko argued that the circumstances surrounding the Gachie inferno cannot be treated as an ordinary mishap. He called on Amin’s detectives to comb through every piece of available forensic evidence, reconstruct the timeline leading up to the blaze, and establish whether criminal negligence, structural failures, or deliberate actions played a role in the fatal outcome.

The investigation, Sonko emphasized, must proceed strictly on professional merits, entirely isolated from external interference, favoritism, or social media speculation. He maintained that every lingering question troubling the family and the surrounding community must be put to rest through a credible, legally sound inquiry.

### Accountability Without Compromise

By tethering his support to formal investigative processes, Sonko has shifted the conversation from social media condolences to institutional culpability. He maintained that if detectives uncover any evidence of negligence or institutional failure that contributed to the fire, the individuals responsible must face the full force of the law.

As the family prepares to lay Angel and Tehila to rest, the scrutiny now shifts toward the Directorate of Criminal Investigations to see how swiftly detectives act on the forensic questions surrounding the Gachie apartment, with Sonko positioning his platform as a public guarantor that the pursuit of answers will not be abandoned.
