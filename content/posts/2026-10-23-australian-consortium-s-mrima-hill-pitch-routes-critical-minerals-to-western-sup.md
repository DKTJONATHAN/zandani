A multi-billion-dollar supply pipeline proposed for Kenya’s premier critical minerals reserve would see high-grade concentrates extracted from Kwale County shipped directly to Western Australia for refining, positioning the site at the centre of an intensifying global contest for technology minerals.

The commercial blueprint, submitted to the Ministry of Mining by an Australian joint venture between RareX and Iluka Resources, seeks exclusive exploitation rights over the Ksh8 trillion mineral field at Mrima Hill. Under the consortium’s operational plan, the separation of advanced rare earth products will not occur on Kenyan soil. Instead, the firms intend to transport primary concentrates to Iluka’s specialized Eneabba rare earth refinery in Western Australia before marketing the refined output to allied manufacturing hubs across the United States, Europe, Japan, and South Korea.

To meet domestic industrialisation mandates, the consortium’s tender proposal delineates a split processing framework. While strategic elements bound for high-technology markets leave the country, secondary industrial minerals extracted from the deposit—primarily phosphate and manganese by-products—would remain within Kenya to establish local processing and manufacturing supply chains.

### Cross-Continental Route for Kwale Ores

![Mrima Hill](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/mrima-hill.jpg.webp?itok=yHaRpo7x)

The tender response follows a formal state bidding round to exploit Mrima Hill’s vast reserves of niobium, rare earth elements, and associated minerals. The Kwale deposit ranks among the world’s most lucrative unexploited carbonatite deposits, long stalled by legal disputes and community land grievances.

In their operational submission, RareX and Iluka outline plans for phased pit evaluations, including assessing potential underground mining operations to safeguard the site’s topography. The consortium also proposes an industrial off-take structure aimed at integrating Kenyan commercial logistics firms into surface handling and transportation contracts.

Technical workforce training forms a parallel prong of the proposal. The Australian bidders have outlined partnerships involving Curtin University to deliver accredited mineral processing curricula, technical scholarships, and skills-transfer pipelines to build a domestic engineering cadre capable of supporting the Kwale operations.

### Geopolitical Crosscurrents at Mining Ministry

![A collage of US President Donald Trump (left) and President Willian Ruto (right), with a hand holding rare-earth minerals in the middle.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/ruto-trump-minerals.jpg.webp?itok=KF13tHL6)

The Australian proposal arrives at a delicate moment for Kenya's mineral licensing authorities, with global powers actively manoeuvring for long-term supply agreements over African energy-transition metals.

Washington heightened its engagement around Kenyan critical minerals this September, offering bilateral assistance frameworks covering processing infrastructure, technical education, and industrial integration. The Iluka-RareX project model closely reflects Western efforts to establish integrated critical-mineral value chains completely outside Chinese control.

Yet the Ministry of Mining is balancing several competing geopolitical interests. Chinese-backed bidding entities have also submitted rival proposals for the Mrima Hill concession, seeking to direct output to Beijing’s expansive smelting and industrial network.

Beyond foreign competition, the venture faces rigorous domestic administrative gates. Local leaders and rights advocates in Kwale have consistently demanded enforceable safeguards regarding cultural preservation, fair land compensation, and strict environmental restoration bonds before exploratory drilling begins. RareX and Iluka have pledged early baseline engagement on cultural heritage and post-mining land rehabilitation, but those commitments remain conditional on securing the mining concession and executing formal statutory agreements.

The Ministry of Mining is now vetting the technical, environmental, and financial viability of all consortium bids as the government prepares to award development rights over the country's most valuable mineral asset.
