When initial snapshots of Stevo Simple Boy’s rural housing project went public, the feedback was blunt. Social media users and radio personality Alex Mwakideu quickly raised flags over the durability of the early structure, prompting the 'Freshi Barida' hitmaker to halt his approach and rethink the blueprint from the ground up.

That critique triggered a major structural pivot in Oyugis, Homa Bay County. Rather than sticking with traditional masonry that had drawn public concern, the musician partnered with a firm linked to contractor Amos Kibaru to adopt modern precast concrete technology. Under this method, structural wall panels are factory-manufactured before being hauled and assembled directly on-site.

By Thursday, November 26, 2026, the overhaul had produced visible milestones. Sharing progress updates from the site, Stevo revealed that ground-floor doors and windows have already been fitted as contractors push interior room work forward. Tagging Kibaru in an Instagram update captioned “Progress ya nyumba ya mathe day 4,” the rapper showcased a much sturdier profile than the initial framing that sparked the design overhaul.

The housing venture stems from a personal commitment to upgrade his mother’s living conditions after she was reportedly forced to vacate her previous residence and move into a single room. As the factory-built panels take shape in Homa Bay, the project has transitioned from a standard domestic construction into a closely watched exercise in heeding public technical advice.
