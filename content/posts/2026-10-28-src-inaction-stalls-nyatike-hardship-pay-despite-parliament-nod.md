A legislative resolution designating Nyatike Constituency a hardship zone remains frozen in administrative machinery, leaving local teachers without enhanced remuneration months after Parliament approved their petition.

The stalemate has placed the Salaries and Remuneration Commission (SRC) at the centre of union scrutiny, with the Kenya National Union of Teachers (KNUT) and local leadership questioning why an approved parliamentary directive has not been integrated into the Teachers Service Commission (TSC) payroll system.

Nyatike MP Tom Odege, addressing a public assembly in the constituency on Wednesday, October 28, confirmed that he took the petition through formal House committee hearings to secure final legislative approval. Despite that milestone, no administrative gazettement or budgetary disbursement instruction has moved to the commission tasked with setting public compensation.

"I do not know why we are experiencing these delays in this constituency," Odege said. "I had already written a petition to Parliament a while back to speed up the implementation of the hardship allowance in this constituency, and Parliament approved it."

![KNUTT Sec Gen Collins Oyuu](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/knutt_sec_gen_0.jpg.webp?itok=bVBUuYRo)

### The Operational Toll

Nyatike, situated along the shores of Lake Victoria in Migori County, routinely grapples with severe ecological shocks, swinging between flash floods and prolonged drought cycles. The resulting infrastructural deficits have historically complicated staffing stability, with public schools struggling to attract and retain teachers unwilling to shoulder high transit and living expenses without compensatory pay.

The delay is now colliding directly with end-of-year national examination schedules, where invigilators and supervisors face demanding fieldwork across rough terrain.

KNUT Secretary-General Collins Oyuu warned that administrative lethargy in Nairobi ignores the physical realities endured by rural educators, specifically as test administration gets underway.

![Teachers from KUPPET during the World Teachers’ Day celebrations at Kasarani on Monday, October 5, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/knut-teachers.jpg.webp?itok=UHGh7TFr)

"Teachers are suffering and especially around this time when we are going to invigilate examinations," Oyuu said. "Teachers will wake up as early as four o'clock to reach centres because of this region being of total hardship. We want to see the Salaries and Remuneration Commission be serious about this report."

### Inter-Agency Bottleneck

While the Teachers Service Commission has signaled institutional readiness to adjust compensation grids, union officials note that the mandate to formalise pay categorisations rests with the SRC. The union maintains that documentation confirming Nyatike’s vulnerability was fully vetted through parliamentary committees, leaving the compensation agency with little justification for sitting on the report.

KNUT Migori County Treasurer Felix Oloo emphasised that local branches will not treat the implementation as an automatic routine, noting that continued union intervention remains necessary to break the institutional silence between the commission and the remuneration authority.

With national exam logistics underway and staff navigating rough access routes before dawn, the spotlight shifts squarely back to the SRC to determine when the legislative verdict will translate into actual payroll credits.
