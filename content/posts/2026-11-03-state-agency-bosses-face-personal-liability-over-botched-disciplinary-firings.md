Heads of state corporations and public universities are facing direct financial and legal exposure for bungled internal disciplinary processes, as the Public Service Commission steps up pressure to curb a surging wave of overturned dismissals.

PSC Chairperson Francis Meja warned that institutional leaders who fail to exercise proper due diligence during human resource inquiries risk saddling government agencies with heavy legal costs while exposing themselves to personal liability. The warning was delivered on Monday during a capacity-building workshop for state entities held at the Kenya School of Government in Mombasa.

![francis-mejja-psc-chairperson.jpg](https://i.ibb.co/HT1ZxQQc/8b1c11e5e037.webp)

The Commission pointed to a steady rise in disciplinary appeals reaching its offices, tracing the backlog directly to non-compliance with statutory guidelines, labor policies, and procedural frameworks. When administrative tribunals and boards bypass due process, the resulting decisions frequently collapse under judicial review or PSC appeal, forcing agencies to bear the financial burden of protracted litigation.

“When technical officers and Boards or Councils exercise due diligence on disciplinary matters, fewer appeals and court cases follow. It is also a win-win scenario for the government and individual officers,” Meja noted.

Beyond the balance sheet, the Commission underscored that arbitrary punitive actions erode institutional trust, depress workforce morale, and ultimately degrade public service delivery across ministries and parastatals.

![Public Service Commission House](https://i.ibb.co/gctRgy9/4f4e28856f67.webp)

The timing of the enforcement push coincides with mounting executive scrutiny over basic administrative oversight. Just a day prior to the Mombasa forum, Cabinet Secretary for Public Service Geoffrey Ruku flagged widespread absenteeism and supervision failures during impromptu inspections of regional offices in Kajiado, Nakuru, Kisumu, Mombasa, and Nyeri. Ruku reported finding government stations largely deserted, with junior interns accounting for nearly 90 percent of personnel present while senior human resource managers remained absent from their duty posts.

With supervisory ranks under investigation for dereliction of duty, the convergence of ministerial crackdowns on absenteeism and the PSC’s crackdown on flawed disciplinary procedures highlights a widening governance gap across public sector administrative layers.

State agencies and university councils must now audit pending human resource cases before year-end to clear compliance backlogs ahead of upcoming holiday leave schedules and stricter PSC oversight audits.
