Former Alego Usonga Member of Parliament Otieno Mak’Onyango has died at Nairobi Hospital, closing a decades-long pursuit of justice that saw the veteran second liberation fighter spend his final years in financial destitution while holding an unhonoured Ksh20 million state compensation decree.

Mak’Onyango passed away on Sunday night, October 25, three weeks after suffering a stroke that triggered an urgent public fundraising appeal to cover his mounting critical care costs.

Current Alego Usonga MP Samuel Atandi confirmed the former legislator's death, noting that the constituency had lost a foundational political figure whose legislative tenure between 1992 and 1997 helped shape the region's early multi-party era.

"We have this evening lost Hon. Otieno Makonyango, the fourth Member of Parliament for Alego Usonga Constituency, who served from 1992 to 1997, while undergoing treatment at a Nairobi hospital," Atandi said in a statement. "Alego Usonga has lost a former leader whose name will remain part of the constituency’s political history."

### Clinical Decline and Medical Bills

Mak’Onyango’s final hospitalisation began on October 3, 2026, when he suffered a stroke at his home in Kibiko, Kajiado County. He was initially admitted to a facility in Ngong Town, where clinicians placed him in the High Dependency Unit (HDU) as his condition deteriorated.

He was subsequently transferred to Nairobi Hospital for advanced management. With specialised care costs rapidly escalating, his family and associates mounted a public fundraiser to bridge the medical deficit—a recurring predicament that shadowed the former lawmaker's later life.

In December 2023, public records highlighted his declining health and severe financial distress in Kajiado, where he lived largely forgotten by the state machinery he helped reform.

### The 1982 Crackdown and Unpaid Reparations

![The Kenyan Parliament building](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/parliament_7.jpg.webp?itok=8Se7wfsD)

Before entering elective politics, Mak’Onyango worked as a journalist at *The Standard*. His career was abruptly halted following the August 1, 1982, attempted military coup against President Daniel arap Moi’s administration. State security agents arrested Mak’Onyango, accusing him of involvement in the plot.

He was subsequently thrown into detention without trial, spending years in maximum-security prisons alongside fellow dissidents, including the late former Prime Minister Raila Odinga.

Following the transition of power in 2002, Mak’Onyango initiated legal proceedings against the state in 2003, seeking damages for unlawful arrest, torture, and extended detention. In June 2012, the High Court ruled in his favour, finding that the Moi regime had violated his fundamental constitutional rights and awarding him Ksh20 million in general damages.

Despite the finality of the judicial decree, successive administrations failed to release the settlement. The unpaid judgment mirrored the plight of dozens of 1980s political detainees whose court-awarded reparations remained trapped within state bureaucracy.

Mak’Onyango’s passing leaves the Ksh20 million award as an outstanding liability against the Attorney General's office, transferring an unsettled historical grievance to his surviving estate.
