President William Ruto took to the floor of Parliament on Wednesday, November 18, 2026, centering his State of the Nation Address on the everyday financial reality of Kenyan households.

Addressing a joint sitting of lawmakers, the Head of State insisted that broader macroeconomic progress must show up where it matters most: inside family bank accounts. Ruto singled out healthcare emergencies and persistent public school demands as two of the biggest financial traps draining domestic savings across the country.

"Our undertaking is to progressively and eventually remove the burden of medical expenses from Kenyan households, protect family savings, and ensure that illness does not become a sentence to poverty," Ruto told the assembled Parliament.

### Clearing the Sh34 Billion Debt Desk

To keep health centers operational and higher learning institutions running, the President announced that the government had approved the settlement of Sh34 billion in pending arrears. 

The bailout package directs Sh16 billion to hospitals to settle outstanding medical debts, while Sh18 billion is earmarked for public and private universities struggling under prolonged liquidity pressures.

Turning to basic education, Ruto described school costs as a constant strain on domestic budgets. He recalled that when his administration took office in 2022, the public school system faced a shortfall of roughly 116,000 teachers.

That gap had regularly forced parents across the country to pay out of pocket to hire extra teachers through school Boards of Management (BoM), while also funding routine classroom maintenance and basic learning equipment.

### Teacher Hires and the Sh784 Billion Budget

To dismantle those informal parent contributions, Ruto highlighted heavy state spending backed by a Sh784 billion education allocation. 

Over the past four years, the government has added 100,000 teachers to the public payroll to close the staffing deficit. Ruto announced that plans have been finalized to recruit an additional 20,000 teachers in December 2026.

Beyond staffing numbers, the President detailed ongoing school infrastructure construction aimed at halting community fundraising drives. The state has completed 23,000 new classrooms and is actively constructing and equipping 1,600 school laboratories nationwide.

Closing his remarks on education, Ruto issued a direct warning to school administrators who continue to charge parents extra monies outside official guidelines. He stressed that with funds being released to institutions, school heads have no justification for imposing unauthorized levies on families.

Attention now shifts to the Ministry of Education and the Teachers Service Commission as recruitment for the 20,000 new teacher positions opens in December 2026.
