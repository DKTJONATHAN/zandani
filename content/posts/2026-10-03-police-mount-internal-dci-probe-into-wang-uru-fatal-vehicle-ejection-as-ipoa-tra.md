According to official statements from Vigilance House, the Directorate of Criminal Investigations has been tasked with expediting inquiries into officers attached to Wang’uru Police Station after a pregnant passenger died when she was allegedly shoved from a moving police vehicle in Kirinyaga County.

Police Spokesperson Muchiri Nyaga confirmed late Friday that the Inspector General of Police received an executive briefing on the incident, designating the fatal ejection along the Piai-Itangi Murram Road as a serious criminal offense warranting rapid intervention by county detectives.

Yet, while the National Police Service (NPS) mobilized its internal investigative branch, regional command stopped short of ceding jurisdiction to independent external watchdogs. Central Region Deputy Police Commander David Wambua declined to confirm whether the matter had been formally submitted to the Independent Policing Oversight Authority (IPOA), indicating instead that administrative sanctions would serve as the primary institutional step.

"The first action will be to interdict the officers if they are found to be culpable of the incident," Wambua stated, framing the immediate response around standard service discipline rather than immediate civilian-led statutory scrutiny.

### The Roadside Scuffle Over a Ksh1,000 Demand

The official investigation stems from an altercation that began when Anthony Muturi Mugweru and his pregnant wife, Catherine Njeri, boarded a Wang’uru police truck while heading toward their miraa farm on Friday, October 2.

According to Muturi’s account from his hospital bed, the transit turned violent when officers demanded a cash payment of Ksh1,000 for the ride. When the couple could not satisfy the crew's demand, the driver ignored pleas to halt the vehicle.

Muturi attempted to draw the driver's attention by striking the rear tailgate, precipitating a physical fight with an armed officer stationed in the rear cargo bed.

“There was a scuffle between me and one officer. I was holding a metal rod when I was hit, forcing me to let go and fall on the tarmac. They took off with my wife,” Muturi said.

Moments after Muturi fell from the moving truck onto the roadway, officers allegedly shoved Njeri out of the accelerating vehicle. She sustained catastrophic injuries upon impact and died on the roadside.

### Discrepancy Over Oversight and Inquest Channels

Spokesperson Nyaga confirmed that detectives found Njeri’s body lying by the roadside along the Piai-Itangi Murram Road bearing extensive trauma, specifically noting severe blunt-force wounds concentrated around her head and hip.

The Kirinyaga County Criminal Investigations Officer (CCIO) personally took charge of the scene alongside Sub-County investigators from Mwea East to reconstruct the incident and map the vehicle's trajectory.

However, by retaining the investigation entirely under the command of the DCI and local sub-county units, police leadership preserves administrative control over evidence gathering and officer identity management. Under standard oversight frameworks, allegations involving police-caused deaths typically invoke mandatory, immediate notifications to IPOA to avoid institutional self-clearing.

As of Saturday morning, Njeri’s body remained at the Embu Hospital Mortuary awaiting a formal post-mortem examination to establish the precise physiological cause of death. Muturi continues to receive treatment for severe trauma injuries sustained during the fall, while DCI investigators prepare their initial field file for the Inspector General.
