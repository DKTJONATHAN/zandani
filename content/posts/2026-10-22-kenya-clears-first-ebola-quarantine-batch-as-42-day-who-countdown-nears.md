Authorities are preparing to trigger the official 42-day national surveillance countdown after confirming that the initial group of individuals placed under Ebola monitoring will exit quarantine facilities this weekend.

Health Director-General Patrick Amoth announced that the first batch of contacts linked to Kenya's October 6 index case will complete their mandatory 21-day monitoring period without developing symptoms, clearing them to return home on Sunday, October 25. The discharge process will be managed by the Kenya National Public Health Institute (KNPHI), led by Acting Director-General Dr. Kamene Kimenye.

![Health Director General Dr Patrick Amoth addressing the press at Afya House, Wednesday, February 12, 2025.](https://i.ibb.co/27H9QCMT/d802c7f65541.webp)

Epidemiological tracking indicates that all nine confirmed infections in the country, culminating in a recent case involving a 24-year-old man who was already isolated, remain tightly clustered around the original exposure event. Out of 135 identified contacts, health officials successfully traced and quarantined 134 individuals by October 20.

The absence of secondary community spread is the prerequisite condition required by the World Health Organisation (WHO) to initiate the official 42-day clock. Under international guidelines, an outbreak can only be declared officially over after 44 consecutive days—or 42 days following the day after the final patient tests negative twice at least 48 hours apart, or following the safe burial of a last confirmed fatality.

![Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale addressing the press on June 30, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/kg2QGkxf/b2997c573caf.webp)

While international support has bolstered local response mechanisms—including an 83.4 million Kenyan Shilling donation from the Government of India comprising 6,000 PPE kits, 30,000 N95 masks, and over 86,000 sample transport kits—the containment effort has also exposed domestic labor friction. The influx of biological samples has prompted medical laboratory professionals to press for enhanced risk allowances, specialized training, and dedicated insurance coverage.

Public health authorities continue to urge vigilance without panic, maintaining that while the country is navigating the containment phase successfully, the monitoring infrastructure remains fully activated. The Ministry of Health has directed the public to report any suspected emergencies through its toll-free line 719, USSD code *719#, or via WhatsApp at 0700 719 719.
