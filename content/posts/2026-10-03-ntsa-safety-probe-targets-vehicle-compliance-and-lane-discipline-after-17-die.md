A multi-disciplinary technical team from the National Transport and Safety Authority (NTSA) has opened an operational probe into mechanical compliance and highway lane discipline following a multi-vehicle crash that killed 17 people at Salama on the Nairobi-Mombasa highway.

The regulatory intervention targets commercial fleet compliance and vehicle roadworthiness along the Mlima Kiu-Salama section, a documented blackspot where heavy freight movement and multi-lane overtaking frequently create severe collision hazards.

### Midnight Collision Sequence

According to police documentation, the chain-reaction crash occurred at approximately 12:10 a.m. on Saturday, October 3, along the Mlima Kiu stretch in Makueni County.

A heavy haulage truck traveling down the corridor toward Mombasa reportedly failed to maintain its lane while attempting to pass a queue of vehicles. The truck lost control, striking two oncoming private passenger cars before swerving into the dedicated climbing lane and colliding head-on with a 14-seater matatu.

![The mangled wreck of a 14-seater matatu involved in an accident at the Salama area along Nairobi-Momba highway on Saturday, October 3, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/s0Vqfg7/1a8aac77abc2.webp)

The impact killed all 13 occupants of the matatu at the scene, including the driver and 12 passengers. The truck driver and three passengers in the freight vehicle also sustained fatal injuries, bringing the death toll to 17. Survivors from the impacted vehicles were transported to Sultan Hamud Sub-County Hospital for emergency treatment.

The deceased passengers in the matatu were confirmed as members of St Mathias Mulumba Catholic Church from Mikindani Parish in Mombasa. The group was traveling overnight toward Nakuru County to attend National Prayer Day celebrations at the Subukia Shrine.

### Technical Audit and Regulatory Scope

NTSA Director General Nashon Kondiwa confirmed that the agency's crash investigation team was deployed to Salama to work alongside the National Police Service (NPS) in reconstructing the incident mechanics.

Rather than restricting the review to driver error, the multi-agency team is auditing whether the heavy vehicle and public transport units involved complied with statutory safety requirements prior to taking the road. The inspection includes evaluating brake integrity, speed limiter calibration, and load parameters.

![NTSA DG Nashon Kondiwa during a past meeting with MPs](https://i.ibb.co/KxFT5H0D/aeada3822677.webp)

"The Authority has dispatched a multi-disciplinary crash investigation team to Salama to establish the full circumstances leading to the crash," Kondiwa stated. "The multi-agency team will examine the vehicles involved and determine whether they complied with safety requirements."

Kondiwa further stressed the imperative for strict lane discipline along climbing corridors, urging drivers to avoid high-risk overtaking maneuvers on designated stretches.

### Corridor Vulnerability

The Mlima Kiu-Salama zone has long posed operational challenges for traffic regulators due to its sharp elevation changes and heavy mixture of long-distance cargo trucks and high-speed public transport vehicles.

While NTSA acknowledged that infrastructural improvements for the Mlima Kiu-Salama route are under government consideration, the corridor remains reliant on visible policing and driver compliance to prevent head-on impacts in climbing lanes.

The joint NTSA and police investigation team is expected to compile a detailed technical report analyzing vehicle maintenance records, speed tracking data, and road geometry factors, which will inform prospective regulatory enforcement actions along the Northern Corridor.
