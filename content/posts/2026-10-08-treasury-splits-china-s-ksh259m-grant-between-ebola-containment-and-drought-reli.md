Faced with overlapping national emergencies, the National Treasury has split an incoming Ksh259 million grant from Beijing squarely down the middle, allocating identical sums to acute disease surveillance and long-standing climate relief.

The funds, handed over on Thursday by China’s Ambassador to Kenya, Guo Haiyan, provide an immediate injection of liquidity as the state scrambles to finance a multi-billion-shilling containment wall. According to Treasury Principal Secretary Chris Kiptoo, the USD 2 million package has been divided into two equal tranches of Ksh129.5 million each—one designated for potential Ebola preparedness and the other earmarked for ongoing drought mitigation.

![Treasury PS Chris Kiptoo receiving the Ksh259 million grant from the Chinese Ambassador to Kenya, Guo Haiyan, at the National Treasury Offices on October 8, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/pBYK4SM2/e4a96be5f5ff.webp)

The bilateral handover at the Treasury offices underscores the complex fiscal juggling act currently facing state accountants. The biological shock arrived just as Kenya confronts severe regional dry spells, forcing economic planners to stretch incoming donor support across two entirely different disaster categories.

The arrival of the Chinese funds coincides with an aggressive state-level resource mobilization drive. Government planners are attempting to assemble a comprehensive Ksh70 billion war chest to contain the threat posed by the country's first-ever imported Ebola virus case, which was confirmed on Tuesday after a Kenyan traveler returning from the Democratic Republic of Congo via Uganda passed away in Nairobi.

While the Treasury has initially set aside Ksh2.6 billion from domestic coffers for emergency operations, the scale of the required response has forced Nairobi to look far beyond local revenues. The state is currently pursuing a Ksh51.8 billion emergency loan from the World Bank through its Rapid Response Option, alongside an anticipated Ksh21 billion injection from the United States government via Department of State direct assistance commitments.

![Treasury PS Chris Kiptoo and Public Health PS Mary Muthoni pose for a photo with the Chinese delegation led by Ambassador Guo Haiyan, at the National Treasury Offices on October 8,](https://i.ibb.co/84mWJHJM/31bfc3f1de4a.webp)

On the ground, public health authorities have maintained a strict containment perimeter. Officials report that all known contacts of the deceased—including family members, traveling companions, and medical personnel who attended to the patient at Nairobi Hospital—have been identified and placed in isolation.

With tracing protocols active and international financial negotiations ongoing, the partitioned Chinese grant represents an immediate, liquid stopgap for a Treasury bracing for extensive institutional expenditures across both public health and food security sectors.
