According to court documents lodged on Monday, six petitioners have moved the High Court to halt state funding for the planned January 5 exhibition match between German champions Bayern Munich and Dutch side AFC Ajax, demanding the immediate public disclosure of commercial agreements backing the fixture.

The constitutional application challenges the Ministry of Sports over the financing architecture of the match, slated for the Raila Odinga International Stadium—also known as Talanta Stadium. The petitioners are seeking conservatory orders restraining national authorities and associated agencies from committing, allocating, or releasing public funds toward the event until the substantive case is heard and determined.

Central to the filing is a seven-day evidentiary ultimatum requiring state respondents to table all underlying contracts executed with both European clubs or their accredited intermediaries.

![Undated image of the Milimani Law Courts](https://i.ibb.co/Ndq9GG2n/5f7e21a0f25e.webp)

"The respondents [must] disclose, within seven days, the full sponsorship agreements and contracts entered into with the two European clubs or their representatives," the petition reads, urging the bench to pierce the commercial confidentiality surrounding the showcase.

Beyond basic hosting rights, the litigants want the court to enforce disclosure of all corporate entities underwriting the event, including their exact financial covenants. The petition demands a verified ledger detailing expected expenditures across appearance fees, international travel, local accommodation, security escorts, specialized event insurance, hospitality suites, and municipal logistical overhead.

The litigation directly confronts the government’s operational narrative. Just 24 hours prior to the filing, Sports Principal Secretary Elijah Mwangi publicly insisted that the exchequer would bear no financial liability for staging the high-profile encounter.

![An image of the Talanta Stadium under construction on June 23, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/k6MwzSt0/b88fe438e87d.webp)

"We are not using government money. We have gotten a private sponsor who has supported us to bring the two teams here," Mwangi stated on Sunday, defending the initiative as a corporate-funded dress rehearsal ahead of Kenya’s co-hosting of the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations (AFCON).

However, the ministry has yet to identify the corporate underwriters or state the total commercial budget required to fly both senior squads into Nairobi. In their court filings, the petitioners point to market estimates pegging the hosting bill at approximately Ksh724.6 million (€5 million), noting that the figure remains unverified in the absence of official documentation.

The fixture, initially made public by President William Ruto as a milestone event to test the newly constructed Talanta facility, has faced swirling speculation regarding player availability. Addressing rumors that the fixture would be relegated to veteran legends or developmental squads, Mwangi maintained that the administration negotiated directly for the clubs' primary senior teams.

With the January 5 date barely two months away, the High Court now faces the task of deciding whether to grant interim orders freezing state commitments. Until the court rules on the disclosure application, the financial terms and private guarantees behind Nairobi’s biggest football exhibition remain sealed.
