Alex Mwakideu broke away from light morning banter on Tuesday, November 10, 2026, using his on-air platform to interrogate the mounting legislative campaign aimed at intensifying state action against the country's LGBTQ community.

Rather than simply reporting the latest political developments from Nairobi, the veteran presenter zeroed in on the groundwork laid by Nyali Member of Parliament Mohammed Ali. Ali had stepped forward as the parliamentary messenger for conservative and religious factions based at the Coast, presenting their demands directly to the National Assembly.

Addressing his listenership in Swahili, Mwakideu outlined the political origin of the new pressure on lawmakers, tracing it directly back to the petition Ali sponsored following localized meetings in Kilifi County.

“Kumbuka ni mapendekezo ya mbunge wa Nyali, Mohammed Ali, ambaye alikua anasema yeye analeta hayo mapendekezo akiwa ametumwa na viongozi wa Kilifi kutoka kwa kanisa la Kikristo, kutoka Waislamu, watu binafsi, yaani maoni ya Wakenya wengi,” Mwakideu explained on air.

### The Coastal Petition on the Airwaves

Mwakideu laid out the core logic championed by Ali’s supporters, noting how proponents of tougher measures have insisted that visible shifts in Kenyan society require direct executive and police enforcement. 

He relayed the arguments dominating the parliamentary submissions, which maintain that the spread of same-sex partnerships has reached a point where official inaction is no longer tenable to petition sponsors.

“Ni kwamba ushoga unaendelea kwa kasi wa kustaajabisha na lazima sasa serikali iingilie kati,” he recounted.

Instead of taking an overt partisan stand on the floor submissions, Mwakideu pivoted the broadcast into a direct public sounding board. He turned the controversy directly over to his listeners, asking whether executive muscle and heightened police vigilance should actually be deployed to police personal conduct across the country.

“Najiuliza maswali. Do you think Kenya should go hard on LGBTQ?” he asked.

### The Legal Machinery Behind the Push

The broadcaster’s intervention comes as the debate moves from rhetoric into formal parliamentary procedure. The National Assembly Committee on Public Petitions, under the leadership of Runyenjes MP Muchangi Karemba, has already recommended concrete executive steps.

Karemba’s committee directed its recommendations squarely at the Ministry of Interior, demanding that law enforcement officers actively invoke existing statutes that penalize same-sex sexual acts. The committee singled out Sections 162, 163, and 165 of Kenya’s Penal Code for rigorous enforcement.

Under Section 162, convictions for certain same-sex offences carry prison terms of up to 14 years, while attempts at the same offences can draw sentences of up to seven years under Section 163. Section 165 prescribes penalties of up to five years for acts classified under gross indecency between men.

By turning an institutional report into an unvarnished on-air referendum, Mwakideu brought the reality of those punitive statutes out of committee rooms and into the morning commute. The discussion leaves the spotlight lingering on both Parliament and the Ministry of Interior, as listeners and lawmakers remain divided over how state machinery will handle the committee's directives.
