Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna’s public pledge on Saturday to buy a replacement vehicle for a taxi driver injured in Homa Bay brings a high-profile end to a month-long dispute over who pays for civilian collateral damage during political rallies.

The commitment follows weeks of uncertainty for Steven Otieno, a taxi driver whose vehicle was burned and who suffered severe physical injuries after being caught in violence linked to the opposition Linda Mwananchi movement on August 16, 2026.

While political movements routinely contract local drivers, private vehicles, and informal logistics crews to move personnel to regional events, the fallout in Homa Bay highlighted the absolute lack of legal protections or clear insurance frameworks for third-party operators when rallies turn violent.

### The Attack on the Road

Otieno had been hired on August 16 to transport Abbie Zuena, a key mobiliser for the Linda Mwananchi movement. As the convoy attempted to exit the area after the event, Otieno’s vehicle encountered armed groups of youths blocking the road.

Attackers intercepted the car, demanding money and personal belongings before launching a physical assault. Otieno sustained multiple deep cut wounds across his face, back, arm, hands, and feet, along with a fractured arm. 

![Embakasi East MP Babu Owino (left), and Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna during their triumphant Entry to Homa Bay Town during the August 16 Linda Mwananchi rally.](https://i.ibb.co/39S5wGng/aebf333aad3c.webp)

After losing consciousness, he was rushed to hospital by witnesses who contacted his family. Following emergency stabilization, he was moved to Nightingale Hospital in Kisumu for a three-day admission and remains under ongoing medical monitoring for non-healed wounds and bone fractures.

While Otieno was seeking medical aid, his vehicle was driven toward a nearby church and set ablaze, destroying the primary economic asset supporting both him and his family.

### Disputed Liability and Private Disclaimers

The loss of the vehicle created a immediate operational and legal rift between the driver and the political coordinators who hired him. 

The car did not belong to Otieno outright; it was owned by his sister, a young widow who had handed him the keys to operate as a commercial taxi to generate household income. 

In the weeks following the attack, a dispute emerged over who was responsible for compensating the family for the destroyed asset. Zuena maintained that she had fulfilled her immediate moral obligation by spending more than Ksh 300,000 out of pocket to clear Otieno’s medical bills at the private hospital, alongside direct cash assistance. However, she rejected claims that she was personally responsible for purchasing a replacement vehicle, insisting that property damage claims had to be processed through legal channels.

With no formal commercial insurance covering political violence and no corporate entity liable for the convoy, Otieno was left without income, facing mounting medical expenses while attempting to navigate legal processes against unknown attackers.

### Political Damage Control

![Large stones have been placed across key roads as security concerns mount ahead of the political gathering in Homa Bay Town on August 16, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/Y45nG8Mm/1b7de5531be9.webp)

Facing a dead end in negotiations with local event coordinators, Otieno issued public appeals to senior figures linked to the Linda Mwananchi platform, including Embakasi East MP Babu Owino.

On Saturday, September 19, Senator Sifuna intervened publicly after reading a news report detailing Otieno’s situation.

"We will replace your vehicle Steven. The Linda Mwananchi team will be in touch," Sifuna announced, effectively overriding the legal disclaimers issued by local event handlers and absorbing the financial loss into the movement's central political budget.

### Law Enforcement and Security Scrutiny

While Sifuna’s pledge addresses the immediate material loss for Otieno's family, the security breakdown that led to the arson remains an open law enforcement matter.

The National Cohesion and Integration Commission (NCIC) issued a formal condemnation of the Homa Bay clashes, warning against the deployment of organized youth groups at political gatherings.

Police investigations into the August 16 violence have centered on an individual identified by authorities as Olivetti, whom detectives allege helped mobilize the armed groups. The Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) has ordered the suspect to surrender for formal questioning as investigations into the arson and assault continue.
