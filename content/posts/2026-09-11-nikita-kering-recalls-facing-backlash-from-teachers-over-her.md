NAIROBI - Kenyan recording artist Nikita Kering has publicly disclosed the extent to which academic faculty discouraged her artistic ambitions during her formative years, describing a period of emotional strain that influenced her professional trajectory.

**What we know:**
*   Nikita Kering reports that her high school teachers frequently reacted negatively to her proactive involvement in performance arts.
*   The artist states that this recurring criticism led her to withdraw from extracurricular activities and conceal her creative talents throughout secondary school.
*   Kering identifies this past experience as the primary reason for her current hesitation toward digital content creation.
*   The singer is sharing these experiences to advocate for young creatives facing similar pressures in educational environments.

Addressing the nature of these encounters, Kering stated, "I remember always volunteering for every performance, but it felt like my passion annoyed the people who were supposed to guide me. It got to a point where I genuinely believed there was something wrong with me just for being visible."

According to the artist, the internalisation of this criticism resulted in a long-term apprehension regarding public performance and self-promotion. Kering noted that the impact of her school experience persisted well into her professional career, necessitating a deliberate effort to overcome a tendency toward reclusiveness.

"That bullying made me hide what I was doing a lot," Kering explained. "And after high school, I never wanted to post, never wanted to. In fact, even until today, the concept of content creation is just one thing that I've had to really force myself to get into and to get back into."

Representatives for the artist confirmed that Kering is currently focusing her efforts on reclaiming her creative space, intending to use her platform to support aspiring artists who feel stifled by traditional educational expectations.
