A sharp fiscal divide underpins the tax adjustments taking effect this financial year, pitting aggressive protectionist penalties against selective relief for domestic consumer goods.

The Kenya Revenue Authority outlined the operational reality of the Finance Act 2026, confirming that the revised tax framework applies immediate pressure on foreign agricultural imports and wagering platforms while lifting burdens from local bottlers.

![An image of the Treasury Cabinet Secretary John Mbadi, speaking during a KRA meeting mid 2025.](https://i.ibb.co/Lh98B2vh/50bc72ce04c0.webp)

At the center of the state's domestic production push is imported sugar. The levy on specified foreign sugar imports jumps nearly fivefold from Ksh7.50 to Ksh40 per kilogram, an adjustment explicitly engineered to price foreign sweetener out of local market dominance. Similar protectionist logic applies to vehicle registrations, with antique, vintage, and classic models now attracting an excise duty set at 50 per cent of their excisable value.

At the same time, the state has tightened its fiscal grip on the digital betting economy. A uniform 5 per cent excise duty now applies to all amounts deposited for gaming purposes, capturing wagering on horse racing alongside standard digital betting activity.

![An undated image of the KRA entrance](https://i.ibb.co/fYK4tHRw/c6ac733fcc44.webp)

Beverage manufacturers face a bifurcated rate card. Unsweetened juices maintain a holding charge of Ksh14.14 per litre, while varieties containing added sugar or sweeteners incur a steeper Ksh20-per-litre levy. In a notable concession to domestic manufacturers, the tax authority completely removed the excise duty on bottled water, erasing the previous Ksh6.41-per-litre charge that had long burdened the sector.

While wider legislative provisions under the 2026 fiscal package continue to phase in, these excise duty modifications became operational immediately upon the commencement of the financial year on July 1, 2026, leaving businesses and consumers adjusting to the new ledger.
