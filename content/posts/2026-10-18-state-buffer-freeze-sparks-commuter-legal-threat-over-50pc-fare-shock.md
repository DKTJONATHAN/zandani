The government’s deliberate decision to lock away its fuel price stabilisation reserves has triggered an escalating regulatory dispute, as commuter representatives prepare a constitutional challenge against an unbuffered pump price surge that is driving bus fares up by as much as 50 per cent.

The Counties Commuters Welfare Association (COCOWA) on Sunday issued a 14-day notice to the Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority (EPRA) and the Ministry of Energy, demanding an immediate reversal of the October fuel review or face formal legal proceedings over what the lobby terms a complete lack of public participation.

At the centre of the dispute is the sharp price revision enacted on October 14, which added Ksh12.93 to a litre of super petrol and pushed diesel up by Ksh9.90. While the regulator attributed the changes to underlying import metrics, it was the state's strategic refusal to activate consumer cushions that cemented the price jump.

### The Stabilisation Shield Held Back

EPRA Director General Engineer Edward Mwirigi Kinyua confirmed that the state intentionally opted against tapping the fuel price stabilisation fund during the mid-month review. According to Kinyua, authorities chose to preserve the reserves to absorb potential international petroleum shocks down the line, rather than shielding current domestic buyers from the October spike.

![Eng. Edward Kinyua, Petroleum and Gas Director at EPRA speaking at a media roundtable](https://i.ibb.co/k65p8Dmx/9584be48a34d.webp)

Kinyua further signaled that the regulator has no plans to issue an interim price adjustment, effectively locking motorists and public transport users into the elevated rates until the next statutory review cycle.

For commuter lobbies, withholding that fiscal buffer without engaging passenger representatives constitutes a failure of administrative fairness. COCOWA argued Sunday that the burden of the state's defensive treasury strategy was passed entirely to the public with zero consultation.

"Within this period, we demand clear communication on the factors driving fuel prices, formal engagement with commuter representatives and concrete proposals to cushion commuters against the escalating cost of transport," the association stated.

COCOWA insisted that unless the Ministry of Energy, EPRA, and civil administration desks initiate joint structured talks within the fortnight, any room for administrative settlement will close in favour of court litigation.

### Street Fares Outrun Official Caps

The immediate consequence of the pricing decision has manifested across public service vehicle stages, where informal operators have far outpaced official fare recommendations.

![An image of a fuel pump.](https://i.ibb.co/WW1TsYnp/6e05a5cc49ec.webp)

Following EPRA's review, the Matatu Owners Association (MOA), through its President Albert Karakacha, advised its member saccos to cap transport increases at 10 per cent to match operational cost increases.

On the ground, however, that advisory ceiling has broken down. Several independent operators and individual matatu Saccos across major urban corridors have instituted fare spikes ranging between 40 and 50 per cent, leaving passengers absorbing disproportionate markups on daily commutes.

Without an institutional mechanism to tie regulated fuel revisions to standardized passenger tariff caps, commuters remain fully exposed to unregulated fare setter decisions.

### The 14-Day Clock

COCOWA’s threatened lawsuit hinges on the statutory requirement for public participation in economic decisions that substantially alter citizens' living costs. The association maintains that public transport consumers are primary end-users in the petroleum chain and cannot be excluded from pricing policy deliberations.

With EPRA ruling out any mid-cycle tariff revisions and the Ministry of Energy yet to table alternative relief mechanisms, the countdown now places state energy administrators on an uncompromising legal timetable that expires in early November.
