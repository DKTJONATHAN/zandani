Rigathi Gachagua turned a Kiambu cultural assembly into a direct recruitment pitch for the legal fraternity, challenging advocates who spent months securing bail for young protesters to deploy that same courtroom stamina as election observers in 2027.

Speaking on Saturday, November 7, 2026, during the second annual Ngemi cia Watho gathering in Thika, Kiambu County, the Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP) leader sought to transform a regional networking forum into a nationwide polling defense network. Gachagua explicitly commended advocates from the region for providing pro bono representation, raising cash bail, and securing releases for youths detained during the 2024 and 2025 Gen Z-led protests.

That historical legal intervention, he argued, must now shift toward monitoring voter registration and polling station operations across the country.

Organized by the GEMA WATHO Association, the Thika event assembled legal practitioners and political figures from the Gikuyu, Embu, Meru, and Akamba communities, drawing notable figures including Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna. Gachagua used the platform to urge advocates to step past routine practice and take up formal observer roles ahead of the next presidential contest.

"The processes are as important as the actual election," Gachagua stated, calling on lawyers to volunteer as observers nationwide to scrutinize voter registration and polling day procedures to ensure electoral integrity under President William Ruto's administration.

Addressing criticism regarding the nature of the meeting, the former Deputy President pushed back against claims that regional caucuses amount to ethnic division. He pointed to assemblies hosted by Western Kenya leaders Musalia Mudavadi and Moses Wetangula, as well as consultative forums held by Northern Kenya pastoralist representatives, questioning why Central Kenya meetings face singular public pushback.

"When the Mulembe nation, Wetangula and Musalia, calls their people together for a conversation, this is regional integration. When the people of Northern Kenya meet, they say the pastoralists are discussing their issues," Gachagua said. "But when the mountaineers meet, like they have done today, we are accused of tribalism. We are who we are, and we have no apologies to make to anybody."

Gachagua challenged the Law Society of Kenya (LSK) leadership to step up its institutional backbone, holding up veteran legal figures Paul Muite, Nelson Havi, and Martha Karua as reference points for challenging systemic administrative overreach.

Directing his message to the presidency, Gachagua insisted that voters in the region are not "refugees in their own country" and affirmed that the electorate would align behind a single presidential candidate in 2027. To sweeten the tie with the legal association, he pledged that should he win the presidency, the third edition of the Ngemi cia Watho event would be hosted directly at Sagana State Lodge.
