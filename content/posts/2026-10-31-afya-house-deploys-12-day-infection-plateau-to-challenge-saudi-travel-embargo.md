Kenya is anchoring its diplomatic challenge against Saudi Arabia's sweeping travel freeze on an uninterrupted 12-day plateau of zero new Ebola transmissions, arguing that Riyadh's border closure directly contradicts the clinical reality on the ground.

Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale issued the formal rebuttal on Saturday, October 31, characterizing the Gulf kingdom's suspension of passenger entry and visa processing as disproportionate. Afya House's defense rests on an epidemiological argument: the domestic outbreak remains strictly confined to the initial imported index cluster, showing no evidence of uncontained community transmission.

“The measures taken are not only excessive but not backed by the current epidemiological situation in the country,” Duale said, contesting the technical basis of Riyadh's risk evaluation.

### The Containment Ledger

![Health CS Aden Duale observing the Ebola Virus Disease (EVD) Rapid Response Team (RRT) Training of Trainers (ToT) programme in Nairobi, June 10, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/ebola-kenya-1.jpg.webp?itok=1Nu_Gwra)

The dispute follows Saudi Arabia’s unilateral decree barring its citizens from travelling to Kenya, freezing all visa issuance categories for Kenyan passport holders, and blocking arrivals who transit through third countries. Riyadh defended the measures as a standard protective cordon enacted by its public health authorities to insulate the Kingdom from external viral exposure.

To counter that assessment, Afya House published granular metrics tracking the containment operation. Surveillance teams have mapped and tracked all 135 contacts originating from the initial imported case, isolating 118 individuals under institutional quarantine.

National caseload records compiled through October 26 detail nine confirmed infections and two fatalities. Crucially, public health monitors have recorded no secondary infections outside the original ring, with the daily transmission count remaining flat since October 19.

### Exit Testing and Isolation Drawdown

The Ministry’s defense also points to clinical recovery milestones inside treatment centres. Out of seven patients currently under isolation, five have cleared active viral infection, posting their first negative polymerase chain reaction tests following therapy.

![Kenya's High Commission in Riyadh, UAE](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/KENYA-EMBASSY-RIYADH.jpeg.webp?itok=LXCe2K4r)

Those five patients are scheduled for confirmatory secondary testing on Monday, November 2. If those samples remain negative, the Ministry plans to discharge them on Tuesday, November 3. 

That release is slated to coincide with the clearance of 46 asymptomatic contacts whose quarantine monitoring periods have elapsed, supplementing 20 contacts previously discharged from monitoring.

### Diplomatic Escalation

With health authorities asserting that domestic containment protocols have stabilized the outbreak, the confrontation now shifts to bilateral channels. The Ministry of Foreign and Diaspora Affairs has assumed responsibility for formal negotiations with Riyadh, seeking to persuade Saudi authorities that travel corridors can safely operate under targeted screening rather than total isolation.

Duale reiterated that multinational disease control relies on reciprocal technical transparency rather than unilateral travel barriers. Whether Saudi Arabia’s public health directorate will review its restrictions hinges on the outcome of Kenya's November 2 verification tests and the subsequent clearance of the isolation wards.
