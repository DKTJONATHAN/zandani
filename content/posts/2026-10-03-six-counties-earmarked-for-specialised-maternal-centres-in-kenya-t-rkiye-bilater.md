Kenya is moving to address regional deficits in maternal and paediatric care through an infrastructure-sharing model that trades state-allocated land for Turkish capital investment across six counties.

The framework, anchored in a newly concluded Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) on health cooperation, will see Ankara finance the construction of Uwezo Hospitals and dedicated Kenya-Türkiye Centres for Children’s Ability and Women’s Health. In return, Kenya will provide the building land and shoulder complete operational and administrative custody once the facilities open their doors.

Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale outlined the arrangement on Saturday, October 3, following bilateral discussions in Nairobi with outgoing Turkish Ambassador to Kenya, Subutay Yüksel. 

### Devolving Specialised Care to Six Hubs

The planned network targets six geographically dispersed counties: Nairobi, Nandi, Taita Taveta, Isiolo, Homa Bay, and Uasin Gishu. 

![Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale meets outgoing Turkish Ambassador to Kenya Subutay Yüksel, October 3, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/60bbdNxb/abf68d01a850.webp)

By placing specialised centres in pastoralist belts like Isiolo and coastal hinterlands like Taita Taveta alongside western hubs such as Homa Bay and Nandi, the Ministry of Health aims to curb the domestic referral bottleneck that routinely funnels complex maternal and childhood complications into national referral facilities in the capital.

The target facilities will specialise in advanced paediatric care, child disability rehabilitation, and women’s health—clinical departments that face acute resource constraints outside Kenya’s top-tier urban private and national hospitals.

“The conclusion of the Kenya-Türkiye Memorandum of Understanding on health cooperation provides a framework for sustained collaboration,” Duale stated, pointing to the bilateral agreement as a direct vehicle for Kenya’s Universal Health Coverage (UHC) rollout.

### Closing the Specialist Staffing Gap

While infrastructure forms the physical core of the pact, the operational sustainability of the six hubs depends on an aggressive expansion of medical specialist training. Kenya has long wrestled with an uneven distribution of sub-specialists, particularly in neonatology, paediatric surgery, and maternal-fetal medicine.

Under the cooperation pact, Kenya will tap Turkish medical faculties and clinical networks for technology exchange, knowledge sharing, and advanced residency training for Kenyan healthcare workers. 

This personnel-exchange channel expands on existing engagements run by the Turkish Cooperation and Coordination Agency (TİKA), which has previously supplied clinical equipment, small-scale hospital structural upgrades, and targeted continuous medical training across the country.

![Officials from the Turkish Cooperation and Coordination Agency (TİKA) and Food Banking Kenya during a past initiative, 2024.](https://i.ibb.co/pjf3VZZ6/4811d00d5c90.webp)

Duale noted that training cadres locally and establishing joint professional exchange channels with Turkish medical institutions is designed to ensure the planned regional centres do not sit empty after their ribbon-cutting.

### Manufacturing and Supply Chain Priorities

Beyond hospital wings and doctor training, the bilateral pipeline touches on structural weaknesses exposed during recent global health emergencies. The discussions between Duale and Yüksel extended into joint pandemic preparedness, healthcare financing mechanisms, and the local manufacturing of medical supplies.

The Kenyan government has been attempting to cut its heavy reliance on imported medical consumables and pharmaceuticals by courting external partners to set up local production lines. Tying procurement, research, and technical collaboration to international partners forms part of the state's broader strategy to stabilise medical supply chains under a digitised primary healthcare architecture.

“As Kenya advances Universal Health Coverage through stronger primary healthcare, digital health, local manufacturing, research and innovation, health financing and climate-resilient systems, we look forward to drawing on Türkiye’s experience,” Duale said.

With Ambassador Yüksel concluding his diplomatic tour in Nairobi, technical teams from the Ministry of Health and Turkish development agencies are slated to transition the MoU into site-specific project timelines, beginning with county land demarcation and architectural validations across the six selected devolved units.
