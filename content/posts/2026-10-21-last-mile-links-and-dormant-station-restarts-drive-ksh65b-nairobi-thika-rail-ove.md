Reactivating closed stations and constructing last-mile pedestrian networks have emerged as the central operational push behind Kenya Railways Corporation's plan to revamp the 57-kilometre Nairobi Central–Thika commuter line.

Under the World Bank-backed Kenya Urban Mobility Improvement Project (KUMIP), the rail operator is targeting 15 station nodes along the busy inter-county corridor to address the structural disconnect between heavy rail assets and surrounding suburban communities.

![Passengers alighting a train at the Nairobi Central Railway Station, Nairobi, December 9, 2024.](https://i.ibb.co/chYnDNp6/b99b12b74fa7.webp)

While eight stations along the route are currently operational—including Nairobi Central, Makadara, Dandora, Mwiki, Githurai, Kahawa West, Ruiru, and Thika—four existing stations have sat non-operational due to accessibility gaps and inadequate station infrastructure. Dormant stops at Kenyatta University, Mutindwa, Kariobangi South, and Kaloleni have left high-density residential and institutional pockets reliant on congested road transit despite sitting directly along the active rail corridor.

To bridge these feeder gaps, the upgrade moves beyond track repair by embedding a mandatory one-kilometer infrastructure radius around each station. Kenya Railways plans to construct dedicated access roads, paved walkways, cycle paths, and street lighting surrounding the facilities, aiming to create safe pedestrian channels that reduce daily commuter reliance on short-distance feeder minibuses and motorbikes.

The plan also expands the line's footprint through three newly proposed railway halts at KPCU, Maili Saba, and Kalimoni in Juja, completing a 15-stop network running from Nairobi's central business district through Kiambu County.

![Trains Railway](https://i.ibb.co/VYtbzw1g/08ed339e1bf4.webp)

Beyond passenger handling, the operational reliability of the 57-kilometre line will rely on a heavy maintenance facility currently under development at Makadara Railway Station, near the Kenya Pipeline Company headquarters. The workshop is designed to handle servicing, repairs, and overhauls for locomotives, passenger coaches, and freight wagons locally, removing the operational delay of moving defective rolling stock off the active line.

The $500 million (Ksh65 billion) World Bank funding commitment for the Nairobi-Thika corridor comes as transport authorities evaluate broader proposals for the wider Nairobi Commuter Rail network, including full track electrification and the acquisition of new electric trainsets.

Civil works and station modernisations along the 15-stop stretch are scheduled to proceed in phases, with priority placed on restoring service to the four idle stations and building out pedestrian feeder links across the urban corridor.
