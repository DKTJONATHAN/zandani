The Agriculture and Food Authority (AFA) has ordered the immediate shutdown of avocado harvesting for sea exports beginning Friday, October 30, 2026, establishing a defensive regulatory cordon to protect immature fruit from being stripped from orchards ahead of the 2027 main season.

The intervention targets long-term export credibility in international terminal markets by starving off-season speculative harvesting. Announcing the policy on Wednesday, October 28, AFA Director General Calistus Efukho confirmed that an exhaustive national assessment of primary growing belts showed tree yields have dropped below commercial maritime shipping thresholds.

“We conducted a countrywide survey in all the major avocado production zones to assess the current production patterns and forecast production for the main season next year,” Efukho stated. “The survey findings are that current production volumes are low. You are hereby notified that the closing of Hass, Fuerte, Pinkerton, and Jumbo harvesting season for sea shipment will come into force with effect from Friday, October 30, 2026.”

### Choking Smuggling Channels

![Ovocado fruits ready for transportation](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/avocado_fruits.jpg.webp?itok=gSFIfu7Q)

Beyond idling sea reefer containers, the Authority moved to seal secondary supply routes through which traders traditionally bypass seasonal suspensions. Regulators issued a direct warning to mango traders against co-packing avocados inside sea-bound mango consignments—a practice that breaches the Horticultural Crops Regulations of 2020.

With mango season entering peak activity as avocado volumes bottom out, port inspectors have been directed to ensure that any container holding an export permit for mangoes contains nothing else.

“Ensure that the quantity of mango being exported for which the permit is being sought should only constitute mango fruits in the consignment,” Efukho instructed exporters.

Similar restrictions were applied to commercial agro-processors. The Authority closed the harvesting season for avocado oil millers and placed a ban on importing avocado fruit for extraction. The cross-border restriction was introduced to neutralize a known industry loophole where domestic traders prematurely pluck local immature avocados, forge paperwork, and present the sub-standard crop at extraction plants as imported raw material.

### The Air Freight Exception

While sea corridors close on Friday, the Authority has left high-value air cargo routes open, permitting vetted commercial exporters to fly select consignments out of international airports. That relief, however, arrives with elevated compliance checks.

All outbound consignments routed through air freight will undergo physical inspection by the Horticultural Crops Directorate (HCD). Consolidators and commercial packing houses must furnish end-to-end traceability documentation for every pallet, tying fruit lots back to registered farms to verify fruit dry matter content and deter farm poaching.

For oil processors caught holding harvested fruit inventory, the state has imposed a tight audit schedule. Facilities holding processed avocado crude or refined oil designated for export must present their inventories for physical state verification by Monday, November 2, 2026, to receive clearance.

Efukho cautioned that failure to adhere to the directives carries severe operational penalties. Consignments assembled in defiance of the suspension will have their phytosanitary and export documentation denied, while non-compliant exporters and millers risk immediate cancellation of their agricultural trade licenses ahead of next year's crop cycle.
