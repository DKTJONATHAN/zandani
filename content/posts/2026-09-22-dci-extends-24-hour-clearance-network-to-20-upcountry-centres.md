The Directorate of Criminal Investigations has extended its live biometric clearance network to 20 stations across Central Kenya and neighbouring counties, formalising the second phase of a decentralisation drive aimed at collapsing processing times for job seekers outside the capital.

The deployment connects regional application desks directly to the directorate’s central automated platform, bringing the total number of linked stations to 26 nationwide. For applicants in the newly integrated zones, the migration removes the procedural delays that historically tied up paperwork in physical transit between upcountry stations and headquarters.

DCI Director General Mohamed Amin stated that under optimal operating conditions, the upgraded Multi-Biometric Identification System (MBIS) compresses clearance processing from a two-week wait down to a single working day.

"For the public, it promises fast-track police clearance certificate processing and thus reducing the turnaround time from 14 days to around one day under optimal operating conditions," Amin said during the system's initial commissioning.

### The Regional Grid

The Phase Two rollout concentrates heavily on the Mount Kenya economic belt, parts of lower Eastern, and northern administrative posts. The newly integrated stations comprise Kajiado Central, Kajiado West-Kisamis, Machakos, Thika, Kamwangi, Murang’a, Nyeri, Nyandarua-Ol Kalou, Nanyuki, Laikipia East-Makutano, and Samburu-Maralal.

Coverage also extends across Kerugoya, Mwea, Embu, Chuka, Marimanti, Maara, Kathwana, Meru, and Isiolo.

Before the latest installations, automated biometric submissions were restricted to six operational hubs within Nairobi. The geographic expansion means applicants across the 20 outlying administrative zones can now submit their digital records locally without facing the multi-week lag common to manual document transfers.

### The 37-Station Integration Gap

Despite the expansion across the Central region, substantial service disparities remain across the national footprint. The DCI currently maintains 63 Police Clearance Certificate application centres countrywide, meaning 37 stations—representing 58 percent of the network—have yet to be migrated to the MBIS architecture.

Applicants outside the 26 live centres must continue relying on older verification pipelines until technical installation teams reach their jurisdictions. The directorate indicated that upcoming phases will target remaining operational Huduma Centres across western, coastal, and rift regions to achieve countrywide parity.

Technically designated as ABIS Version 5, the new clearance infrastructure replaces isolated fingerprint cards with a unified profile capturing fingerprints, palmprints, and facial recognition data. Beyond applicant convenience, the agency is leveraging the platform to modernise criminal records management and improve forensic suspect matching across devolved police formations.
