State assurances and the clearing of historical arrears failed to coax the national football team onto the pitch, as a dispute over payout scales left Harambee Stars without training sessions for a third consecutive day.

While Sports Cabinet Secretary Salim Mvurya issued a formal breakdown confirming that the ministry had honoured financial commitments for prior assignments—including outings in Zambia, South Africa, Turkey, and an Under-20 CECAFA engagement in Tanzania—players rejected the remedy as inadequate for their current service.

![Harambee Stars](https://i.ibb.co/ymsqcMVj/761180cfb0c6.webp)

The friction centers on a stark valuation gap between government disbursement structures and player expectations. Members of the squad received Ksh62,400 for last November’s assignment in Turkey and an equivalent sum for June’s fixture against Lesotho in South Africa. The amounts fall well short of the six-figure allowances the players maintain they are accustomed to collecting for national team duty.

The financial stand-off persists even as the Football Kenya Federation maintains a line of communication with the sports ministry to process pending dues through administrative channels. Technical bench salaries have similarly been cleared through direct transfers and FKF mechanisms, yet the intervention has done little to alter the mood in the camp.

![An image of Sports CS Salim Mvurya with an insert of Harambee Stars players](https://i.ibb.co/KcdrDK9Q/c5764891b357.webp)

The standoff places immediate pressure on national preparations. Kenya is scheduled to host Eritrea in an opening Group D match for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers at Nyayo Stadium, with kickoff slated for 4 pm on Saturday.

With just 72 hours remaining before the opening whistle at Nyayo Stadium, the unresolved disagreement over compensation structures leaves the technical staff navigating a severe pre-match disruption ahead of a tournament that Kenya is co-hosting alongside Uganda and Tanzania in July 2027.
