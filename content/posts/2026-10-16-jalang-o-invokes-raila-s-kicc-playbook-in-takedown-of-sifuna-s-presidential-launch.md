Lang'ata MP Phelix Odiwuor, widely known as Jalang'o, has taken direct aim at Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna's presidential ambitions, framing the senator's newly launched campaign vehicle as a flawed exercise in basic political stagecraft that fell far short of the standard set by the late ODM leader Raila Odinga.

Speaking during a media engagement in Nairobi, the media-personality-turned-lawmaker offered a harsh assessment of Sifuna’s strategic readiness for the 2027 General Election, predicting that the senator would ultimately finish fourth in the presidential contest. Jalang'o argued that Sifuna's current approach lacks structure and momentum, characterizing his campaign apparatus as having "zero plan, zero anything."

### The KICC Playbook Blueprint

At the center of Jalang'o's critique was the recent public unveiling of The Equitable Party (TEP), the outfit designated to anchor the Linda Mwananchi Movement ahead of the 2027 elections. While the convention brought together several opposition figures and culminated in Sifuna’s endorsement as party leader and candidate, Jalang'o argued that the physical execution of the event failed to convey national clout.

Drawing on his background in entertainment and mass gathering logistics, Jalang'o contrasted Sifuna's venue selection with the disciplined political theater that Raila Odinga used to project authority during major political declarations.

“The one day my brother Sifuna would have made a statement was during the launch of his party,” Jalang'o said. “You would have made sure that he does things the way Baba used to teach us. Go to KICC, the boardroom, and call the 5,000 delegates seated there branded in your party. Outside at the stairs, you then have a rally and make a statement.”

Jalang'o asserted that Sifuna's organizers confined the launch to a small room filled with only a fraction of the supporters required to demonstrate genuine national reach, turning what should have been a major political moment into a muted affair.

### Nairobi Escalation and 2027 Rivalries

The critique highlights growing friction among Nairobi's top political figures as politicians begin positioning themselves ahead of the next election cycle. While Sifuna has pivoted toward national movement-building through TEP, Jalang'o signaled that the shift will not shield the senator from intense competition back home in the capital.

Without detailing specific electoral targets, the Lang'ata MP noted that local factions across Nairobi are actively consolidating power and readying themselves for direct confrontations once campaign season fully takes shape.

With Sifuna now officially positioned at the helm of TEP and Jalang'o publicly challenging his political strategy, the rivalry between two of the capital's most visible communicators signals an early clash over both organizational muscle and political visual management ahead of 2027.
