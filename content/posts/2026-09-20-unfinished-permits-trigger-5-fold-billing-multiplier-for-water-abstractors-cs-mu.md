Water abstractors who halt their regulatory permitting process midway face a steep fivefold increase in monthly billing rates, Water and Sanitation Cabinet Secretary Eric Mugaa stated on Sunday, September 20.

According to the ministry, initial permit applicants who set up extraction infrastructure but fail to finalize their licenses are being default-billed by the Water Resources Authority (WRA) based on unverified maximum volume declarations rather than actual measured consumption.

### Default Estimations vs. Validated Tariffs

The financial gap created by incomplete documentation is substantial. Unmetered abstractors drawing raw water from underground aquifers, rivers, or dams are assigned a default penalty rate of Ksh 10 per 1,000 litres.

By contrast, abstractors who complete the full authorization cycle and operate functional meters pay significantly lower rates. Water abstracted for combined domestic and livestock use is priced at Ksh 2 per 1,000 litres, while water designated strictly for domestic consumption carries a rate of Ksh 2.50 per 1,000 litres.

![Wtaer CS Eric Mugaa during a past event](https://i.ibb.co/YTkwXYjB/794fdfa823ea.webp)

"When the Water Resources Authority are billing you every month, they charge you based on the approximation you provided during your application, because you don't have an active meter," Mugaa explained during a Sunday briefing.

He noted that the maximum estimated tariff remains active automatically until abstractors verify their actual draw with operational measuring hardware.

### Smart Meter Integration and Digital Reporting

To eliminate reliance on unverified volumetric projections, the ministry is pushing for a broader technological transition across extraction catchments. Mugaa revealed that the government is urging abstractors to adopt smart, GSM-enabled water meters capable of transmitting automated readings directly from extraction points to WRA databases.

For abstractors currently operating older volumetric meters, the authority has deployed a mobile application allowing permit holders to submit monthly physical meter readings digitally, bypassing manual site audits and preventing inflated default billing.

![Apartment Water](https://i.ibb.co/JjHjHdcF/aa890c4b0730.webp)

"For those with old volumetric meters, there is an application by the agency... whereby you can actually transmit your meter reading every month to the agency," Mugaa added.

### Abstraction Levies vs. County Utility Bills

Mugaa also addressed persistent confusion between national abstraction levies and domestic tap water bills, noting that WRA charges operate under a completely distinct legal framework from municipal supply invoices.

While county water service providers bill urban households for treated tap water distribution, WRA charges are levied strictly at the primary point of natural extraction. This applies to individuals, agricultural enterprises, and commercial operators drawing raw water directly from natural sources.

"The water bill that comes from your county government or the utility that provides water, and water use charges by WRA are two different things," Mugaa said, emphasizing that settling a local utility bill does not discharge financial obligations owed to the national water resources regulator.

The ministry's clarification comes as WRA steps up monitoring across major river basins and commercial groundwater points, leaving unmetered abstractors with the choice of completing their pending documentation or continuing to absorb default financial penalties each month.
