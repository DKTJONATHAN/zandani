A Ksh60 billion perimeter road cutting across five lakefront counties will form the fiscal backbone of the government's newly cleared Ksh92 billion highway expansion, setting up an intensive construction push across western Kenya and the Rift Valley before the end of the year.

The investment, sanctioned during a Cabinet meeting on Friday, October 9, commits public resources to 909 kilometres of trunk infrastructure divided into three discrete commercial corridors under the Roads for Rural Economic Development Programme.

Rather than dispersing capital evenly, the layout directs roughly 65 percent of the total Ksh92 billion portfolio into a single project: the 400-kilometre Lake Victoria Ring Road. That aquatic transport crescent, designed to bypass congested inland junctions, runs through Busia, Siaya, Kisumu, Homa Bay, and Migori.

Treasury Cabinet Secretary John Mbadi confirmed in September that the state had secured concessional backing from the World Bank to underwrite the works, clearing the primary financing hurdle for the multi-county route.

### The Lake Victoria Perimeter

![President William Ruto chairing a cabinet meeting on Friday, October 9, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/5PKydrN/2cfc54c1fbef.webp)

The 400-kilometre ring alignment is engineered to link remote fishing bays, border zones, and agricultural deltas that have historically lacked continuous tarmac access.

In Busia County, the survey cuts through Bunyala and Funyula before crossing into Siaya, where it skirts the Yala Swamp and weaves through Bondo, Usenge, Osieko, and Dhogoye.

The southern arc extends through Kendu Bay and Homa Bay town, pressing outward along the lake peninsula to Mbita, Rusinga Island, and Suba South. From there, the tarmac traverses the Gwassi hills before terminating at Muhuru Bay along the Migori border, creating an uninterrupted commercial link between the Tanzanian frontier and the Ugandan entry points.

### Opening High-Altitude Agricultural Belts

The remaining Ksh32 billion of the approved package will finance two inland corridors designed to siphon farm produce out of high-altitude agricultural belts without routing traffic through regional urban gridlock.

The first leg, the 253-kilometre South Rift-Lake Region Gateway Corridor, bridges Nakuru, Bomet, Narok, Kericho, and Migori. The alignment connects Neissuit, Salgaa, Elburgon, Turi, Muchorwe, Langwenda, and Kamwaura, dividing into interconnected operational sections:

![A collage of sections of KeNHA-maintained road and a section of the Kisumu Busia Highway during maintenance works](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/kenha_6_0.jpg.webp?itok=1iFmp31E)

* Neissuit–Salgaa–Elburgon
* Turi–Embomoss
* Embomoss–Tengecha
* Tengecha–Labotiet
* Labotiet–Kilgoris
* Kilgoris–Mariwa–Sare

Parallel to that route, the 256-kilometre Great Highlands Connectivity Corridor targets the tea and dairy zones spanning Kericho and the greater Rift Valley. It tracks from Kericho town through Ainamoi, Poiywek, Chepkoiyo, and Girimori toward the Kenegut Border.

Past the boundary, the survey pushes through Mbogo Valley, Kamelilo, Maraba, Kabirer, Sirwa, Lessos, Chuiyet, Kipkabus, and Nyaru, formalizing a heavy freight link capable of moving farm inputs and produce directly into regional processing nodes.

### November Ground-Breaking

Cabinet officials stated that the three road programmes are meant to slash domestic haulage expenses and shorten delivery turnarounds for rural growers who face steep transport penalties during wet weather.

With key technical designs and pre-construction surveys in their final stages, the Ministry of Roads plans to break ground across priority segments in November 2026. Contractors face immediate pressure to establish site camps and mobilize plant machinery to meet the fourth-quarter rollout timeline.
