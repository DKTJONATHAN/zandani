The newly minted partnership between President William Ruto’s United Democratic Alliance (UDA) and the Orange Democratic Movement (ODM) is facing an early structural test, as local turf wars and provocative national rhetoric threaten to derail their cooperative framework ahead of the 2027 General Election.

The immediate flashpoint has emerged in Mombasa, where ODM’s regional leadership has demanded formal disciplinary action against UDA Secretary General Hassan Omar. The dispute centers on Omar’s recent controversial comments suggesting that "even the dead would rise to ensure President William Ruto wins"-remarks he has since defended as metaphorical, but which ODM officials argue cross the line into election-related provocation.

Speaking on behalf of the ODM Coast caucus, Mombasa County Chairman Mohamed Khamis warned that such rhetoric actively undermines the mutual trust required to sustain their national partnership. Khamis raised a fundamental question that now hangs over the entire alliance, asking whether ODM is viewed as a genuine partner or an adversary within the broad-based arrangement.

Beyond the rhetorical sparring, the clash in Mombasa exposes unresolved structural pressures that have plagued the coalition since its inception. Despite formally agreeing in August to draft a joint manifesto and build a unified front for the 2027 polls, the two parties remain deeply divided over electoral geography.

ODM is aggressively pushing to protect its traditional strongholds, particularly along the Coast and in Nyanza, by restricting UDA from fielding rival candidates in those zones. Conversely, UDA’s national leadership has maintained that political parties within the arrangement must remain free to field candidates across the country, rejecting any localized zoning agreements.

This local resistance directly challenges President Ruto’s ongoing efforts to project a unified front. While Ruto has consistently characterized ODM as a vital partner-reaffirming this stance during his recent political engagements in Nyanza-the ground reality in Mombasa reveals that local leaders are struggling to reconcile national executive agreements with regional political survival.

By challenging UDA to field candidates openly if they believe they have sufficient support, ODM’s Mombasa wing has signaled that the party is unwilling to cede ground quietly. As both sides continue their delicate negotiations over power-sharing, the dispute highlights that the road to a cohesive 2027 joint campaign will require resolving deep-seated territorial rivalries that executive handshake agreements have yet to heal.
