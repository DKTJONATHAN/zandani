According to the Higher Education Loans Board, thousands of university loan beneficiaries across Kenya stand to safeguard their credit ratings and clearance status after the state fund ordered all public and private employers to surrender monthly payroll deductions by the 15th day of every month.

### What we know
* The revolving fund is tightening enforcement against negligent payroll managers to protect cash reserves needed for incoming university cohorts.
* Workers have historically suffered uncredited loan accounts because corporate HR departments delayed remitting statutory deductions already withheld from paychecks.
* Statutory fines will now accumulate rapidly against defaulting firms, transferring the financial burden of delayed remittances entirely onto corporate balance sheets.
* Portal enforcement requiring mandatory digital CSV uploads eliminates manual reporting delays that previously masked unremitted employee deductions.

Emphasising the strict compliance calendar, the student loan agency issued a firm directive to corporate HR desks and public agency accounting officers to eliminate administrative processing lags.

“Ensure all HELB loan deductions are remitted by the 15th of every month to maintain timely compliance,” the board stated in an official notice issued on Monday, September 14, 2026.

Under existing statutory guidelines, employers operate under a three-tier obligation framework requiring them to disclose any beneficiaries on their payroll, deduct the prescribed monthly installments from salaries, and discharge the funds directly to the board. To complete the monthly submission, payroll officers must generate employee schedules using a standardized CSV template and upload the records through the digital employer portal under the remittance portal tab before executing bank transfers.

Failure to adhere to the submission protocol triggers immediate financial sanctions against non-compliant entities. The board levies a standard statutory fine of KSh3,000 per month for every unremitted or undeclared employee, alongside an additional five per cent monthly surcharge on all outstanding balances until the debt is cleared.

This statutory crackdown addresses a long-standing grievance among Kenyan workers who find themselves penalized for loan defaults despite having the money regularly deducted from their payslips. When accounting departments retain collected funds to bolster their own cash flows, the board's system continues to reflect the employee's loan account as unpaid, effectively blocking young professionals from obtaining credit compliance certificates required for public appointments and commercial loans. By aggressively enforcing the 15th-day deadline, the board is shifting the pressure of compliance back onto corporate leadership, ensuring that corporate liquidity challenges do not ruin the financial records of individual workers or starve the national higher education revolving fund.
