By invoking Section 20(5) of the Political Parties Act on September 10, the Office of the Registrar of Political Parties (ORPP) closed a month-long regulatory maneuver that effectively decoupled Embakasi East MP Babu Owino’s political outfit from a contested trademark dispute.

Documents made public by the party on Thursday reveal a synchronized administrative process designed to formalize "The Mwananchi Party" after its previous working moniker, Linda Mwananchi, became the subject of litigation before the Political Parties Disputes Tribunal (PPDT).

The paper trail indicates that party principals initiated the rescue mission on August 4 during an in-person consultation at the ORPP offices to establish the statutory status of the Linda Mwananchi name. Faced with conflicting ownership claims, the political vehicle pivoted toward a full statutory alteration, leveraging existing party machinery.

### The Thirteen-Day Gazette Window

![Embakasi East MP Babu Owino (left), and Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna during their triumphant Entry to Homa Bay Town during the August 16 Linda Mwananchi rally.](https://i.ibb.co/tpDKGHp2/873e452a873c.webp)

According to the official correspondence, the Registrar published a Gazette Notice on August 28 inviting public views on changing the name from Muungano Party to The Mwananchi Party. 

The statutory window immediately drew formal resistance. On August 30, an objection was registered against the proposed change. 

However, administrative friction cleared eight days later. The petitioner formally dropped the challenge on September 8, notifying the Registrar that the disputed grievances had been resolved. 

With the sole barrier removed and no other submissions tendered before the statutory deadline lapsed, the ORPP issued its conclusive determination on September 10. The Registrar notified the party leadership that the change had officially taken effect under Section 20(5) of Cap. 7D, cementing The Mwananchi Party onto the official register.

![An image of the LINDA Party preparing for a press conference during the party's launching](https://i.ibb.co/dNrpGz0/38d1f8b4a844.webp)

### Bypassing the Tribunal Challenge

The statutory clearance offers a tactical escape hatch from an escalating fight at the PPDT. 

Officials representing the Liberty National Democratic Alliance (LINDA) had mounted an urgent challenge against the movement, asserting prior rights over the "Linda" designation, its associated symbols, and corporate identity. The LINDA faction maintained that its own registration process began in November 2024, arguing that the subsequent adoption of the Linda Mwananchi branding breached protected political identity rules.

By securing certification as The Mwananchi Party through the Muungano Party framework, the faction effectively strips the tribunal dispute of its primary target. While the litigation concerning the reservation of the "Linda Mwananchi" title remains on the tribunal's docket, the political formation now possesses fully executed state certification under an uncontested brand.

The regulatory papers leave one practical question ahead of future electoral engagements: whether rival factions claiming intellectual ownership of the "Linda" movement will continue pursuing the now-vacated name before the PPDT, or redirect their challenge against the party's newly gazetted instruments.
