When an artificial intelligence laboratory in San Francisco flagged a synthetic echo chamber singing praises for Energy Cabinet Secretary Opiyo Wandayi, the official response was a classic Nairobi brush-off-delivered, curiously, with the exact same campaign hashtag the bots were trained to push.

### What we know

* **The AI Campaign:** Anthropic’s September 2026 threat intelligence report identified a domestic Kenya-based operation using its Claude AI model to mass-produce social media posts in automated batches of 50 to mimic organic public commentary.
* **The Target & Tags:** The network flooded online timelines with praise for CS Opiyo Wandayi's management of Kenya Power electricity tariffs using hashtags `#PowerReliefKE` and `#PoweringTheNewKenya`.
* **Commercial Crossover:** The unknown operator used the identical AI workflow to run commercial marketing accounts under the personas "SHANKI" and "Elkins Marketer."
* **Official Denial:** CS Wandayi denied any ministry involvement or authorization on Friday, arguing that coordinated anonymous accounts exist by the millions and demand hard evidence of sponsorship.

The mechanics detailed in Anthropic’s report paint a granular picture of how modern political astroturfing is evolving in Kenya. Rather than hiring rooms full of keyboard operatives to type out manual tweets, a single unnamed actor fed structured prompts into an AI engine, instructing it to output exactly 50 localized, spontaneous-sounding comments per batch. The goal was simple: manufacture a groundswell of public gratitude over scheduled electricity tariff adjustments.

What makes the operation particularly revealing is its apparent dual-use nature. The threat intelligence team found that the exact same technical setup generating pro-government energy commentary was simultaneously being deployed to market local retail brands under the accounts "SHANKI" and "Elkins Marketer." This commercial crossover suggests a mercenary for hire operating within Nairobi's digital PR ecosystem rather than a dedicated state-run intelligence operation.

While Anthropic explicitly noted there was no evidence linking the Ministry of Energy and Petroleum or any government entity to the campaign, its pro-administration stance naturally drew scrutiny.

Responding on Friday, September 11, CS Wandayi pushed back against the implication that positive digital noise equates to state funding.

“My attention has been drawn to some unverified report alleging the creation of some activity on social media that is purported to have been in praise of the actions of the Ministry of Energy and Petroleum regarding electricity tariffs,” Wandayi stated, dismissing the claims. “There are probably millions of anonymous and coordinated accounts on social media pushing different narratives every day. What matters is evidence.”

Yet, in a twist that underscores the fluid boundary between official communications and digital trend-jacking, Wandayi’s official Friday statement concluded with `#PoweringTheNewKenya`-one of the very hashtags flagged in the threat report.

While utilizing popular or trending tags in government press releases is standard PR practice and does not establish a physical link between the Cabinet Secretary and the bot network, it highlights an uncomfortable reality: in Nairobi's hyper-online political landscape, distinguishing between authentic ministerial messaging, agency-driven PR campaigns, and rogue AI bot farms is becoming increasingly difficult.
