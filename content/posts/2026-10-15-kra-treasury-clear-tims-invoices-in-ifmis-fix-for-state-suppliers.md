The Kenya Revenue Authority and the National Treasury have cleared both legacy electronic tax registers and web-based billing platforms for state procurement settlements, lifting an administrative logjam for thousands of commercial vendors seeking payment through government accounting channels.

In a joint public circular issued Thursday, October 15, the tax agency confirmed that the Integrated Financial Management Information System (IFMIS) now validates claims submitted via either the Electronic Tax Invoice Management System (eTIMS) or the hardware-based Tax Invoice Management System (TIMS).

“The Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA), in collaboration with the National Treasury, wishes to clarify that the integration between the Electronic Tax Invoice Management System (eTIMS) and the Integrated Financial Management Information System (IFMIS) supports the validation of tax invoices generated through both the Electronic Tax Invoice Management System (eTIMS) and the Tax Invoice Management System (TIMS),” the notice stated.

### Resolving the August Directive Gap

The clarification directly addresses mounting payment delays reported by businesses supplying goods and services across national ministries, departments, and state agencies. Confusion had intensified after KRA announced in August that eTIMS was being integrated directly into IFMIS, mandating automated validation before claims could clear public accounting desks.

That earlier directive singled out eTIMS without specifying whether older physical TIMS registers—still widely utilized by small and medium-sized enterprises—retained compatibility with the new public procurement payment gateway. The silence created operational paralysis in procurement accounting units, where finance officers hesitated to commit public funds against invoices issued through older physical devices.

Thursday's notice eliminates that distinction, reassuring vendors that functional hardware units remain fully recognized by the state's central financial ledger.

### Automated Compliance at the Checkout Desk

The dual-track acceptance framework maintains the state’s broader push to seal revenue leaks and curb fictitious procurement billing. Under the integrated system, payment processing clerks cannot route disbursements in IFMIS unless the submitted invoice matches electronic records lodged in KRA’s central repository in real time.

By tying disbursement strictly to pre-validated fiscal receipts, the Treasury aims to eliminate discrepancies where suppliers delivered items on paper but bypassed statutory tax liabilities on the proceeds. Invoices generated through either TIMS or eTIMS must satisfy identical verification benchmarks before any public funds can be disbursed to vendor bank accounts.

KRA indicated that technical teams from the tax body and the National Treasury will continue assisting state accounting officers and private businesses encountering validation errors during claims submission, directing affected vendors to access support through its centralized help desk.
