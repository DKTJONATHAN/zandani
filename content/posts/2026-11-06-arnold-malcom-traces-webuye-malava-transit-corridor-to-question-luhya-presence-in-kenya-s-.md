US-based Kenyan content creator Arnold Malcom has turned his lens on regional digital economics, mapping a specific transit corridor across Kakamega County to highlight what he sees as a striking visibility gap in Kenya’s online forex trading ecosystem.

Tracing the commercial highway stretching from Webuye down toward Malava, Malcom questioned why the bustling transport stops and market centres along Western Kenya’s major transit route appear disconnected from the country's viral retail currency trading wave.

In a video commentary shared across digital platforms, Malcom singled out key locations along the route, questioning the level of exposure to online currency markets among local youth.

![Arnold Malcom // Instagram](https://i.ibb.co/DgrKzWpK/64ea901fc83f.webp)

"Kwani hakuna forex trader Mluhya? Kwani hakuna Mluhya anajua forex trading ni nini?” Malcom posed during his observation.

He pushed the argument further by detailing the specific driving route through Kakamega County, casting doubt on whether retail market trading has established any footprint along the highway belt.

"Unaniambia utoke Webuye ushike ile barabara kwenda Kakamega alafu upige choche uingie Stendi Kisa uteremke uteremke hadi Malava hakuna mtu anajua forex ni nini?" he remarked.

Malcom’s observations highlight a distinct geographic contrast in how digital financial trading is projected on Kenyan social media. Over recent years, retail foreign exchange trading has evolved beyond financial management into a high-visibility creator subculture, where prominent figures frequently anchor their digital brand in regional identities.

![Akothee Calls Out Fashion Designer For Keeping Her Dress For Two Months Before Offering A Refund](https://i.ibb.co/Wbgt5c2/75c682346395.webp)

Online figures such as Kenyan Prince and Sammy Boy have built public profiles associated with the Kisii community, while Chuka Kenyan Prince highlights his roots in Meru County. Similarly, Onyango Tate represents the Luo community in the digital trading conversation, alongside creators like Nyathira and Mordechai within Kikuyu online spaces.

Across the country, retail forex participation has expanded significantly, fueled by widespread mobile internet penetration, digital payment integration, and aggressively marketed trading applications tailored for smartphones.

This rapid growth has drawn increased scrutiny from Kenyan financial market regulators, who continue to monitor the spread of unauthorized online trading platforms and digital asset schemes targeted at urban and peri-urban youth.

While regulatory bodies focus on market oversight and investor protection, Malcom’s commentary points to a different structural question: whether the absence of prominent Western Kenya traders signals a genuine gap in digital market participation, or simply a lack of creators packaging forex trading into viral online lifestyles along the Webuye-Kakamega corridor.
