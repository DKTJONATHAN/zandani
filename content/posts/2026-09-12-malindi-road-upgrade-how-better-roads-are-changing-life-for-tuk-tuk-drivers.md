**MALINDI** - The ongoing Ksh15 billion upgrade of the Mombasa-Malindi road corridor is yielding immediate operational shifts for local transport operators, as expanded road capacity significantly reduces travel times along the busy northern coastline route.

For commercial operators like Patrick, a tuk-tuk driver with a decade of experience on the route, the completion of initial road sections has translated into increased trip frequency. Improved surface quality and wider lanes have mitigated the chronic congestion and mechanical wear previously associated with the road’s narrow, pothole-ridden layout.

"I can move passengers faster, return to the road sooner, and make more trips during the day," Patrick said, noting that the reduction in gridlock caused by stalled vehicles and bottlenecks has streamlined his daily working schedule.

The project, which forms a critical component of the multinational Bagamoyo-Tanga-Horohoro/Lunga Lunga-Malindi Road corridor, aims to bolster trade and tourism connectivity between Kenya and Tanzania. Funding for the infrastructure works is provided through a tripartite arrangement involving the Government of Kenya, the African Development Bank, and the European Union.

While the project has faced implementation challenges-most notably delays attributed to land acquisition and compensation-the focus remains on completing the Mombasa-Mtwapa, Kilifi, and Malindi sections. Planners have highlighted these improvements as essential for enhancing movement across the Mombasa northern mainland, with long-term urban designs also incorporating facilities for non-motorized users, such as pedestrians and cyclists.

Infrastructure experts observe that while smoother roads lower the operational costs linked to vehicle maintenance, income growth for individual operators remains subject to external market variables, including fuel pricing, competitive saturation, and fluctuating passenger demand.

Despite these variables, the ongoing expansion is viewed by local operators as a stabilizing factor for the transport economy. For drivers currently navigating the project's construction zones, the primary objective remains the eventual completion of the entire corridor to ensure consistent journey times and sustained commercial throughput.
