A seven-day statutory countdown is underway at the Office of the Registrar of Political Parties, triggered by a batch of official filings that expose how prominent political figures are re-engineering fringe outfits into insulated personal campaign vehicles.

The administrative revisions, published in the Kenya Gazette on October 9, 2026, cover the People’s Renaissance Movement (PM), the National Economic Development Party (NEDP), and the Kenya Moja Movement Party. Under the Political Parties Act, the notices give party members and the public until October 16 to lodge formal objections before the Registrar ratifies the altered constitutions, addresses, and governing councils.

### Familial Handover for City Hall

The most straightforward transfer of institutional control occurred within the Kenya Moja Movement Party, where former Starehe MP Bishop Margaret Wanjiru officially assumed the role of party leader, replacing her son, Steven Kariuki.

![sonko-amisi.jpg](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/sonko-amisi.jpg.webp?itok=N-xT4bMA)

Kariuki founded the outfit in August 2025. Wanjiru’s ascension to the formal helm directly aligns the party’s administrative machinery with her declared run for Nairobi governor in 2027, where she is set to mount a challenge against incumbent Johnson Sakaja. By formalizing direct ownership of the family-registered entity, Wanjiru eliminates the vulnerability of relying on coalition ticket negotiations within larger alliances.

### Vacant Helm and the NEDP Purge

By contrast, the paperwork submitted by Mike Sonko’s National Economic Development Party reflects lingering administrative friction rather than seamless succession. The party gazetted its top post—Party Leader—as vacant, alongside multiple deputy leadership slots.

Sonko assumed the public face of NEDP upon its full registration in December 2025, but the filings do not state whether his formal absence from the register is temporary or the result of a quiet step back following months of bitter internal feuds. Across 2026, NEDP’s secretariat was upended by disciplinary actions: former Secretary General Ben Mulwa was suspended and replaced by an acting official identified as Oyieyo, while Executive Director Daniel Onsomu was ousted in June and stripped of his authority as a bank signatory.

Leaving the party leader position officially vacant creates a formal opening through which Sonko could be legally installed once disputes settle—or, conversely, leaves the outfit shielded from legal challenges tied to his personal standing.

![Mike Sonko 1](https://i.ibb.co/jZ6tDfRK/f8588877851f.webp)

### Lavington Pivot After a Fractured Alliance

In the People’s Renaissance Movement, linked to Saboti MP Caleb Amisi, the adjustments combine physical relocation with a fresh roster of national administrators. PM is seeking constitutional amendments and transferring its national headquarters from River Bank Court in Kileleshwa to Riara Gardens, off Riara Road in Lavington.

The party also lodged a complete leadership slate: Antony Samba Buluma as Secretary General, Teddy Ngumbao Mwambire as National Chairperson, Jean Njeri Njoroge as Organising Secretary, and Geoffrey Shumanyula as Executive Director.

Amisi rolled out the outfit in August 2026, initially positioning it as an electoral vessel for the Linda Mwananchi movement during its own registration difficulties. That alliance has since unraveled; Linda Mwananchi organizers have moved to set up their own vehicle, the Equitable Party, leaving PM to establish its own independent footprint in Nairobi's suburban diplomatic belt.

With the gazette notice published, the formal fate of all three entities rests on whether rival factions, suspended officials, or opposing party members file legal challenges with the Registrar before the October 16 deadline lapses.
