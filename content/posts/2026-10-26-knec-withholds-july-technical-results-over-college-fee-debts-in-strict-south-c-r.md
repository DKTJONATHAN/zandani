According to directives issued by the Kenya National Examinations Council on Monday, technical and business training institutions must settle all pending examination fee balances before receiving the newly released July 2026 examination results, a condition that gives the state examiner immediate financial leverage over indebted colleges while leaving thousands of candidates reliant on their schools' accounting compliance.

The examination body announced that result slips and related documentation for the July 2026 Business and Technical series are ready for in-person collection by institutional heads at New Mithani House in South C, Nairobi. Under the terms set out in the October 26 council circular, access to the physical materials remains tied directly to an institution's financial clearance status.

### Centralised Collection and Portal Gatekeeping

Under KNEC’s operational protocol, candidate access has been completely centralised through college administrators. Individual examinees cannot obtain their results directly from the council and have instead been instructed to liaise with their respective centres to verify their academic standings.

![KNEC Headquarters](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/knec%20offices.jpg.webp?itok=t4ClOj2B)

To manage performance discrepancies, the council opened a 30-day dispute window closing on December 7, 2026. All verification requests must be routed digitally through KNEC’s Query Management Information System (QMIS) at `qmis.knec.ac.ke/externallogin`.

“Queries must be submitted through the council’s online portal at qmis.knec.ac.ke/externallogin and should reach KNEC within 30 days of the results being released,” the notice stated.

### Financial Penalty on Lapsed Appeals

The examiner attached a strict punitive mechanism to the appeals timeline. Institutions that fail to lodge discrepancy inquiries within the prescribed 30-day window will be assessed a non-refundable fine of Ksh5,000 for every late query processed.

Candidates who detect irregularities in their results are barred from petitioning the council independently. All formal challenges—whether relating to scoring, administrative details, or missing grades—must be processed and submitted solely by college principals.

![An undated image of the Kenya School of TVET](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/kenya-school-of-tvet.jpg.jpeg.webp?itok=HlAcgG8c)

“Institutions need to comply with the collection requirements and submit their queries before the December 7 deadline to avoid paying a Ksh5,000 charge for late submission,” KNEC emphasised.

### Heightened Administrative Vigilance

The strict conditions imposed on technical and vocational colleges land amidst a broader enforcement push by KNEC across national testing calendars. Just a week earlier, the agency published rigorous procedural guidelines for secondary schools administering the Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education (KCSE).

Those measures enforced mandatory security frisks outside examination rooms and ordered candidates to take their designated desks at least 15 minutes before the start of test sessions, standardising morning starts at 8:00 a.m. and afternoon papers at 2:00 p.m.

For technical and business college heads, the immediate priority shifts to New Mithani House, where the council's accounts desk will determine which centres collect their certificates unhindered and which must reconcile their ledgers before December 7.
