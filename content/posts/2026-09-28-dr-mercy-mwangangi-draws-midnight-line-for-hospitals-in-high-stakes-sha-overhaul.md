Dr. Mercy Mwangangi has taken center stage in Kenya’s health sector with a decisive midnight ultimatum to hospital managers across the country, placing public and private healthcare facilities on high alert as current provider contracts expire.

The Social Health Authority (SHA) Chief Executive Officer issued a clear public notice confirming that every existing healthcare provider agreement in the country will officially terminate on September 30, 2026, at 11:59 p.m. Dr. Mwangangi urged all facility administrators planning to continue treating scheme beneficiaries to complete their compliance documentation and sign new contractual agreements before the midnight deadline transition into October 1.

Under the chief executive's directive, hospitals operating without fully executed contracts for the incoming 2026–2029 cycle will be strictly barred from rendering medical services to scheme beneficiaries. Dr. Mwangangi warned that non-compliant institutions face immediate administrative isolation, with their credentials deactivated directly on the SHA provider portal. This credential shutdown cuts off hospital finance and administration desks from the scheme's central claims submission engine, pre-authorization channels, and routine operational systems.

To prevent patient care disruptions during the midnight rollover, the authority instructed uncontracted facilities to begin immediate coordination for active patients. Managers unable to execute new contracts in time are required to arrange the transfer of patients undergoing active inpatient and specialized treatment to accredited, fully contracted health facilities.

The deadline marks the formal commencement of a three-year contracting framework running from October 1, 2026, through June 30, 2029. The updated framework, dubbed HAKIKA, was officially introduced alongside a dedicated E-Contracting Platform on September 18. Under this restructured format, executed agreements will consolidate service delivery funded through four specialized state health financing pools: the Primary Health Care Fund (PHCF), the Social Health Insurance Fund (SHIF), the Emergency, Chronic and Critical Illness Fund (ECCIF), and the Public Officers Medical Scheme Fund (POMSF).

The rollout represents the first full-scale digital contracting test for the authority since the transition from legacy schemes. By replacing physical paperwork with the digital portal, SHA leadership aims to enforce strict compliance and standardize service terms across both private clinics and public referral hospitals before the new funding cycle begins.

Ministry of Health planners designed the E-Contracting Platform to digitize end-to-end provider onboarding, enabling automated license verifications, document uploads, and digital contract signatures. The portal was rolled out to eliminate long-standing operational bottlenecks that previously plagued health facilities, including delayed claims processing, pre-authorization friction, and unexpected payment rejections. Official ministry details indicate that the HAKIKA framework sets explicit guidelines on benefit packages, fixed reimbursement schedules, quality assurance benchmarks, and formal dispute resolution mechanisms.

However, Dr. Mwangangi’s uncompromising timeline arrives amid widespread anxiety across the medical sector. Medical associations and private facility operators have voiced strong reservations about specific legal provisions tucked into the HAKIKA framework. Facility owners are particularly uneasy about terms imposing automatic claim deductions, strict provider liability obligations, and the reliance on vague protocols when state fund disbursements encounter operational delays.

With the September 30 deadline looming, healthcare providers across the nation are trapped in a high-stakes race against the clock, balancing contractual concerns against the immediate threat of portal deactivation and forced patient transfers come midnight.
