Self-proclaimed preacher Paul Nthenge Mackenzie saw his years-long effort to disown responsibility for the Shakahola tragedy collapse in court after the High Court found him and 28 co-accused guilty on all 191 counts of murder.

In a landmark ruling delivered on Monday, October 19, 2026, Justice Diana Kavedza held that prosecutors had proven beyond reasonable doubt that Mackenzie and his inner circle were criminally responsible for the deaths of 190 children and one adult. The decision directly dismantled the primary argument deployed by the defense, which claimed throughout the trial that the victims had voluntarily undertaken prolonged fasting in pursuit of spiritual salvation.

Justice Kavedza firmly rejected the notion of voluntary participation. Instead, the court sustained prosecution evidence showing that Mackenzie and his 28 co-accused ran a tightly controlled, structured system of religious radicalisation and enforced starvation that systematically drove followers to their deaths inside Kilifi County's Shakahola Forest.

### Dismantled Defense and the Enforced Fasting Machine

During the trial, prosecutors presented extensive witness testimony to detail the precise roles played by each of the 29 accused persons. The evidence established that Mackenzie used extreme religious doctrines to lure followers into relocating to remote land in Shakahola, where the group then established systematic controls to direct and sustain the deadly fasting practices.

The court focused heavily on the culpability of the accused in directing practices that caused the deaths of 190 children. Justice Kavedza’s findings established that the young victims could not be considered voluntary participants in a deadly fast, pointing to the organized framework maintained by Mackenzie and his associates to enforce adherence to his teachings.

### From Mass Exhumations to November 6 Sentencing

The guilty verdicts mark the most significant judicial milestone since early 2023, when authorities first uncovered shallow graves hidden across the expanse of Shakahola Forest. Exhumation operations eventually yielded more than 400 bodies, exposing a devastating scale of casualty that sparked national outrage and urgent calls for oversight of extreme religious groups.

While the conviction on 191 murder counts represents a major step toward legal accountability for surviving relatives and victims' families, it does not exhaust the legal liabilities facing the former preacher. State investigators continue to maintain ongoing inquiries into other deaths connected to the wider Shakahola tragedy, meaning additional criminal cases linked to the Kilifi site remain active and separate from this murder verdict.

Mackenzie and his 28 co-accused remain in custody as they await final penal determination. Justice Kavedza ordered all 29 convicted individuals to return to court on November 6, 2026, when the High Court will formally deliver sentencing for the 191 murder convictions.
