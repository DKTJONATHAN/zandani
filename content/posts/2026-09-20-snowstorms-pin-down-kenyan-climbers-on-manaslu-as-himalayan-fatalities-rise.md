Heavy snowfall across Nepal's Gorkha District has forced high-altitude climbing teams into an indefinite standstill on Mount Manaslu, stranding international expeditions—including Kenyan mountaineers Swabra Swaleh Breik and Daniel Moenga—at intermediate camps after an avalanche pushed the seasonal death toll on the peak to four.

Having finished their high-altitude acclimatization rotations, Breik and Moenga remain held in position along the mountain's upper staging areas. Continuous blizzard conditions have blanketed the 8,163-meter (26,781-foot) peak, wiping out established routes and preventing teams from initiating final summit pushes during the Autumn 2026 climbing season.

![A collage of mountaineers Daniel Moenga (right) and Swabra Swaleh Breik (left).](https://i.ibb.co/21zfhjDk/bc5cb877f662.webp)

The severe weather hold comes directly after an avalanche struck Camp III. Indian-German climber Dhruba Jyoti Guha and Sherpa guide Fursemba Sherpa, from Makalu, departed Camp II on the evening of September 19 aiming for an early summit attempt. Sliding snow and ice buried their tent site overnight. Search teams reached Camp III on September 20 and confirmed both mountaineers dead.

That incident brought total casualties on Manaslu to four within three weeks. On September 5, 20-year-old Nepalese porter Tashi Sherpa was found dead at base camp, followed by 50-year-old Singaporean climber Lau Wee Seng, who died on September 17.

The mounting loss of life underscores the severe operational limits governing emergency responses on the world's eighth-highest mountain, whose Sanskrit name *manasa* translates to "mountain of the spirit." Heavy blizzards at extreme elevations rapidly cover climbing tracks, conceal deep crevasses, and accumulate heavy snowpacks that destabilize upper slopes.

![Mt Manaslu, Nepal.](https://i.ibb.co/QjcFBwB8/9c9bf95916f9.webp)

Emergency evacuations on the peak face technical bottlenecks. High winds and whiteout visibility frequently ground rescue helicopters above Base Camp, while sub-zero temperatures accelerate battery drain on satellite radios and handheld GPS devices, leaving stranded crews unable to broadcast steady location coordinates.

Breik and Moenga are attempting to become the second and third Kenyans to complete a successful ascent of Manaslu. The route was first climbed by a Kenyan in September 2023, when the late Joshua Cheruiyot Kirui reached the summit without supplemental oxygen or Sherpa support, despite experiencing high-altitude pulmonary edema symptoms during his early rotations. Kirui later died in May 2024 during an unassisted push on Mount Everest.

With high-altitude snowfall continuing across the Manaslu Himal range, expedition leaders and guide services are keeping teams anchored at intermediate camps, awaiting a stable barometric pressure window before authorizing any further movements toward the summit ridge.
