Kenyan Prince brought his signature high-stakes posture to the Milele FM studio, telling online critics that their opinions on his driving carry no weight unless they own vehicles of their own.

Sitting down with presenter Tony Mwirigi on Thursday, November 5, 2026, the forex trader—born Raymond Omosa—addressed the persistent commentary trailing his luxury vehicle videos. Rather than engaging with specific questions about road safety compliance, Omosa drew a sharp economic line between motorists and non-motorists.

![Kenyan Prince //Instagram](https://i.ibb.co/27F0K1sn/f78f647cc800.webp)

“Hauwezi ni advice kama hauna gari. Unatembea kama mang'ombe ya Maasai alafu unaniambia naendesha gari vibaya,” he told the presenter, dismissing the feedback with a harsh comparison.

The exchange throws light on the uneasy relationship between online luxury content creation and public scrutiny in Kenya. Omosa has built a visible digital footprint around his car collection, regularly posting footage that highlights his lifestyle. However, those same displays have invited sharp questions from social media users tracking whether his handling of high-end automobiles adheres to traffic regulations.

Public attention around his driving intensified in October 2026, when videos of motorists racing on the Nairobi Expressway placed several high-profile personalities under observation. Rather than cooling the debate, Omosa's latest radio appearance makes it clear that he views the criticism as noise from outside his tax bracket.

Whether the exchange will quell the ongoing discussions regarding luxury vehicle antics on Kenyan roads remains to be seen, as traffic authorities continue monitoring high-speed displays across major urban transport corridors.
