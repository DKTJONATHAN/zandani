JOHANNESBURG - Social media users in South Africa are grappling with a series of unsettling updates posted to the Facebook profile of Rapea Nditsheni, a man whose account continued to publish content days after he was reported to have died.

**What we know:**
* Rapea Nditsheni authored multiple posts and videos that were released automatically via Facebook’s post-scheduling feature.
* A message posted on September 2 at 9:00 AM cited ongoing struggles with mental health as the reason for his decision to end his life.
* A follow-up post at 10:00 AM on the same day clarified that the content was pre-scheduled and not sent from beyond the grave.
* The subsequent video updates featured Nditsheni inquiring about his own funeral proceedings, asking followers for details on how the ceremony unfolded.

The incident has sparked widespread discussion regarding the digital footprint left behind by individuals during times of crisis. In one of the viral video clips, Nditsheni addressed his audience directly, stating, “I was just checking in, wanted to find out how did my funeral go and stuff?” He further requested that his followers provide feedback in the comments section regarding the events of his burial.

Prior to the videos appearing, a poignant message was shared on his timeline in which he addressed his family and friends. “I'm so sorry friends and family, for leaving you all like this. If you are wondering why I did what I did to myself - I have been suffering for so long with mental issues - the day it happened I just woke up and decided I gotta go I'm exhausted,” the post read.

In response to the confusion generated by the updates, Nditsheni clarified the technical nature of the posts on his profile. He informed his followers, “And yes I'm the one who posted the post not from beyond but you can schedule posts on Facebook.”

The event has highlighted the functionality of social media scheduling tools, which allow users to compose and queue content for automatic publication at a future date and time. As of this report, the digital community continues to react to the sequence of events as they appear on the timeline.
