Rigathi Gachagua stepped onto the pulpit at AIC Bathi in Kimende on Sunday determined to play political bouncer, serving President William Ruto a direct warning to stop window-shopping across the Opposition benches.

The Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP) leader used the Kiambu church service to run through an impromptu roll call of political figures he claims to have individually vetted and locked down. According to Gachagua, State House operatives hoping to pluck influential figures from the counter-camp are chasing ghosts, as private consultations have yielded firm pledges to field a single presidential candidate against Ruto in 2027.

“Ruto, let me tell you not to wait for anybody from the Opposition. They are not available,” Gachagua declared. “Do not wait for Sifuna, he is not available.”

### The VIP Roll Call

Gachagua did not stop at Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna. He went down his contact list to name Wiper leader Kalonzo Musyoka, Jubilee Party chief Fred Matiang’i, and People’s Liberation Party leader Martha Karua as politicians who had personally assured him they would not cross the aisle.

His delivery carried the familiar theatrical cadence that has characterized his post-deputy presidency rallies, treating the political affiliations of seasoned party heads as a secured ledger.

“Martha Karua has confirmed to us, she can never join Ruto. You already know Martha, do not even wait for her,” Gachagua said. “Don’t wait for Matiang’i and me, of course you know my stand.”

The former Deputy President insisted he had handled the diplomatic legwork in private living rooms and backroom sessions, asking the tough questions himself before giving the public his seal of approval.

“I want to tell you that all those leaders I have met individually have committed to unity,” he told the congregation. “I have asked Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna if he could abandon us, he swore never. Jubilee Party leader Fred Matiang’i also ruled it out.”

Under this purported pact, the leaders have agreed to a strict code: once the coalition settles on a single flagbearer to challenge Ruto, nobody walks away or strikes a side bargain with the government. 

### Countering the 43 Percent Tag

Gachagua’s assertive posture in Kimende was not merely an offensive strike against State House; it was a targeted defense against damaging research numbers circulating back in Nairobi.

Just three days earlier, on November 12, TIFA Research and Consulting published survey findings that struck right at Gachagua’s standing within his adopted political home. The poll revealed that 43 percent of Opposition supporters identified Gachagua as the individual most responsible for internal friction and divisions in their camp.

By comparison, President Ruto and the ruling United Democratic Alliance (UDA) apparatus were blamed by only 15 percent of respondents, while Sifuna registered at seven percent.

Standing before the Kimende faithful, Gachagua waved off the statistical verdict, presenting his recent private meetings as living proof that the pollsters had misread the room.

“That recent poll that said Gachagua is the divisive leader in Opposition is misleading,” he countered. “I am the one bringing the Opposition together.”

### The Christmas Deadline

To cement his claim as the bloc’s chief unifier, Gachagua pledged to invest his own political capital and personal hours into shepherding the coalition toward consensus.

“I want to assure the people of Kenya that Gachagua will make whatever sacrifices, create time and opportunity, reach out and will play a central role in identification of the joint candidate to face Ruto,” he said.

Rather than leaving the timeline open-ended, the DCP boss handed observers a concrete benchmark, asserting that the fruits of these backroom talks will be unmistakable before the year winds down.

“Before Christmas we will start to see how things are shaping,” Gachagua insisted. “Opposition will not split under my watch. I will be working very hard to unite the Opposition.”

Whether the independent political machines behind Sifuna, Karua, Matiang'i, and Musyoka view Gachagua’s Sunday announcements as an authorized briefing or an overreach of his self-styled referee whistle remains to be tested as the December calendar advances.
