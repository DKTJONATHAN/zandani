Content creator Dennis Elnino has stepped into the ongoing political debate surrounding national coalitions, publishing a public commentary that urges a departure from rigid power-sharing pacts in favor of flexible political partnerships.

Writing under the banner of the ‘Usawa’ agenda, Elnino argued that locked-in bilateral agreements often serve narrow interests rather than broader public aspirations. According to the commentary, forcing fixed shares of government offices creates administrative bottlenecks that exclude vital voices and reduce governance to rigid spreadsheets and exclusive boardroom deals.

The intervention centers on giving President William Ruto the strategic leverage required to welcome diverse political partners, grassroots leaders, and varied communities into the national framework without being restricted by rigid quotas.

Elnino’s commentary emphasizes that true inclusion must reflect Kenya's diverse regional , explicitly listing communities from the Luo, Kamba, and Kikuyu to the Giriama, Mijikenda, Kalenjin, Luhya, and Somali. By embracing an open-door policy, the administration could weave together varied perspectives from every corner of the country, shifting away from rigid deals to allow marginalized voices and smaller communities a seat at the table.

The framework posits that open partnerships foster long-term national cohesion by diffusing political tensions and replacing competition with cooperation. Framing the ‘Usawa’ agenda around equality, fairness, and mutual respect, the commentary concludes that expanding the leadership table ensures no community is left behind as the administration navigates its broad-based governance model.
