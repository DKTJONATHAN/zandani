The state's ambitious pledge to push green-leaf payouts to at least KSh 100 per kilogram by 2027 runs against a sobering historical curve, even as the Tea Board of Kenya records a surge in national export revenues to KSh 186.9 billion.

Official performance metrics show average green-leaf payments climbed from KSh 35 per kilogram in 2021 to a peak of KSh 64 in 2024, before slipping back down to KSh 56 per kilogram in 2025. While growers across the country have acknowledged short-term financial relief from fertilizer distributions and factory upgrades, the widening gap between the 2027 policy goal and the 2025 settlement price highlights the persistent financial pressure smallholders navigate.

Behind the macro figures sit targeted state interventions rolled out since 2022. Records indicate that roughly 650,000 smallholder farmers received approximately 290,000 metric tonnes of subsidized fertilizer. Concurrently, KSh 850 million flowed into 17 smallholder factories nationwide to modernize processing machinery and reduce operational drag.

On the export front, total earnings expanded from KSh 136.5 billion in 2021 to KSh 181.6 billion in 2024, peaking at KSh 186.9 billion in 2025. Production volumes experienced their own fluctuations over the same window, moving from 537 million kilograms in 2021 to a high of 598 million kilograms in 2024, before registering 550 million kilograms in 2025 across key buying markets including Pakistan, Egypt, the United Kingdom, the UAE, China, Saudi Arabia, Germany, and Malaysia.

Fresh fiscal adjustments introduced in 2026 aim to redirect more value capture back home. The state eliminated Value Added Tax on factory-sourced tea meant for domestic value addition, zero-rated import duties on specialized packaging materials, and issued a KSh 100 million grant to Ketepa to set up a shared value-addition facility. These provisions are anchored by the newly operational Tea Registration and Licensing Regulations and Tea Levy Regulations.

Growers continue to push for stringent oversight to protect their margins against climbing operational overheads. The next test for sector regulators lies in tightening factory governance, lowering grid energy costs, and securing direct sales channels before the 2027 target deadline arrives.
