Prime Cabinet Secretary Musalia Mudavadi pulled back the curtain on how political leverage translates into national resources, challenging Western Kenya residents to face the cold arithmetic of parliamentary representation instead of relying on executive goodwill.

Speaking during a meeting with bishops in Nyang’ori, Vihiga County, Mudavadi argued that the region has undermined its own bargaining power by failing to elect a critical mass of lawmakers affiliated with President William Ruto’s ruling United Democratic Alliance (UDA).

“You don’t vote for us and then you say give us. Wacheni mchezo. That is the problem that we have in this Western region,” Mudavadi told the gathering.

### The Legislative Math on National Budgets

Framing resource allocation as a tactical numbers game settled on the floor of the National Assembly rather than in State House corridors, Mudavadi reminded residents that constitutional budget power rests with lawmakers.

“When it comes to the allocation of resources in this country, it is no longer the prerogative of the Executive. The Executive proposes, but Parliament allocates,” he explained.

Pointing to parliamentary departmental committees, Mudavadi highlighted a structural gap in the region's current legislative influence. He questioned why Western MPs hold almost no leadership positions in crucial spending committees such as Budget, Energy, and Education, leaving them with only oversight bodies.

“Let me ask our MPs from Western, which committees do we have as chairs from Western apart from that of the watchdog committee of Public Accounts?” he asked.

### Target: Budget and Energy Committee Leadership

Mudavadi set a direct political objective for the region ahead of upcoming legislative alignments, stating that regional bargaining power requires sitting at the head of executive oversight desks.

“I want us to win. Next year, I want to be sitting here with the Budget Committee from Western. I want us to have the Energy Committee chair from Western,” he said.

He accused competing political strategists of deliberately exploiting regional divisions to ensure Western lawmakers remain sidelined during committee selections.

“If we are fragmented and everyone is investing to make sure we are divided, that is what they are doing. They are spending time to make sure you are divided so that you don’t get a chance to be in any committee substantively,” Mudavadi warned.

### Grassroots Seats and Infrastructure Payoffs

Beyond the National Assembly, the Prime CS extended his directive to local government, pushing voters to return more UDA members to county assemblies to secure nomination slots and grassroots legislative power.

“Give me more MCAs through UDA. We will nominate some of you as MCAs in the county assembly. That is how it works,” he stated.

Addressing local dissatisfaction over the pace of road construction and major capital projects, Mudavadi targeted critics who questioned why the region was not seeing expected infrastructure investments. He recalled his pre-2022 push for the region to join Ruto's winning coalition.

“When I was telling you Ruto is winning, let us move together, you thought it was a joke. Now you are saying, ‘Where is the 30 per cent of roads? Why is this happening?’ Where were you when I was going there?” he asked.

Looking ahead to the 2027 General Election, Mudavadi maintained that President Ruto remains positioned for a second term, dismissing the strength of the opposition line-up. “And again, I am telling you Ruto is going to win. Tell me, who is serious in that opposition?” he asked, leaving local leaders to weigh their political independence against committee leadership posts in Parliament.
