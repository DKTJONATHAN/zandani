According to Gazette notices published on Friday, October 30, President William Ruto and Agriculture Cabinet Secretary Mutahi Kagwe have installed veteran cooperative-era administrators at the twin apexes of Kenya’s coffee regulatory and scientific bodies.

The appointments benefit smallholder coffee producers and marketing cooperatives seeking firmer state intervention, placing former Cooperative Development and Marketing Minister Njeru Peter Ndwiga at the helm of the Coffee Board of Kenya, while Henry Gichuhi Kinyua takes over the Council of the Coffee Research and Training Institute of Kenya.

Under powers invoked through Section 7 (1) (a) of the Coffee Act, President Ruto designated Ndwiga as Non-Executive Chairperson for a three-year term effective October 30, 2026. In concurrent ministerial actions, CS Kagwe gazetted Kinyua to direct research policy across the crop's state institutes.

"In exercise of the powers conferred by section 7 (1) (a) of the Coffee Act, I, William Samoei Ruto, President of the Republic of Kenya and Commander-in-Chief of the Defence Forces, appoint Njeru Peter Ndwiga to be the Non-Executive Chairperson of the Coffee Board of Kenya, for a period of three (3) years," the Gazette notice stated.

### Dual Focus on Agricultural Value Chains

![A collage of former Embu Senator Peter Nwiga and former Bura MP Ali Wario who were newly appointed by President William Ruto to different agencies on October 30, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/RTfvJX8s/cf757891c1a4.webp)

Ndwiga steps into the Coffee Board after a decades-long trajectory across agricultural policy and parliamentary oversight. He served as Cabinet Minister for Cooperative Development between 2002 and 2007 under the late President Mwai Kibaki, an era marked by initial efforts to revive distressed farmer unions and clean up milling cartels.

Later elected Embu Senator on a Jubilee ticket between 2017 and 2022, Ndwiga chaired the Senate Standing Committee on Agriculture, Livestock and Fisheries, steering legislation touching on crop cess, direct marketing, and primary society oversight. At the Coffee Board, he now inherits operational implementation responsibilities over crop earnings, licensing frameworks, and marketing systems alongside Kinyua's research council.

### Cross-Cabinet Governance Reshuffle

The agricultural announcements anchored a broader sequence of statutory appointments executed across seven ministries.

At the National Treasury, Cabinet Secretary John Mbadi restructured the Insurance Institute of Kenya Council, designating Peter Kagia to lead an incoming eight-member board for three years. Mbadi separately gazetted a five-member contingent to the Kenya National Council for Population and Development (NCPD), matching Ruto’s appointment of former Bura MP and former Assistant Minister Ali Wario as NCPD’s Non-Executive Chairperson.

In the health sector, Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale confirmed the reappointments of Dr Erneo Nyakiba and Judith Jerotich to the Board of Directors at Moi Teaching and Referral Hospital in Eldoret.

Gender, Culture and Children Services CS Hanna Wendot similarly reappointed Jimmy Nuru Ondieki as Non-Executive Chairperson and David Lebarleyia as a board member of the National Heroes Council for another three-year tenure.

### Education and Regional Council Changes

Education Cabinet Secretary Julius Migos Ogamba carried out extensive adjustments within higher education and administrative security panels. Ogamba tapped Gideon John Kamau Maina to join the Kenya Universities and Colleges Central Placement Service (KUCCPS) Board for three years, alongside five council members deployed to the Kerio Valley University College Council: Judson Obiria Nyabuto, Simon Ole Masi, Netty Chemutai, Mary Amboka, and Japheth Kibet Sugut.

Pursuant to the Basic Education Act and Gazette Notice No. 16733 of 2026, Ogamba also expanded the Multi-Stakeholder Task Team on Unrest in Schools, appointing Abdirashid Mohamed Hussein, David Muchunguh, and Prof. Joseph Githui Kabiru as additional members, with Evelyn Owoko, Catherine Matara, Dr Fred Odhiambo, and Dr Wilfred Marube joining as joint secretaries.

In regional development, EAC Affairs and ASALs CS Beatrice Askul Moe retained institutional memory by reappointing Noah Lokamar and Jeremiah Sang to the Kerio Valley Development Authority.

The incoming parastatal leaderships, gazetted with immediate effect through October 2029, face early accountability tests across pending sector audits, school safety reporting schedules, and ongoing coffee marketing structural reforms.
