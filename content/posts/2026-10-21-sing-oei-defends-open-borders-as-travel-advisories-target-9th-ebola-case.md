Foreign Affairs Principal Secretary Korir Sing’oei has positioned Kenya's port-of-entry screening architecture against blunt international border shutdowns, a diplomatic stance intended to shield the country’s commercial aviation, logistics, and hospitality sectors as foreign partners escalate travel warnings following a ninth confirmed Ebola infection.

Speaking on Wednesday, October 21, the Principal Secretary clarified that the United States government's revision of its Kenya travel advisory from Level 2 to Level 3 does not constitute a travel ban, but rather an administrative caution to American citizens that preserves entry channels between both nations.

“The United States is duty-bound to advise its citizens with respect to decisions that it deems important,” Sing’oei stated. “Level 3 is more of a caution and there are no implications with respect to barring travellers coming from Kenya associated with that advisory.”

### The Economic Risk of Blanket Closures

The diplomatic clarification follows an updated advisory issued by Washington on October 19, which cited health concerns tied to Ebola alongside existing warnings regarding violent crime, terrorism, and kidnapping. While Level 3 instructs travellers to reconsider plans, it stops short of an embargo.

![singoei](https://i.ibb.co/ZzZkc42n/0f02b906db9b.webp)

Sing’oei warned that panic-driven travel embargos and unilateral border shutdowns pose severe, collateral damage to regional economies that often exceed the epidemiological threat when health systems are already operational.

“Unless we are able to have similar standards and the same level of rigour with respect to dealing with these issues, we are all exposed,” he said, warning that disproportionate travel measures disrupt trade, tourism, cross-border schooling, and basic social services.

He argued that public health protocols must remain strictly proportionate to verified infection metrics rather than shifting into blanket prohibitions that choke commercial corridors.

### Cross-Border Screening Gaps

Kenya’s push for open borders arrives alongside an admission of regional screening vulnerabilities. Sing’oei disclosed that the patient responsible for introducing the current Ebola strain into Kenya had successfully transited through state screening systems across multiple jurisdictions before being detected.

![Police officers inspecting cars arriving at JKIA](https://i.ibb.co/gZ4frvRg/647be49112d0.webp)

That undetected movement, the Principal Secretary noted, exposes a structural weakness across Eastern and Central African transit corridors, where disparate monitoring protocols allow infectious carriers to navigate multiple checkpoints without triggering clinical alarms.

Rather than sealing frontiers, Sing’oei stated that national and regional security depends on standardising surveillance protocols and tightening real-time data exchange across ports of entry.

### Divergent Global Reactions

The diplomatic fallout has already produced divergent responses from foreign capitals. While Washington maintained open flight corridors under an advisory rubric, El Salvador enacted an outright ban on travellers originating from or transiting through Kenya.

The Central American nation extended the prohibition to eight other African states, including Uganda, Ethiopia, Rwanda, Burundi, the Democratic Republic of Congo, Angola, and the Central African Republic.

Domestic health officials maintain that Kenya's current caseload remains epidemiologically bounded. Data released by the Ministry of Health on October 20 confirmed that all nine recorded Ebola patients stem from a single, documented transmission cluster, with laboratory surveillance showing no active community spread beyond identified tracing lines.
