A presidential directive to outfit Eldoret’s Kipchoge Keino Stadium with an expansive canopy has widened the scope of Kenya’s preparation for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations (AFCON), even as unresolved contractor bills leave key national venues stalled.

Speaking from the unfinished arena during Mashujaa Day celebrations on Tuesday, President William Ruto pledged that the Eldoret facility would be completed with a full overhead covering over the coming months. The ground was visibly incomplete as it hosted the national holiday crowd, with structural works still underway across multiple seating sectors.

“I commit that this very stadium will be completed with a full canopy in the coming months,” President Ruto said, linking the infrastructure drive to Kenya's bid to stage international sporting showcases alongside Uganda and Tanzania rather than relying solely on overseas tours for local athletes.

![President William Ruto stands with other officials during the Mashujaa Day celebrations at Kipchoge Keino Stadium in Eldoret, October 20.](https://i.ibb.co/KxGtj1SM/8930b38e62e8.webp)

The additional design commitments at Eldoret arrive against a backdrop of acute cash-flow problems across the broader sports sector. The Ministry of Sports is overseeing construction and refurbishments across 33 stadiums nationally, but fiscal friction has begun to halt heavy machinery at primary sites.

Just a fortnight before the Eldoret announcement, a Senate committee review on October 6 laid bare the financial strains behind the tournament preparations. Renovation work at Nyayo National Stadium in Nairobi—designated as a vital training hub for the 2027 continental tournament—ground to a standstill after the lead contractor down-tools over an unpaid balance of Ksh984 million. Out of an approved Ksh1.184 billion contract, the state had disbursed only Ksh200 million, freezing mechanical and civil works midway.

Whether Kipchoge Keino Stadium will operate primarily as an auxiliary training ground or host tournament fixtures remains tied to an ongoing review. Technical inspectors from the Confederation of African Football (CAF) toured Eldoret in August, evaluating the complex as a prospective alternative match venue alongside regional healthcare facilities, transit nodes, and high-capacity hotel beds.

![School children form a spear shape on the field at Kipchoge Keino Stadium during a performance to mark Mashujaa Day in Eldoret, October 20, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/WNjQFM7C/37f383f21d44.webp)

Installing a full canopy significantly increases the structural and engineering work required at the site, which must pass strict continental safety and comfort standards before CAF signs off on its tournament status.

The Eldoret deadline also aligns with long-promised handovers across the athletic heartlands of the North Rift. During the Tuesday address, President Ruto stated that construction work would conclude in the coming months at Kipchoge Stadium in Kapsabet and Kamariny Stadium in Iten, both of which have experienced protracted multi-year delays under successive development cycles.

Beyond the East Africa Pamoja AFCON framework, the state is simultaneously financing the ground-up build of Raila Odinga International Stadium and executing overhauls at Moi International Sports Centre, Kasarani. That expansive dual track is intended to position Nairobi as the first African host of the World Athletics Championships in 2029.

With CAF inspection timetables closing in, the installation of Kipchoge Keino Stadium’s canopy now stands alongside Nyayo’s stalled Ksh984 million debt as a critical test of whether the state’s sports funding can keep pace with its building pledges.
