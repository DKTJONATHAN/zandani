The ruling United Democratic Alliance's top administrator is facing a formal regulatory reckoning at Anniversary Towers, after the electoral agency activated its disciplinary machinery to challenge political rhetoric surrounding the integrity of the voter register.

The Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) has ordered UDA Secretary-General Hassan Omar to appear before its Electoral Code of Conduct Enforcement Committee on October 21, 2026. 

The proceedings place the ruling party’s chief executive under scrutiny over statements that touch on one of Kenya's most sensitive electoral fault lines: whether deceased citizens remain on active polling registers.

### The Mombasa Rally Transcript

![President William Ruto interacts with his supporters during a tour of Baringo County on October 13, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/xqJkDGMk/ece827654d23.webp)

According to formal notification issued by the electoral management body, the disciplinary inquiry arises from public utterances Omar allegedly delivered during a political rally in Mombasa County on September 11, 2026.

At the coastal meeting, the former Mombasa senator reportedly addressed the contentious issue of voting by deceased individuals. While political rallies frequently trade in allegations regarding register hygiene, invoking specific claims about deceased persons casting ballots breaches the boundary between political posturing and statutory election rules.

The commission confirmed that its enforcement unit decided the statements warranted a formal explanation under the Electoral Code of Conduct, which binds registered political parties and their office bearers to refrain from remarks that undermine public confidence in the electoral system.

### A Test for the Enforcement Committee

![President William Ruto interacts with his supporters during a tour of Baringo County on October 13, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/Ys1rryY/20b25c25b15d.webp)

The summons tests the institutional independence and disciplinary teeth of the IEBC's enforcement committee, particularly when calling ruling party heavyweights to order outside an active general election window.

The Electoral Code of Conduct gives the committee statutory power to summon party officials, investigate allegations of misconduct, administer formal cautions, or levy penalties against violators. By targeting Omar directly in his capacity as party secretary-general, the commission is signalling that party secretariats will be held institutionally accountable for campaign-style statements delivered from the podium.

Voter register maintenance has historically drawn sharp legal and political battles in Kenya, with past audits by independent firms focusing heavily on purging dead voters from the biometric system to prevent proxy balloting on voting day. Public commentary that suggests dead voters still participate directly challenges the commission’s statutory mandate to maintain an accurate and cleansed roll.

Omar is expected to present himself at the commission's national headquarters at Anniversary Towers in Nairobi on October 21 to respond directly to the panel's queries regarding the Mombasa speech.
