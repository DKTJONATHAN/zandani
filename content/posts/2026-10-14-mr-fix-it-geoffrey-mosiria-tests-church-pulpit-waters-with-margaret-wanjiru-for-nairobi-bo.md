Branded as “Mr Fix It” and armed with a promise that a single term is plenty to overhaul Nairobi, County Chief Officer Geoffrey Mosiria has officially thrown his hat into the 2027 gubernatorial ring.

Mosiria’s campaign machinery shifted gears over the weekend when he took his political ambitions to the pulpit at Jesus Is Alive Ministries on Sunday, October 11, 2026. Addressing the congregation, the customer engagement and citizen service official publicly floated church founder Bishop Margaret Wanjiru as a potential running mate for his City Hall bid.

While the proposal caught the room's attention, church corridors confirmed that no formal pact had been struck between the two figures. 

The pulpit overture was quickly followed by a digital double-down on Monday, October 12, where Mosiria took to Instagram to lay out his administrative philosophy. Declaring that “the time to fix Nairobi is now,” he argued that previous county bosses squandered momentum by immediately obsessing over re-election strategies rather than cleaning up municipal systems.

Working under current Governor Johnson Sakaja, Mosiria is pitching his internal departmental stints—spanning health, environment, and citizen services—as the practical boot camp that separates him from career politicians. His core pitch relies on completing stalled development blueprints left behind by past administrations, promising an execution-heavy tenure where he does not need successive terms to deliver results.

Yet, even as he polishes his governance profile, Mosiria’s public-facing interventions involving women and children have brought him into direct friction with the digital sphere. Alongside his political roadmap, the county official fired back at online commentators whom he accused of pocketing payouts to orchestrate social media attacks against him.

“Ni uchungu sana viongozi fulani hawapendi kuona nafanya kazi ya kusaidia kina mama, warembo na watoto,” he wrote, defending his outreach work while warning political players to leave vulnerable beneficiaries out of turf wars.

As the 2027 race takes shape, Mosiria’s early maneuvering places him squarely in the expanding field of contenders eyeing Governor Sakaja’s seat, testing whether grassroots service delivery and pulpit alliances can convert into ballot box momentum.
