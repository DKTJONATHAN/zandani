The Moi family has embarked on a systematic, multi-month administrative campaign to reconstruct and regularize ownership documents for their extensive real estate holdings in Trans Nzoia. 

This coordinated effort became clear following the publication of a Gazette Notice on October 2, 2026, which initiated the formal replacement process for missing land documents registered under former Baringo Senator Gideon Towet Moi. The filing is not an isolated incident, but the latest step in a structured family push to secure provisional certificates for multiple prime properties in the region.

![a](https://i.ibb.co/kghnKtL4/53a1597302a7.webp)

According to the official notice, Gideon Moi is registered as the proprietor of a municipal parcel in Kitale, designated as L.R. No. 2116/1054, held under a grant registered as I.R. 53898/1. The Registrar of Titles confirmed that the family presented sufficient evidence to prove the original document could no longer be accounted for, prompting the launch of the statutory reconstruction process.

This administrative drive began taking shape in late July, when Raymond Kipruto Moi—Gideon’s brother and son of Kenya’s second president—initiated a similar recovery process. His petition, gazetted on July 31, similarly cited lost paperwork for his property holdings. By early August, the family had broadened its efforts, culminating in the simultaneous publication of four related notices on October 2, all addressing missing paperwork on the same set of properties.

While the Lands Registry routinely processes such applications to resolve lost documentation without implying ownership disputes or legal wrongdoing, the scale and timing of these filings point to a deliberate estate-wide cleanup. By aligning their filings under the Land Registration Act, the family is systematically closing documentation gaps that could otherwise complicate the management or transfer of these regional assets.

![Baringo Senator Gideon Moi gives an address](https://i.ibb.co/ccHw4zTb/1793905eb651.webp)

The publication of the notices triggers a strict statutory timeline. The public has been granted a 60-day window from October 2 to file any formal objections to the replacement of the documents. 

If no objections are lodged with the Lands Registry by the close of this period in early December 2026, the Registrar of Titles will officially issue provisional certificates of ownership to Gideon Moi, marking the completion of this phase of the family’s administrative regularization.
