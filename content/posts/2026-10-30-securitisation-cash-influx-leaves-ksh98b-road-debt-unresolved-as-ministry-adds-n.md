A three-year push to bypass the National Treasury with Ksh208.6 billion in asset-backed securitisation has failed to fully untangle Kenya’s road construction debt, leaving the sector saddled with Ksh98.17 billion in verified contractor arrears.

The liability figure, tallied at the close of the 2025/26 fiscal year in June, was tabled by Roads and Transport Cabinet Secretary Davis Chirchir during an oversight session with the National Assembly’s Departmental Committee on Transport and Infrastructure in Naivasha. Lawmakers had convened to review ministerial budget absorption, active project status, and the mechanics of clearing legacy commercial debt.

The disclosure highlights persistent friction in infrastructure financing: while the state has engineered sophisticated debt instruments to inject liquidity into strategic corridors, old invoices continue to tie down private builders.

### The Three-Phase Cash Injection

To keep construction plants operating without overwhelming the Consolidated Fund, the State Department for Roads structured a multi-year securitisation model designed to raise private capital against designated revenue flows.

![Transport Cabinet Secretary Davis Chirchir during the signing of the firts phase of Nairobi Transport Intelligence System on Wednesday, November 27, 2024 in Nairobi.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/transport%20cabinet%20secretary%20davis%20chirchir%20during%20the%20signing%20of%20the%20firts%20phase%20of%20nairobi%20transport%20intelligence%20system%20on%20wednesday%2C%20november%2027%2C%202024%20in%20nairobi..jpg.webp?itok=ZdBsAxaH)

Chirchir told the committee that the ministry released the Ksh208.6 billion in structured phases across 27 months:

* **2024/25 Financial Year:** Ksh61.7 billion disbursed to jumpstart delayed trunk corridors.
* **2025/26 Financial Year:** Ksh115.8 billion issued during the peak restructuring cycle.
* **2026/27 First Quarter:** Ksh31.0 billion released between July and September to sustain site operations.

Portions of that capital injection were directed into critical arterial projects that had suffered perennial delays, most notably the expansion of the James Gichuru–Rironi highway and foundational grading along the Garissa–Isiolo trade corridor. The special financing framework was explicitly crafted to isolate project sites from exchequer liquidity crunches, allowing contractors to certify works and draw down funds through commercial conduits.

Yet, even with that cash flowing into active sites, the gross pending bill inventory remained lodged just under the Ksh100 billion threshold by the end of June.

### Budget Absorption vs. Arrears

The persistence of the debt pile stands in contrast to the ministry’s operational spending performance. Chirchir reported that development budget absorption reached 99.8 per cent in the 2025/26 financial year, reflecting near-total utilization of allocated funds.

![Construction workers at the Wikililye–Katulani Road site in Kitui during a KeNHA inspection, September 25, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/kenha-wikililye-katulani-road-construction-update-september-2026.jpg.webp?itok=s1Jo4_i5)

Concurrently, the Road Maintenance Levy Fund generated Ksh127.4 billion across the same 12-month period, buoyed by statutory fuel collections. Although the levy provides dedicated cash for routine network upkeep, broad capital obligations and legacy contractual interest continue to weigh on the state's broader development accounts.

The ministry maintained that settling certified contractor claims remains an operational priority alongside daily site oversight, arguing that uninterrupted disbursements are required to prevent commercial insolvencies across tier-one and mid-level domestic construction firms.

### Expanding Commitments on the Horizon

The pending bills balance sheet lands against an aggressive rollout of new national infrastructure under President William Ruto's administration.

Just days before the Naivasha committee retreat, Deputy President Kithure Kindiki signaled that preliminary ground clearing for the Nairobi–Thika Expressway expansion is slated to commence before December. Simultaneously, the state has finalized preparatory documentation for a Ksh77.8 billion rural road program backed by the World Bank, which targets upgrades to 536 kilometres of access roads spanning seven counties.

With new project commitments advancing alongside the existing contractor backlog, the Transport Committee is expected to scrutinize how the remaining 2026/27 quarterly allocations balance fresh procurement awards against the Ksh98.17 billion waiting to be cleared from ministry ledgers.
