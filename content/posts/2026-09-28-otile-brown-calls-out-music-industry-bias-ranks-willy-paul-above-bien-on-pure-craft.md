Otile Brown has taken aim at the local music industry's tendency to weigh personal reputation over raw vocal ability, declaring that Willy Paul holds a distinct edge over Bien-Aimé Baraza when evaluated strictly on musical talent.

Speaking on the *Over 1M Podcast* hosted by Tony Mwirigi, the *Baby Love* hitmaker argued that industry gatekeepers and listeners frequently deny Willy Paul—popularly known as Pozee—his deserved credit due to public friction surrounding his personal conduct rather than his actual performance.

For Otile, the conversation surrounding Kenyan pop music needs a clear line drawn between character assessments and artistic output.

“Do you think Bien is better than Willy Paul? No, mtamnyima Pozee haki yake lakini yeye ni mdeadily,” Otile said during the sit-down, emphasizing that his appraisal was uncolored by personal relationships. “Haijalishi mi nazungumzia mziki, talanta.”

The public validation from a mainstream peer prompted a swift reaction from Willy Paul, who took to social media to lean directly into Otile’s assessment. The gospel-turned-pop singer framed the comments as rare validation in an environment he claims penalizes non-conformity while rewarding sycophancy.

Pozee drew a direct comparison to Nigerian Afrobeats star Burna Boy, arguing that global music markets routinely separate personal controversy from professional recognition.

“Somebody finally said it, ethics kitu gani? Look at Burna Boy. Anapiga hadi watu kwa stage na bado haki yake anapata,” Willy Paul wrote. “Just because I don’t kiss A*** my efforts zinaogelea tu kwa maji. But you know what? I’m not stopping now. I’ve come this far na chuki yote, but still standing strong, nilishakubali mimi ndio mmbaya wengine ni wazuri.”

The online dialogue took another turn when Bien joined the exchange directly in Willy Paul’s comment section. The former Sauti Sol vocalist appeared to acknowledge Otile’s talent assessment without resistance, prompting a brief and direct response from Willy Paul as the thread filled with fan reactions.

### A Long-Running Industry Rift

Otile’s willingness to contrast Willy Paul with Bien arrives against a backdrop of established friction between Otile and the former Sauti Sol singer.

The two prominent artists have repeatedly locked horns over the direction and international reach of Kenyan music. Their public disagreements first gained traction in 2024 after Bien countered remarks by Otile that questioned whether Kenya possessed genuine crossover international stars.

The debate escalated further in July 2025, when Bien posted a video set to his single *All My Enemies Are Suffering*, which was widely interpreted as a jab following the departure of Otile’s longtime manager, Mumbi Maina. Otile responded aggressively at the time, calling out Bien for corporate hypocrisy and using social media antics to maintain industry leverage.

That fallout quickly expanded into public spats over digital streaming metrics, commercial dominance, and the subjective criteria used to crown the leaders of modern Kenyan sound.

By elevating Willy Paul’s catalog over Bien’s on a public microphone, Otile has reopened an ongoing debate over how success and acclaim are distributed in the local scene—leaving the industry to grapple with whether artistic merit can ever truly be separated from public persona.
