President William Ruto stated on Tuesday that the national treasury will recoup the full paper value of Kenya Pipeline Company despite reducing government ownership to a 35 per cent minority stake, arguing that private capital will deliver higher market earnings than decades of unlisted state control.

Speaking at a cement factory groundbreaking in Kitui, the Head of State positioned institutional foreign buyers and local bourses as the primary vehicles for unlocking state-held balance sheets, asserting that the strategic petroleum transporter had already gained 10 per cent in trading value within seven days of Nigerian industrialist Aliko Dangote taking an equity position.

"In just one week of Dangote investing in the KPC, the share price had gone up by 10 per cent," Ruto told the gathering. "In fact, in a short time, what we owned was 100 per cent, and now we own 35 per cent. That 35 per cent may be of the same value as the 100 per cent we initially owned in just a few years."

### The Arithmetic of the Offload

The transaction, first disclosed by Dangote on October 21 during the Financial Times Africa Summit in London, transferred a 25 per cent interest in Kenya’s downstream fuel distribution spine to the Nigerian conglomerate for Ksh42.5 billion ($327 million).

![President William Ruto and Nigerian businessman Aliko Dangote talk during the refinery groundbreaking ceremony in Lamu, September 30, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/president-william-ruto-and-aliko-dangote-lamu-refinery-groundbreaking-september-2026.jpg.webp?itok=3y8iOwpL)

The equity trade moved 4.5 billion KPC shares through the Nairobi Securities Exchange at between Ksh9.02 and Ksh9.36 per share, providing the exchequer with a fast liquidity injection while reducing state dominance in the transport of refined petroleum products across East Africa.

Under Ruto’s commercial projection, the remaining 35 per cent public holding would need to nearly triple in market value to preserve the sovereign net worth previously tied to the entire corporation. The administration maintains that capital expansion under private operational oversight will broaden KPC’s profitability faster than debt-funded public management.

### Backlash Over Capital Origins

The divestiture has run directly into political resistance in Nairobi, where questions linger regarding how state-backed institutions handled KPC equity ahead of the foreign acquisition.

Kiharu Member of Parliament Ndindi Nyoro raised flags over whether public pension schemes were marshaled to accumulate KPC blocks shortly before portions of the company were reallocated to private interests. The mechanism of how local public funds moved shares prior to the block sale remains one of the transaction's primary points of friction.

![Kenya Pipeline Company (KPC) Limited](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/KPC_0.jpg.webp?itok=A-AEjlJE)

Ruto offered no direct reply to the pension allegations in Kitui, framing criticism of the deal as economic protectionism engineered by established interests seeking to freeze out external capital.

"Who told them that it is only they who can own things in this country? Every Kenyan citizen has a right to own any share they want to buy," Ruto said, characterizing critics as extortionists seeking illicit leverage over commercial allocations on the exchange.

### Lingering Corporate Clarity

While the transaction represents one of the largest single-ticket private entries into Kenya’s energy infrastructure, regulatory disclosures surrounding the deal remain incomplete.

Dangote’s address in London confirmed the purchase of "25 per cent of their pipeline network for the distribution of petroleum products" but did not name the specific corporate subsidiary or vehicle within his broader group used to execute the purchase.

With KPC shares now responsive to open-market movements and public equity funds under parliamentary review, market attention shifts to the formal transaction filings due at the Capital Markets Authority and the specific corporate governance rights retained under the state's residual 35 per cent stake.
