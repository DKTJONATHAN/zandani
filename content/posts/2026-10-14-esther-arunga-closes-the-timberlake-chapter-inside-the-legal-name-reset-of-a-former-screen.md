In 2008, Esther Adongo Arunga was the undisputed darling of Kenyan television. Armed with effortless diction and an engaging screen presence, she claimed the CHAT Award for Best Teen TV Anchor/Presenter at KTN, cementing her status as the crown jewel of local primetime news.

Eighteen years later, her name has returned to the official public record—not on a television promo, but in the dry, unsparing text of the Kenya Gazette. 

Through Gazette Notice No. 16203, the former broadcaster has formally excised the surname Timberlake from her legal persona. According to documents registered at the Registry of Documents in Nairobi, the move was formalized by a deed poll dated October 2, 2026, executed by her legal representatives, LAK Advocates.

The notice leaves no room for ambiguity. Arunga stated that she had “formally and absolutely renounced and abandoned the use of her former name Esther Adongo Timberlake and in lieu thereof assumed and adopted the name Esther Adongo Arunga, for all purposes.”

The declaration represents an absolute legal untethering from a union that captivated, bewildered, and ultimately horrified the Kenyan public across nearly two decades.

### The Golden Girl and the Preacher’s Orbit

Arunga’s exit from mainstream media remains one of the most sensational unravelings in Kenyan pop culture history. At the height of her broadcast influence, she severed ties with mainstream news networks to align herself with the controversial Finger of God Church, an outfit led by jazz musician Joseph Hellon and Quincy Timberlake.

Quincy, who presented himself as an ambitious political figure spearheading the PlaCenta Party, quickly became the central figure in Arunga’s world. The whirlwind romance turned litigious almost immediately. Quincy was arrested in Nairobi over suspected involvement in an illegal sect, though the charges were subsequently dropped after he alleged mistreatment in state custody. When Kenyan authorities rejected his visa applications, he sought asylum overseas, taking Arunga with him into self-imposed exile.

The couple eventually settled in Kallangur, a quiet suburb north of Brisbane, Australia. But far from providing a sanctuary, the relocation set the stage for an unimaginable domestic nightmare.

### The Brisbane Trial and the Unraveling

In June 2014, Australian emergency responders rushed to the couple’s Kallangur residence, where their three-year-old son, Sinclair Timberlake, had sustained fatal trauma. 

Quincy initially told paramedics and police that the boy had fallen down the stairs. The physical evidence told a vastly different story, setting off years of complex criminal proceedings in the Queensland justice system.

During the trial, the court heard chilling details of Sinclair’s final hours. Crown Prosecutor Clayton Wallace revealed that Quincy had claimed the “devil is in the house” and had beaten the toddler in a bid to “slap the devil out” of him. Arunga later admitted that she had emerged from the shower to find her husband striking the child in the stomach.

While Quincy was originally charged with murder, he later entered a guilty plea to the lesser charge of manslaughter, prompting prosecutors to withdraw the murder indictment. Supreme Court Justice Paul Freeburn sentenced Quincy to 11 years behind bars, though the court credited the more than nine years he had already served in pre-trial detention.

Arunga’s own public fall became complete when she pleaded guilty to being an accessory after the fact to manslaughter. Australian authorities established that she had deliberately lied to police investigators in the immediate aftermath of Sinclair's death to protect her husband from scrutiny.

### Reclaiming the Arunga Identity

The registered deed poll in Nairobi does not erase the devastating legal and personal wreckage of the last decade. It does, however, mark the definitive legal conclusion of an association that cost Arunga her family, her career, and her public standing.

By publishing Gazette Notice No. 16203, the former news anchor officially discards the name that came to define her darkest years, ensuring that on paper and in law, she is once again solely Esther Adongo Arunga.
