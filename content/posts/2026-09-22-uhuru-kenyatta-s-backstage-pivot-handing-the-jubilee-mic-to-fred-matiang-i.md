Uhuru Kenyatta is not leaving the political theater; he is simply changing his seat to the VIP director's booth.

On Tuesday, September 22, 2026, the former President pulled off a calculated rebranding of his political twilight. Instead of fighting a losing battle against legal timelines, Kenyatta officially handed the front-facing leadership of the Jubilee Party to his longtime cabinet enforcer and former Interior Cabinet Secretary, Fred Matiang’i. 

The handoff, announced during a high-stakes gathering of party officials in Nairobi, solves a nagging legal headache for Kenyatta while keeping him firmly in the script as a "senior advisory" force. 

“While we wait for NDC, I have decided to let my deputy take over the party leadership,” Kenyatta told the gathered officials, making it clear that his exit from the marquee role is not a departure from the party itself. “I am still in Jubilee. I will be a senior member of the party and will be offering advisory services.”

By stepping back into an advisory role, Kenyatta is effectively executing a tactical retreat to bypass a tight judicial chokehold. Just over a week earlier, on September 14, the High Court delivered a definitive blow to retired heads of state holding active political offices, ruling that they must relinquish party leadership within six months of leaving the presidency.

Rather than challenging the court's decree directly, Kenyatta’s office framed the timing of the transition as a delay caused by messy internal party disputes that required legal resolution, rather than any reluctance on his part to hand over the keys.

For Fred Matiang’i, the transition represents a massive promotion from the shadows of deputy leadership, where he had been co-managing the party alongside Secretary General Jeremiah Kioni. Known during his Cabinet days as a no-nonsense administrator who rarely shied away from high-stakes confrontations, Matiang’i accepted the baton with familiar composure. He pledged to maintain the party’s momentum ahead of the upcoming National Delegates Convention (NDC), where the transition is expected to be formally ratified.

During the Nairobi session, Kenyatta urged the party's rank and file to drop the internal rivalries that have fractured the outfit since his term ended. With Matiang’i now holding the gavel and Kenyatta pulling strings from the advisory suite, Jubilee is betting that this corporate-style division of labor will keep them relevant as they attempt to reorganize their structures ahead of the 2027 General Election.
