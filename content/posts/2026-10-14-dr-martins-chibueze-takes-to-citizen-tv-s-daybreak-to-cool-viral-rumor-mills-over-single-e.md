Dr. Martins Chibueze stepped onto the set of Citizen TV’s *Daybreak* on Wednesday morning with a single, clear objective: pull the plug on runaway online panic before social media gossip could outpace public health reality.

Addressing viewers across the country on October 14, 2026, the World Health Organization Kenya Head of Health Emergencies directly tackled circulating anxieties following recent headlines of a confirmed infection. Chibueze stated unequivocally that the country is not enduring an active domestic outbreak, countering whispers that have gained traction across digital platforms over the past week.

“There’s no evidence of an ongoing Ebola outbreak in Kenya following the confirmation of one imported case,” Dr. Chibueze stated during the live broadcast, emphasizing that active field surveillance and contact tracing protocols are functioning at full capacity to block any secondary spread.

### The Broadcast Reality Check

The medical chief used his morning airtime to lay out the laboratory data behind the state’s reassurance. According to Chibueze, individuals closely linked to the single index patient have remained under intense, daily monitoring by specialized surveillance teams.

Crucially, all suspected contacts who subsequently presented symptoms and underwent diagnostic testing have returned negative results. The containment perimeter, he stressed on television, has successfully held firm.

Chibueze noted that active field teams are systematically tracking the movements and contacts of the original patient. The aggressive follow-up protocol is designed to eliminate blind spots before any localized chain of transmission can take root.

### The October 6 Origin

The anxiety Dr. Chibueze addressed on air stems from events documented eight days earlier. On October 6, public health authorities confirmed an imported infection involving the Bundibugyo strain of the virus in an individual who had traveled into Kenya from the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

Following that positive laboratory identification, public health officials moved quickly to track down primary contacts. Dozens of individuals were placed into isolation protocols and subjected to laboratory screening to determine whether the pathogen had managed to jump beyond the traveler.

The negative findings across those secondary tests formed the core of Chibueze’s reassurance on Wednesday, demonstrating that the initial detection has remained an isolated, imported occurrence rather than the flashpoint of a wider domestic wave.

### Curbing Timeline Speculation

Beyond sharing laboratory data, health authorities and the WHO are actively contending with the speed at which unverified medical claims spread across Kenyan social feeds. Speculative commentary, alarmist posts, and unverified neighborhood sightings tend to proliferate rapidly after any high-consequence pathogen makes the news.

Officials are repeatedly cautioning the public against feeding the digital rumor mill. Instead, citizens are being urged to cross-check claims against formal notices issued jointly by the Ministry of Health and the WHO, while promptly reporting any suspected symptoms to established healthcare facilities rather than broadcasting unconfirmed claims online.

To keep that containment barrier robust, the WHO is coordinating directly with the Ministry of Health to reinforce rapid-response teams on the ground. Teams are maintaining tight screening procedures across designated border posts and entry points while keeping open lines for rapid public health alerts.

For Dr. Chibueze and the emergency response desk, the televised appearance served to anchor the public discourse in verified clinical facts: dozens of monitored contacts remain non-infectious, secondary tests have yielded entirely negative returns, and the single confirmed Bundibugyo case from October 6 has not translated into active domestic transmission.
