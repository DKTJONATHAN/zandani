With just ten days remaining before the scheduled groundbreaking of Kenya’s Ksh2.2 trillion ($17 billion) Lamu oil refinery, President William Ruto has taken the high-stakes project directly to New York’s financial corridors, seeking to secure anchor backing from Nigerian industrialist Aliko Dangote and regional development financiers.

The President departed Nairobi on Saturday, September 19, for the 81st United Nations General Assembly (UNGA), where he is framing the ambitious 700,000-barrel-a-day processing facility as East Africa’s central energy security asset.

Key to the Manhattan diplomatic push is a series of targeted investment roundtables co-chaired by Ruto and Dangote. Hosted jointly by the Africa Finance Corporation (AFC) and the Global Africa Business Initiative (GABI), the sessions aim to assemble institutional capital, private equity, and international lenders around the coastal megaproject ahead of its planned September 30, 2026 construction launch.

State House Spokesperson Hussein Mohamed confirmed that discussions with Dangote and prospective international partners will focus on capitalizing the East Africa Refinery in Lamu, which government projections calculate could generate over 60,000 direct and indirect jobs while reducing regional reliance on imported refined petroleum.

![RUTO AND DANGOTE](https://i.ibb.co/W4Y32k5F/4e80622cd424.webp)

### Industrial Scale and Financing Pressure

The proposed Lamu facility represents one of the largest single greenfield industrial investments attempted in East Africa. Engineered for a daily throughput of 700,000 barrels, the refinery is positioned to transform Kenya into a refined fuel exporter servicing inland markets across the East African Community.

Involving Dangote introduces significant operational precedent. His conglomerate recently commissioned a 650,000-barrel-per-day refinery complex in Lekki, Nigeria, establishing a blueprint for mega-scale, private-led refining on the continent. By bringing the AFC into the co-hosting structure, Nairobi is seeking to establish a syndicated development-finance framework capable of absorbing the $17 billion price tag.

The swift turnaround—rallying investor commitments on the sidelines of UNGA days before breaking ground on the Kenyan coast—underscores the intense effort required to move the project from state announcements to operational civil works.

### Dual Pitch: Energy Security and Digital Infrastructure

While energy infrastructure anchors the physical capital agenda, the Kenyan delegation has structured its UNGA itinerary around a parallel bid for tech finance. Ruto is scheduled to co-lead the AI Middle Powers Initiative alongside Finnish President Alexander Stubb, a platform designed to assist mid-tier economies in pooling computing power, data networks, and engineering talent.

State House indicated that Kenya will also promote the African Development Bank-supported AI 10 Billion Initiative, an effort aimed at mobilizing $10 billion in technology investments across Africa by 2035.

Before concluding the trip, Kenya is expected to sign a multi-nation roadmap on child online AI safety together with governments including Spain, the United Kingdom, Canada, and Australia.

However, the immediate metric of success for the New York delegation rests on whether the AFC and Dangote roundtables produce firm financing structures before the September 30 target date in Lamu.
