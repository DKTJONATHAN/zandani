The Political Parties Disputes Tribunal has nullified Moitalel Ole Kenta’s installation as Jubilee Party Secretary-General, handing an immediate legal victory to internal reform petitioners led by former Nyeri Town MP Ngunjiri Wambugu while leaving party leader-allied executives facing an administrative vacuum ahead of the 2027 General Elections.

### What we know
* The Political Parties Disputes Tribunal (PPDT) ruled that Moitalel Ole Kenta was not a registered member of the Jubilee Party at the time of his appointment as Secretary-General.
* The tribunal faulted the Office of the Registrar of Political Parties (ORPP) for failing to exercise proper regulatory oversight before ratifying the changes in May.
* Former Nyeri Town MP Ngunjiri Wambugu lodged the challenge, arguing the leadership slate was gazetted without procedural exhaustion of his formal objections.
* Kenta was designated to the post in January to replace former Ndaragwa MP Jeremiah Kioni, who had been shifted to Second Deputy Party Leader.

Former Nyeri Town MP Ngunjiri Wambugu confirmed the bench’s verdict, stating that the tribunal upheld basic compliance protocols over administrative convenience: “Political Parties Disputes Tribunal (PPDT) nullifies appointment of Hon Koitalel Ole Kenta as Jubilee Party Secretary General for not being registered party member. Faults Office of Registrar of Political Parties (ORPP) for failure to play oversight role on this.”

The revoked changes originated from a January restructuring drive intended to reboot Jubilee’s grassroots apparatus. In that reshuffle, the party elevated Kenta to oversee party administration, reassigned Kioni, and lined up a front-bench roster featuring National Chairperson Vincent Kemosi, Deputy Party Leader Fred Matiang’i, Deputy Secretary-General Zack Kinuthia, and National Organising Secretary Yasir Noor. When the ORPP formally approved the changes in May, Wambugu petitioned the tribunal under certificate of urgency, asserting he was summoned to internal consultations but frozen out before his counter-arguments could be determined.

The ruling bolsters rival factional players seeking to wrest back administrative control of the former ruling party, leaving Jubilee's executive council obligated to restart the recruitment process or revert to previous secretariat structures. With the outfit still contending with recent grassroots exits to the Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP)-moves that Matiang’i earlier dismissed as standard housecleaning of previously suspended elements-the nullification complicates Jubilee’s consolidation schedule just as coalition dynamics accelerate across the country.
