Pan-African investment firm Zaria Group and US-based Helios Sports & Entertainment Group are scaling up their regional sports infrastructure footprint, planning a $294.5 million (Ksh38.2 billion) indoor arena in Nairobi. The project represents a major financial escalation for the developers, costing more than ten times their initial mixed-use development in Kigali.

The partners have signed a long-term lease agreement with Kenya Railways for land in central Nairobi to build the 10,000-seater multipurpose facility. It will form the anchor of a wider sports and entertainment hub within the Nairobi Railway City urban renewal plan, which is designed to revitalize the area surrounding the capital's Central Railway Station.

This expansion follows the group's initial entry into the East African market through Zaria Court Kigali, a $26 million (Ksh3.37 billion) mixed-use development in Rwanda. The partners are also involved in the management and commercialization of Kigali's 10,000-seat BK Arena and Amahoro Stadium. The Nairobi project represents a significant step up in capital deployment as the developers build a broader portfolio of sports and entertainment facilities in major African cities, with Lagos and Johannesburg targeted for future projects.

According to regulatory filings cited by Bloomberg, the proposed Nairobi arena will host basketball tournaments, international music tours, cultural programs, and Meetings, Incentives, Conferences, and Exhibitions (MICE) events. Masai Ujiri, co-founder of Zaria Group and President of the Dallas Mavericks NBA basketball team, stated that the development is designed to provide a platform for athletes, artists, and other players in the creative economy.

The developers project that the wider entertainment district will have a substantial employment footprint. The construction phase is expected to create approximately 3,500 direct jobs, followed by 1,500 permanent positions once the facility becomes operational. , events hosted at the arena are projected to generate up to 25,000 temporary jobs annually across the hospitality, retail, and live production sectors.

The private venture is expected to complement other major sports infrastructure developments in the capital. This includes the state-backed Talanta Sports City, as Nairobi positions itself to attract a larger share of international sporting and entertainment events.
