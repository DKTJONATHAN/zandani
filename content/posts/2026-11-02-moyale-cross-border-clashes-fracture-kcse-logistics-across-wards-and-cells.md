The administration of the Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education entered an unprecedented logistical emergency along the northern frontier on Monday, as cross-border gunfire forced examination officials to split test delivery across surgical wards, police cells, and emergency relocation hubs in Moyale.

With national candidates entering the decisive window for core compulsory subjects, stray bullets originating from active armed clashes on the Ethiopian side of the border penetrated classrooms in Marsabit County, leaving seven candidates hospitalised with injuries and scattering standard invigilation machinery.

Education Cabinet Secretary Julius Ogamba confirmed that a total of 12 registered candidates in the frontier town had their standard testing conditions severed in a single day. Beyond the seven students receiving medical treatment for bullet wounds, another five candidates were placed in correctional facilities following security interventions during the Monday flare-up.

### Gunfire in the Classroom

The cross-border spillover struck two key institutions in the border town: Sessi Mixed Secondary School and St Mary’s Mixed Secondary School. In both compounds, rounds fired during clan confrontations across the international boundary hit school infrastructure, immediately halting ongoing academic routines and jeopardising the national examination schedule.

![Education CS Migos Ogamba speaking to the press after overseeing the distribution of 2026 KJSEA exam papers to candidates at Kiambani Comprehensive School in Makueni on October 26,](https://i.ibb.co/Vc0n5mBL/9487fb9e200e.webp)

To keep the affected cohort within the national testing cycle, the Ministry of Education rolled out emergency contingency protocols rather than cancelling papers. Invigilators and security personnel were dispatched directly to local medical facilities to supervise hospitalised students, while a separate secure chain of custody was established to deliver question papers to candidates behind bars.

"Special arrangements have been made to deliver and administer examinations to seven hospitalised candidates and five candidates in correctional facilities," Ogamba confirmed in a ministerial briefing issued hours after the incident.

### Shifting Frontiers Under Exam Fire

The security risk has also forced the Ministry to abandon at least two established examination centres in Moyale, relocating candidates to safer inland facilities to prevent further exposure to cross-border trajectories. 

Relocating exam centres during active testing introduces complex administrative vulnerabilities for the Kenya National Examinations Council. National examination papers are distributed under strict dual-custody protocols between council officials and sub-county security teams, tied strictly to geo-mapped routes and secure container depots opened at dawn. 

![Secondary School students sitting for their Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education (KCSE) examinations.](https://i.ibb.co/HLPS8r5T/3b1fe60f4930.webp)

Transferring entire student bodies to alternative hosting premises—while simultaneously dispatching separate security details to guard individual beds in hospital wards and cells in holding facilities—stretches local administrative capacity at the most demanding juncture of the academic calendar.

### Crucial Exam Stage Under Border Threat

The timing of the border disruption compounds the pressure. Candidates nationwide are moving into high-volume compulsory papers, where student turnouts are universal and subject scheduling leaves minimal room for administrative delay. Any localized failure to administer a paper on the uniform national timetable creates severe integrity and comparability challenges for the council.

Ogamba condemned the insecurity triggered by clan factions operating along the Kenya-Ethiopia corridor, maintaining that the government would enforce security sweeps along the perimeter to safeguard remaining test sessions.

To counter potential integrity breaches or administrative confusion created by the sudden transfers, KNEC has placed border testing points on heightened monitoring, directing field managers and local communities to flag any irregularities through toll-free reporting hotlines 0800721410 and 0800724900.

With military and police units reinforcing the border perimeter around Moyale, education authorities face the delicate balance of sustaining a rigid national testing timetable while operating inside an active international conflict corridor.
