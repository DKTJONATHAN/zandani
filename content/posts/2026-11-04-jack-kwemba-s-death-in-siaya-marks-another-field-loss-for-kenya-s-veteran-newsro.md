The death of veteran sports journalist Jack Kwemba in a rural Siaya road accident brings to an end a print career that spanned four of Kenya's major newspaper newsrooms over more than two decades.

Kwemba died on Tuesday, November 3, following a collision involving a motorcycle and a cow along a road in Siaya County. His passing deprives the country’s sports media ecosystem of a long-serving figure whose reporting helped shape domestic football coverage across several generations of sports publishing.

Throughout his career, Kwemba established himself as a mainstay in print sports journalism. He served on the sports desks of *The Standard*, the former state-aligned *Kenya Times*, and *People Daily*, before most recently working with *The Independent*.

### A Footprint Across Legacy Print Desks

![Veteran sports Journalist, the late Jack Kwemba.](https://i.ibb.co/YB057TgR/edb964fde63e.webp)

Kwemba’s trajectory reflected a distinct era of Kenyan sports writing characterized by press-box reporting, detailed local league analysis, and active newsroom mentorship.

Radio Citizen sports presenter Torome Tirike highlighted Kwemba's key role in guiding younger reporters through the demands of print sports desks as newsroom environments evolved.

"The media fraternity is mourning the demise of Jack Kwemba—a veteran Kenyan journalist, sports writer, and media executive," Tirike said, citing his passing as a significant institutional loss to the profession.

Football followers and press colleagues similarly noted Kwemba's deep institutional memory regarding domestic club football, national team selections, and sports administration across his tenures at different national dailies.

### Field Hazards Facing Regional Journalists

Kwemba’s fatal crash in Siaya underscores the persistent risks facing media personnel working outside primary metropolitan bureaus in Nairobi. Road transport hazards, particularly involving motorcycles and untethered livestock on rural feeder roads, remain a constant operational threat for field reporters.

![Undated image of an inside of a radio studio](https://i.ibb.co/8LXfStHj/42027ebb8758.webp)

His death marks the second loss of a seasoned regional correspondent within a single week.

On Friday, October 30, veteran Nation Media Group correspondent Kitavi Mutua died while undergoing treatment at Kenyatta University Teaching, Referral and Research Hospital (KUTRRH) in Nairobi.

Mutua suffered a venomous red spitting cobra bite at his home in Kitui South. After initial medical treatment failed to stabilize him, he was transferred to KUTRRH, where he spent two weeks in an intensive care unit before succumbing to organ failure and cardiac arrest.

Both losses highlight the exposure and physical vulnerabilities faced by veteran journalists operating away from centralized desk setups.

Colleagues and family members are coordinating memorial arrangements in Siaya and Kitui, with formal funeral announcements expected in the coming days.
