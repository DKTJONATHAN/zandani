According to the Kenya Urban Roads Authority (KURA), non-motorised transport corridors and automated traffic management systems designed to safeguard pedestrians and ease transit at the Parklands Road–Wangari Maathai Road junction were brought to an unannounced standstill by jurisdictional delays in relocating underground utility services with the Kenya National Highways Authority (KeNHA).

The state agency affirmed over the weekend that inter-agency standoffs surrounding sub-surface service transfers have been formally concluded, enabling the designated contractor to redeploy plant machinery and expedite construction across the arterial intersection in Westlands.

### Bureaucratic Friction Halts Civil Works

Public queries over the neglected state of the busy Westlands node intensified after excavation crews carved into walkways and roadside verges months ago before abruptly halting operations. The stalled zone left pedestrians navigating active vehicular traffic alongside unsecured open ditches and mounds of earth.

KURA confirmed the prolonged suspension stemmed from complex service realignment processes required across shared road corridors, specifically involving KeNHA infrastructure.

![Screengrab of piles of soil and open trenches at the Parklands Road- Wangari Maathair Road Junction on October 17, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/9H6qttn4/3d8854f3011f.webp)

"Please note, KURA is improving this junction with NMT, drainage & ITS works for pedestrians & motorists. Works stalled over service relocation by other agencies; now resolved & the contractor is expediting," the authority said in an official statement detailing the technical deadlock.

The agency maintains that the engineering design for the junction incorporates three major components: dedicated non-motorised transport (NMT) paths to isolate foot traffic from fast-moving vehicles, modernised stormwater drainage outfalls to prevent perennial pooling, and intelligent transport system (ITS) infrastructure meant to integrate with Nairobi's automated signal management grid.

### Footpath Hazard and Commercial Speculation

The prolonged dormancy of the construction zone triggered sharp civic criticism over pedestrian safety and the management of municipal contracts. Former Law Society of Kenya (LSK) president Nelson Havi publicly challenged the agency over deep trenches cut along the pedestrian path and existing stormwater channels, questioning why the excavation appeared abandoned for several months.

Havi argued that the roadside alterations deviated from standard road maintenance, alleging the works were executed to carve out a commercial access point for an adjacent private fast-food restaurant rather than serving public transport utility.

Site assessments revealed loose gravel, deep unshielded trenches, and soil heaps flanking the roadway edge, forcing commuters boarding public service vehicles to walk directly inside traffic lanes during peak hours. The incomplete civil works heightened risks along a section heavily relied upon by thousands of pedestrians transitioning between Parklands and commercial offices in Westlands.

![A section of Outer Ring Road at the Donholm flyover in Nairobi](https://i.ibb.co/tTBtMRVg/52117178a4a6.webp)

KURA rejected accusations of commercial favoritism, asserting that the realignment serves exclusively to expand traffic capacity and pedestrian connectivity at the junction. The authority stated the roadside excavations form part of the scheduled civil foundation works required for the broader NMT and drainage footprint.

### Pressure on Capital Corridor Deadlines

The delay at the Parklands junction highlights recurring coordination challenges among national transport agencies operating within the capital, where utility lines, telecommunication conduits, and overlapping agency mandates regularly interrupt civil contracts mid-stream.

While KURA works to clear the construction backlog in Westlands, the agency is juggling major capital bottleneck projects across Nairobi's major arteries. The authority completed civil construction on the Ngong Road–Naivasha Road flyover to decouple freight and commuter movement in the western suburbs.

Concurrently, engineers are pushing to deliver the Kenyatta Avenue viaduct in the central business district, where statutory completion benchmarks target commissioning by May 2027.

Contractors at the Parklands Road–Wangari Maathai Road junction face immediate pressure to backfill open excavations, rebuild damaged pedestrian walkways, and clear arterial drainage networks before the onset of heavy seasonal rains tests the exposed earthworks.
