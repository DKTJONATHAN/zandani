Deputy President Kithure Kindiki stepped onto the podium at the State of the Judiciary report launch in Nairobi on Friday with a milestone for the bench: formal title deeds securing the Supreme Court’s physical grounds. But as soon as the ceremonial transfers were wrapped up, Kindiki turned the spotlight straight back onto the courts, challenging judicial officers to aggressively clear more than 341,000 unresolved cases sitting in trial registries.

The Friday launch of the State of the Judiciary and Administration of Justice Annual Report for the 2025/2026 financial year marked the resolution of long-standing land ownership uncertainty for the nation’s highest court. Lands Cabinet Secretary Alice Wahome officially delivered two title deeds covering the Supreme Court premises and its parking area in Nairobi, alongside a third title deed dedicated to a proposed court in Kapsabet town. For an institution that had long operated without registered ownership of its apex building, the transfer represented a significant institutional milestone.

The physical land security was paired with a substantial boost from the National Treasury. Kindiki revealed that President William Ruto’s administration has raised the Judiciary’s budgetary allocation for the 2026/2027 financial year to Sh30.4 billion, up from Sh27.1 billion in the preceding cycle. The financial boost follows the enactment of the Judges’ Retirement Benefits Act, 2026, which Ruto signed into law in March to bolster judicial security of tenure and welfare.

Beyond statutory allocations, the Executive highlighted major operational updates aimed at modernizing courtroom infrastructure. In June, Cabinet gave the green light to Phase II of the Judicial Performance Improvement Project—a Sh26 billion initiative supported by the World Bank. The program focuses on modernizing Supreme Court and Court of Appeal facilities, consolidating tribunal infrastructure, and establishing a permanent campus for the Kenya Judiciary Academy. To address manpower deficits across trial stations, the government has also backed the appointment of 53 additional judges.

Those operational upgrades helped drive the Judiciary to a 103 percent Case Clearance Rate for the second consecutive year, with courts concluding 576,702 cases against 561,580 newly filed matters over the reporting period. Yet Kindiki underscored that strong annual clearance percentages have not yet dismantled the deep historical pileup of unresolved disputes.

Data presented at the launch indicates that 190,779 cases across the country have remained unresolved for over a year. More critically, 37,757 matters have lingered in the justice system for more than five years without a final outcome, contributing to a broader pool of over 341,000 legacy cases.

Addressing assembled judicial officers, Kindiki urged the bench to accelerate timelines on long-standing matters, noting that speedy resolution in commercial and civil disputes is directly linked to legal certainty, enterprise, and broader economic development. With the Supreme Court’s land titles officially secured and Sh30.4 billion set aside for the coming financial year, the burden now returns to court halls nationwide to turn resource gains into cleared dockets.
