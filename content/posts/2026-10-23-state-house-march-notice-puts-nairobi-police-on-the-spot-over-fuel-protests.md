By formally designating State House as the destination for Monday’s fuel price demonstration, civic organisers have shifted the battle over rising pump costs directly toward the presidency, handing police commanders an acute operational test along an assembly corridor already marked by enforcement abuses.

The Coalition of Human Rights Defenders delivered its official notification to National Police Service Headquarters on Thursday, October 22, detailing plans to gather between 500 and 900 marchers on Monday, October 26. Under the itinerary submitted to law enforcement, demonstrators will assemble along Kimathi Street in Nairobi’s central business district before proceeding uphill toward State House.

The choice of assembly point immediately revives scrutiny of police conduct in the capital. Barely 48 hours before the coalition lodged its paperwork, plainclothes officers on the same street violently disrupted an earlier gathering against fuel costs on Tuesday, October 20. During that operation, officers seized Citizen TV journalist Odee Francis near the Kenyatta Avenue junction, dragging him along the pavement before bundling him into an unmarked police Land Cruiser. While authorities announced an inquiry into the incident following public outrage over media harassment, the episode established a tense backdrop for any subsequent assembly on the corridor.

![odee-francis-arrested.jpg](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/odee-francis-arrested.jpg.webp?itok=zB1JGD9F)

In their statutory notification, organisers sought to preempt operational justifications for a clampdown, pledging that the procession would adhere to strict public-order standards.

“We undertake to promote peaceful and orderly participation and to respect the rights of the public throughout the demonstration,” the coalition stated in its letter received at police headquarters. “We submit this notification for your attention and necessary coordination in accordance with the law.”

The march comes as street-level agitation diverges sharply from corporate transport lobbies over the Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority’s (EPRA) October–November pricing cycle. The latest tariff adjustments lifted Nairobi retail rates to Ksh226.96 per litre for super petrol, Ksh227.76 for diesel, and Ksh219.87 for kerosene.

![Small-scale traders protesting in Nairobi CBD on Friday, August 28, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/traders-protesting-1.jpg.webp?itok=uVWFQhdq)

While the figures triggered immediate outcry from motorists, digital taxi operators, and informal sector workers whose margins depend directly on mobility, commercial fleet operators opted against confrontation. Matatu Owners Association president Albert Karakacha dismissed calls for an industry-wide strike, confirming that passenger service vehicles would remain on the road and absorb the higher diesel bills by transferring the financial burden onto daily commuters.

That corporate withdrawal has left civil society networks and commuter welfare groups to lead the pushback alone. Rather than staging service withdrawals, the Counties Commuters Welfare Association (COCOWA) opened a parallel legal front on October 19, issuing a 14-day statutory ultimatum demanding that EPRA rescind the maximum pump caps or prepare to defend the pricing formula in court.

With COCOWA’s legal countdown expiring in early November and matatu operators passing costs down the line, Monday’s planned march represents the first coordinated effort to force high fuel prices past regulatory boardrooms and straight to the gates of executive authority.

Police headquarters has not publicly indicated whether officers will facilitate the procession along Kenyatta Avenue and State House Road or attempt to seal off Kimathi Street before the crowd can form.
