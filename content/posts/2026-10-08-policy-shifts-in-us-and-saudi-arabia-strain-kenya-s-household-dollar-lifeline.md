Tightened immigration enforcement in Washington and shifting employment regulations in Riyadh have directly hit the primary channels powering Kenya’s diaspora cash flows, leaving local families with reduced monthly foreign transfers.

Data released by the Central Bank of Kenya (CBK) on Thursday, October 8, 2026, reveals that diaspora remittances contracted by 1.3 percent over the 12 months ending August 2026. The slowdown directly reflects earnings disruption across the country’s two most critical foreign labor destinations: the United States and Saudi Arabia.

Addressing a briefing following the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) meeting, CBK Governor Kamau Thugge noted that while overall inflows faced a sustained mid-year dip, a minor rebound in July and August helped limit the year-to-date decline.

"The remittances have remained resilient, although from the twelve months to August 2026 we saw a decline of 1.3 per cent," Thugge said. "In the last two months we have seen some recovery, and between January and August the decline was -0.7."

### Labor Corridors Under Pressure

![A photo depicting a person holding Kenya Shilling notes and US Dollar notes](https://i.ibb.co/fGLsyRGV/8050b3c3d09d.webp)

The contraction breaks consecutive years of aggressive growth in diaspora inflows, exposing how deeply domestic liquidity depends on policy environments in key partner states.

The United States remains Kenya’s largest source of foreign cash transfers, generating approximately 43.5 percent of all money sent home by citizens living abroad. However, inflows from North America faced headwind pressures linked to intensified immigration enforcement and stepped-up deportations since President Donald Trump took office in January 2025. The removal and employment disruption of undocumented workers directly shrank the pool of active wage earners sending dollars home to dependents.

Concurrently, money sent from Saudi Arabia—Kenya's second-largest labor market for migrant workers—slowed following structural adjustments to host-country labor regulations. Revised employment policies and stricter compliance frameworks governing foreign contracts altered migrant worker turnover and earnings transfers across the Gulf region.

### Reserve Buffers Cushion Market Friction

Despite the strain on family budgets reliant on monthly stipends, the broader foreign exchange market has avoided systemic volatility. The central bank maintained that diversified earnings channels and substantial reserve holdings successfully insulated the Kenya shilling against dollar shortfalls.

![cbk-kamau-thugge.jpg](https://i.ibb.co/kgMS2cg8/3c895f915189.webp)

Thugge emphasized that foreign currency reserves held by the central bank provided an adequate cushion to absorb short-term supply fluctuations, preventing sharp currency depreciations.

"Despite elevated global uncertainties, the Kenyan shilling has remained stable, supported by diversified foreign exchange inflows, confidence in the economy and adequate foreign exchange reserves," Thugge stated.

### Recovery Projections

Official monetary estimates project the remittance decline to remain temporary. The central bank anticipates total remittances to grow from USD 5.037 billion (Ksh 653.8 billion) recorded in 2025 to USD 5.073 billion (Ksh 658.5 billion) by the end of 2026.

Medium-term models forecast further expansion to USD 5.22 billion (Ksh 677.4 billion) in 2027, driven by expected stabilization in global labor placement networks.

Whether that growth materializes will depend heavily on how quickly Kenyan labor export channels adjust to legal requirements in the Gulf and whether remittance corridors can adapt to persistent immigration scrutiny in the United States.
