National and county disaster management teams have escalated preparedness measures across high-risk counties, directing residents in flood-prone zones—including selected Nairobi estates—to relocate to safer ground ahead of the October–December short rains.

The heightened vigilance comes as global forecasters warn that the unfolding El Niño weather pattern is set to strengthen in the coming months, with its effects projected to persist into early 2027.

While Kenya, Somalia, and Tanzania face heightened threats of river overflows, flash floods, and mudslides, the underlying atmospheric shift is driving an entirely opposite crisis across the wider region. According to assessments from the United Nations and the World Meteorological Organisation (WMO), the same climate mechanism generating excess rain across the eastern horn is cutting off vital moisture across southern Africa and major Asian agricultural belts.

### Continental Divide in Rainfall

![Situation along Mombasa Road following heavy rains in Nairobi, May 1, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/ynzGmk9b/e1715b8920bc.webp)

In Southern Africa, the strengthening system is forecast to suppress seasonal rains across Zambia, Malawi, Mozambique, and South Africa. Rather than managing excess runoff, authorities in those countries are bracing for shrinking water reservoirs, failing crops, and depleted pasture for livestock.

UN forecasters note that this sharp geographical split underscores why the phenomenon cannot be managed under a uniform continental response strategy.

The global impacts of the shift are already registering in key agricultural systems:

* **India:** Below-average monsoon rains were recorded between June 1 and September 9, with national meteorological data showing a 15 percent deficit that threatens irrigation reserves and domestic crop yields.
* **Indonesia:** Prolonged dryness has triggered severe forest and peatland wildfires, with meteorological projections showing 61 percent of the country facing a delayed and compressed rainy season.
* **Australia:** Eastern and southern territories are recording unseasonably hot, dry conditions aligned with traditional Pacific warming patterns.

### Atmospheric Disruptions

El Niño develops when sea surface temperatures across the central and eastern tropical Pacific warm above historical averages. That ocean warming alters broader atmospheric circulation, shifting where moisture travels and where cloud systems form.

While the UN clarifies that the cycle is a natural phenomenon rather than one directly created by climate change, broader environmental shifts can magnify the severity of its localized disruptions.

In Kenya, where previous heavy rain cycles have triggered widespread infrastructure damage and displacement, emergency coordination teams are focusing resources on early warnings in low-lying settlements and riparian buffers.

With rainfall activity expected to pick up over the coming weeks, national authorities are keeping county disaster committees on alert as monitoring teams track river basins and urban drainage systems.
