President William Ruto took direct aim at the brewing political feud within his own camp on Sunday, calling out United Democratic Alliance (UDA) figures Patrick and Gabriel after their competing ambitions sparked violent clashes at the Maa Cultural and Tourism Festival in Narok.

Addressing attendees following the weekend chaos at Sekenani Gate, the Head of State refused to sweep the disturbance under the rug. Instead, he placed the blame squarely on elite positioning for the Narok gubernatorial seat, urging residents not to let personal political rivalries poison public life.

"Patrick and Gabriel are both members of UDA. There is no major difference between them; they are our brothers," President Ruto said, addressing the bad blood between the two politicians. "Their only difference is that they are seeking leadership positions. I urge everyone to calm down, particularly the people of Narok, so that we can have a peaceful election. The governor’s seat cannot be decided by one person. Every seat will be decided by the voters. The same applies to the presidency."

### Chaos at Sekenani Gate

The public rebuke followed an ugly turn of events on Saturday, November 14, when rival youth factions clashed violently barely 500 metres from the festival’s main grounds. The fourth edition of the annual cultural showcase had drawn large delegations to celebrate regional heritage, arts, and tourism, with the President arriving to preside over the festivities and officially open the Maasai Women Ushanga Curio Hall.

Instead of a clean ceremonial showcase, the event was abruptly halted when running confrontations broke out near the perimeter. More than ten people sustained injuries during the skirmishes, scattering festival attendees and briefly bringing the official programme to an uncertain standstill.

The violence unmasked the volatile undercurrents shadowing Narok’s leadership contest, where competing factions aligned with Patrick and Gabriel have sought to assert dominance on high-profile public platforms.

### Setting the Boundary for 2027

For Ruto, the sight of allied politicians turning a cultural celebration into an arena for street confrontation crossed a clear line. Reflecting on the previous day's disorder, the President warned that turning political differences into physical friction would not be tolerated as the country heads deeper into the electoral cycle.

”Yesterday, we witnessed some tension and disagreements, and that was not a good picture. I want to urge our brothers and sisters not to turn political differences into confrontations among citizens,” he told the crowd.

His message underscored that elective mandates—from county headquarters up to State House—are not won by bullying rivals or orchestrating sideline scuffles. By telling Narok residents *"msikubali kubebewa akili"*, Ruto sought to strip away the manufactured hostilities between supporters, reminding voters that the contest between Patrick and Gabriel is an internal ambition contest, not a tribal or communal war.

### Eyes on the Ballot

The tension in Narok comes against a wider backdrop of political realignments and friction between the ruling administration and the opposition. While Ruto reiterated his broader stance that competing political voices should counter the government with concrete alternate agendas rather than mere confrontation, his most pointed intervention remained local.

By laying down an explicit marker before both Patrick and Gabriel, the President made it clear that party seniority offers no cover for disruptions at public state events. With the general election approaching, the clash at Sekenani Gate serves as an early signal that the battle for regional supremacy inside UDA will face intense scrutiny straight from the party leader himself.
