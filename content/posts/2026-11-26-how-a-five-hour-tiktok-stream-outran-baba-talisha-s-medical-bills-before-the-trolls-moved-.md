When a livestream can pull together close to KSh 2.2 million in five hours, the digital machinery of Kenyan content creation moves at a pace traditional medical financing can only envy. 

That was the velocity seen on Monday, November 23, 2026, when fellow TikTokers, brands, and well-wishers rallied behind digital influencer and photographer Faustine Lukale, widely known as Baba Talisha. The marathon digital session easily surpassed an initial target of KSh 1.6 million, a sum originally calculated to cover specialised medical management and examinations abroad.

Yet the speed of the digital response quickly collided with the heavy machinery of online commentary. Days after the livestream closed with KSh 2,198,541 raised—bolstered by another KSh 323,000 collected ahead of the event to bring his total medical war chest to roughly KSh 2.52 million—Baba Talisha found himself addressing social media discussions questioning the integrity of the kitty.

Taking to social media on Thursday, November 26, 2026, the creator dismissed narratives that he argued were designed to cast doubt on the intentions of those who contributed to his treatment.

“You can spread false information, twist narratives and create stories just to make people talk and question the intentions of those who stood by me. But one thing you cannot change is the truth,” he wrote, adding that he refused to engage with manufactured claims.

Behind the digital back-and-forth lies a severe clinical timeline. Baba Talisha has been battling Acanthamoeba keratitis, a rare and aggressive eye infection that severely damaged his cornea and impaired his eyesight. On November 21, 2026, he underwent a therapeutic cornea transplant at a local hospital after the infection caused his cornea to perforate.

Even with the local procedure completed, his medical team pressed for specialised follow-up care in India to safeguard his remaining vision. That specialized travel, however, arrived with financial hurdles that outpaced household safety nets. His wife, businesswoman Wanjiru C. Wanjiru, popularly known as Miss Wanjey, previously noted that their private insurance cover had been completely exhausted settling the bills for the initial Kenyan surgeries.

For an influencer ecosystem accustomed to turning daily banter into viral engagement, the fundraiser demonstrated how quickly community-led digital mobilization can fill a systemic healthcare gap. Whether the unfolding discussions around the funds will subside before his departure for India remains to be seen as the creator turns his focus toward his upcoming medical travel.
