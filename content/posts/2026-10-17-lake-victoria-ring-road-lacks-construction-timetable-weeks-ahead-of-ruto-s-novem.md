Weeks before a promised November start date, the government has yet to establish a firm construction schedule for the multi-billion-shilling Lake Victoria Ring Road, even as technical teams take to the field to resolve baseline design hurdles.

On Saturday, Roads and Transport Cabinet Secretary Davis Chirchir led an on-site route inspection alongside World Bank Division Director for Kenya, Rwanda, Somalia, and Uganda Qimiao Fan. The joint delegation surveyed existing road alignments and identified structural bottlenecks across western Kenya that must be engineered out before heavy machinery can move in.

While the mission signals multilateral backing for the corridor, the Ministry of Roads has stopped short of issuing a formal civil works rollout program. That leaves contractors and regional transport sectors without clarity on how the State intends to meet President William Ruto’s public pledge to launch the Ksh60 billion first phase within weeks.

### Expanding the Corridor Footprint

![Transport CS Davis Chirchir, World Bank officials during the inspection of the proposed Lake Victoria Ring Road project on October 17, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/dwKmKKMt/28a280cc9555.webp)

The project’s physical scope has swollen significantly since its initial conception as a roughly 500-kilometre loop hugging the lake shoreline. 

In June, Treasury Cabinet Secretary John Mbadi announced that the State had integrated the 112-kilometre Kisumu–Busia highway into the project framework under the Roads for Rural Economic Development Programme. That decision pushed the total corridor network to roughly 612 kilometres.

Incorporating the Kisumu–Busia stretch altered both the financing profile and the strategic purpose of the project. Rather than serving purely as an inter-county littoral link, the corridor now intersects directly with the Northern Corridor, which carries heavy transit cargo moving between the port of Mombasa, Uganda, and the wider Great Lakes hinterland.

### Five Riparian Counties in Transit Path

On paper, the proposed highway is designed to alter the economic map across five counties: Busia, Siaya, Kisumu, Homa Bay, and Migori. 

Large sections of the Lake Victoria coastline currently rely on poor, weather-vulnerable feeder roads to move daily catches from inland landing beaches to urban processing centers. Chirchir noted during the field review that the infrastructure aims to link these isolated landing sites and agricultural tracts directly with commercial trading hubs, alongside boosting regional tourism access.

![chirchir-lake-victoria-ringroad.jpg](https://i.ibb.co/bjgHwTjZ/c6aab8268b41.webp)

Yet transitioning those plans into active construction requires synchronizing World Bank environmental and social impact frameworks with national land acquisition processes, particularly along densely populated sections in Siaya and Kisumu.

### The Scoping Race Against the Calendar

The ground assessment follows Cabinet approval of the ring road alongside two other primary economic transit arteries. However, the operational clock is tightening.

President Ruto first flagged a 2026 construction launch during a tour in March, reaffirming in September that Ksh60 billion had been earmarked for initial works starting in November. 

Saturday's joint review with World Bank directors confirms that design reviews, route surveys, and obstacle mapping remain in active technical appraisal. Until the Ministry of Roads publishes a gazetted works schedule, contractor tender awards, and site handovers, the corridor remains in the engineering preparation phase.
