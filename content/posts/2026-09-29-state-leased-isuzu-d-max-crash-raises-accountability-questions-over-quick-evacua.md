The operational protocols governing state-leased vehicles face renewed scrutiny following a highway collision in Kikuyu involving Kitutu Masaba MP Clive Gisairo and a double-cab pickup tied to the Ministry of Interior.

According to accounts provided by the legislator, his vehicle was travelling from his constituency toward Nairobi at approximately 8 p.m. on Monday when a speeding Isuzu D-Max, bearing registration number KDX961T and leased from Isuzu Kenya to the interior ministry, struck his car from behind.

![A collage showing the wreckage following a crash involving Kitutu Masaba MP Clive Gisairo’s car and a vehicle leased to the Ministry of Interior, September 28, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/7xVXpGfF/14f27279150c.webp)

The impact forced the government-registered vehicle to lose control and roll onto the road shoulder. Meanwhile, Gisairo's driver managed to bring their vehicle to a halt despite the rear right tyre being torn off the rim. While Gisairo sustained muscle pulls, the focus of the aftermath quickly shifted to the swift removal of the other vehicle's passengers.

Gisairo stated that he and his team assisted the two occupants at the scene. However, before standard investigative procedures could fully play out on-site, additional ministry officers arrived and reportedly transported the pair away.

“The ministry has identified the two persons in the car together with the other ministry officers who whisked them away,” Gisairo noted, confirming that formal reports have been lodged with relevant investigative agencies.

![Clive Ombane Gisairo](https://i.ibb.co/MdJCn7c/ea1ce087cf10.webp)

The incident places a spotlight on the oversight of state-contracted transport assets and the gray areas of emergency response when government property is involved in highway mishaps. While the MP has reported cooperating with authorities and expects disciplinary action against any officers found culpable, the interior ministry has yet to issue a comprehensive public accounting of why the occupants were removed from the scene prior to complete traffic police processing.

Investigations into the exact speed, driver identity, and official assignment of the vehicle at the time of the 8 p.m. crash remain ongoing.
