When institutional tech portfolios stumble, traditional economic theory suggests gold should gain from the fallout. The past week proved the inverse: as heavy losses ripped through artificial intelligence and technology shares, institutional funds dumped their most liquid safe-haven assets to free up immediate cash, dragging international bullion down with them.

The Central Bank of Kenya (CBK), tracking the shift in its weekly statistical bulletin, recorded a 3.5 per cent decline in spot gold over seven trading days. By Thursday, October 22, the metal was trading at USD 4,024.24 per ounce, sliding down from USD 4,170.25 on October 15. The retreat erased USD 146.01 per ounce in a single week.

Because the Kenyan shilling held exceptionally flat over the same window—closing Thursday at Ksh129.91 per dollar compared to Ksh129.81 the previous week—the international price correction translated almost untouched into the domestic market. In local currency, an ounce of gold shed roughly Ksh18,551 in value, dropping from Ksh541,340 to Ksh522,789 without any foreign-exchange buffering to soften the move.

### The Liquidity Squeeze

Central bank analysts attributed the contraction to sudden asset reallocations triggered by tumbling technology equities. In periods of acute equity repricing, multi-asset funds often find themselves compelled to settle margin calls or offset trading declines elsewhere by liquidating profitable, high-liquidity holdings. Gold, having experienced an extended multi-year rally, served as the primary pool of cash.

International trading tracked by the *Financial Times* showed that momentum continued to deteriorate through the end of the week, with spot rates breaking beneath the psychological threshold of USD 4,000 for the first time since July, touching intraday lows of USD 3,998 (about Ksh518,756).

The retreat exposes the limits of gold's defensive reputation during concentrated market pullbacks. While gold retains sovereign value over multi-year cycles, it routinely functions as a trading piggy bank when high-conviction momentum trades, particularly the multi-billion-dollar bets placed on artificial intelligence infrastructure and enterprise software, begin to sour.

### Extended Reset From January Peaks

The October slump also deepens a broader cooling cycle that has been quietly unfolding across global precious metal desks throughout 2026. After hitting historic zeniths above USD 5,400 an ounce in January, the metal has shed more than a quarter of its value as stubbornly elevated oil prices renewed inflation anxiety and altered investor bets on the path of central bank interest rates worldwide.

With benchmark borrowing rates remaining tighter than anticipated, non-yielding assets like bullion face heightened competition from interest-bearing government debt.

For domestic balance sheets and regional investors who entered gold positions during early 2026 highs, the steady transmission of the sell-off leaves domestic valuations directly tethered to the next turn on foreign equity exchanges, with local currency stability ensuring that every dollar lost in global trading registers immediately in Nairobi.
