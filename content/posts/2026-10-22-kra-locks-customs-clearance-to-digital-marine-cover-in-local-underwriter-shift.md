The Kenya Revenue Authority and the Insurance Regulatory Authority have jointly ordered that all inbound maritime shipments secure locally underwritten digital insurance, redirecting import underwriting entirely to licensed Kenyan insurers while shutting out clearing agents who fail to link their policies electronically.

Under the directive taking effect on November 1, 2026, cargo declarations cannot be processed through customs without an active digital certificate verified within state revenue systems.

Importers and clearing logistics firms must now obtain cover strictly through underwriters registered with the Insurance Regulatory Authority. These policies must be registered on the dedicated Digital Marine Cargo Insurance System, which will automatically validate and feed the certificate directly into the taxman’s Integrated Customs Management System before any entry documentation can be submitted.

### The Digital Clearance Gate

![Fille image of the busy Mombasa Port in Mombasa County, Kenya](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/Mombasa%20Port_0.jpg.webp?itok=-NDaypDN)

In a joint public notice released on Thursday, October 22, the tax agency outlined that the automated linkage will operate as an uncompromising clearance barrier rather than an administrative cross-check.

“Consignments failing to meet these requirements will be blocked from clearance, and stakeholders are urged to ensure full compliance to prevent trade disruptions,” KRA stated, confirming that enforcement follows earlier procedural notices issued alongside the insurance regulator.

The technical integration removes manual verification from port entry operations. By requiring the Digital Marine Cargo Insurance System to transmit policy records to iCMS before declaration lodging, the platform creates a digital choke point that stops non-compliant paperwork before cargo arrives at terminal gates.

Importers who previously relied on offshore or blanket foreign marine coverage must transition their logistical documentation to IRA-licensed providers. The regulatory authority has posted operating directions on its official portal to direct traders through the certification steps.

### Broader Overhaul of Cargo Oversight

The marine cover requirement forms part of a wider modernization program across Kenya's freight inspection channels. Alongside the November 1 insurance deadline, KRA is finalizing a structural shift in transit cargo surveillance under the Regional Electronic Cargo Tracking System.

The authority is scheduled to phase out all government-owned electronic cargo tracking seals by Monday, October 26. Under the revised RECTS framework, commercial importers and cargo hauliers must source approved monitoring devices directly from authorized private vendors, removing state equipment from day-to-day transit logistics.

With the October 26 tracking equipment transition concluding days ahead of the November 1 digital insurance enforcement, clearing desks and freight forwarders face a dual-track compliance window to avoid immediate cargo holdups at entry ports.
