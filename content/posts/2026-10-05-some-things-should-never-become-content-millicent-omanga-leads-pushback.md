Former nominated senator Millicent Omanga and key public figures have called out content creator Ciggie Johnson over his decision to broadcast a private paternity dispute, after the club host published a recorded conversation revealing a DNA test excluded him as the biological father of a 12-year-old girl he raised from birth.

The release of the audio clip on Saturday, October 3, 2026, quickly turned a personal family revelation into an intense debate over creator ethics, minor privacy, and the boundaries of social media publishing. Omanga delivered a direct rebuke aimed at the decision to post the conversation online.

“Some things should never become content,” Omanga stated, arguing that the emotional stability and dignity of a 12-year-old minor ought to take precedence over social media views, public sympathy, or viral engagement.

In the recorded phone call published by Ciggie, whose real name is Ciggie Okebiro, the child’s mother explained the official medical phrasing, telling him he had been “excluded as the biological father.” Ciggie challenged the clinical terminology in the clip, responding: “Stop complicated English. DNA says I am not the father.”

The conversation captured the mother expressing surprise at the outcome, maintaining she had fully expected the laboratory results to confirm Ciggie as the biological parent. Ciggie insisted on obtaining a physical copy of the official report to review the findings personally, while questioning whether the mother knew the true identity of the child’s biological father.

Over the past 12 years, Ciggie had taken on full parental obligations, paying for school fees, rent, and daily maintenance expenses since the child’s birth. The recording also revealed underlying friction between the two, with Ciggie citing previous instances where the mother had reported him to children’s officers and police stations during prior disputes.

Prior to the testing, Ciggie said he had refused to fund the DNA test upfront, asking the mother to cover the initial cost while promising to reimburse her if the findings confirmed his paternity. Towards the end of the published call, he pushed for the girl to be informed immediately, with the mother agreeing to break the news after school that afternoon.

As the published audio circulated across social platforms, public figures split between condemning the public exposure of the child and pleading for the preservation of parental bonds. Comedian Terence Creative made a public plea asking Ciggie not to walk away from the child, emphasizing that 12 years of active parenting established a real bond regardless of genetic results.

Pastor Victor Kanyari offered a similar perspective, questioning whether publishing painful private truths served any constructive purpose and encouraging Ciggie not to let laboratory outcomes completely redefine his relationship with the child. Media personality Oga Obinna separately highlighted the broader emotional fallout and relational strain that DNA test reveals bring to families.

As of Monday, the underlying DNA document has not been independently verified by official third parties. The child’s mother has made no public statement beyond the recorded telephone conversation, and Ciggie has not publicly confirmed whether he plans to continue providing financial support or remain active in the girl’s life.
