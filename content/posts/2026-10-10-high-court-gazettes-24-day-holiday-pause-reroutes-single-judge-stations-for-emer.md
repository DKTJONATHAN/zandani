Litigants facing urgent legal emergencies during the year-end holidays will rely on designated duty benches and cross-station transfers as Kenya’s superior trial courts enter a 24-day recess starting December 21, 2026.

The administrative calendar, gazetted by High Court Principal Judge Eric Ogola, formally winds down standard hearings across three parallel tiers: the High Court, the Employment and Labour Relations Court, and the Environment and Land Court. The break runs through January 13, 2027, with both dates included.

While regular trial schedules pause, the gazette notice activates specific contingency measures under the High Court (Practice and Procedure) Rules to prevent legal disputes from languishing in procedural limbo during the festive shutdown.

### Cross-Station Safety Net

![File photo of Milimani Law Courts in Nairobi](https://i.ibb.co/m5xt2x7g/96d0fd03efc8.webp)

The most acute operational shift falls on court stations staffed by a solitary judge. In those outlying jurisdictions, urgent applications arising during the 24-day window cannot be heard locally. Instead, matters must be transferred directly to the nearest High Court station operating with a gazetted recess duty judge.

To ensure litigants and advocates are not blindsided by empty courtrooms, presiding judges at affected single-judge stations have been directed to post formal notices identifying the designated receiving station prominently on public notice boards before the break takes effect.

At multi-judge stations and specialised divisions, presiding judges will select in-house duty judges charged specifically with hearing applications certified as urgent, applications for interim conservatory orders, and time-sensitive criminal bail reviews.

Parallel administrative assignments will run across the labour and environmental courts, where their respective principal judges will designate duty officers to cover employment strikes, workplace injunctions, and contested land disruptions.

### Registries Retain Full-Day Counters

![A photo of the Court Bench](https://i.ibb.co/hR0dH5q3/a66daabe946e.webp)

Despite the suspension of regular open-court hearings, registry services will not experience an operational blackout. Court registries across all covered stations will remain open to the public from 8:00 am to 5:00 pm on all standard weekdays throughout the recess.

This continuous counter service excludes only the statutory public holidays, allowing advocates and litigants to file fresh suits, lodge replies, pay court fees, and extract processed orders without waiting for the new judicial term.

### Appellate and Subordinate Calendars Pending

The directive issued by Principal Judge Ogola applies strictly to the High Court and its equal-status specialised counterparts. It leaves the calendars for the rest of Kenya's judicial hierarchy unfinalised.

Neither the Supreme Court, the Court of Appeal, nor the subordinate magistracy has published gazetted recess schedules for the 2026–2027 festive transition. While both appellate levels and local magistrates traditionally observe an end-year break, their operational notices and duty bench lineups remain pending as December approaches.
