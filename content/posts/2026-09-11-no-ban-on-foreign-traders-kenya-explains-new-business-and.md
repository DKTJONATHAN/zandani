Nairobi, September 11, 2026 - The State House has officially pivoted to a policy of strict regulatory compliance regarding foreign traders, effectively quashing rumors of a blanket expulsion of non-Kenyan business owners.

The directive, issued jointly by Prime Cabinet Secretary Musalia Mudavadi and Foreign Affairs Principal Secretary Korir Sing’oei, establishes that while Kenya remains an open market, all foreign nationals-including East African Community (EAC) citizens-must now operate within a rigid legal framework. The government maintains that the issue is one of administrative legitimacy rather than nationality, focusing on the mandatory requirement for valid residency permits, formal business registration, and sector-specific licenses.

The sequence of events follows a period of intense public scrutiny regarding the saturation of the informal retail sector by foreign operators. Officials have clarified that the September 2 policy intervention serves as a measure to protect indigenous, compliant traders from unfair competition. Under the new guidelines, foreign participation in small-scale commerce is permitted only if the business entity is fully documented and tax-compliant.

Confirmation from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs indicates that the state is shifting from a posture of informal allowance to active enforcement. PS Sing’oei has issued a call for all affected individuals to regularize their status immediately. The government intends to provide administrative guidance to facilitate this transition, framing the move as an effort to bring informal trade under the formal tax umbrella rather than a targeted removal of foreign personnel.

Statistically, the government aims to account for 100 percent of business operations currently functioning outside the national regulatory database. While specific enforcement quotas remain undisclosed, the administration has signaled that the current "regularization phase" will be followed by systemic audits to ensure that the residency status of all foreign traders aligns with the nature of their commercial activities.
