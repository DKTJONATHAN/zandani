Austin Bolo, the fashion designer known professionally as Bolo Bespoke, has drawn a firm boundary at the door of the operating theatre as his wife, Josephine Mueni, prepares to undergo a Caesarean section for their third child. Rather than following the growing trend of public figures accompanying their partners into surgical suites, Bolo has publicly rejected the proposition, framing attendance during surgical delivery as a direct breach of traditional African manhood.

The friction surfaced during a joint exchange shared on Friday, November 27, 2026, when Mueni pressed her husband to stand alongside her during the delivery. After navigating the physical demands of pregnancy over nine months, she appealed directly to him for emotional backing during the final clinical step, arguing that the experience required shared physical presence.

“You are supposed to be in that theatre, you gave me that pregnancy, I had the trauma for nine months alone, here is the last step, I want your moral support,” Mueni urged, laying out her expectation for spousal solidarity in the operating room. Her position was supported by their attending physician, who explicitly advised Bolo that joining his wife in the surgical theatre during the C-section would assist in her care.

Despite the combined push from his wife and the medical advice from the doctor, the designer stood firm on his refusal. Invoking his cultural upbringing, Bolo insisted that witnessing childbirth fundamentally conflicts with his personal code and traditional norms.

“Daktari ananiambia na bibi yangu I should be in the theatre room when my wife is delivering and having her C-section. I am not okay with it,” Bolo stated. “Am a typical African man, how can I be where my wife is giving birth? It is not allowed in our culture. Mimi sikuji huko.”

Rather than quietly settling the matter with the hospital team, Bolo put the issue directly to his male followers who have navigated birthing rooms, asking whether his refusal was justified or if he was succumbing to an unnecessary trend.

“Uyu mama azae tu aki. Hizi ni gani tena sai? Eti sasa mimi niingie theatre akizaa? Surely. Kwani, what happened to being an African man?” he asked his audience. “What do you think? Niingie nisiingie? Do I have an option? Ama ni punishment? Men with theatre experience ebu mniambie.”

The stance highlights an ongoing friction in modern lifestyle expectations, where online creators increasingly document their presence inside delivery rooms to signal active participation, standing in contrast to long-standing traditional taboos that historically designated labor spaces as off-limits to men. While medical facilities routinely permit partners into non-sterile zones of surgical suites to mitigate maternal distress, cultural practices among traditional communities often view the delivery room as exclusively reserved for women and medical staff.

As the couple prepares to welcome their third child, Bolo has left his decision anchored to his cultural stance, leaving the surgical room boundary intact as the delivery date approaches.
