County governments are staring at a severe administrative squeeze after the Council of Governors (CoG) formally rejected the Commission on Revenue Allocation's (CRA) proposed Ksh440.8 billion equitable share for the 2027/2028 financial year.

The pushback, which demands an additional Ksh100 billion injected into the shareable pool, is driven by a stack of mandatory financial commitments that county bosses argue have been left out of the central planning framework.

![Council of Governors during 2026 full council meeting on October 12, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/VW77mDFV/baa99b8217c6.webp)

At the heart of the standoff is a mounting ledger of personnel and operational costs. According to CoG, the proposed ceiling fails to account for Ksh8.6 billion required to sustain Universal Health Coverage staff who have transitioned to permanent and pensionable terms under county payrolls. Another Ksh2 billion is slated for health workers' Collective Bargaining Agreements and allowances to forestall industrial action during an electioneering cycle.

Beyond health sector liabilities, county governments are absorbing a Ksh21 billion bill following salary adjustments directed by the Salaries and Remuneration Commission (SRC) for devolved personnel. , governors are demanding Ksh65.97 billion to match unbundled functions that were pushed down to county administrations while the corresponding budgetary resources remained domiciled in respective national ministries.

Compounding these recurring expenditures is a designated Ksh10 billion provision for transitional costs as the 47 counties navigate the 2027 election period, alongside an estimated Ksh30 billion adjustment tied to a projected 5 per cent rise in local revenue collection costs.

![governors](https://i.ibb.co/Q7261jz3/353ae4624e87.webp)

Beyond the raw figures, the dispute has laid bare deep institutional friction over how revenue ceilings are formulated. Governors took issue with the CRA for holding bilateral engagements with the National Assembly and the Senate before conducting prior consultations with county leadership, accusing the commission of leaning closer to National Treasury projections than ground-level realities.

These contested recommendations serve as the baseline for determining how nationally raised revenue is divided between the national government and the 47 county governments, setting the stage for a protracted legislative battle in Parliament.
