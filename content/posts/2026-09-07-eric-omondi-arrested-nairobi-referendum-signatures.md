Comedian-turned-activist Eric Omondi was arrested by plainclothes police officers outside the Nairobi National Archives on Monday morning while collecting citizen signatures for a constitutional referendum petition aimed at downsizing Kenya's 47 counties to eight.

### Signature Drive Interrupted at City Center

Plainclothes officers approached Omondi alongside his mobilization team as members of the public lined up to sign petition forms in the Nairobi Central Business District. The officers escorted Omondi to a waiting police vehicle without resistance, seizing clipboards, banners, and signed petition sheets before driving him away from the scene.

The public drive aimed at triggering a popular initiative under Article 257 of the Constitution to petition the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC). Omondi's proposed legislative reform bill seeks wide-ranging austerity measures across the national and county governments, including shrinking devolved units from 47 back to the historical eight regional boundaries.

Under the proposed reform bill, the initiative also seeks to eliminate the Senate, abolish all 47 County Woman Representative posts, remove nominated slots for Members of County Assembly (MCAs), and reduce the total number of elective parliamentary seats. ### Pattern of High-Profile Detentions

Monday's interception adds to a series of police detentions involving Omondi during civic protests. In June 2026, security personnel detained the activist at Jomo Kenyatta International Airport over an anti-tax demonstration, following an earlier arrest outside Parliament buildings during economic protests in 2024.

Civil society groups and onlookers along Moi Avenue condemned the police action, emphasizing that the exercise was conducted peacefully within constitutional parameters. Witnesses confirmed that citizens were voluntarily queuing to record their names and national identification details on the IEBC petition schedules before officers halted the process.

Organizers affiliated with the initiative stated that the signature collection campaign will continue across other major urban hubs despite the disruption in Nairobi. Legal representatives for Omondi confirmed they were visiting several police stations within the capital on Monday afternoon to establish his exact detention center and apply for police cash bail.
