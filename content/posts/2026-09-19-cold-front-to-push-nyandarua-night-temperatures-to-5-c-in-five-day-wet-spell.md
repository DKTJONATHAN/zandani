A sharp nocturnal chill will dominate weather patterns across Kenya’s high-altitude spine over the next five days, with overnight temperatures in Nyandarua expected to plunge to 5°C as intermittent showers spread across more than twenty counties.

The latest five-day advisory issued by the Kenya Meteorological Service Authority (KMSA), spanning Sunday, September 20 through Thursday, September 24, 2026, points to a sustained dampening of temperatures rather than isolated downpours. While urban centres in the central belt prepare for wet mornings, the defining feature of the forecast period is an expansive cold front extending from the agricultural highlands deep into semi-arid pastoral districts.

### The Highland Freeze and Pastoral Chill

Nyandarua sits at the extreme edge of the incoming cold spell. KMSA projections indicate the county will record an overnight drop to 5°C on Monday, with lows hovering near 6°C through Thursday. 

![Mist covering a street in Nairobi.](https://i.ibb.co/ynxC8g5X/ec902da19298.webp)

Surrounding agricultural zones in Central Kenya and the Rift Valley will register comparable, if slightly milder, nighttime depressions. Laikipia is forecast to record minimums between 11°C and 13°C, while the tea-growing belts of Nandi, Kericho, and Bomet will see night readings settle between 12°C and 13°C.

In the capital and its immediate commuter periphery, the drop will be less severe but persistent. Nairobi’s night temperatures are expected to fluctuate between 13°C and 14°C, while Kiambu and Nyeri will see nocturnal minimums between 13°C and 15°C.

Crucially, KMSA highlighted that cold conditions will not remain confined to traditional highland escarpments. The authority warned of intermittent cold spells touching lowland pastoral counties, including Wajir and Garissa, alongside Kajiado and Murang’a, signaling wide geographical exposure to unseasonably brisk conditions.

### Coast and Eastern Slopes Face Sustained Moisture

Alongside the drop in temperatures, precipitation will follow two distinct tracks: persistent marine showers along the Indian Ocean coastline and localized morning drizzle in the eastern catchments.

The coastal strip will see active conditions from Sunday morning. Mombasa is slated for moderate showers across Sunday morning, afternoon, and night. Kwale will record moderate rainfall on Sunday before breaking into sunny intervals, whereas Kilifi is forecast to experience moderate showers continuously from Sunday through Thursday. Further north, Lamu will receive light rains, while Tana River and Taita Taveta record light showers concentrated on Sunday and Monday.

![Motorists using a flooded highway during cold and rainy weather in Nairobi](https://i.ibb.co/Rdr3TJ0/42a207337db5.webp)

Further inland, Embu is marked out for the most persistent rainfall profile within the eastern quadrant. The county will see morning rains on Sunday, stepping up to moderate showers on Monday, followed by lighter falls through Thursday. Neighboring lowland counties Kitui and Machakos are scheduled for light morning showers over the first three days of the outlook.

### Urban Wet Spells and Western Coverage

For Nairobi and Kiambu, the moisture footprint will arrive primarily as early-week dampeners. Both counties are forecast to record light rains on Sunday, Monday, and Tuesday, creating slick commuting conditions during early morning transit hours before tapering later in the week. By contrast, upper Central counties Nyeri and Murang’a will see rainfall recur throughout the entire five-day period.

The broader weather system also covers a large swathe of the western agricultural belt and the Rift Valley floor. Light to moderate activity is projected for Tharaka-Nithi, Nakuru, Narok, and Kajiado, as well as West Pokot, Trans Nzoia, and Uasin Gishu. Western and Lake Victoria basin counties—including Kakamega, Vihiga, Bungoma, Kisii, Nyamira, and Homa Bay—remain within the active rain band through Thursday.

With low cloud cover and depressed daytime heating likely to slow ground evaporation, the convergence of early-morning drizzle and biting night lows will place an immediate operational premium on livestock warmth in the highlands and transport management along busy highland commuter corridors.
