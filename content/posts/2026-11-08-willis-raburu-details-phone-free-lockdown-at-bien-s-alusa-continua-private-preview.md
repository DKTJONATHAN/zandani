Willis Raburu had to deposit his mobile phone and cameras at an entrance station marked by a blunt instructional sign—"Please leave your gadgets here"—before hearing a single bar of Bien-Aimé Baraza’s upcoming studio project, *Alusa Continua*.

The Saturday night preview was deliberately stripped of the usual red-carpet livestreaming and instant social media clipping. Instead, Bien’s camp instituted a total digital embargo at the door, forcing invitees into an uninterrupted, analog session with the 17-track album nearly two weeks ahead of its official public debut.

For Raburu, the enforced focus turned the private screening into a raw emotional marathon.

"We left all gadgets away to experience an evening of pure bliss," Raburu shared after retrieving his phone. "I have danced, cried, felt happy, sad, oh my goodness."

### The Anatomy of an Embargoed Room

![Konshens Confirms December 18 Concert at Sarit Expo Centre](https://i.ibb.co/WpfjWyGS/de354d8d9842.webp)

The media personality described the gathering not merely as a standard media playback, but as a carefully curated showcase designed to present an authentic African sound intended for a global audience. Stripped of notification dings and screen glow, the playback brought out dramatic mood shifts across the project's soundscape.

"What a legend, a superstar, a national treasure... my goodness, what was that we have listened if not the album of this decade!" Raburu said. "Pure, rich. African, surprising, the epitome of depth and authentic African music, a gift to the world, a package of greatness."

Raburu credited the executive and staging execution behind the private session to Bien's long-time manager, Chiki Kuruka, alongside a collaborative ensemble of choreographers and visual artists who built out the room's thematic atmosphere.

"Oh my goodness, I promise you this album will cause ripples across the industry and the world," he added. "Catchy, feisty, alluring, bold, sweet, deep, rooted and centered. Just pure."

### A Roster Built for Heavy Cross-Border Reach

Beyond the theatrical atmosphere of the phone-free room, the preview confirmed the official architecture of *Alusa Continua*. The tracklist revealed a deliberate, multi-market curation strategy that bridges Western hip-hop, West African Afrobeats, and regional East African staples across 17 titles.

![George Ruto's Hilarious Reaction to Sister Charlene Ruto’s Wedding](https://i.ibb.co/CKqZCnyf/883831589058.webp)

On the international front, American rapper 2Chainz joins forces with Papa Gen Z on the track "SIGNS", while US-based artist KayCyy appears on "MUENI." Nigerian vocalist Oxlade links up with Ontiri on "HOW CAN I FORGET," locking in Bien’s footprint in West Africa's streaming hub.

The regional roster draws heavily on East African heavyweights. Tanzanian veteran Alikiba features on "FINALE," while Ugandan vocalist Joshua Baraka partners with live instrumental outfit The Compozers on "DON'T GO."

Domestically, Bien balanced the global names with local lyricists and vocalists. Kenyan rapper Femi One teams up with Papaa Maasai on "MAYOLO," complemented across the broader tracklist by appearances from Coster Ojwang, Ywaya Tajiri, Bryce One, Tulwap Kipsigis, and Nono Melodies.

### The Follow-Up Test

The high-stakes rollout follows Bien's full-length solo debut, *Alusa Why Are You Topless?*, which arrived in November 2023. That 16-track precursor yielded chart-topping singles such as "My Baby" and "Lifestyle," picking up multiple regional industry awards and testing his viability outside the collective framework of Sauti Sol.

With *Alusa Continua* scheduled for public release on Friday, November 20, 2026, the strict conditions of Saturday's private preview signal an artist looking to bypass casual streaming culture and command undivided attention for his second chapter.
