The Social Health Authority has released compliance guidelines opening instalment-based health insurance for informal sector households, offering low-income earners a path to maintain active cover through automated micro-payments rather than lump-sum annual premiums.

The framework, detailed on Monday by SHA Senior Officer for Registration and Compliance Jacqueline Ngonyo, is structured to accommodate fragmented earnings in the informal economy while establishing strict credit criteria for those financing their contributions through state micro-lending channels.

“Those in the informal sector, we have those who are able to comfortably afford their annual premiums and pay to SHA,” Ngonyo stated. “We have those who are not able to afford that lump sum payment, and they enrol in certain products such as the Lipa Pole Pole, but then we have those who cannot even manage the Lipa Pole Pole, and they have been clearly identified by the government as indigents.”

### The Hustler Fund Credit Barrier

![SHA Senior Officer for Registration and Compliance, Jacqueline Ngonyo](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/sha-senior-officer-for-registration-and-compliance-jacqueline-ngonyo.jpg.webp?itok=9NDDtmex)

While formally employed workers contribute a mandatory 2.75 per cent gross salary deduction processed through employer payrolls, self-employed and informal earners must undergo means testing via the Afya Yangu digital portal to determine their assessment tier, with baseline premiums starting at approximately Ksh300 per month.

To activate the Lipa Pole Pole instalment option, however, informal contributors face specific micro-credit eligibility hurdles. Applicants must hold an active Hustler Fund profile with no outstanding loan defaults. Accessing the facility requires dialing *147# or *254#, navigating to payment options, selecting self-payment under Lipa Pole Pole, and querying their available Hustler Fund credit ceiling.

Crucially, enrollment is not immediate upon application. The scheme requires informal workers to either advance an initial four months of contributions upfront or settle all existing premium arrears before benefits are unlocked. Approved contributors can then split remaining liabilities into daily, weekly, or monthly deductions, backed by automated M-PESA Ratiba standing orders.

### Default Penalties and Safety-Net Provisions

![Social Health Authority building in Nairobi.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/sha-build.jpg.webp?itok=8NB0AL6M)

The financing terms carry tight financial consequences for delayed payments. While timely repayments accrue an annual interest rate of 8 per cent, the financing charge jumps to 9.5 per cent once a 30-day payment cycle is missed. A single lapsed 30-day window triggers an automatic deactivation of health insurance cover, locking households out of secondary and tertiary hospital benefits until arrears are rectified.

For households with zero declared income or those unable to meet even the lowest instalment schedule, the authority outlined alternative statutory tiers that operate outside the micro-credit mechanism. The national government fully subsidises premiums for verified indigent households—including qualifying elderly citizens, orphaned minors, and vulnerable families confirmed through the state's means-testing database.

Kenyans unable to afford insurance premiums remain legally entitled to free walk-in primary care at Level 1, 2, and 3 public and mission health facilities through the tax-funded Primary Health Fund, which supports community health promoters and basic dispensary care.

Critical medical interventions also remain insulated from premium status. Under the Emergency, Chronic and Critical Illness Fund, medical emergency stabilisation, cancer therapy, and renal dialysis are provided without requiring active contribution records.

The practical test for the authority now shifts to means-testing verification on the Afya Yangu platform, where thousands of informal workers must establish their baseline incomes before the automated standing orders take effect.
