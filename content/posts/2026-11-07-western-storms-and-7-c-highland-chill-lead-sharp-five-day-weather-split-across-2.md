A five-day atmospheric divide is set to sweep across Kenya starting Sunday, November 8, subjecting western counties to intense thunderstorms and driving nighttime temperatures in the central highlands down to single digits, while the Indian Ocean coastline remains entirely dry.

The latest five-day advisory released on Saturday by the Kenya Meteorological Service Authority (Kenya Met) tracks rain-bearing systems across 29 counties through Thursday, November 12. However, the bulletin reveals an uneven national picture: while agricultural zones in the Rift Valley and Lake Victoria basin face heavy downpours accompanied by lightning, arid northern counties will endure daytime highs touching 38°C.

### Thunderstorm belt locks in the West

The most severe precipitation is concentrated in Western Kenya and the Lake Victoria Basin. Kenya Met projects heavy, sustained rains in Kakamega, Vihiga, Bungoma, and Busia throughout the five-day window, with every spell accompanied by active thunderstorms.

![Motorists using a flooded highway during cold and rainy weather in Nairobi](https://i.ibb.co/gbr7Q0df/7e6342166575.webp)

A parallel storm corridor will affect Kisumu and Siaya, where sunny mornings are scheduled to give way to afternoon rains charged with lightning strikes on Monday, Wednesday, and Thursday. Further south along the basin, moderate showers and lightning will persist from Monday through Thursday across Homa Bay, Migori, Kisii, and Nyamira.

Similar convective storm activity will track north-to-south along the Rift Valley escarpment, impacting West Pokot, Trans Nzoia, Nandi, Elgeyo-Marakwet, Baringo, Kericho, and Bomet.

### Highland cold snap down to 7°C

Away from the western storm tracks, the weather agency flagged a sharp drop in nocturnal temperatures across the high-altitude central ridge, where light morning and afternoon showers will prevail across Nyeri, Kirinyaga, Murang’a, Kiambu, and Nyandarua.

Nyandarua will register the steepest temperature drop nationwide, with the mercury expected to plunge to 7°C by Thursday night. Laikipia is forecast to hit a chilly 11°C minimum on Monday and Thursday nights, while high-altitude Rift Valley hubs—including Nakuru, Narok, Kericho, Bomet, and Elgeyo Marakwet—will hover between 10°C and 15°C after dark.

![Floods along Waiyaki Way during rains experienced on June 1, 2024.](https://i.ibb.co/1fK6q27T/62974dc39e8c.webp)

In Nairobi, where light afternoon showers are forecast daily through Thursday, daytime highs will settle between 26°C and 27°C before retreating to nighttime minimums between 12°C and 14°C.

### Coastal strip decoupled from rainfall belt

East of the central ridge, a milder distribution of light morning rain and afternoon showers will reach Meru, Embu, Tharaka-Nithi, Kitui, Machakos, and Makueni counties over the five days.

Yet the rain systems will bypass the lower eastern seaboard entirely. The forecast establishes that the coastal belt—stretching across Mombasa, Kwale, Kilifi, and Lamu—will remain fully dry and sunny throughout the five-day window, decoupled from the moisture feeding the interior.

That coastal dryness contrasts with severe daytime thermal stress in the northern lowlands. While Nyandarua shivers at night, daytime conditions in Garissa and Turkana will remain among the hottest in the country, with peak temperatures projected to reach 37°C and 38°C respectively before the mid-week weather patterns begin to shift on Thursday.
