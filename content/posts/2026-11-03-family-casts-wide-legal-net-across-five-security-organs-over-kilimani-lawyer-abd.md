Faced with an institutional wall of silence after a daylight kidnapping just yards from a major police facility, the family of Nairobi advocate Abshir Ahmedsadak Abdi has cast a sweeping legal net across virtually every major security organ in the country.

In an urgent habeas corpus application lodged at the Milimani High Court, the family formally named five distinct state security and intelligence agencies—including the National Intelligence Service (NIS), the Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI), the National Police Service (NPS), the Counter Terrorism Bureau, and Military Intelligence—demanding they produce the missing partner from Abshir & Njagi Advocates.

![CCTV snippets of city lawyer Abshir Ahmedsadak Abdi of Abshir & Njagi Advocates abducted by armed men just outside Kilimani Police Station on November 1.](https://i.ibb.co/8D62nbxY/853a3e02a345.webp)

The extraordinary breadth of the court filing reflects mounting desperation over the November 1 incident. Newly surfaced CCTV footage captures Abdi running down the street outside Kilimani Police Station before being overtaken by an assailant brandishing an AK-47. The recording shows the lawyer being dragged to the ground, kicked in the stomach by a second accomplice, and ultimately blindfolded and drugged into a black SUV while armed spotters coordinated the extraction using a Prado TX and a double-cab vehicle.

According to preliminary reports pieced together alongside the investigation, the abduction did not occur in a vacuum. Abdi has been central to active litigation involving claims against senior police officers, enduring various intimidation tactics prior to the violent street interception that left his personal vehicle damaged and subsequently towed to Kilimani Police Station.

![CCTV snippets of City Lawyer Abshir Ahmedsadak Abdi abducted by armed men just outside Kilimani Police Station on November 1.](https://i.ibb.co/XZn1rxrT/181770de4d79.webp)

With his communication devices remaining switched off and state authorities yet to officially account for his whereabouts, the legal strategy pivots away from informal inquiries toward compelling the entire national security architecture to answer for a disappearance executed in broad daylight near a staffed police installation.

The High Court is expected to review the emergency filing to determine the immediate accountability of the named security agencies as the deadline for a formal state response approaches.
