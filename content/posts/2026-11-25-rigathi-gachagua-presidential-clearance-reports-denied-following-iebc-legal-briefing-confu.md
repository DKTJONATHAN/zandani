Former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua found himself at the center of a fast-moving political chatter storm after reports spread across national television and digital platforms claiming the electoral body had cleared him to contest the 2027 presidential election.

The surge of speculative headlines prompted a direct response from Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) Chairperson Erastus Edung Ethekon, who clarified that the commission has made no determination regarding Gachagua’s eligibility for the upcoming General Election.

The public confusion originated during an engagement between political reporters and IEBC leadership in Nairobi on Tuesday, November 24, 2026. During the session, IEBC Director of Legal Services Chrispine Owiye delivered a presentation outlining constitutional rules governing qualifications and disqualifications for election under Articles 99 and 193 of the Constitution.

Owiye explained Articles 99(3) and 193(3), noting that under standard constitutional provisions, an individual is not barred from contesting an election while active appeals or legal review proceedings remain open. He detailed the legal avenues available to prospective candidates to exhaust all statutory reviews.

That general overview of election law was quickly translated by broadcast commentators and online channels into a definitive, green-light clearance for Gachagua's potential 2027 presidential campaign.

However, the commission noted that Owiye’s presentation focused strictly on general candidate qualification rules and did not address Article 75, which explicitly governs removal from office through impeachment or the eligibility of an impeached official to run for public office.

In a statement issued to address the circulating stories, Ethekon labeled the clearance claims misleading and expressed regret over what the electoral body described as a misrepresentation of facts.

"The Commission has neither cleared Gachagua nor made any formal determination concerning his eligibility to contest the 2027 Presidential Election," Ethekon stated, urging media outlets to maintain accuracy when reporting on complex constitutional matters.

Ethekon emphasized that qualifications for presidential candidates are governed by Article 137 of the Constitution, which must be read alongside other statutory provisions on eligibility. He made it clear that any decision on candidate suitability will occur strictly during the formal nomination phase.

, the commission pointed out that candidate registration for the 2027 General Election has not commenced and will only take place within timelines stipulated by law once the election window officially opens.

Reaffirming the agency's commitment to neutrality and legal compliance, Ethekon called on members of the public to rely on official commission communication for accurate electoral updates.

"The Commission remains steadfast in discharging its constitutional mandate independently, fairly and in accordance with the law," the statement read.

With candidate registration still months away, any definitive determination regarding Gachagua’s presence on the 2027 presidential ballot remains tied to formal nomination procedures and upcoming legal interpretations.
