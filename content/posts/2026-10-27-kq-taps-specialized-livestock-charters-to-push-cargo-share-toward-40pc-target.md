Kenya Airways is leaning into high-spec niche freight to buffer its bottom line against passenger market volatility, using a maiden charter of over 50 cattle to Nigeria to test its expanded commercial cargo capabilities.

The five-hour-and-15-minute flight from Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (JKIA) to Murtala Muhammed International Airport in Lagos on Tuesday marked the carrier's first dedicated livestock shipment. Operated on a Boeing 737-800 freighter registered under tail number 5Y-KCA, the operation was managed by the airline's specialized handling arm, KQ Live, which maintains strict environmental controls including temperature regulation and cabin ventilation during transit.

![A Kenya Airways Cargo plane being loaded with cows at the Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (JKIA) on Tuesday, October 27, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/kq-cargo-cows-jkia-october-2026.jpg.webp?itok=tuuXswY1)

While airlifting live animals across regional borders represents a specialized operational task, the charter forms part of a calculated financial expansion by KQ Cargo. The airline has set a long-term target to grow its cargo market share from 11 per cent to 40 per cent, turning non-passenger freight into a core financial buffer.

Financial results released in August demonstrated the growing weight of the division within the airline's overall portfolio. KQ Cargo generated Ksh8.77 billion in revenue over the first half of the year, marking an 18 per cent increase from Ksh7.46 billion recorded in the corresponding period last year. That growth rate outpaced the carrier's broader operational expansion, contributing to a total half-year turnover of Ksh81.25 billion.

"Live animal shipments require meticulous planning, specialised handling and close coordination throughout the journey," said Fitsum Abadi, Cargo Director at Kenya Airways. "This milestone reinforces our commitment to supporting intra-African trade through flexible and reliable cargo solutions."

![Cows loaded in crates before being placed in the Kenya Airways Cargo plane at the Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (JKIA) on Tuesday, October 27, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/kq-cargo-cows-crates-jkia-october-2026.jpg.webp?itok=FMfSofyg)

To capture higher-yielding freight contracts along intra-African trade lanes, the airline has built specialized handling capabilities covering pharmaceuticals, perishables, dangerous goods, e-commerce, and live animals.

Alongside dedicated charter operations on its cargo freighters, the airline continues to rely on scheduled passenger links to maintain freight flow across key regional corridors. Kenya Airways operates four weekly direct passenger flights between Nairobi and Lagos using Boeing 787 Dreamliners, which offer regular bellyhold space for West Africa-bound freight forwarders.

With cargo revenues delivering expanding margins, the carrier's commercial focus turns to converting one-off specialized charters into recurring intra-African freight services as fleet utilization scales up.
