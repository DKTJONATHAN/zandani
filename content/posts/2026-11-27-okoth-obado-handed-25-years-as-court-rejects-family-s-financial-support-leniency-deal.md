Former Migori Governor Okoth Obado will serve 25 years in prison for the 2018 murder of Rongo University student Sharon Otieno, after the High Court firmly rejected a private financial arrangement between the former county chief and the victim’s family.

Delivering the sentence on Friday, November 27, 2026, High Court Judge Cecilia Githua ruled that private reconciliation attempts cannot override public justice. Otieno’s family had petitioned the court seeking leniency for Obado, arguing that a non-custodial outcome would allow him to continue providing financial assistance for her surviving children.

Justice Githua commended the family’s attempt to find common ground, but drew an unyielding line between private financial arrangements and the state's obligation to punish murder.

"I note that the victim’s family have placed considerable emphasis on leniency so that financial assistance can continue being given to Sharon’s children," Justice Githua stated in her ruling. "While I commend the victim’s family’s efforts towards reconciliation, it cannot displace the court’s mandate."

The judge also declined to award financial compensation to Otieno’s relatives, noting that the defense and prosecution had provided insufficient evidence to establish quantified financial loss.

Obado’s former personal assistant, Michael Juma Oyamo, was also handed a 25-year prison sentence. Former Migori County Clerk Caspal Ajwang Obiero received an 18-year term. All three sentences will factor in the time each convict has already spent in lawful custody.

The sentencing resolves a legal saga that began when Obado and his co-accused were found guilty on July 23, 2026. The three were convicted of murder with malice aforethought under Section 203 of the Penal Code for the killing of Otieno, who was seven months pregnant at the time of her death.

In arriving at the custodial terms, Justice Githua took a middle stance. The court turned down the Office of the Director of Public Prosecutions’ request for the death penalty, while simultaneously rejecting the defense’s application for non-custodial sentences.

The murder dates back to the night of September 3 and 4, 2018. Otieno and Nation Media Group reporter Barrack Oduor were abducted after leaving a hotel in Rongo. Oduor survived the ordeal by jumping from the moving vehicle, but Otieno was driven away.

Her body was later discovered dumped near Kodera Forest in Uriri, Homa Bay County. An autopsy conducted by Chief Government Pathologist Dr. Johansen Oduor revealed that Otieno died from severe hemorrhage after being strangled and stabbed multiple times.

Obado and his co-accused were first arraigned on September 24, 2018. Over the course of the long-running trial, prosecutors called 42 witnesses and submitted extensive mobile phone call records, forensic evidence, and circumstantial timelines linking the suspects to the abduction and killing.

With Friday's decision, the court marked the formal conclusion of one of the country's most high-profile murder trials, placing the former governor into prison custody to serve out his 25-year sentence.
