Ferdinand Waititu has once again pulled off the ultimate courtroom paradox: walking away with a headline-grabbing 13-year prison sentence that will not add a single extra day to his time behind bars.

The flamboyant former Kiambu Governor, whose larger-than-life political persona has kept him firmly in the public eye through years of legal battles, learned his fate on Monday, November 2, 2026, at the Milimani High Court. While the numbers read like a crushing blow on paper—a cumulative 13-year jail term or an alternative fine of Ksh 21 million—the fine print of the ruling delivered a completely different reality for the former county boss.

His wife, Susan Wangari, did not get off quite as lightly on the balance sheet. In the very same courtroom session, she was hit with heavy individual penalties totaling Ksh 22 million across three counts, placing the immediate financial strain of the long-running graft trial squarely on her shoulders.

### The Breakdown Behind the 13-Year Number

Delivering the sentence, Justice Harrison Barasa ed the penalties linked to multiple money-laundering charges stemming from the high-profile Ksh 588 million Kiambu County graft case.

On Count 10, Justice Barasa handed Waititu a five-year prison term with the option of paying a Ksh 10 million fine. The court then slapped him with four years on Count 11 and another four years on Count 12, taking the cumulative tally to 13 years.

Yet, despite the weighty total, Waititu will not serve any extended stretch in correctional facilities. Justice Barasa ruled that all three terms will run concurrently with the existing 12-year sentence Waititu received in February 2025 during an earlier corruption conviction, alongside operational directions issued by the High Court in an October 14, 2026 judgment.

“Having considered the mitigation tendered by the accused persons, the prosecution’s submissions, the nature and the gravity of the offenses, and the applicable statutory provisions, I hereby sentence the accused persons as follows,” Justice Barasa stated in his ruling.

### The Concurrent Cushion

The legal cushion keeping Waititu from stacking years on his prison calendar traces back to earlier procedural findings in the trial. In paragraph 317 of the primary judgment, Justice Rose Ougo had established that any custodial sentences arising from the money-laundering counts would run alongside those already meted out under the Anti-Corruption and Economic Crimes Act.

That legal technicality means the 13-year term effectively folds into his ongoing February 2025 custodial clock, turning what sounded like a massive escalation of jail time into an absorbed sentence.

### Susan Wangari Takes the Cash Hit

While Waititu absorbed the prison term without adding days, his wife Susan Wangari faced an unforgiving financial tally from the bench.

Sentenced alongside her husband on the same three money-laundering charges, Wangari was ordered to pay a collective Ksh 22 million across the board. Justice Barasa directed her to clear a Ksh 12 million fine on Count 10, an Ksh 8 million fine on Count 12, and an additional Ksh 2 million fine on Count 11—with an alternative two-year prison sentence should that specific count remain unpaid.

The contrasting outcomes close another dramatic chapter in the Ksh 588 million Kiambu proceedings, leaving Waititu insulated by his concurrent calendar while his household confronts an immediate Ksh 22 million demand from the state registry.
