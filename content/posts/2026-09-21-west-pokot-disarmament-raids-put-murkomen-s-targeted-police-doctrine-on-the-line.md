When the Ministry of Interior outlined its disarmament doctrine for the North Rift, the explicit operational instruction was clear: security forces were to abandon indiscriminate community sweeps in favour of intelligence-led operations targeting verified individual suspects.

Events on the ground in Pokot Central last week suggest that doctrine is fraying.

According to findings released by the Kenya National Commission on Human Rights (KNCHR) on Sunday, September 20, multi-agency units deployed to recover illicit arms in the Nasolot and Sarmach areas allegedly turned on entire settlements on September 17, assaulting residents and setting homesteads ablaze.

The allegations have triggered immediate demands for independent criminal scrutiny. KNCHR called on the Independent Policing Oversight Authority (IPOA), the Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI), and the National Police Service (NPS) to establish command responsibility for the violence and determine why civilian settlements were torched during what was sanctioned as a lawful mop-up exercise.

### The Operational Breach in Pokot Central

![KNCHR Chair Claris Awuor Ogangah](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/knchr-chair-claris-awuor-ogangah.jpg.webp?itok=rxusUsRz)

The contested raids strike at the core of Interior Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen’s stated security playbook. For months, the state has assured pastoral communities that ongoing mop-ups would steer clear of collective punishment—a legacy tactic that has historically entrenched local hostility toward state authority across the Kerio Valley basin.

Residents in Nasolot and Sarmach reported that the September 17 operation deviated sharply from that mandate. Instead of pinpoint extractions, multi-agency units allegedly subjected local households to physical violence and scorched-earth tactics, leaving families without shelter.

The human rights watchdog emphasized that voluntary disarmament programs cannot coexist with unlawful force without alienating the very informants the state relies on for tactical intelligence. In March, police authorities confirmed the recovery of 97 firearms and 193 rounds of ammunition in West Pokot, pointing to steady progress under voluntary surrender frameworks. Heavy-handed tactics risk reversing those gains by forcing armed groups back into defensive posture.

To prevent further excesses, the commission has petitioned the state to institute an institutional monitoring structure specifically tailored for multi-agency deployments. While single-service police contingents fall under direct IPOA jurisdiction, joint formations—frequently combining regular police, specialized units, and administrative security formations—often operate in an accountability gray zone where chain of command is diffused.

### Escalating Border Volatility

The fallout in Pokot Central arrives against a sharp deterioration in regional stability along the administrative boundary between West Pokot and Turkana counties.

KNCHR documented sustained clashes across border belt settlements, including Kakong, Aroo, Lokwar/Nakuse, Romos, Morita, Ompolion, and Kases. These confrontations have resulted in multiple fatalities, extensive property destruction, and the loss of hundreds of cattle to organized cattle-rustling cartels.

![A contingent of police officers gathered in Kerio Valley to hunt down bandits](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/police_dealing_with_bandits.jpg.webp?itok=YhpWQcwf)

The commercial transport corridor has also come under direct pressure. On September 11, gunmen ambushed a passenger vehicle near Kakong’u along the Lodwar–Kapenguria Highway, leaving three commuters nursing gunshot injuries. The highway assault underscored how swiftly rustling activity can spill into armed banditry targeting civilian transit lines.

### Widening Enforcement Strain Across Northern Frontiers

The commission’s alert extended well beyond the Kerio Valley corridor, signaling that multi-agency resources are stretched thin across simultaneous flashpoints:

* **Isiolo County:** KNCHR called for sustained, intelligence-led patrols following the September 16 killing of five people in Gotu. The deceased—comprising two police officers and three community elders—were ambushed while tracking cattle rustlers as part of an official search party.
* **Meru–Isiolo Frontier:** Inquiries remain open into the shooting of a police constable in Burat and the August killings of two civilians along the disputed county border line.
* **Eastern Basin (Embu):** The rights body pushed for answers regarding an armed altercation at Nembure Grounds on September 12, where two men succumbed to fatal gunshot wounds.

With political temperatures expected to rise ahead of the next electoral cycle, the commission cautioned that unchecked communal raids and heavy-handed tactical responses provide fertile ground for incitement. It urged the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) and the Registrar of Political Parties to actively monitor local leadership rhetoric across the arid and semi-arid frontier.

For residents in Sarmach and Nasolot picking through the ashes of their homes, the immediate test rests with IPOA and the DCI: establishing whether the destruction was the result of a rogue tactical unit or a systemic failure in the state's disarmament command.
