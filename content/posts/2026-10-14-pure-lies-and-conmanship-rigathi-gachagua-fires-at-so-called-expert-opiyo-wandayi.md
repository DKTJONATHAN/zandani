Rigathi Gachagua is not letting political decorum temper his vocabulary, turning his Wednesday megaphone directly onto Energy Cabinet Secretary Opiyo Wandayi and painting the former minority leader as a scripted front-man running cover for State House campaign schemes.

In a blistering public dispatch issued on Wednesday, October 14, 2026, the Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP) leader bypassed polite opposition banter to stage an aggressive preemptive strike against the government's energy managers. His central charge was as sharp as it was personal: accusing President William Ruto of engineering back-channel fuel price inflation to bankroll political vote-buying machines ahead of the 2027 General Election, while delegating the public defense to technocrats.

Rather than framing the looming pump shifts as cold macroeconomic pain, Gachagua zoomed in on Wandayi’s podium appearances, stripping away the Cabinet Secretary's policy authority with barbed relish.

### The 'So-Called Expert' Takedown

The former Deputy President reserved his sharpest vitriol for the Ministry of Energy's top seat, dismissing Wandayi’s anticipated defense of the energy markets as rehearsed political theatre rather than independent stewardship.

“That so-called expert, Mr. Opiyo Wandayi, has been instructed to call a press conference and justify the increase, citing global prices,” Gachagua said in the public statement. “Pure lies and conmanship!”

The barb targeted the delicate political tightrope Wandayi walks as a key figure tasked with presenting technical petroleum data to a public already weary of crushing utility bills. By writing off the Cabinet Secretary's planned public briefings as manufactured excuses meant to blame external crude dynamics, Gachagua sought to deny the government any cushion of economic neutrality.

### The KSh 226 Pump Math

Beyond the verbal barbs, Gachagua arrived with specific numbers, outlining what he characterized as a deliberate, politically routed levy on Kenyan motorists and commuters.

According to the DCP leader, the Head of State issued instructions for an arbitrary upward price adjustment of KSh 9 per liter on diesel and KSh 12 per liter on super petrol. Gachagua alleged that the cash generated from the retail adjustments was structured to flow through Gulf Energy to build war chests for future political patronage.

Should the figures detailed in Gachagua’s statement materialize at retail filling stations, the financial blow to ordinary road users would be instantaneous. The projected markups would propel super petrol to KSh 226.03 per liter in Nairobi, while diesel would climb sharply to KSh 226.86 per liter—erasing recent price stability and reigniting broad inflationary pressure across logistics, public transit, and household kitchens.

### Diagnosing a 'Desperate' Presiding Power

Gachagua framed the entire pricing maneuver through the personal temperament of his former running mate, depicting Ruto as an embattled leader scrambling to secure leverage as ground-level goodwill deteriorates.

He characterized the President as “desperate and angry,” arguing that mounting public weariness over economic distress, unresolved corruption scandals, and deepening security issues has left State House anxious about its re-election prospects. In Gachagua's reading of the political landscape, squeezing pump margins is a desperate funding mechanism born of a regime that realizes it has lost organic popular support.

The timing of Gachagua’s intervention was calibrated to maximize friction. The Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority (EPRA) is scheduled to officially gazette and publish its monthly fuel pricing review for the October 15 to November 14 pricing cycle on Wednesday evening. By placing his explosive KSh 226 projection on the national record hours before regulators sign off on the official pump caps, Gachagua ensured that every cent of EPRA's evening dispatch will be scrutinized through the prism of his vote-buying allegations.
