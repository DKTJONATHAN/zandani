According to regulatory public notices and parliamentary filings, the Capital Markets Authority (CMA) and legislative committees face renewed scrutiny over digital financial oversight after an unlicensed offshore entity froze account withdrawals, trapping the savings of over 12,000 Kenyan investors.

The fallout primarily impacts public sector employees, particularly teachers who were recruited through informal workplace networks, with many leveraging salary loans to meet entry thresholds.

In a public warning statement issued after investors reported locked funds, the Capital Markets Authority explicitly flagged the scheme and its local promotional channels, adding the entity to a growing advisory listing.

“The Capital Markets Authority (the Authority) wishes to caution the public against several entities purporting to offer investment services in Kenya without the requisite licensing or approval,” the regulator stated, confirming that 15 unauthorized entities were actively operating in the domestic market without required regulatory clearance.

The crisis escalated after the U.S.-linked platform abruptly suspended capital disbursements, citing compliance with the United States PATRIOT Act and rules attributed to the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (FINRA)-a regulator with which the platform maintains no legitimate affiliation. Subsequent demands requiring users to deposit additional capital to unlock existing balances signaled classic indicators of a fraudulent withdrawal freeze.

Marketed locally under the banner of Global Investment Group, the operation required a minimum entry deposit of approximately Ksh65,000. Promoters enticed participants with promises of guaranteed daily yields derived from automated copy-trading, a mechanism designed to replicate the trading activities of seasoned international asset managers.

The scheme spread through peer-to-peer workplace referrals. In several instances, educators introduced colleagues within school staff rooms, prompting participants to secure commercial salary loans to raise the mandatory initial capital.

The regulatory timeline highlights a critical delay between early legislative warnings and formal administrative action. Matungulu Member of Parliament Stephen Mule formally petitioned the National Assembly’s Finance and National Planning Committee to probe the ownership structure and operational control of the platform following initial participant complaints.

Despite the legislative query, formal public intervention by the market regulator occurred weeks later when the CMA added the entity to its unlicensed register, leaving thousands of investors exposed to unrecoverable debt and frozen capital.
