According to formal notifications published by the National Land Commission (NLC), the national government has initiated compulsory acquisition proceedings across five counties, clearing the path for the Ministry of Defence and regional transport agencies to absorb hundreds of hectares of privately held land into strategic public installations.

The statutory process, executed under Section 110 of the Land Act through gazette notices dated October 30, hands the Kenya Defence Forces (KDF) critical operational footprints in the northern frontier and the Rift Valley, while simultaneously securing extensive rights-of-way for trade and transport corridors along the coast and western transit belts.

### Defence Footprint Expands in Wajir and Nakuru

Under the defence schedules, the NLC designated private acreage in Wajir County to facilitate the construction of a designated military air installation. The gazette notice marks Title Deed Number Wajir Township Block 2/1424, measuring approximately 0.8401 hectares, for state takeover on behalf of the Ministry of Defence.

![An image of a KDF helicopter rescue mission drill.](https://i.ibb.co/VKhBrTk/b8d4add16bb4.webp)

“NOTICE is given that the National Land Commission intends to acquire parcels of land shown in the schedule hereunder on behalf of the Ministry of Defence in accordance with section 110 of the Land Act for Proposed Wajir Air Base Land Acquisition: Title Deed Number Wajir Township Block 2/1424 in Wajir County,” the commission stated.

The northern acquisition runs parallel to another military expansion in Nakuru County. There, the commission has scheduled valuation inquiries to absorb five acres of land adjoining Kenyatta Barracks, formalizing the consolidation of defence buffers around established military compounds.

### LAPSSET and Western Corridors Claim Major Acreage

While the defence acquisitions focus on tactical operational space, the largest land absorption targets the Lamu Port-South Sudan-Ethiopia-Transport (LAPSSET) Corridor. In Lamu County, the NLC has scheduled formal inquiries into two massive parcels collectively measuring 303.55 hectares.

The Lamu holdings, registered to multiple private entities, comprise distinct tracts of 84.65 hectares and 218.9 hectares. State inquiries on these properties will scrutinize both the propriety of the acquisitions and the final financial compensation due to the titleholders.

Infrastructure acquisitions have similarly been scheduled across the South Rift and Nyanza:

![Lamu port under construction, a part of the LAPSSET projects](https://i.ibb.co/67S8ftLx/c10eebe5c718.webp)

* **Kaplong-Kisii Highway:** Four land parcels totalling 2.6588 hectares across Kisii and Bomet counties have been earmarked for the construction of the Mugenu Bridge.
* **Kilgoris-Longorian Road:** An additional 1.2523 hectares has been designated for the Mahor River Project to protect bridge and drainage works along the corridor.

### Grassroots Valuation Hearings and Landowner Demands

To manage the displacement and compensation schedules, the NLC has decentralized the initial stages of inquiry, directing that proceedings take place within selected chiefs’ offices in the host localities. 

However, the commission has placed a strict procedural burden on affected landowners ahead of the scheduled hearings. To substantiate compensation awards, property owners must submit detailed documentation prior to the sitting dates, including original ownership titles, personal identification documents, verified Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA) PIN certificates, and certified bank details.

The upcoming sittings will determine whether private claims withstand legal and land-registry scrutiny before the Treasury disburses compensation awards for the surrendered acreage.
