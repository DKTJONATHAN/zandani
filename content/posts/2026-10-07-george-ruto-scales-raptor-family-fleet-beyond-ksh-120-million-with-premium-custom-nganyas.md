George Ruto is rapidly transforming what began as a personal passion project into one of Nairobi’s most heavily capitalized private transport operations, scaling his "Raptor Family" fleet to 17 customized minibuses. 

The aggressive expansion, which saw six new high-spec 33-seaters integrated into the 98 Sacco last month, represents a capital investment running into tens of millions of shillings. In a transit market where visual appeal directly dictates passenger volumes, the fleet is leveraging premium street-culture designs to capture two of Nairobi's most lucrative commuter corridors.

### The Math Behind the Custom Upgrades

Operating a standard commuter service in Nairobi is a capital-intensive venture, but George’s fleet has chosen to operate at the premium end of the scale. 

A factory-standard Isuzu 33-seater minibus starts at approximately KSh 6.8 million. However, to compete in Nairobi's competitive "nganya" subculture, vehicles must undergo extensive post-purchase modifications. 

Each of George’s newly unveiled vehicles is estimated to have cost upwards of KSh 7 million once customized. This puts the combined value of the six minibuses introduced in late September at more than KSh 42 million, while the entire 17-vehicle fleet represents a cumulative value well exceeding KSh 120 million. 

The modifications are extensive. The vehicles feature hand-painted graffiti artwork, bucket seats, bespoke rims, LED mood lighting, and high-fidelity sound systems. One of the fleet's flagship units, named *Mood*, features integrated DJ decks installed directly inside the passenger cabin to cater to younger commuters.

### Veteran Hands on High-Spec Wheels

While the fleet is heavily branded around youth culture, the day-to-day operations rely on experienced transit workers to protect the heavy capital investment. 

Robin Ondieki, a veteran driver with 15 years of experience on Nairobi’s chaotic transit corridors, was hired to operate one of the six newly launched 33-seaters. Speaking on the street, Ondieki noted that the high-spec vehicles have stabilized operations and improved customer relations since hitting the road.

"The experience so far has been great," Ondieki said. "We have been dealing with customers well. I am grateful because it has created job opportunities, and now the youth can benefit."

Ondieki’s vehicle operates under 98 Sacco, running routes that connect Nairobi's Central Business District to Embakasi and Ongata Rongai—two of the city's most densely populated and intensely contested commuter paths.

### From Passion Project to Corporate Fleet

George’s interest in the matatu sector is not new. He has previously traced his interest in the industry back to his school days, though he did not enter the business as an owner until 2025. 

He began with a single vehicle named *Raptor*, which established the "Raptor Family" brand identity. Within less than two years, that single vehicle has multiplied into a fleet of 17, with an additional six vehicles reportedly in the pipeline for assembly and customization.

The growing lineup of vehicles features highly stylized names designed to resonate on the streets, including *Money Fest*, *Mood*, *Matrix*, and *Manifest*. 

The public rollout of the six newest vehicles on September 19, 2026, in Nairobi’s CBD highlighted the scale of the operation. The launch event featured a traditional religious dedication, where a pastor sprinkled holy water over the newly customized minibuses before they were cleared to pick up their first passengers. 

With six more vehicles currently in production, the Raptor Family is positioned to become one of the dominant private transport brands operating on Nairobi's southern and eastern routes.
