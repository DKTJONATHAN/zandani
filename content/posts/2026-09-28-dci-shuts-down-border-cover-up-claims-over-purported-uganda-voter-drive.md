The Directorate of Criminal Investigations has moved to dismantle claims of an internal cover-up within its ranks, flatly denying that any of its detectives intercepted electoral gear along the Uganda border or suffered disciplinary transfer for probing cross-border voter fraud.

In a rejoinder issued on Monday, September 28, the investigative agency pushed back against reporting published by *The Standard* and broadcast by KTN News, which alleged that security personnel had uncovered a clandestine operation moving statutory registration materials into Uganda.

At the center of the dispute is an investigative exposé asserting that Kenyan biometric voter registration kits were ferried across the border from Trans Nzoia County to enroll Ugandan citizens who had secured Kenyan national identity cards. The media report further stated that a local detective who halted a vehicle transporting the equipment was ordered to drop the inquiry under duress and subsequently reassigned to DCI headquarters in Nairobi.

DCI rejected every operational detail of the alleged interception, maintaining that no such border roadblock occurred, no suspects were processed, and no personnel changes were executed to suppress an inquiry.

"There is no such officer, no such arrest as portrayed and no such transfer linked to any electoral investigation as described in the report. The publication is therefore misleading," the agency said in an official statement.

### Disputing the Trans Nzoia Interception

The media reports detailed an illicit logistics pipeline in Trans Nzoia County, asserting that Kenya Integrated Election Management System (KIEMS) kits and supplementary Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) materials had crossed into neighboring territory. According to the broadcast, the covert operation aimed to artificially pad voter rolls ahead of the 2027 General Election by issuing Kenyan identification to foreign nationals before booking them onto the voter register.

The reports claimed that three individuals traveling in an intercept vehicle were briefly detained and questioned before senior directives intervened, leading to the alleged punitive redeployment of the investigating officer.

By categorically denying the existence of the officer and the incident itself, the DCI sought to sever its operational footprint from the claims. The Directorate characterized the narrative as fabricated, pointing out that an interdiction of that magnitude—involving border security, restricted electoral hardware, and multiple state departments—would have generated verifiable administrative and custodial records.

### The Timing of Electoral Integrity Disputes

The clash exposes heightened institutional sensitivity as preparations for the 2027 polls get underway. The DCI specifically took issue with the timing of the broadcast, noting that claims touching on cross-border manipulation risk eroding public trust in statutory electoral frameworks long before official campaigns take shape.

"These allegations are false, inaccurate and wholly without foundation. Of particular concern is the timing. The report has been released as the country begins preparations for the 2027 General Elections," the Directorate stated.

The agency noted that reports purveying unverified interdictions by law enforcement threaten to inflame border communities and sow early mistrust around identity documentation processes. Trans Nzoia has historically operated as a politically sensitive frontier zone, where cross-border trade, shared kinship across the boundary, and dual-community residency frequently invite heightened scrutiny during national registration drives.

With the DCI rejecting any record of the vehicle interception or retaliatory transfers, the dispute shifts attention to the documentation underpinning the border claims. As administrative agencies prepare boundary logistics for the upcoming electoral cycle, the standoff leaves the veracity of the cross-border voter allegations locked in an escalating contest between media disclosures and official security denials.
