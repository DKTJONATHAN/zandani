Tumi Mathenge and Jakes Nyanjom carry some of the heaviest legacy names in Kenyan entertainment history, but their strategy for navigating the public eye involves trading high-profile glamour for deliberate, domestic ordinariness.

During a joint appearance on Homeboyz Radio—where Jakes co-hosts alongside presenter Chunkz—the two offered their clearest acknowledgment yet of their relationship. However, rather than leaning into the dramatic reveals typical of modern digital creators, they pivoted to a quieter defense mechanism: the mundane details of home life.

When pushed by Chunkz about their frequent online appearances and hand-holding, Tumi kept her response coy but cooperative. 

"We are doing well, that is true, or what do you think?" she said.

Jakes immediately mirrored the stance, throwing the question back to his co-host. 

"We are doing well, or what do you think? When you see us online, what do you think?"

This tag-team coyness served as a prelude to a more telling reveal of how they spend their time away from the social media spotlight. Challenged by Chunkz to settle dating speculations on air, Jakes brushed off the pressure with a joke, comparing the idea of public affection to watching one's own parents show intimacy. Instead, he steered the conversation toward their private routine.

According to the pair, their relationship is built around low-key domestic activities. Jakes revealed they spend time playing FIFA together, adding, "I teach her." Tumi, the daughter of veteran musicians Nameless (David Mathenge) and Wahu Kagwi, shared that their dates often consist of watching movies and cooking at Jakes’ house, taking a moment to publicly praise his culinary skills.

This insistence on normalcy acts as a calculated shield. In Kenya's fast-paced entertainment scene, relationships involving second-generation public figures are often consumed and dissected rapidly by digital audiences. By framing their bond around video games, home-cooked meals, and quiet afternoons, Tumi and Jakes are actively resisting the pressure to perform their relationship for the cameras.

Yet, they have not entirely shied away from playful public markers. The speculation surrounding them reached a peak when Tumi gifted Jakes a customized Manchester United football jersey for his birthday. The back of the shirt featured the bold declaration, "I Love My Girlfriend"—a cheeky, direct gesture that allowed them to define the terms of their closeness on their own timeline.

The stakes are naturally higher because of their respective family trees. Tumi’s parents, Nameless and Wahu, have been the gold standard of Kenyan celebrity marriages for two decades. On the other side, Jakes is the son of Joyce Gituro, a highly respected veteran of Kenyan radio and media.

Instead of keeping these formidable parental figures at a distance, Tumi and Jakes have integrated them into their public narrative. Nameless has previously shared lighthearted videos where he playfully "vets" Jakes as a suitor for his daughter. Similarly, Joyce Gituro has maintained an active, supportive presence in their public digital spaces.

By involving their parents in these controlled, humorous snippets, the couple has managed to disarm the potential gossip storm. Rather than letting tabloids frame their relationship as a rebellion or a secret, they have turned parental approval into part of the content.

By choosing kitchen dates over club appearances and playful on-air deflections over dramatic confirmations, Tumi and Jakes are showing a new way forward for Kenya's media dynasties: keeping the public curious, but keeping the actual relationship firmly behind closed doors.
