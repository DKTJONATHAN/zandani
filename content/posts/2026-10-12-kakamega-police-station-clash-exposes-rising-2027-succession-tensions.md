The grounds of Kakamega Central Police Station have become the latest frontline for political jockeying in Western Kenya, after a high-stakes petition delivery by the Democracy for Citizens Party (DCP) degenerated into tear gas, live rounds fired into the air, and a scramble for safety by journalists.

The confrontation on Monday, October 12, erupted shortly after a delegation led by DCP Secretary General Cleophas Malala and lawyer Ken Echesa arrived at the station. The group intended to formally lodge grievances accusing Kakamega Governor Fernandes Barasa of orchestrating a campaign of political intimidation and violence against opponents.

![Screengrabs of heavy police presence inside Kakamega Central Police Station, with DCP Secretary General Cleophas Malala matching inside the station to present a petition against Ka](https://i.ibb.co/rKgHBkRp/ef0b920a1f15.webp)

Instead of receiving the document administratively, security personnel responded with tear gas canisters and aerial gunfire to disperse the activists and leaders. During the ensuing chaos, journalists covering the standoff found their equipment temporarily seized before Malala intervened to secure its return.

At the heart of the confrontation is a deteriorating security environment in the county ahead of the August 2027 general elections. Malala, who is positioning himself to challenge Barasa for the gubernatorial seat, presented claims that more than 300 individuals have been recruited and trained as a political militia to target rivals, burn homes, and terrorize local residents. To support the petition, the DCP leadership displayed photographic evidence of damaged property and injured citizens to the press.

“We have presented a public petition to the police leadership in Kakamega in what seems to be runaway political violence, goonship and intimidation of the people from compromised officers,” Malala stated, pointing directly at alleged institutional protection of the county leadership by local law enforcement and the Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI).

![Snippets of violence inside Kakamega Central Police Station after DCP leaders led by Secretary General Cleophas Malala stormed the station o present a petition accusing Governor Fe](https://i.ibb.co/Xf7myKCB/84bda3dfb897.webp)

The friction at the station also underscores a parallel battleground over online expression. Malala used the briefing to protest the recent arrest of Nairobi-based blogger Anyama wa Anyama, which followed a contentious social media exchange linked to the rivalry between the gubernatorial hopeful and the incumbent. DCP leaders argued that criminal proceedings are being weaponized to muzzle digital creators and suppress criticism of the county administration.

With Barasa set to defend his seat on an Orange Democratic Movement (ODM) ticket and Malala mounting his challenge under the DCP banner, the confrontation signals an increasingly volatile political season in Kakamega. Whether state investigative agencies will act on the militia allegations or if security installations will remain flashpoints for partisan skirmishes remains an open question as the 2027 polls draw closer.
