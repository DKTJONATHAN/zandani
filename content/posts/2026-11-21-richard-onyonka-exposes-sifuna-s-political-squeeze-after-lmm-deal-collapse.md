Kisii Senator Richard Onyonka has pulled back the curtain on the internal mechanics of the Linda Mwananchi Movement (LMM), revealing how secret leadership deals left Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna navigating an unworkable political dilemma before the outfit's coalition plans fell apart.

Speaking during an interview on NTV, Onyonka disclosed that the movement’s inner circle had finalized key leadership roles behind closed doors long before public friction disrupted the strategy. Under the agreed blueprint, Sifuna was positioned to assume executive control as party leader, while Embakasi East MP Babu Owino was earmarked as the outfit’s preferred candidate for the Nairobi gubernatorial race.

The strategy relied on building a broader coalition structure where affiliated parties would line up behind Babu’s city hall bid while propelling Sifuna into top-tier national politics.

"We had agreed Sifuna would be the party leader. We had agreed, and there were discussions taking place about which role Orengo was going to play," Onyonka said during the television appearance, referring to former Siaya Governor James Orengo. "We had agreed Babu would be our candidate in Nairobi and we were supporting him as the candidate for governor and having all these other affiliated parties because we know we are going to form a coalition."

However, Onyonka emphasized that as the overarching arrangements unravelled, Sifuna was left in a tactical bind. The collapse threatened to force the Nairobi senator into a high-stakes national campaign without an operational political outfit under his direct leadership.

"Edwin Sifuna was being boxed into a corner for him to run for president without having an outfit that allows him to be the party leader," Onyonka stated. "Now, honestly, that’s not possible."

Despite public speculation pointing to internal power struggles among LMM's heavyweights, Onyonka insisted that the core leadership was aligned on the distribution of titles. Instead, he pointed to external optics and public interpretation as the catalyst that dismantled the alliance.

"For us, we were very comfortable," Onyonka remarked. "But then the thing again, public perception, interpretation blew it up."

The senator's disclosures offer a rare accounting of the informal pacts that shaped LMM’s pre-election strategy ahead of the 2027 polls. Rather than a lack of consensus among senior members, Onyonka framed the outfit's collapse as an example of how public commentary and outside readings can override private boardroom consensus.

With the initial machinery fractured and Sifuna’s party leadership platform unresolved, the key players in the movement are now left navigating their individual political trajectories as they recalculate their party vehicle strategies for the next general election.
