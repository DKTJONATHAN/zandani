Foreign visitors heading to Kenya can no longer rely on commercial international travel policies to clear immigration, after the government mandated direct digital health coverage purchases exclusively through the state’s online portals.

The restriction, highlighted in an advisory released on Thursday, October 22, by the Kenyan Embassy in Abu Dhabi, firmly closes the door on private overseas underwriters. Under the directive, existing travel covers issued abroad are void for entry purposes, leaving foreign travellers with no option but to purchase domestic policies directly through the electronic Travel Authorisation (eTA) system or the eCitizen portal.

The digital gatekeeping mechanism builds on border rules that took effect on October 5, 2026. The policy applies universally to non-Kenyan visitors entering the country for stays under 12 months, shifting the compliance burden directly to airline departure counters where boarding clearance hinges on state-verified documentation.

### Per-Traveller Quotas and Minors

![Cars going through security at the main entrance of the Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (JKIA) in Nairobi, October 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/jkia-nairobi-october-2026.jpg.webp?itok=zV46oy81)

Under the framework, foreign policies cannot be pooled or extended across family units through umbrella guarantees. Every non-Kenyan visitor, including infants and minors, must hold an individual, standalone electronic health insurance cover purchased via the designated state platforms.

Once payment clears on the eCitizen platform, authorities generate an electronic insurance certificate valid for 90 days. The state-brokered policy accommodates multiple entries into Kenya, provided the document remains within its active validity period on each respective arrival date.

Airlines operating international routes into Kenya have been positioned as the frontline enforcement mechanism. The embassy warned that travellers must hold active digital verification documents at check-in desks abroad, as carriers are instructed to verify individual policies prior to issuing boarding passes, with immigration officers repeating the inspection upon touchdown.

### The Exemption Grid

![An undated photo of a airplane window.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/airplane-2588202_1920.jpg.webp?itok=COaGIbBo)

While the requirement is enforced across general foreign visitors regardless of departure point, authorities have carved out distinct statutory exemptions to shield regional commerce and diaspora movement.

Kenyan citizens and individuals holding verified Kenyan residence status are exempt from purchasing the inbound policy. The waiver also applies to citizens of East African Community (EAC) partner states, registered asylum seekers, and foreign delegations travelling on diplomatic, official, or service passports. Flight crews and air passengers remaining in international transit areas without crossing border controls also avoid the levy.

The regulatory net, however, tightens around non-EAC African nationals. Visitors from African states outside the EAC trade bloc who enter Kenya under visa-free or non-eTA arrangements remain bound by the insurance mandate, requiring them to buy the cover via eCitizen before arrival unless protected by diplomatic status.

With commercial carriers tasked with verifying the electronic certificates prior to takeoff, arriving visitors now face strict administrative rejection at foreign boarding gates if they present third-party international policies instead of the mandated eCitizen slips.
