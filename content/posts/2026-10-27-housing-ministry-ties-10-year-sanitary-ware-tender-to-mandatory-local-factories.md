Suppliers seeking a slice of the government’s Affordable Housing Programme face an aggressive industrial policy clause: win the contract, but build a domestic manufacturing or assembly footprint within 24 months, all without any baseline guarantee on how many units the state will actually buy.

According to a procurement notice issued Tuesday, October 27, by the State Department for Housing, the ministry is seeking between three and five companies for a 10-year framework agreement to supply, deliver, and install bathroom fittings across state housing developments nationwide.

Bidders have until November 17, 2026, to submit proposals covering toilet bowls, washbasins, and assorted bathroom hardware. Yet the commercial terms underline the state's intent to use housing procurement as an industrial leverage tool rather than a standard import pipeline.

### The Two-Year Factory Rule

![affordable Houses](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/affordable%20houses.jpeg.webp?itok=ZiGohwJq)

Under the specifications of the framework, the ministry requires successful firms to establish local assembly or full-scale manufacturing facilities in Kenya within two years of signing the deal. 

Beyond simply delivering ceramics and hardware to building sites, winning vendors will be required to handle the full installation cycle: manufacturing, assembling, delivering, testing, and commissioning the bathroom fixtures, followed by ongoing maintenance obligations across project sites.

The framework is calibrated to anchor the Kenya Kwanza administration's stated construction goals. The ministry reiterated its target of delivering 200,000 affordable residential units each year, aiming for an aggregate footprint of 1,000,000 homes over the next five years.

### Zero Quota Guarantees

While the 10-year window offers long-term standing on the ministry's approved supplier register, the contract explicitly shifts volume risks onto private balance sheets. The State Department warned bidders that securing a slot on the panel does not translate into guaranteed order volumes.

Requisitions will be triggered strictly by project-level demand as ground works advance across various counties. For standard batches, the state reserves the right to issue direct call-off orders using the fixed unit prices submitted in the initial bids.

![President William Ruto pushes a wheelbarrow during the handover of houses at the Pioneer Affordable Housing Estate in Eldoret, Uasin Gishu County, October 18, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/ruto-pushing-wheelbarrow-affordable-housing-eldoret-october-2026.jpg.webp?itok=7Z124a_j)

When projects require large bulk deliveries, however, the government will pit panel members against each other. In those instances, the ministry will run secondary mini-competitions among at least three empaneled suppliers to secure the lowest feasible unit pricing for specific estates.

That mechanism means vendors investing in local manufacturing lines will continually compete on margins throughout the decade-long tenure, balancing their plant overheads against project-by-project purchase orders.

### Submission Window

The procurement drive comes as the state hands over initial completed phases to buyers across select counties while ground breaking continues on secondary sites.

Prospective bidders have three weeks to structure their technical and financial submissions. The tender documents have been made accessible without charge through the State Department for Housing’s official portal and the Public Procurement Information Portal (PPIP), with the submission window shutting at the mid-November deadline.
