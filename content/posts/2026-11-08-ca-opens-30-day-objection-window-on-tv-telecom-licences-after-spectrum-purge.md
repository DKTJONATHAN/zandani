Kenya’s broadcast regulator has initiated a 30-day public objection window for five television broadcasters and one radio operator seeking national permits, opening fresh scrutiny of new airwave entries weeks after expelling dozens of non-compliant stations.

In a gazette notice published on Friday, November 6, the Communications Authority of Kenya (CA) gazetted a roster of commercial firms and institutions petitioning for fresh authorization or renewing operational permits under the Kenya Information and Communications Act (CAP 411A).

The review arrives at a sensitive transition point for the country's spectrum management. Just weeks earlier, the authority revoked the licences of more than 22 television stations—predominantly free-to-air and faith-based channels—citing breaches of regulatory requirements and clearing congested frequencies.

### The Applicant Roster

![Communications Authority of Kenya](https://i.ibb.co/0yV8Jdpd/fe31766edf7c.webp)

The gazetted applicants are dominated by television operators, with five stations seeking entry or continuity. Four of the applications focus on commercial free-to-air broadcasting, while one provider has petitioned for the renewal of a subscription broadcast licence.

Among those seeking regulatory re-authorization is Kenyatta University, which has formally lodged an application to renew its permit for Kenyatta University Television (KUTV). Private investors are also angling for commercial TV allocations and a single radio transmission permit, alongside several prospective internet service providers and courier operators.

Beyond consumer media, the regulator placed eight infrastructure firms under review for licences to build and lease telecommunications infrastructure—a capital-intensive segment critical to expanding mobile backhaul and broadband delivery across rural counties.

### Statutory Objection Architecture

![A TV studio](https://i.ibb.co/3YPbWRZT/3762109b3b67.webp)

Under Section 411A, the authority is legally bound to invite public input before issuing broadcast and carrier licences, acknowledging that newly deployed frequencies and utility works impact local authorities, competing commercial entities, and nearby communities.

"The licences, if granted, will enable the applicants to operate and provide the services as indicated," the regulator stated in the statutory notice, cautioning that the operations could affect institutional and local stakeholders across the country.

By subjecting both established institutional stations like KUTV and untested commercial entrants to public objection, the CA effectively tests whether the applicant firms carry unpaid regulatory fees, commercial disputes, or municipal conflicts.

Members of the public, competing media houses, and local authorities have until the close of the 30-day window to submit formal, written representations or objections directly to the Director General of the Communications Authority. If no substantive challenges are sustained, the regulator will proceed to finalize spectrum assignment and operational certificates for the cleared applicants.
