President William Ruto has laid down a sweeping manufacturing directive targeting Kenya’s entire student population, ordering local footwear makers to gear up for a massive nationwide rollout of school shoes.

Speaking at the Kenyatta International Convention Centre (KICC) during the Innovation Summit, the Head of State framed the push around the country's vast educational demographics. Rather than relying on scattered imports or fragmented supply lines, the directive places guaranteed state-backed demand squarely onto domestic tanneries and shoe factories to clothe the feet of 14 million school-going children.

The administrative strategy hinges on decentralizing production away from Nairobi's traditional industrial zones. Officials are looking to anchor the supply chain in regional processing hubs, anchored by the Ksh 5.8 billion Kenya Leather Industrial Park situated in Kenani, Machakos County. Unveiled in September by Deputy President Kithure Kindiki, the facility features modern tanning and manufacturing infrastructure designed to handle large-scale commercial output.

This classroom-focused directive follows a similar protectionist playbook deployed earlier in the year. In June, the administration issued a strict ban on foreign military and police boots, a policy that successfully transitioned all security agency footwear sourcing to domestic producers by October.

Despite sitting on massive livestock wealth—ranking third in Africa and eleventh globally with an estimated 80 million animals—Kenya's actual trade footprint in finished leather has historically lagged far behind its raw potential. World Bank and Kenya Leather Development Council records place the country 87th globally in leather apparel exports, netting a modest Ksh 68 million ($527,000), while importing Ksh 285 million ($2.2 million) worth of foreign leather goods.

By routing the daily footwear needs of 14 million learners straight into local production lines, the administration aims to correct those trade deficits while creating youth employment along regional satellite ecosystems.
