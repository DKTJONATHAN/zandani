A persistent administrative loophole between devolved county revenue departments and national drug safety regulators is enabling uninspected retail pharmacies to open across the country, creating an unmonitored distribution channel for falsified prescription medicines.

The Pharmaceutical Society of Kenya (PSK) is now demanding an immediate halt to the practice of chemist shops launching retail operations backed only by county single-business permits. While municipal trading licenses fulfill local taxation and commercial registration mandates, professional pharmacists caution that they offer zero verification of medical storage integrity, qualified personnel, or legitimate sourcing.

Under current decentralized arrangements, some retail chemists secure local trading licenses and open their doors without securing clearance from the Pharmacy and Poisons Board (PPB). The society warns that this allows operators to bypass the clinical and premises audits that serve as the public’s primary defense against adulterated drugs.

### High-Risk Injections Breach the Market

![Members of the Pharmaceutical Society of Kenya gather to mark World Pharmacists Day, September 25, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/whysjLNn/c6e9e3b28c51.webp)

The urgency behind the professional body's push follows a concrete clinical threat intercepted on Monday, September 28. The PPB issued an alert confirming that counterfeit batches of Lucentis (Ranibizumab) 0.3mg injections had infiltrated Kenyan supply networks, detected during ongoing post-marketing surveillance sweeps.

Lucentis is an advanced biologic administered via direct ophthalmic injection to prevent irreversible vision loss from retinal conditions. Because the drug targets delicate intraocular tissues, any variation in chemical composition, sterility, or cold-chain maintenance presents an immediate danger of infection, severe inflammatory reactions, or permanent blindness. 

When retail outlets operate outside statutory regulatory oversight, the cold-chain storage equipment, temperature logs, and supplier invoices required to protect such sensitive treatments routinely go unexamined.

### The Post-Opening Deficit

PSK’s primary operational concern rests on what happens after a retail pharmacy begins operating behind a local commercial license. Devolved single-business permits treat chemist shops identically to general retail enterprises, offering no mechanism to audit who dispenses the prescriptions or how regulated compounds are handled.

Without direct board clearance before commissioning, inspectors cannot verify whether premises employ registered pharmacists or pharmaceutical technologists, or whether inventory is acquired from verified distributors. The society points out that holding a county permit does not confirm that stock on the shelves has passed national regulatory testing, leaving patients exposed once counterfeit packaging reaches the display cabinets.

![A photo collage of drugs on shelves in a pharmacy and an agrovet](https://i.ibb.co/5hD6jdkJ/ecca836c3c51.webp)

Addressing the Kenya Healthcare Federation during preparatory sessions for the Kenya Public Health International Conference (KEPHIC) 2026, Public Health Principal Secretary Mary Muthoni acknowledged the escalating threat of rogue distribution channels.

“We have advised members of the public to avoid medicines sold through unverified websites, social media pages, informal traders or other unauthorised outlets, and also check the packaging to ensure the drugs are safe,” Muthoni said, pointing to unvetted digital sellers and unregulated street kiosks that mirror the oversight gaps seen in unlicensed physical storefronts.

### Enforcing the Clearance Line

While the Ministry of Health continues to urge patients to scrutinize serial numbers and packaging details, pharmacists argue that the responsibility for drug safety cannot be shifted to consumers buying life-saving medication at the retail counter.

The regulatory battle now turns on closing the space between municipal revenue offices and the PPB’s enforcement arm. Unless county administrations mandate formal clearance from the national pharmacy regulator before generating commercial operating licenses, unvetted storefronts will continue to offer counterfeit syndicates a low-risk point of sale.
