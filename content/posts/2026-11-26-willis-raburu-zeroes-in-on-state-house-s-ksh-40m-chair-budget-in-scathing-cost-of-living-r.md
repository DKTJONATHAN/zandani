Media personality Willis Raburu has singled out State House’s multi-million-shilling request for office furniture, turning a micro-detail in parliamentary budget filings into a sharp critique of government spending choices.

Reacting to filings submitted to lawmakers, Raburu questioned why the executive priority list includes tens of millions for new seating while ordinary Kenyans battle persistent surges in electricity and fuel expenses.

“Stima Juu, fuel juu, cost of living Juu, but please, do we need new chairs? Cmon! This is crazy! This regime is heartless, brutal and completely tone deaf!” Raburu stated in a public post.

His intervention focused on details presented by State House Comptroller Katoo Ole Metito during appearances before the National Assembly’s Departmental Committee on Administration and Internal Security. Lawmakers are currently deliberating on the 2027 Budget Policy Statement for the 2027/2028 financial year.

According to documentation submitted by Metito, State House has requested KSh 43.4 million for office furniture against a current Treasury allocation of just KSh 3 million. The shortfall leaves a KSh 40.4 million deficit for seating and office furnishings alone—the exact figure that drew Raburu’s ire.

The furniture request is part of a larger KSh 8.42 billion top-up that State House is seeking from the National Treasury to bridge its overall operational gap. While the Office of the President was earmarked KSh 15.1 billion for the 2027/2028 financial year, administrators told the committee that running executive functions effectively requires KSh 23.6 billion.

Beyond office chairs, the breakdown presented to MPs highlights several capital expenditure targets:

* **KSh 526.5 million** requested to refurbish and upgrade State House Nairobi alongside regional State Lodges nationwide.
* **KSh 124.9 million** allocated for specialized communication and technology equipment upgrades.
* **KSh 33.8 million** slated for specialized plant, machinery, and operational equipment.

Defending the renovation figures before the departmental committee, Metito argued that regional State Lodges across the country have suffered from years of underfunding and require urgent structural maintenance to remain functional for official presidential duties.

For Raburu, however, the financial scale of those institutional upgrades sits in direct tension with the daily friction felt by consumer households. By isolating the furniture deficit from the broader KSh 8.4 billion envelope, the former television presenter framed the request as an example of executive detachment from public economic reality.

The budget proposal remains before the National Assembly committee, where lawmakers will determine whether to approve the additional KSh 8.42 billion allocation or force State House to work within its baseline Treasury ceiling.
