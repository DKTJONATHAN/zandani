Moving school transport from standard public service compliance into the exclusive bracket of legally prioritized traffic would require a fundamental rewrite of Kenya's road right-of-way framework.

Speaking on the floor of the National Assembly on Wednesday, September 30, Githunguri MP Gathoni Wamuchomba argued that current road conditions expose learners to severe transit delays and hazards. The lawmaker pressed for school buses to be accorded the same legal right of way currently reserved for emergency vehicles, fire engines, police units, and high-ranking state officials' convoys.

![School buses awaiting inspection by NTSA officials](https://i.ibb.co/j9z0yv89/8c77a06771b2.webp)

The proposal follows travel disruptions experienced by student groups visiting Parliament, throwing light on the daily friction school transporters face when navigating congested transport corridors against aggressive public transport operators.

Under current Kenyan legislation, the legal hierarchy of the road is strictly defined. Rule 83 of the Traffic Rules, issued pursuant to the Traffic Act, mandates all motorists to immediately yield, pull to the near side, and halt upon hearing warning gongs or sirens from emergency responders. Presidential and Cabinet-level convoys similarly enjoy formal police escort and road clearance clearances. School buses, despite carrying large numbers of children, currently share the same legal footing as conventional public service vehicles.

Wamuchomba pointed to international jurisdictions such as Canada, the United States, and South Korea, where yellow buses operate under strict legal protections that compel surrounding traffic to grant immediate right of way.

![Wamuchomba](https://i.ibb.co/8L87pHDf/e5c64dff2892.webp)

Any move to incorporate school buses into Kenya's priority framework would run parallel to existing regulatory mandates enforced by the National Transport and Safety Authority. Under the Traffic (School Transport) Rules, heavy school buses exceeding 30 feet must already feature dual red-light indicators and reflectorised mechanical stop signal arms on their right flanks.

When these vehicles halt to embark or disembark learners, drivers are statutorily required to activate flashing red lights and extend the stop arm, obliging both oncoming and trailing motorists to come to a complete stop until the signals are retracted. 

Formalizing convoy-style priority for school buses would bridge the gap between mechanical safety compliance and actual road courtesy, transforming how matatus and private motorists yield to student transit fleets across major urban and rural routes.
