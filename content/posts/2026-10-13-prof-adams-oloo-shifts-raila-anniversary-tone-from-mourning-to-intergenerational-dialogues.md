Prof. Adams Oloo has drawn a hard line under how the country remembers the veteran opposition leader, stepping up to frame the statesman's first death anniversary not as a season of draped black cloth, but as an exercise in national gratitude.

Opening the three-day calendar on Tuesday at Ofafa Memorial Hall in Kisumu, the Raila Odinga Memorial Anniversary Committee chairman signaled an intentional departure from conventional political grief. Instead of podium speeches centered solely on loss, the opening conclave brought together the Luo Council of Elders, women’s collectives, religious groups, and youth leaders for a structured review of peace, tolerance, and civic engagement.

“This is not a week of mourning alone. It is a week of gratitude,” Oloo stated in the committee's governing rollout, anchoring the commemorations in Odinga’s long-standing calls for cross-generational problem-solving.

The initial day's programming deliberately blurred political legacy with grassroots participation. Local youth took over portions of the Kisumu gathering to roll out artistic performances, musical tributes, and poetry readings tracing decades of political milestones. Running parallel to the closed-door and plenary discussions, a public blood donation drive was mounted outside the hall, pitched by organizers as a direct nod to Odinga's enduring emphasis on public welfare and community health.

From Kisumu, the multi-venue schedule shifts gears through October 15. The itinerary maps out an international candle-lighting ceremony at Kang’o ka Jaramogi, culminating on Thursday with a primary memorial gathering stationed at the Migwena Cultural Grounds in Bondo.

With elders, supporters, and regional delegations converging across the Nyanza circuit, the committee’s opening gambit has established a distinct cultural rhythm for the week—one that balances political lineage with participatory community events before the final Bondo gathering.
