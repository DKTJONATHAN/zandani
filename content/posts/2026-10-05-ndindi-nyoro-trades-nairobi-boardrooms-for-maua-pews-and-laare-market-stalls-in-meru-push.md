Ndindi Nyoro has officially traded high-level political bargaining in the capital for the dusty church steps and bustling market stalls of Meru County, accelerating his party’s national expansion blueprint ahead of the 2027 polls.

Rather than cutting deals behind closed doors in Nairobi, the People’s Party leader plunged straight into rural Meru over the weekend. His itinerary kicked off on Sunday, October 4, 2026, with a morning service at the East Africa Pentecostal Church in Maua Town, before shifting gears to an open-air public rally at Athiru Gaiti Market in Igembe South. From there, the political tour pressed further north toward Laare Town in Igembe North Constituency.

Dubbed the “People’s Tour,” the aggressive road trip is designed to bypass traditional brokerage networks and lay down formal party scaffolding at the village, ward, and constituency levels. Meru County has long operated as a volatile and critical electoral battleground in Kenyan politics, making it a high-stakes proving ground for any emergent national movement attempting to assemble a broad-based coalition.

By taking the campaign directly to local traders, rural residents, and community leaders on their home turf, party strategists are betting that deep organizational roots will translate more reliably into voter turnout than capital-city endorsements.

With political competition across the country intensifying well ahead of schedule, Nyoro’s boots-on-the-ground presence highlights a growing shift toward early retail campaigning in Kenya’s rural heartlands.
