Wicknell Chivayo may have commanded headlines across southern Africa as a flamboyant, high-spending tycoon, but inside Harare’s Johane Masowe eChishanu Apostolic Church, his identity was defined by a simpler title: Madzibaba Wicknell.

That spiritual standing took centre stage on Wednesday, October 7, 2026, as hundreds of white-garment congregants joined family, business associates, and political figures in Harare to conduct the funeral service for the late businessman and his wife, Lucy Muteke. Chivayo and Muteke died on September 30 in a helicopter crash, an incident that cut short the life of one of Zimbabwe’s most visibly lavish personalities.

While public discourse around Chivayo often revolved around his extravagant lifestyle displays and political proximity, the emotional core of Wednesday’s service rested firmly with his religious peers. Speaking during the proceedings, congregants openly wrestled with the vacuum his sudden death leaves behind within their ranks.

“Madzibaba Wicknell was the backbone of the church,” one member of the Johane Masowe eChishanu sect stated during the service, emphasizing that his “shoes will be difficult to fill.”

### The Anchor of the Sect

Chivayo’s ties to the Apostolic movement went far deeper than passive Sunday attendance. Within the Johane Masowe eChishanu fellowship, the businessman was widely recognized as a primary structural patron whose personal wealth backed church assemblies, financed welfare efforts, and sustained communal programmes.

Following the September 30 aviation tragedy, members of the sect maintained an active vigil at Chivayo’s Harare residence, assembling daily to pray with the bereaved family and perform traditional apostolic chants. On Wednesday, Vapostori clergy took formal charge of the service, conducting rites strictly aligned with their longstanding ecclesiastical traditions.

Yet the ceremony also captured the complex, dual-track standing Chivayo cultivated in life. Alongside the austere, barefoot traditions of the white-robed clergy, the state stepped in with formal political protocol. Authorities declared Chivayo a Provincial Hero, resulting in his casket being formally draped in the Zimbabwean national flag as public delegations filed through.

### Glen Forest Cemetery Resting Place

The dual pageantry set up an unprecedented visual spectacle in Harare, drawing massive crowds of ordinary citizens, church devotees, and prominent figures eager to witness the final transition of a man whose public footprint was seldom quiet.

According to arrangements confirmed by the family, Chivayo and his wife Lucy are scheduled to be laid to rest on Thursday, October 8, 2026, at Glen Forest Cemetery on the outskirts of Harare. Organizers noted that the couple will be interred side by side in separate graves, allowing the public unhindered access to witness the final graveside committal.

As the funeral cortege prepares for the final drive to Glen Forest, the apostolic community faces an uncertain transition. For the Vapostori who called him Madzibaba, the farewell is less about the passing of a socialite and entirely about losing the benefactor who held their institutional roof in place.
