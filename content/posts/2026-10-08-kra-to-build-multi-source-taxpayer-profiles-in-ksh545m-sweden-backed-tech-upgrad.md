The Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA) is preparing to integrate its siloed databases and external data streams into an automated taxpayer profiling engine, backed by a Ksh545 million funding allocation from Sweden.

The initiative, structured as the Data Warehouse and Business Intelligence Programme, represents a shift from traditional auditing to algorithmic compliance mapping. By consolidating information from various internal and external sources, the system will generate a single, comprehensive profile for individual taxpayers, making it easier for the taxman to identify anomalies and map out compliance risks.

![Sweden's Ambassador to Kenya Håkan Åkesson meets with Treasury Principal Secretary Chris Kiptoo, October 7, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/RG3FM6rx/fc8f28ba7b0a.webp)

Details of the financial and technical backing emerged on Wednesday, October 7, following a meeting in Nairobi between National Treasury Principal Secretary Chris Kiptoo and Sweden’s Ambassador to Kenya, Håkan Åkesson. The funding will be channeled through the Public Financial Management (PFM) Reform Secretariat at the National Treasury.

Rather than relying on disjointed manual audits, KRA’s upcoming platform is designed to aggregate information from diverse external databases. This consolidated view will allow the authority to run automated risk analyses, helping tax administrators identify specific risk groups and target compliance interventions more precisely.

To support the technical rollout, the Swedish Tax Agency is entering into a direct institutional cooperation agreement with KRA. Under this framework, Sweden will provide hands-on technical advice, system support, and capacity development to Kenyan tax officers as they transition to the new business intelligence tools.

![KRA](https://i.ibb.co/ycyczyPN/ea62acea6ce8.webp)

The revenue modernization project is a key component of a broader bilateral push between Nairobi and Stockholm under a newly launched 2026–2031 development strategy. This five-year framework focuses heavily on digitalization, the mobilization of private capital, and green transition initiatives.

Sweden's technical interventions are expanding across other critical Kenyan public infrastructure as well. Alongside the KRA database integration, Stockholm recently finalized a deal to modernize air traffic management systems at the Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (JKIA). That project is embedded within the government's wider Ksh154.2 billion airport modernization masterplan. Under the JKIA agreement, Swedish aviation consultancy Independent Business Group (IBG) is slated to provide airspace design, communications, navigation, surveillance infrastructure, and personnel training.

As the 2026–2031 development strategy gets underway, KRA’s transition to a centralized data warehouse will serve as a primary test of whether technical automation and bilateral intelligence sharing can systematically address Kenya's revenue collection bottlenecks.
