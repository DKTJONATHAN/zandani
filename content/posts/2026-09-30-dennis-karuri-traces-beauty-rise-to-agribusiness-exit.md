Before Dennis Karuri became one of Kenya's most recognizable faces in professional makeup and digital content creation, his life was mapped out in a completely different sector. The creator, known for his striking aesthetics and high-profile clientele, recently pulled back the curtain on a past that involved agribusiness lecture halls and the quiet monotony of a government office—a life he nearly settled for before a deep personal crisis forced a radical reinvention.

In a reflective TikTok video shared on Monday, September 28, 2026, Karuri shared a series of throwback photographs, charting the distance between his current beauty empire and his days as an agribusiness economics and food industry management student.

### From Agribusiness Lectures to the Government Desk

During his university years, Karuri’s self-perception was far removed from the beauty industry. He described his younger self as masculine and mature, with zero inclination toward cosmetics or fashion. 

"Beauty was nowhere close to anything that I thought I would have done," he admitted, reflecting on a period when a career in agricultural economics seemed his only logical destination.

That academic path eventually led him to a standard post-graduation milestone: a secure job in a government office. Yet, the safety of a public sector desk quickly transformed into an existential checkpoint. Karuri recalls the growing unease of sitting in that office, questioning whether he was prepared to trade his entire working life for bureaucratic routine. It was a career path that felt increasingly misaligned with his internal drive.

### The Weight of Grief and a Survival Crisis

But the career mismatch was only part of a much deeper, more painful personal transition. During his second year at university, Karuri suffered a devastating blow with the death of his mother.

To Karuri, his mother was more than a parent; she was his primary anchor and loudest cheerleader. She had constantly pushed him to dream beyond standard boundaries, frequently telling him that he was destined to be a superstar and would eventually build houses for her. Her sudden departure shattered his support system, plunging him into a dark period of disorientation.

The weight of that loss left Karuri feeling entirely disconnected from his identity. The grief was so profound that he admits to reaching a point where he contemplated ending his life. 

"I kind of felt like I’d lost myself for a minute," he shared, capturing the isolation of that period.

### The Makeup Chair as a Catalyst for Reinvention

His route out of that darkness came from an unexpected detour into modeling. Stepping onto runways and in front of cameras introduced him to the structural world of styling, eventually placing him in a makeup chair for the first time.

That single session, having professional makeup applied, served as a psychological reset. Karuri described the experience as a profound awakening.

"That feeling came back like I was born again," he recalled. The physical transformation on the outside sparked an internal realignment, restoring a sense of purpose that had been missing since his mother's passing.

That moment of clarity reshaped his entire trajectory. Karuri abandoned the government desk and the agribusiness blueprints to master makeup artistry. Today, he operates as a sought-after professional makeup artist, collaborating with prominent public figures and commanding a massive digital footprint.

Closing his reflection, Karuri offered a piece of hard-won advice to his followers, urging them to resist the pressure of external expectations. "Find who you are and stay authentic," he said, framing his transition not just as a career shift, but as a necessary act of survival.
