Content creator Nyathira has laid down a direct financial ultimatum to Bahati, demanding the musician refund the KSh 1 million her husband parted with to attend what had been billed as an ultra-exclusive celebrity wedding.

The demand followed a high-profile gathering on Friday, November 6, at Nairobi’s Argyle Grand Hotel, where 100 invited guests assembled under the impression that they were showing up to witness Bahati renew or formalize marital vows with his wife, Diana Marua. Instead of exchanging rings, the musician took to the microphone to unveil his political comeback, formally declaring his bid for the Mathare parliamentary seat in the 2027 General Election.

Taking to her Instagram account, Nyathira made it clear that her household did not write a seven-figure cheque to finance a political rally.

"Bahati, give back my husband his KSh 1 million or stay with him," she stated in a video published to her page, laying bare the transactional friction that has rippled through the attendees since Friday evening.

### The Seven-Figure Invitation List

The Argyle Grand Hotel event had spent weeks building curiosity across entertainment circles primarily due to its aggressive price floor. Bahati and Diana had set entry at KSh 1 million per seat, pitching the gathering as an intimate, once-in-a-lifetime registry limited strictly to a curated cohort of 100 high-net-worth guests and entertainment peers.

Securing entry carried an explicit social contract: attendees were paying for proximity to an exclusive marital milestone. By converting an intimate social ceremony into a hard-nosed political rollout, Bahati effectively disrupted the terms under which those funds were surrendered.

For Nyathira, the sudden bait-and-switch crossed the line between high-society celebration and deceptive fundraising. Her public intervention strips the romance away from the gathering, treating the KSh 1 million ticket not as an unreturnable gift, but as an entry fee collected under false pretenses.

### From Altar Setup to Political Platform

Bahati’s announcement marks his formal re-entry into elective politics after his spirited, emotionally charged campaign in Mathare during the 2022 general cycle. While his ambition to represent the constituency has remained an open secret, anchoring the rollout to a ticketed social function caught even his closest circle of attendees off guard.

The shift caught invited guests flat-footed. Tables arranged for champagne toasts and romantic tributes quickly turned into an impromptu campaign platform as Bahati laid out his plans to capture the parliamentary seat in 2027. Rather than celebrating matrimonial stability, guests found themselves positioned as the inaugural audience—and effectively the financial underwriters—for his next legislative bid.

Nyathira’s complaint points to an unresolved line in Nairobi's celebrity economy: where promotional theater ends and financial accountability begins. While public figures routinely orchestrate attention stunts to drive visibility, demanding a KSh 1 million buy-in elevates the stakes beyond standard social media engagement.

Bahati has yet to publicly respond to Nyathira’s reimbursement demand or clarify whether any segment of the revenue collected from the 100 attendees will be accounted for as campaign contributions or redirected following the surprise pivot.
