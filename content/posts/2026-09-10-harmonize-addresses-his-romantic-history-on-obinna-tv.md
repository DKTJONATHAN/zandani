Tanzanian musician Harmonize sat down with Oga Obinna in a candid interview on Obinna TV Extra to discuss his high-profile romantic past. The Konde Music Worldwide CEO reflected on his previous relationships, including his ties to Italian model Sarah Michelotti, actress Frida Kajala, and Jacqueline Wolper, while explaining why he chooses not to rank his ex-partners.

The singer deflected attempts to name a favorite woman, asserting that he holds all his past partners in high regard. He noted that publicly naming one favorite could trigger unnecessary drama or disrespect the current partners of these women. He prioritized maintaining professional distance rather than engaging in public comparisons that might complicate the lives of those he once loved.

Regarding his habit of returning to past flames, Harmonize admitted that the comfort of familiarity often outweighs the effort required to build a connection with someone new. He noted that the daunting prospect of starting from scratch often pulls people back to what they already know. During the session, he acknowledged his own role in the failures of his past romances, stating that he is human and prone to mistakes.

The singer spent a significant portion of the interview discussing the concept of growth through experience. He stated that his previous heartbreaks have served as necessary lessons, refining his approach to intimacy. While he did not rule out future relationships, he suggested that he is now leaving his romantic life to faith, waiting for the right person to appear at the right time.

Harmonize remains one of the most bankable stars in East Africa, often commanding performance fees upwards of 2,000,000 KSh for private events. His public visibility, especially across Nairobi’s entertainment circles, ensures that every move he makes becomes a conversation starter from Westlands to the furthest ends of Kasarani.

### Why it matters

Harmonize is playing a game that many of our local celebrities fail to master: he is monetizing his own vulnerability. By sitting down with Oga Obinna and speaking openly about his failures, he turns his messy personal life into a structured narrative. It is a tactical move. The average person stuck in traffic on Thika Road has likely been through a breakup that felt like the end of the world. Seeing a star admit that he is just as confused and tempted to crawl back to an ex as the rest of us makes him relatable.

However, do not let the charm fool you. This interview acts as a cleanup operation for his brand. By framing his past instability as a series of "lessons" and "human mistakes," he softens the image of the volatile celebrity. It shifts the focus from his actual breakups to his "growth." Fans looking for the dirt will find little here, but those looking for a reason to keep streaming his music will find a man who sounds like he is finally maturing. He is not just talking about ex-lovers; he is rebuilding his reputation before his next project drops. In this industry, looking like you have learned your lesson is just as valuable as the lesson itself.
