Former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua has stepped directly into the spotlight of Kenya's pre-election debate, taking aim at President William Ruto and Interior Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen over their public defense of the country's electoral body.

According to an account published by news outlet Ghafla on Wednesday, November 25, Gachagua warned that opposition figures will launch a nationwide campaign across 29 constituencies starting January if the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) fails to engage them on comprehensive electoral reforms before the close of December.

Gachagua delivered the ultimatum following a closed-door consultative session with opposition leaders in Nairobi aimed at hashing out strategy ahead of the 2027 General Election. Central to his grievance was the accusation that top executive officials have effectively usurped the commission's institutional voice.

### The 'Referee' Confrontation

Addressing reporters, Gachagua questioned why senior government figures were publicly handling communication that constitutionally belongs to an independent referee.

“Why is William Ruto, a contestant, speaking for you? He is a competitor in the player. He cannot speak on behalf of the referee. Why is IEBC not speaking?” Gachagua stated, according to the report.

The former deputy president insisted that the boundary between political contenders and the electoral referee must remain absolute, arguing that executive commentary undermines the perceived neutrality required to oversee national ballots.

He also dismissed a separate consultative meeting convened by the State Department of Interior, characterizing the gathering as an attempt by the state to validate administrative decisions without broad consensus among key political actors.

“We are unwilling to participate in a charade,” Gachagua said. “The meeting called this afternoon is organised by the State Department of Interior with briefcase parties to come there and sanitise what IEBC is trying to do.”

### Administrative Vulnerabilities Under Scrutiny

Beyond political rhetoric, Gachagua pointed to concrete administrative and operational issues currently facing the electoral agency. He noted that the contract governing the election management system had lapsed, while unresolved litigation continues to cloud agency procedures.

He further referenced an upcoming by-election in Mwingi West as an immediate stress test, arguing that operating under unresolved legal and infrastructural constraints casts doubt on the commission's readiness to administer credible votes.

For its part, the IEBC offered an alternative account of the deteriorating relationship with opposition figures. According to statements cited by the outlet, commission officials indicated that opposition representatives staged a walkout during a recent preparatory meeting in Nairobi before substantive talks on the 2027 framework could properly get underway.

Opposition leaders, including Gachagua and Wiper Democratic Movement leader Kalonzo Musyoka, maintained that the walkout was prompted by the commission's reluctance to engage transparently on core safeguards. Among the primary demands tabled by the coalition are a comprehensive audit and cleanup of the national voter register, alongside strict statutory guarantees that vote counting and final declarations remain strictly anchored at the primary polling stations.

### The January Deadline

Gachagua dismissed warnings from government officials that street-level mobilization would be suppressed, asserting that the opposition would not back down from challenging the administration's political positioning.

“Let William Ruto not threaten us,” Gachagua said, adding that opposition figures are focused on organizing toward the next general vote.

If the electoral commission does not open formal dialogue channels before the end of December, Gachagua stated that the political opposition will bypass official boardrooms entirely and take their reform agenda directly to citizens across 29 selected constituencies when the new year begins.
