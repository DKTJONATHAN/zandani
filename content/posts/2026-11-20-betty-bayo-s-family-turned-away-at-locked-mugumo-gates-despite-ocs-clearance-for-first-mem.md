One year after the passing of gospel musician Betty Bayo, her family’s attempt to mark her first death anniversary at her Mugumo residence ended in a tense standoff at the compound's locked entrance.

Her sister, Irene Wanjiku, and brother, John Mbugua, traveled to the property expecting to hold a quiet family remembrance, having taken formal administrative steps ahead of the planned gathering. According to Wanjiku, the family had issued formal invitation letters to Bayo's children and sought administrative clearance from the local Officer Commanding Station (OCS) to ensure the gathering proceeded smoothly under official knowledge.

Despite those formal notifications, the relatives encountered locked gates upon arrival. The family stated outside the property that Bayo’s widower, Hiram Gitau—widely known as Tash—refused to grant them access to the grounds. Wanjiku noted that previous exchanges between the family and Gitau had been channeled strictly through legal representatives, leaving ground-level communication unresolved when they arrived for the ceremony.

Speaking outside the residence in video footage circulated by MBC TV on TikTok, John Mbugua emphasized that the journey to Mugumo was driven by a commitment to establish an annual tradition in honour of the late singer. Another relative present, identified as Wendo, reiterated that the group had gathered solely to execute a modest, peaceful service alongside close friends and immediate family.

At the time of the gathering, Gitau had provided no direct explanation to the family waiting outside regarding why entry was withheld.

Betty Bayo rose to prominence in Kenya's faith music sector through hit tracks that anchored her widespread public following. Her passing left a significant mark on the local gospel scene, making the first anniversary a key milestone for her estate and family. The unresolved dispute outside her Mugumo residence leaves the long-term arrangements for her memorial rites unsettled between her surviving siblings and her widower as legal channels remain the primary avenue of contact.
