Interior Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen was handed the microphone at Nandi Hills Stadium for what was billed as a celebratory school-bus harambee, but he quickly turned the gathering into a direct showdown with village administrators over illicit alcohol.

Presiding over a fundraising drive meant to purchase a bus and construct new branch offices for the Kenya National Union of Teachers (KNUT) Tinderet and Nandi Hills branch, Murkomen sidestepped polite pleasantries to issue an uncompromising order to National Government Administrative Officers (NGAO). With union executives and elected lawmakers seated beside him, the Cabinet Secretary challenged Chiefs and Assistant Chiefs to stop looking the other way while illicit brew operations damage young lives across rural neighborhoods.

Murkomen argued that local administrators cannot plausibly plead ignorance about who brews, packages, and retails harmful alcohol because these operations run right inside their administrative borders. He stated that Chiefs, their assistants, and Nyumba Kumi elders already possess the granular intelligence required to dismantle supply lines before they spread.

“Illicit brew is harming our youths. The content in that alcohol is so harmful to the consumers,” Murkomen told the crowd at the stadium. “I want to call on the community to be at the forefront in eradicating the harmful brews by giving information to relevant authorities, which can enhance eradication.”

### Putting Local Administration on the Spot

Murkomen emphasized that standard police raids will never permanently wipe out toxic brewing without honest cooperation from grassroots authorities. He instructed administrative officers to actively coordinate with security teams, pointing out that village elders and Nyumba Kumi clusters can easily identify the exact compounds where illicit alcohol is prepared.

His direct warning was delivered before an influential lineup of regional leaders. Sharing the dais were Tinderet MP Julius Melly, Nandi Hills MP Benard Kitur, Nandi County Woman Representative Cynthia Muge, Members of the County Assembly, and Nandi County Commissioner Wilberforce Kilonzo. Also listening closely was KNUT National Executive Secretary Collins Oyuu, who had assembled teachers from across the area to fundraise for branch infrastructure.

Murkomen stressed that community assistance remains the missing link in pinning down the financiers and distributors behind the trade so they can face formal prosecution rather than temporary operational pauses.

### Linking Rural Discipline to Broader Security

While his immediate dressing-down targeted village-level vice, Murkomen situated the illicit brew drive within a broader national campaign against organized lawlessness, drawing parallels to ongoing operations in northern border zones.

Pivoting to wider security updates, the Interior CS revealed that law enforcement agencies have recovered approximately 3,000 illegal guns and 8,000 rounds of ammunition over the past eighteen months across active rustling belts. He cited persistent challenges across the Kerio Valley, Pokot, Turkana, Samburu, Elgeyo Marakwet, Isiolo, and Laikipia, maintaining that the government will continue disarming armed groups until civilians everywhere can go about their lives unhindered.

“Within a period of one and a half years, we have been able to recover about 3,000 illegal guns and 8,000 rounds of ammunition in the banditry-prone areas,” Murkomen said. “We will ensure that all illegal firearms are confiscated as a measure to ensure an end to deaths of innocent Kenyans as well as livestock.”

He confirmed that security details in Isiolo and Samburu are actively tightening enforcement to dismantle criminal networks and retrieve stolen herds, though he explicitly cautioned officers to exercise maximum restraint to prevent civilian harm during disarmament pushes.

Murkomen noted that displaced households in parts of the secured belts have begun returning to farming as patrols firm up. However, he maintained that restoring regional stability is meaningless if rural communities are hollowed out from within by toxic brews and unchecked local criminality.

Wrapping up his address in Nandi Hills, Murkomen made it clear that administrative officers will be evaluated on practical enforcement, warning that Chiefs and Nyumba Kumi representatives must immediately start passing actionable intelligence on local brewers to the police.
