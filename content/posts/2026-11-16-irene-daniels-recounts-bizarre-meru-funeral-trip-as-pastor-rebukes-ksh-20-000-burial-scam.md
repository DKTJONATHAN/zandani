Irene Daniels had prepared herself for an emotionally draining day of mourning, having pooled money alongside church members and friends to buy a small coffin and help a grieving father transport his child’s body to Meru. Instead, she ended up documenting one of the most bewildering village scenes of the year: standing in a rural homestead while a pastor laid hands on a completely healthy boy who had no idea he was supposed to be dead.

The drama unfolded on Friday, November 13, 2026, when a convoy of well-wishers arrived in Meru expecting a sombre burial service. The group had collectively raised approximately KSh 20,000 in bereavement contributions after being informed by the boy's father that his child had passed away and urgently required burial arrangements.

### The KSh 20,000 Collection and Vanishing Hearse

According to Daniels, the fundraising effort began in earnest when the man notified his circles about the purported loss, requesting urgent financial intervention to clear pending bills and secure a coffin.

“We contributed towards the burial arrangements na tukatuma some money hili aweze kubuy coffin na other bills,” Daniels recounted in her public account of the ordeal.

The logistical plan began unravelling on the morning of Friday, November 13. The father reportedly placed an urgent call to the Nairobi group, claiming that departure from his location had stalled because he lacked adequate funds to hire a vehicle to transport the body upcountry. Moved by the distress call, the contributors sent an additional batch of funds to ensure the transport arrangements were settled before they set out on the highway to Meru.

Yet the father's responsiveness ceased the moment the mourners reached the local area. Daniels stated that when the delegation arrived in Meru and contacted him for precise directions to the venue, he asked them to wait for ten minutes, promising to meet them on the road. Minutes later, his mobile phone went completely dead.

### An Empty Morgue Ledger

Stranded on the roadside with switched-off phone lines, the delegation took matters into their own hands. They proceeded to the local mortuary where the father had claimed the child’s remains were being preserved.

Upon arrival, mortuary attendants pulled up their internal documentation. The boy’s name appeared nowhere in the facility’s admission logs, nor was there any record of a body brought in under those particulars.

“We decided to visit the morgue where he said the child was, uuuui kumbe hakuna mwili ata jina hakuna kwa bookings,” Daniels said.

With suspicion turning into alarm, the mourners drove directly to the man’s family homestead, expecting either an explanation or a home deep in private grief.

### Prayers on the Lawn

What greeted the visitors on the compound bore no resemblance to a funeral. There were no funeral tents, no gathered elders, and no quiet murmurs of grief. Instead, neighbours and relatives were going about routine Friday chores—and right in the middle of the compound, the boy they had travelled across counties to bury was running around, playing alongside other children.

The discovery triggered disbelief among the travelling party, drawing church leaders into an impromptu intervention. Rather than demanding their money on the spot, the group pivoted to prayer. Rev Mainge Nathan stepped onto the homestead to conduct an open-air deliverance session over the child, publicly overturning the pronouncements that had brought them to Meru.

“Tumekataa, sauti ambazo zimenenwa, tuliambiwa umekufa, tumetangaza yu uhai,” Rev Mainge Nathan declared, laying hands on the boy in the presence of the dumbfounded delegation.

The clergyman noted the dramatic contrast between the sombre ritual they had anticipated and the unexpected sight of the child alive.

“Tumekataa, sauti ambazo zimenenwa, tuliambiwa umekufa, tumetangaza yu uhai. Tumeambiwa tufanye maombolezi, tumetangaza leo. Hautapewa maua. Leo tume tangaza, kulikua kuwe na machozi; tumetangaza kuwa na furaha,” Rev Nathan added during the prayer session, declaring the boy's life blessed beyond the deceit attached to his name.

The confrontation has left church congregants and friends grappling with how a KSh 20,000 bereavement drive was manufactured, with Daniels’ public account remaining one of the starkest reminders of how far some individuals go to turn genuine communal goodwill into personal gain.
