President William Ruto has moved beyond standard opposition critique, opting instead to frame the political futures of Rigathi Gachagua and Kalonzo Musyoka as a collective countdown to 2032. During his recent development tour of the Gusii region, the President appeared to be workshopping a new, dismissive narrative: that his rivals are not merely opponents for the 2027 ballot, but a generational cohort already past their expiration date.

Speaking to supporters in Kisii and Nyamira, Ruto leaned into the colloquial concept of 'rika' (age-mates) to shrink the perceived stakes of his opponents' current mobilization efforts. By casting both the former Deputy President and the Wiper leader as members of his own 'retirement class,' the President shifted the conversation away from their current coalition-building tactics and toward their eventual exit from the stage.

“Mimi hawa watu wote hawa ni rika yangu na mimi nataka niwahakikishie vijana wa Kenya, hawa watu wote, huyu Mr 40 (Kalonzo), na huyo mwingine Bwana Texas (Gachagua), hawa watu wote nitawakusanya, nitawasafirisha, mimi na wao tutaondoka 2032,” Ruto declared.

The deployment of specific, borderline-derisive monikers—referencing Kalonzo Musyoka as 'Mr 40' and Rigathi Gachagua as 'Bwana Texas'—signals a desire to keep the discourse personalized. By suggesting he will personally 'collect' and 'transport' them into retirement, Ruto is attempting to reframe the 2027 election cycle from a high-stakes competitive threat into a mere stopover before a forced exit.

This rhetoric arrives at a moment of genuine mechanical movement within the opposition. Gachagua’s Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP), Kalonzo’s Wiper Patriotic Front, and Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna’s Linda Mwananchi Movement have been increasingly visible in their efforts to forge a unified front. The recent strategic consultations on November 8, which included a meeting with Jubilee leader Dr. Fred Matiang’i, suggest an attempt to resolve long-standing friction over power-sharing and coalition architecture.

While the opposition focuses on the logistics of a 2027 challenge, Ruto’s strategy in Gusii served to highlight the optics of project delivery over political negotiation. His four-day tour featured the commissioning of the Kenyoro SDA–Riana Market and Kerina–Mwata road, part of a Ksh22 billion allocation for the region, alongside a Ksh1.7 billion expansion of last-mile electricity connectivity.

For now, the President’s framing is clear: while Gachagua and Kalonzo build their houses for 2027, the incumbent is already drawing the curtains on their career timelines. Whether this 'retirement' narrative gains traction with the electorate or serves as a rallying cry for the opposition to prove their staying power remains the central question heading into the next two years of campaigning.
