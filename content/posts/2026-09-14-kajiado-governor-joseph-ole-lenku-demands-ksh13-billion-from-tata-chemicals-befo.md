Kajiado Governor Joseph Ole Lenku has set a Ksh13 billion condition for the reopening of Tata Chemicals Magadi Limited, demanding the multinational clear historical land rate arrears and surrender nearly 190,000 acres of unutilised territory before its suspended operations can resume.

### What we know
* The national government’s sudden shutdown of Magadi Soda has handed devolved leadership rare leverage to aggressively settle decade-long land grievances.
* Lenku wants the Indian conglomerate stripped of idle acreage while enforcing an unprecedented multi-billion-shilling municipal tax bill that threatens corporate viability.
* State House is actively resetting extractive sector contracts, forcing century-old resource extractors to offer local equity and processing or face outright expulsion.

Speaking during a national television interview, Governor Lenku framed the soda ash miner’s footprint in the Rift Valley as an ongoing historical injustice that has systematically disenfranchised the local pastoralist community. The county chief maintained that the company, which has extracted trona from Lake Magadi for decades, cannot resume industrial activity under the old terms.

"We have many concerns about it. We in the county government are supposed to get our land rates. The company is the only one in the country that does not pay land rates. They owe the county government Ksh13 billion," Lenku said.

Central to the county's escalation is the scale of the company's land tenure. Lenku stated that while Tata Chemicals holds titles over roughly 200,000 acres, active extraction and processing occupy barely 11,000 acres, leaving vast tracts of ancestral grazing grounds walled off from Maasai herders.

"Magadi is occupying huge grazing land for our community. The salt is only 11,000 acres, yet they occupy over 200,000 acres of land," the governor added, demanding the immediate excision and return of the surplus acreage.

The county's hardline stance arrives on the heels of an executive shutdown ordered by President William Ruto, who halted the company’s century-long operations over concerns that raw mineral exports had failed to translate into meaningful socio-economic gains for the surrounding population.

Mining Cabinet Secretary Hassan Joho has since constituted a high-level technical committee to audit the firm's compliance. The inter-agency team is tasked with reviewing mineral beneficiation, in-country value addition mandates, royalty remittances, and the contentious land tenure agreements before any operating license is restored.

The convergence of county revenue claims and national industrial policy leaves Tata Chemicals in a corner. If the state enforces mandatory in-country processing alongside Kajiado’s multi-billion-shilling land claim, the era of low-cost raw extraction at Lake Magadi is effectively over-and foreign miners across Kenya’s resource belt will have to recalibrate their balance sheets to survive the new regime.
