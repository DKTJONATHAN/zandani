Nyandarua Senator John Methu took to the Citizen TV studios on Sunday night to deliver a scathing personal outing of his political peers, publicly labeling Kiharu MP Ndindi Nyoro as State House’s prime insider operative and admitting his friendship with former Interior Cabinet Secretary Fred Matiang’i is effectively over.

Speaking during a broadcast on Sunday, October 11, 2026, the Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP) Secretary General pulled back the curtain on behind-the-scenes maneuvering within the United Opposition, alleging that President William Ruto is actively deploying political statecraft to fracture the coalition before the 2027 General Election.

According to Methu, the key to that strategy lies with Nyoro, whom he accused of secretly fronting new political vehicles engineered directly by the ruling administration. Methu revealed that similar party-formation overtures were once extended to him personally, making Nyoro's current moves all the more revealing.

“When I heard him say he has a party, I couldn’t believe my ears because it is he who told me that another party is a creation of William Ruto,” Methu stated during the broadcast. “This same offer of forming a party others were given. If I was to name moles, then I think the name Ndindi Nyoro will feature prominently.”

The lawmaker’s most striking personal disclosure, however, was reserved for Matiang’i. Once considered a close ally, the former Interior CS has raised eyebrows across opposition ranks through recent joint public appearances and visible political alignment with Nyoro.

For Methu, those public outings signaled a disturbing shift that left him questioning where Matiang'i's true loyalties reside.

“I cannot speak about Matiang’i because I thought I knew him very well. These days, I don’t think I know him,” Methu admitted. “If I say Ndindi Nyoro is a mole, what can I say about Matiang’i, and they are friends?”

### A Widening Rift in the Opposition Front

Methu’s television comments are not an isolated outburst. They echo warnings issued recently by DCP leader Rigathi Gachagua, who cautioned that several high-profile principals within the opposition alliance are privately threatening to walk away if they fail to secure the coalition's presidential nomination ticket.

Gachagua’s initial remarks triggered speculation across the political scene that Wiper Patriotic Movement leader Kalonzo Musyoka might be considering an exit route. However, Wiper officials moved firmly to reject those claims, reiterating their commitment to remaining inside a unified opposition front aimed at confronting Ruto at the polls.

By putting specific names to the betrayal narrative on national television, Methu has shifted the friction from quiet boardroom whispers into open political drama. His assertion that both Nyoro and Matiang’i could chart competing paths leaves the opposition's single-candidate plan facing its sharpest internal scrutiny yet.

Whether Nyoro or Matiang’i choose to publicly respond to Methu's televised unmasking remains the next critical test of how deep the fracture runs.
