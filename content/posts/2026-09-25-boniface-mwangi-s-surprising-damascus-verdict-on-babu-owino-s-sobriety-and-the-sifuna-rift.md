In an unexpected shift from his typical hardline stance against political figures with checkered pasts, human rights activist Boniface Mwangi has offered a striking validation of Embakasi East MP Babu Owino’s personal transformation. 

Speaking on the *Iko Nini* podcast on Thursday, September 24, 2026, Mwangi pointed to the high-profile shooting of Felix Orinda, widely known as DJ Evolve, as the catalyst for a profound lifestyle overhaul that has redefined the controversial lawmaker's private and public conduct.

Mwangi framed this transition not merely as political damage control, but as a genuine behavioral turning point.

"I think people do change. I think Babu had a Damascus moment when he shot DJ Evolve," Mwangi observed during the podcast episode. "According to him, he stopped drinking, and stopped using any form of… that impairs his judgement."

### The Activist’s Dilemma

For an activist like Mwangi, whose public brand has long been anchored on uncompromising ethics and fierce opposition to the political class, validating Owino is a notable pivot. Historically, human rights defenders have kept a strict distance from politicians implicated in violence or public scandals. 

However, Mwangi indicated that he is willing to support the legislator’s ambitions—including potential countywide leadership—so long as those pursuits remain aligned with the country's constitutional standards. 

Rather than permanently writing the lawmaker off, the activist chose to highlight Owino's formidable grassroots track record, drawing on his past work organizing campaigns for the late former Prime Minister Raila Odinga as proof of his exceptional capabilities.

"If he can operate within the Constitution, I have no problem with him," Mwangi stated. "The other thing I think about Babu and I respect him, he is a very good mobiliser. Babu is a brilliant mobiliser. And he was a really good mobiliser for Baba."

### Pragmatism Over Purity

Mwangi's commentary extended beyond personal rehabilitation into tactical political maneuvering. He addressed the ongoing public friction between Babu Owino and Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna, two of the most vocal young politicians in the country.

Dismissing the rivalry as something largely inflated by onlookers, Mwangi urged the two leaders to settle their differences behind closed doors. In his view, their collective political influence is too critical to be wasted on internal squabbles if they hope to challenge President William Ruto's administration.

"My hope is that this small disagreement, which has been amplified more by strangers than the people inside, could be dealt with," Mwangi added. "Because I think Babu and Sifuna are very necessary to remove Ruto."

By focusing on the tangible changes in Owino's lifestyle and calling for a truce with Sifuna, Mwangi signals a rising wave of political pragmatism. In this new landscape, personal rehabilitation and strategic alliances are beginning to take precedence over ideological purity.
