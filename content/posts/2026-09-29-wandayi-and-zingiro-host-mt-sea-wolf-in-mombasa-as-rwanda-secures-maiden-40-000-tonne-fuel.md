High-level energy delegations from Nairobi and Kigali converged on the offshore Kipevu Oil Terminal 2 facility in Mombasa to oversee a historic logistics milestone for East African cross-border trade.

Energy and Petroleum Cabinet Secretary Opiyo Wandayi stood alongside Rwanda’s Minister of State for Infrastructure, Armand Zingiro, to mark the arrival of the MT Sea Wolf. The vessel docked with 40,000 metric tonnes of fuel, serving as the inaugural shipment for the Rwanda National Energy Company (RNEC).

The waterfront ceremony drew a dense lineup of regional trade figures, including Rwandan High Commissioner to Kenya Janet Obel, Kenya Ports Authority Managing Director Capt. William Ruto, and Kenya Pipeline Company Acting CEO Pius Mwendwa. The heavy diplomatic and institutional turnout underscored the shifting economics of Northern Corridor fuel transit.

For years, bulk fuel distribution to landlocked Kigali relied heavily on long-haul trucking from coastal terminals—a logistical route prone to highway congestion, extended transit delays, and product losses. The docking of deep-draft vessels like the MT Sea Wolf at the modern KOT2 facility signals a pivot toward integrated pipeline-and-port scheduling.

Under the arrangement, KPC channels the imported product through its arterial pipeline network stretching from Mombasa through Nairobi into Western Kenya nodes like Eldoret and Kisumu. This infrastructure bypasses major highway bottlenecks, offering RNEC a streamlined conduit to secure national fuel reserves and stabilize consumer pump prices against external supply shocks.

The maiden offloading positions KPC as a central energy transit hub for the wider Eastern and Central African market, bringing regional trade integration frameworks closer to operational reality along the Northern Corridor.
