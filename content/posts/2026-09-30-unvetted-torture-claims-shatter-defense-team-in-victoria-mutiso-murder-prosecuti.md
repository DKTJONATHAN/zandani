A scheduled bail hearing at the High Court in Nairobi collapsed into a defense realignment on Tuesday, September 29, after uncoordinated legal filings alleging state bribery, torture, and tribalism forced prominent defense attorneys Danstan Omari and Cliff Ombeta to walk away from representing three primary suspects in the murder of Dr. Victoria Mutiso.

The advocates arrived at court prepared to push for their clients' release on bail, only to encounter parallel applications submitted on the same day that introduced severe allegations against state investigators and prosecutors—claims that had not been vetted or incorporated into the agreed defense strategy.

### Parallel Filings Trigger Counsel Exit

Addressing the High Court, Omari and Ombeta stated that the unsanctioned filings altered the fundamental premise of their representation, creating an unbridgeable rift between counsel and clients. The attorneys emphasized that while their initial objective was to secure an immediate bail determination, the introduction of fresh allegations forced a tactical withdrawal.

![Lawyer Danstan Omari](https://i.ibb.co/Q3KbP1sH/771054d1a4ca.webp)

"We walk into court knowing that today is a bail application, but another application that has been filed today tries to bring about a bail application, whereby the state looks at it, says that they have got issues that have been raised that are very weighty," the defense team stated, referencing claims of bribery and torture that took both the prosecution and lead counsel by surprise.

Disputing suggestions that the withdrawal was tied to legal fees or media attention, the lawyers maintained that stepping aside was the only ethically sound option remaining. The High Court formally allowed the advocates to discharge themselves from the matter after the three accused persons raised no objection to the application.

### Plea Entered Amid Ambush Charges

Following the departure of their defense team, all three main suspects—alongside two police officers charged as co-defendants—pleaded not guilty to coordinating, planning, and executing the July 29 murder.

![An undated photo of clinical psychologist and mental health researcher Dr Victoria Nthunya Mutiso, who served as the Head of Research at the Africa Mental Health Research and Train](https://i.ibb.co/wF5qygtS/523b217ec7b5.webp)

Investigators allege that Dr. Mutiso, a prominent clinical psychologist and former Head of Research at the Africa Mental Health Research and Training Foundation (AMHRTF), was assassinated as part of a long-running family land dispute. According to police files, relatives contracted hitmen, including active-duty police officers, who ambushed her taxi at a Nairobi road junction using a motorcycle and a secondary vehicle. A gunman opened fire, inflicting fatal gunshot wounds; Dr. Mutiso died shortly after arriving at the hospital.

### Compressed Timeline Before October 6 Hearing

The tactical collapse leaves the three primary defendants without lead legal counsel at a critical juncture in the trial. The presiding judge set October 6 for the formal bail hearing, leaving the accused less than a week to retain new representation or finalize their defense strategy.

Simultaneously, the court granted the Office of the Director of Public Prosecutions (ODPP) a three-day window to draft, file, and serve responding affidavits. Prosecutors must now address both the routine opposition to bail and the specific claims of state torture, bribery, and tribalism introduced in Tuesday's parallel filings before all parties return to court next week.
