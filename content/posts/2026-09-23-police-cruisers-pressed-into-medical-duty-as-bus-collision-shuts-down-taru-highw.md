First responders along the Taru-Mackinnon stretch of the Nairobi-Mombasa Highway were forced to turn police Land Cruisers into makeshift medical transports on Wednesday morning, September 23, after a high-impact collision between a long-distance coach and a freight trailer trapped multiple passengers inside a crumpled front cabin.

The crash involved a Dreamline passenger bus traveling along the country’s primary commercial arterial corridor. Heavy front-end impact severely crushed the forward structure of the vehicle, particularly damaging the front VIP seating area—a elevated-fare section situated directly behind the driver's cab.

Preliminary reports established at least one fatality at the scene, with the victim located within the impacted forward compartment. Several other passengers sustained varied injuries and remained pinned within the twisted metal frame as early emergency efforts got under way.

### Improvised Medical Transport at Taru

With specialized highway medical services absent during the initial response window near Taru, police officers and local responders initiated a manual extraction to pull injured travelers out of the wreckage. Among those trapped was a male passenger suffering from severe bleeding who required immediate intervention.

To prevent critical delays in medical care, police deployed their on-scene utility Land Cruisers as emergency ambulances. Officers loaded injured survivors directly onto the police vehicles to shuttle them to regional health facilities for urgent treatment.

The rescue operations and physical obstruction of the wreckage halted regular flow across the Taru-Mackinnon section of the highway. Police officers directed approaching drivers to slow down and use single-lane bypasses while emergency teams worked to secure the scene.

![A collage of the accident scene of the dreamline bus on the Nairobi- Mombasa Highway at Taru on September 23, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/mrwyC6xc/7d5f8564fd5a.webp)

### Recurrent Hazards on the Mombasa Route

The Taru collision highlights persistent safety vulnerabilities along the two-lane transit trunk connecting Nairobi to the Coast. The corridor handles high daily volumes of intercity passenger buses, heavy cargo trailers, and fuel tankers operating under tight haulage schedules.

Wednesday’s crash comes exactly one week after another fatal incident along the same highway. On September 16, a head-on collision between a passenger matatu and a trailer at Kapiti Plains resulted in multiple deaths and casualties, underlining the continuous risk involved when light passenger vehicles and long-distance freight trucks share narrow, undivided carriageways.

Police officers at Taru have opened investigations to establish the technical causes of the collision, including speed indicators, lane discipline, and road visibility conditions at the time of the impact. Recovery crews remained on site through Wednesday morning to tow the damaged bus frame and fully clear the highway for normal transit.
