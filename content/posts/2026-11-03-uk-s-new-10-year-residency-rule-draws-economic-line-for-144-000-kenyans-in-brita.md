Thousands of Kenyans living and working across the United Kingdom face a starkly stratified path to permanent residency following an overhaul that ties the right to stay directly to individual earnings and tax compliance.

Under the Earned Settlement framework announced by Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood, the standard waiting period for indefinite leave to remain has doubled from five years to a decade. The policy dismantles blanket residency timelines in favor of an income-tiered model that separates foreign workers based on their gross annual salaries.

![An image of the U.K. Visa signage.](https://i.ibb.co/SXZJpgjR/401d4bfac148.webp)

For the estimated 144,000 Kenyans residing in the UK—making it the second-largest diaspora community outside the United States—the financial thresholds determine whether settlement takes three years, five years, or ten.

Workers earning below £50,271 (Ksh8.76 million) annually absorb the heaviest blow, pushed into a 10-year waiting window before they can apply for permanent status. Conversely, migrants clearing the £50,271 mark retain the traditional five-year route, while top earners grossing above £125,000 (Ksh21.78 million) are fast-tracked to a three-year eligibility window.

The policy incorporates specific carve-outs for public service sectors heavily populated by foreign labor. Care workers meeting visa conditions on a minimum salary of £25,000 (Ksh4.36 million) maintain a five-year pathway, alongside doctors, nurses, and teachers. The Home Office also introduced provisions for care workers who missed previous visa conditions, offering a secondary five-year visa to secure compliant employment.

![Police officers attached to the United Kingdom's Metropolitan Police Service.](https://i.ibb.co/JFStnn9s/25231893c8ed.webp)

Beyond earnings, applicants must maintain sustained National Insurance contributions, clear criminal records, hold no outstanding public debt, and pass high-level English proficiency standards. Meanwhile, stricter penalties apply outside the legal employment framework: asylum seekers arriving via unauthorized channels face waiting periods stretching up to 20 or 30 years.

While the government stated the framework applies at the point of decision regardless of arrival date, transitional protections remain restricted to specific existing visa holders, leaving many long-term Kenyan residents reassessing their long-term financial trajectories in Britain.
