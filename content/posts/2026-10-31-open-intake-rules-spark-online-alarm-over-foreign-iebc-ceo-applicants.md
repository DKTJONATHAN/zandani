The Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission’s adherence to constitutional disclosure rules has run headfirst into Kenya’s perennial anxieties over electoral sovereignty.

Faced with swelling public scrutiny after foreign nationals surfaced on the unvarnished roster of 304 applicants for the position of Commission Secretary and Chief Executive Officer, the electoral body spent Friday clarifying a routine procedural reality: an open application portal catches every submission, but statutory screening eliminates those without citizenship at the first gate.

In an official clarification issued on October 30, the commission confirmed that every foreign applicant who submitted papers was eliminated during preliminary document verification, long before the compilation of the final interview roster.

### The Open Portal Friction

The public backlash gathered momentum on social media platforms after observers noticed foreign nationals, reportedly including Nigerian citizens, catalogued on the master ledger of applicants for an office central to national security and democratic management.

IEBC explained that publishing the complete log of all 304 submissions was an express constitutional obligation rather than an endorsement of eligibility. Under Articles 35 and 232 of the Constitution, state agencies must guarantee public access to information, transparency, and fair competition during public sector hiring.

![Acting IEBC Ruth Kulundu.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/ruth-iebc.jpg.webp?itok=xAaauNMl)

Because the vacancy was advertised openly in daily newspapers, across digital channels, and on the commission’s portal, anyone with internet access could lodge an application. The commission maintained that accepting an incoming application does not confer compliance.

“Upon verification of national ID, citizenship and academic credentials, the final shortlist contains only Kenyan citizens,” the commission stated. “All foreign applicants were disqualified at the preliminary stage for not meeting the citizenship requirement.”

### The External Sieve

To insulate the intake from internal interference and manage the logistical load, the commission outsourced the operational heavy lifting to KPMG Kenya.

The professional services firm was contracted to design the advertisement, host the application intake, conduct initial screening, authenticate academic and professional credentials, and build the preliminary competitive pool.

Beyond the non-negotiable threshold of Kenyan citizenship verified through national identity records, the statutory sieve required applicants to hold a recognized university degree, demonstrate at least five years of senior executive management experience, and satisfy the stringent leadership and integrity benchmarks set out under Chapter Six of the Constitution and the IEBC Act.

![IEBC CHAIR](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/iebc-chair-ethekon.jpg.webp?itok=0oh8Ss0O)

Once KPMG applied those filters, the field collapsed from 304 aspirants to a concentrated shortlist of nine Kenyan contenders, published on October 28.

### Vetting the Final Nine

The surviving nine-member shortlist leans heavily on seasoned public administrators and career technocrats with experience in high-scrutiny state oversight.

Among those advancing to the oral assessment stage are Halakhe Waqo, the former Chief Executive Officer of the Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission, and Anthony Omerikwa, who currently heads the National Authority for the Campaign Against Alcohol and Drug Abuse.

The roster also features Edmund Odiwuor Malitt, Harley Mutisya, Andrew Gitau Kimani, Mohamed Abdullahi Abdi, Edward Katama Ngeywa, Martina Wato Yattani, and Nelly Ilongo.

The executive post is currently held in an interim capacity by Ruth Kulundu, the Deputy Commission Secretary for Operations. Installing a substantive chief executive remains a pivotal operational milestone for the secretariat as it begins structural preparations, procurement roadmaps, and administrative realignments for the 2027 General Election.

Formal oral interviews for the nine finalists are set for November 12 and 13, when the commission will determine who inherits the operational steering wheel at Anniversary Towers.
