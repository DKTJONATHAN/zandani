Heavy contractor machinery arriving along the Allsops-Taj Mall corridor marks the first physical movement on Nairobi’s Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) Line 5, seven months after state officials signed off on the project’s framework.

The Kenya Urban Road Authority (KURA) confirmed that contractor teams are actively clearing the right-of-way and mobilizing equipment along Outer Ring Road. The official confirmation follows weeks of public questioning regarding whether the transit project had stalled after its initial signing in March 2026.

According to road authorities, heavy civil construction along the corridor is scheduled to begin immediately once site clearance is complete.

![A Bus Rapid Transport (BRT) station under construction along Thika Road at Safari Park footbridge](https://i.ibb.co/S4CCMxdM/641c04f9bfdf.webp)

Line 5, designated in city transit plans as the Nyati route, is designed to link Ridgeways through Balozi to Outer Ring Road, terminating around Taj Mall and Imara Daima. The current phase targets the heavily congested stretch between Allsops—where Outer Ring Road joins the Thika Superhighway—and the Taj Mall junction near North Airport Road.

Outer Ring Road has long operated as Eastlands' main vehicular bypass, carrying high volumes of freight and commuter traffic between the city's northern highway outlets and the industrial and airport districts to the south. Transforming this section into a functional mass transit corridor is expected to cut commuter transit times across east Nairobi, provided dedicated lanes can be successfully integrated into the existing highway footprint.

The mobilization along Line 5 comes shortly after Nairobi Governor Johnson Sakaja outlined financing strategies for all five planned BRT lines, which form part of a broader mass transit blueprint intended to connect commuter rail services and a proposed underground network.

![An artistic representation of a BRT pick-up point on Thika Superhighway](https://i.ibb.co/gZjm1n2d/061adc39aabd.webp)

City Hall estimates place the overall capital expenditure for the multi-modal transit system between Ksh905.15 billion and Ksh129.45 trillion ($7 billion to $1 trillion), with national and county authorities exploring a mix of public and private financing structures.

Beyond Line 5, the city's master plan includes four other dedicated corridors: Line 1 (Ndovu), spanning Limuru through Kangemi, the CBD, Imara Daima, and Athi River to Kitengela; Line 2 (Simba), connecting Rongai through the CBD and Ruiru to Kenol; Line 3 (Chui), running from Tala through Dandora and the CBD to Ngong; and Line 4 (Kifaru), linking Mama Lucy Hospital through Donholm, T-Mall, and Bomas to Kikuyu.

With site preparation now underway between Allsops and Taj Mall, the immediate test for KURA and its contractors will be managing traffic disruptions along Outer Ring Road while retrofitting dedicated bus lanes onto one of Nairobi's busiest urban highways.
