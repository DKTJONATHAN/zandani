The State has turned to veteran administrator Nelson Marwa to steer the rehabilitation of the Nairobi River Basin, swapping out political and clerical patronage for a seasoned provincial enforcer.

President William Ruto confirmed Marwa’s appointment as chairperson of the Nairobi Rivers Commission in a Gazette notice dated Friday, October 16, 2026. The appointment, which takes effect immediately for a three-year term, formally revokes the leadership of Bishop Margaret Wanjiru.

“The President of the Republic of Kenya and Commander-in-Chief of the Defence Forces, William Samoei Ruto, appoints Nelson Marwa to be the Chairperson of the Nairobi Rivers Commission for a period of three years, with effect from October 16, 2026,” the State directive reads.

### An Enforcement Hand on Metropolitan Waters

![nelson-marwa.jpg](https://i.ibb.co/tTJYcSGK/7f21a40746fa.webp)

Marwa takes control of an agency whose scope crosses multiple metropolitan county boundaries. The commission is mandated to coordinate comprehensive ecological restoration, police industrial and residential effluent, and dismantle physical encroachments strangling the river system.

The installation of Marwa signals a shift toward strict operational execution. A career public servant, Marwa made his name as Mombasa County Commissioner and later Coast Regional Coordinator. In those administrative roles, he developed a reputation for uncompromising public confrontations over local security, narcotics crackdowns, and clashes with regional political figures, including then-Mombasa Governor Hassan Joho, who currently serves as Cabinet Secretary for Mining.

Former President Uhuru Kenyatta tapped Marwa into the executive ranks in January 2018 as Principal Secretary in the State Department for Devolution. His return to frontline metropolitan administration places an official experienced in county coordination directly at the head of a multi-jurisdictional environmental project.

### Formalising the Wanjiru Break

The gazette notice concludes a drawn-out exit for Bishop Margaret Wanjiru, who had led the commission since October 2024. 

![nairobi-river.jpg](https://i.ibb.co/bj6jbNB7/0a90833c3549.webp)

Wanjiru submitted a resignation notice in August 2026, citing a desire to pursue other interests. The political rationale behind that departure surfaced quickly: in September, the former Starehe lawmaker declared she would contest the Nairobi governorship in 2027 under the Kenya Moja Movement banner. She compounded the political distance from the administration by endorsing former Interior Cabinet Secretary Fred Matiang’i for the presidency.

While Wanjiru stepped away two months ago, Friday’s formal revocation tidies the State’s executive machinery and removes the residual ambiguity surrounding the commission's helm.

### Cross-County Ecological Basin

Marwa inherits an extensive mandate that goes beyond municipal trash collection. The Nairobi River Basin encompasses catchment tributaries spanning upstream agricultural zones, heavily populated informal settlements, and dense commercial corridors where riparian lines have been severely blurred by private developments.

His primary test will be enforcing pollution controls and managing the inter-county mechanics of the basin's ecological recovery—responsibilities that demand aggressive inter-agency coordination across national ministries and regional county governments.
