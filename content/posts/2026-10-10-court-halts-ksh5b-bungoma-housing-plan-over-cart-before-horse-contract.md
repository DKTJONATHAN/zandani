The Environment and Land Court has halted the government’s plan to convert Bungoma’s Kanduyi Airstrip into a Ksh5.02 billion residential estate, handing a decisive reprieve to local aviation infrastructure after determining that state officials committed billions to a contractor before seeking citizen views.

In a judgment delivered by Justice Kossy Bors on October 8, 2026, the court determined that the proposed Kanduyi Airstrip Smart City Affordable Housing Project violated core constitutional safeguards governing land-use changes, environmental protection, and genuine public participation. 

The ruling directly benefits local aviation interests, protecting the reserved airstrip grounds from unprocedural degazettement and setting a strict operational ceiling against retrospective public consultations.

### Contract Preceded Public Consultation

At the core of the court's finding was an inverted administrative sequence. The national government had structured a Ksh5.02 billion contract to build 2,034 residential units across the facility. However, judicial scrutiny of the procurement record revealed that the commercial construction agreement was finalized and awarded prior to a public participation session arranged on January 14, 2026.

![Ksh5 billion Kanduyi Airstrip affordable housing project](https://i.ibb.co/78cQMwQ/ca5e0b2e4b07.webp)

Justice Bors noted that presenting a signed multi-billion-shilling construction deal to residents after the fact reduced mandatory constitutional consultation to a cosmetic exercise. 

Three petitioners had moved to court challenging the conversion, arguing that the parcel was formally zoned for aviation operations and remained earmarked for the future expansion of Kanduyi Airstrip. They maintained that reallocating public transport assets into residential real estate required formal degazettement, comprehensive physical planning approvals, and prior community consent.

The court concurred, ruling that state respondents failed to show any verifiable paper trail complying with statutory procedures for reclassifying aviation reserves into municipal housing estates.

### Forty-Five Days to Restore Airstrip

Beyond stopping work on the 2,034 planned units, the court barred the state from initiating any unauthorized activity on the property and handed down an immediate physical remediation order. 

![President William Ruto laying a stone at the Affordbale Housing Programme in Garissa, Thursday, February 6, 2025.](https://i.ibb.co/pvqNrqHC/0a2bd6200b9d.webp)

The government must restore the Kanduyi grounds to their pre-construction state within 45 days at its own expense. Justice Bors further ordered a formal environmental and land-use audit of the affected acreage and directed the state respondents to bear the petitioners’ legal fees.

The decision establishes a firm administrative precedent: executive urgency under national development flagships cannot override statutory town planning frameworks or environmental assessment thresholds.

### Legal Landscape Post-Appellate Nod

The Bungoma ruling lands amid an evolving legal environment for the national housing agenda. Just two weeks earlier, on September 25, 2026, a five-judge bench at the Court of Appeal upheld the constitutionality of the overarching Affordable Housing Act, 2024, securing the legal basis for the state’s mandatory 1.5 per cent housing levy.

Yet the Kanduyi verdict makes clear that while the legislative foundation of the housing fund has survived scrutiny at the appellate level, individual site acquisitions remain vulnerable to local land-tenure challenges. 

State agencies now face an unresolved operational dilemma in Bungoma: whether to appeal Justice Bors's remediation timetable or begin expending public funds to reverse excavation work on an airstrip where construction contracts have already been signed.
