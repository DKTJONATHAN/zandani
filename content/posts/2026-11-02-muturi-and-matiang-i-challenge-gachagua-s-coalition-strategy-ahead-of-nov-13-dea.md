Just 11 days before Kenya’s United Opposition is scheduled to unveil its single presidential candidate, open friction over election strategy has erupted among top alliance figures, with senior partners accusing Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP) leader Rigathi Gachagua of preparing for post-election bargaining rather than a unified campaign for State House.

Democratic Party (DP) leader Justin Muturi and Jubilee Party chief Fred Matiang’i issued public rebukes against the former Deputy President on Monday, November 2, following remarks Gachagua made during a broadcast interview on Sunday. Muturi specifically questioned whether Gachagua is committed to the shared goal of defeating President William Ruto in 2027 or primarily focused on securing personal leverage by controlling parliamentary and devolved seats.

In a formal statement, Muturi warned that Gachagua’s reliance on independent regional maneuvers and tripartite political arrangements risks reducing the national alliance into a post-election bargaining vehicle.

![Jubilee Party leader Fred Matiang'i alongside Former Cabinet Secretary Justin Muturi during a funeral service for Canon Penina Ciambogo Ngùngì in Mbeere North, Embu County, on Satu](https://i.ibb.co/fV2KVgYn/f76f4a22505b.webp)

"Mr Gachagua must understand one fundamental truth: No Kenyan President has ever been made by one community alone," Muturi stated. "I wish to put the United Opposition on notice: the current political trajectory being pursued by Mr Gachagua raises serious questions about his commitment to the collective presidential ambition of the Wantam movement."

Matiang’i echoed those concerns, attributing mounting tensions within the coalition to Gachagua’s authoritarian political style and refusal to build broad-based consensus across constituent parties. 

Muturi questioned whether Gachagua's primary objective is to build a winning presidential ticket or to construct a legislative bloc capable of dictating terms after the vote.

![United Opposition](https://i.ibb.co/QF8hqL0c/6678db0db539.webp)

"Is the objective to win the presidency or to control MPs, legislators and devolved units while sacrificing the bigger national ambition?" Muturi asked. "Let it be clear: No individual owns the United Opposition, and no community has a monopoly on Kenya's presidency. Political partnerships must be built on mutual respect, not ultimatums, political bullying or supremacy contests."

The fallout hits the coalition at a critical juncture in its timetable. Wiper Patriotic Front leader Kalonzo Musyoka recently announced that the United Opposition could name its flagbearer as early as November 13. 

With Gachagua seeking to consolidate Mount Kenya's voting base behind his DCP platform and other partners demanding equal standing in coalition decision-making, the opposition faces an immediate test of internal discipline. Whether the alliance can bridge the rift between regional seat protection and a single national ticket will determine how effectively it enters the mid-November candidate selection.
