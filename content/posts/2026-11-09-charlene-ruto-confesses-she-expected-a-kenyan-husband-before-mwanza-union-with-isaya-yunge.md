Charlene Ruto admitted to a Tanzanian congregation that she fully expected her future husband to come from Kenya, describing her marriage to Tanzanian entrepreneur Isaya Yunge as an unexpected divine redirection.

Addressing worshippers in Ngorongoro on Sunday, November 8, 2026, the newlywed shared how years of personal prayers for a life partner ended up taking a cross-border turn.

Speaking two days after her white wedding in Mwanza, Charlene broke into tears of gratitude as she reflected on her long wait for a companion who shared her spiritual values. She told the church assembly that while her prayers for a devout partner remained constant, her assumptions about where he would originate from were entirely upended.

“I had prayed to God for many years to give me a good husband, a husband who loves God and one who would be with me all my life,” Charlene told the congregation. “I did not know he would come from Mwanza. I thought perhaps I would marry a Kenyan or God would give me someone from that other side. But by the grace of Almighty God, He gave me Isaya.”

Charlene took time to publicly thank the local community in Mwanza for shaping her new husband’s early life, expressing appreciation for the environment that nurtured him long before their paths crossed.

“I do not have many words to say, but I am very grateful to this community for taking care of my husband until the day I met him,” she added.

Taking the podium alongside his wife, Isaya Yunge attributed his upbringing to the Africa Inland Church (AIC) Tanzania community. He credited several church families who stepped in as extended relatives—serving as uncles, aunts, and grandparents—throughout his upbringing. Yunge singled out one aunt in particular, noting that her care had been second only to that of his biological mother.

The Sunday testimony capped off an intensive week of matrimonial milestones across East Africa. On Friday, November 6, 2026, the couple tied the knot during a white wedding ceremony at the Malaika Beach Resort on the shores of Lake Victoria in Mwanza. President William Ruto and First Lady Rachel Ruto accompanied their daughter down the aisle before an audience of family members, government dignitaries, and regional leaders.

The Mwanza church wedding served as the religious culmination of a nuptial sequence that began three months earlier in Kenya. On August 8, 2026, the Ruto family hosted Yunge’s delegation for a traditional Kalenjin Koito ceremony at their Intonna Heritage Farm in Kilgoris, Narok County, establishing the customary foundation before transitioning to the church rites in Tanzania.

With the church testimonies concluded in Ngorongoro, the couple’s cross-border union formalizes a high-profile link between Kenya’s executive family and Tanzania’s entrepreneurial sector.
