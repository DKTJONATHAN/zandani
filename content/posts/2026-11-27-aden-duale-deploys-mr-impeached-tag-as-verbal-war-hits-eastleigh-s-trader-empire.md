Aden Duale did not hold back on Friday, November 27, 2026, opening a sharp verbal barrage against former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua with a direct reference to his ousted status: "Mr Impeached Gachagua." The Health Cabinet Secretary moved quickly to draw a defensive line around Nairobi's Eastleigh commercial sector, accusing Gachagua of leveraging ethnic politics to target the Somali business community.

Duale's public reaction came straight after Gachagua publicly addressed the community's economic standing, setting off an intense exchange between two of Kenya’s prominent political figures. Rather than ignoring the former Second-in-Command's social media statements, Duale met them head-on, characterizing Gachagua's political outlook as outdated and inherently divisive.

“Mr Impeached Gachagua, your political philosophy is based on tribal allegiance above national unity, promotes division instead of peaceful coexistence,” Duale stated. He added that political ambition should never serve as a shield for lawlessness or hostile rhetoric aimed at established commercial districts.

At the core of Duale’s defense was a warning against bringing business owners into high-stakes political skirmishes. Emphasizing the economic footprint of Eastleigh, Duale insisted that local entrepreneurs would not tolerate intimidation or damage to their investments under the banner of political mobilization.

“No tribal identity, political ambition, or claim to power can excuse the assault of innocent citizens or the destruction and theft of their hard-earned property in our communities,” Duale asserted, declaring firmly that the Somali Eastleigh business community would not succumb to pressure.

The direct confrontation was triggered by Gachagua’s own public post on Thursday, November 26, 2026. In that message, the former Deputy President had weighed in on the relationship between national development funds allocated to the North Eastern region since 2013 and the economic reality of Somali entrepreneurs in Nairobi.

Gachagua had argued that despite the community’s reputation for enterprise, its political leadership had failed to deliver corresponding benefits. “The Somali Community in Kenya, a community of hardworking and astute businessmen and women – has been taken for granted by its leaders for far too long,” Gachagua wrote, calling on residents to reject divisive leadership models.

Duale’s swift re-framing—anchored by the blunt "Mr Impeached" descriptor—signals a refusal among government figures to let Gachagua redefine his political profile around regional commerce. As public statements continue to fly between the two, the ongoing exchange underscores how deeply Eastleigh’s business ecosystem remains tied to Kenya’s central political debate.
