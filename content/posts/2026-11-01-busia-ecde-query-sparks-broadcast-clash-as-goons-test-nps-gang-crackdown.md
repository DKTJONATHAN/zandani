A routine public inquiry into county education spending escalated into violent intimidation on Saturday, October 31, as suspected hired goons stormed a live town hall broadcast at Alupe University in Busia County, cutting short citizen questioning and putting the National Police Service's anti-gang directives to an immediate test.

The town hall, convened under Radio Citizen’s *Utepeni* programme and hosted by presenters Josephat Odipo and Joseph Njane, was structured to allow residents direct engagement with local administrators over education, healthcare, and infrastructure. The session collapsed abruptly when a resident from Butula constituency took the microphone to ask the Busia County government to account for its early childhood development education (ECDE) funding by naming the specific schools where devolved classroom projects had been built.

Rather than receiving an administrative answer, the inquiry triggered an orchestrated uproar. Suspected political goons invaded the university grounds, forcing their way through the audience and disrupting the broadcast. The escalating hostility compelled Odipo and Njane to pull the transmission off the air to protect participants and equipment as physical confrontation halted proceedings entirely.

### The Anatomy of an Interrupted Baraza

![Snippets of the disruptions of Radio Citizen event in Busia County on October 31.](https://i.ibb.co/fGNFBPkQ/3451fa8fc3ba.webp)

The Alupe University confrontation illustrates the tightening physical perimeter around grassroots accountability in devolved units. Public barazas and syndicated outdoor broadcasts have long served as rare platforms where ward-level residents can demand verification of capital expenditure directly from political leadership.

When the Butula question focused on tangible devolved deliverables—demanding physical locations for classroom infrastructure financed through public coffers—the response shifted from public administration to physical deterrence. Residents who had gathered to cross-examine authorities on stalled clinic works and regional road grading were scattered before county representatives offered answers.

Police officers stationed in the vicinity eventually deployed into the university compound to disperse the intruders and contain the skirmishes. While law enforcement restored physical order and cleared the premises, the intervention came only after the forum was permanently shut down, leaving the fiscal questions on the record but entirely unaddressed.

### Command Liability and Political Retinues

![Snippets of the Radio Citizen event disruption at Alupe University on October 31.](https://i.ibb.co/WpBJSc1W/f02bb35caaa0.webp)

The disruption arrives amid mounting scrutiny over state tolerance for organized political interference in public and journalistic spaces. The Busia melee follows an October 2 incident in Nairobi where pro-government demonstrators attempted to breach the Standard Media Group headquarters on Mombasa Road before being turned back by police lines.

Local leaders and civic groups in Busia condemned the Alupe University chaos, asserting that devolved governance relies on the constitutional guarantee that residents can evaluate elected leadership without fear of organized reprisal.

Crucially, the incident places local security chiefs under the spotlight of a recent National Police Service directive ordering an aggressive nationwide crackdown on political gangs and hired retinues. That service-wide circular explicitly warned field commanders of severe administrative sanctions should they fail to anticipate, prevent, or decisively neutralize gang activity within their jurisdictions.

With the Butula participant's query regarding county ECDE infrastructure still unanswered, local investigators now face the dual task of identifying the sponsors behind the university raid and demonstrating whether command accountability directives will be enforced against officers who let an open-air public audit descend into violence.
