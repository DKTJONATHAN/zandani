Residents in Nyeri and Malindi who currently have to route their passport applications through Nairobi for biometric capture are set for relief as the Interior Ministry moves to upgrade local immigration offices.

Interior Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen announced that the two towns are being equipped to handle full passport registration and processing services locally. Currently, while both stations handle basic immigration queries, final biometric capture and document processing are redirected to Nairobi headquarters.

![Interior CS Kipchumba Murkomen during a past meeting with Immigration officials on October 6, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/rR1XsKr9/ceb4cb8abff8.webp)

The upcoming rollout forms part of an ongoing decentralisation framework meant to shift passport issuance away from Nyayo House. The state's regional network already spans Mombasa, Kisumu, Eldoret, Kericho, Embu, Bungoma, Kisii, Nakuru, and Garissa.

"We are well on course with the decentralisation of passport issuance, bringing this crucial document closer to where citizens live," Murkomen stated following a high-level briefing with the Directorate of Immigration Services.

![Kenyans on a queue at the immigration offices in Nairobi (left) and photo of the new Kenyan passport (right).](https://i.ibb.co/1Jf1CzNh/97421dd01300.webp)

The administrative push coincides with separate operational overhauls at Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (JKIA), where installation works for electronic gates are continuing to accelerate traveller clearance times. At the same time, the National Employment Authority (NEA) has secured a dedicated desk inside the Nairobi immigration offices to streamline exit processing for the government's foreign job placement schemes.

While the ministry confirmed that Malindi and Nyeri centres are steadily coming up, officials have yet to publish a definitive launch schedule or specify the capital outlay required to transition the partial-service offices into fully functional biometric hubs.
