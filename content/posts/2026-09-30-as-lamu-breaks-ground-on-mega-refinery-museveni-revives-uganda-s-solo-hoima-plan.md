Even as a constellation of African leaders and billionaire industrialist Aliko Dangote broke ground on a massive coastal processing plant designed to re-engineer East Africa's petroleum supply chain, Kampala is maintaining its parallel push for sovereign refining capacity.

Speaking at the launch of the Dangote East Africa Refinery in Lamu on Wednesday, September 30, Ugandan President Yoweri Museveni made it clear that his administration remains committed to developing a domestic facility in Hoima. The proposed plant, sized between 60,000 and 120,000 barrels, represents a continuation of long-standing national strategy rooted in a strict rule: Uganda will not export its crude oil without first processing it into finished petroleum products.

![President William Ruto and Nigerian businessman Aliko Dangote talk during the refinery groundbreaking ceremony in Lamu, September 30, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/RT18pN0p/38f6ff7769a7.webp)

The announcement highlights a complex matrix of regional energy ambitions. While Kenyan President William Ruto positioned the Ksh2.2 trillion Lamu project as a vital instrument to help cut Kenya's Ksh530 billion annual petroleum import bill over a 40-month construction window, Uganda's parallel roadmap underscores a desire to avoid relying on a small handful of regional refineries.

Museveni revealed that plans for the Ugandan facility had previously been discussed with Dangote, Ruto, and Tanzanian President Samia Suluhu. At the same time, the Ugandan leader raised unresolved questions regarding an earlier proposal for a refinery in Tanga, Tanzania, which had initially been contemplated alongside a planned pipeline corridor.

![Ugandan President Yoweri Museveni gives a speech during the Dangote refinery groundbreaking ceremony in Lamu, September 30, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/ks1cBNkW/ebc71ec802fc.webp)

Dangote noted that Tanga was initially weighed due to the pipeline layout before logistical advantages—including water availability, deep-sea access, and suitable land—tipped the scales in favor of Lamu. While Museveni expressed public support for the Lamu venture, he confirmed he would withhold direct financial investment for now, opting instead to seek bilateral consultations with Ruto and Suluhu regarding the stalled Tanga proposition.

With construction timelines ticking in Lamu and Hoima's capacity targets set, East Africa’s refining landscape is navigating a dual track of massive private coastal investment and fiercely guarded state-level processing goals.
