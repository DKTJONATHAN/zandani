Sandra Mbuvi is not leaving any room for negotiation when it comes to the daily habits of anyone seeking her attention.

The digital creator and entrepreneur, widely recognised online as Thickyy Sandra, used a direct question-and-answer session on Instagram to establish a firm boundary around romantic compatibility. Asked by a follower for her views on men who chew khat—known locally as jaba or muguka—Sandra made it clear that the recreational stimulant represents a complete, non-negotiable barrier to entry.

For Sandra, the habit is not a casual quirk that can be overlooked in the name of compromise; it is an outright disqualifier.

"Personally, I could never date or marry a man who chews jaba. I find it so unattractive and honestly disgusting it just doesn't align with my standards or values. Immediate turn-off," Sandra said.

### Habit as a Compatibility Filter

The consumption of jaba remains a deeply entrenched and debated social habit across urban Kenya, often cutting across social classes and age groups. Yet Sandra’s dismissal of the custom was absolute, extending beyond casual courting into long-term commitment. By specifically highlighting marriage alongside dating, she framed personal aesthetic and lifestyle choices as core values rather than superficial preferences.

Her position reflects a deliberate approach to personal boundaries that she carried throughout the digital exchange. Rather than deflecting personal inquiries, she treated her follower interactions as an audit of the lifestyle standards she chooses to maintain.

When another follower pressed her on what fuels her confident public image and asked what inspires her to embody a "baddie" persona, Sandra pointed away from external applause. She maintained that her outward posture is dictated entirely by an internal blueprint.

"Honestly? The vision I have for myself," she explained. "I love being THAT girl confident, independent, feminine, ambitious and unapologetically myself. I inspire myself. I'm always chasing a better version of me. Pretty face, big dreams, bigger standards."

### Blueprint for New Enterprises

![Raburu’s Key Takeaways After Attending Bien’s Album Watch Party](https://i.ibb.co/G4KZ7wTR/942e420141a6.webp)

That same insistence on clear-headed discipline carried over when the conversation shifted from personal relationships to commercial ventures. Aspiring business founders asked Sandra for practical direction on launching a beauty enterprise, a sector marked by intense competition and low barrier to entry in Nairobi.

Rather than offering vague encouragement, Sandra outlined a grounded operational approach built around patience, continuous learning, and capital discipline.

"My advice would be to start with what you have, learn your craft, be consistent and don't rush the process," she advised. "Build your brand, understand your customers and reinvest in your business. Most importantly, don't let competition intimidate you. There's room for everyone to win. Start small, but think big!"

Her commercial guidance emphasized self-reliance over quick returns, urging young women entering the cosmetics and beauty trade to reinvest their earnings back into their enterprises rather than siphoning cash early.

Across both romance and commerce, Sandra's exchange delivered a consistent message to her audience: setting strict boundaries is not an exercise in arrogance, but an essential mechanism for protecting personal and professional growth.
