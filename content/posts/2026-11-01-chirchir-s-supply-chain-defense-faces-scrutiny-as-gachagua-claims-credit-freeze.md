Kenya's flagship Ksh200 billion Rironi-Mau Summit highway expansion has been drawn into an acute transparency dispute, with the Ministry of Roads and Transport defending a pushed-back delivery timetable on supply-chain shocks while political opponents allege an abrupt foreign financing halt.

The friction centers on why a critical 60-kilometer section of the Western transit corridor failed to open on its promised October 30 milestone, forcing transport planners to push targeted completion into early December 2026.

### The State's Supply Chain Rationale

Appearing before a Senate committee on Friday, October 30, Transport Cabinet Secretary Davis Chirchir formally notified lawmakers that the initial stretch would miss its scheduled opening. During a follow-up inspection tour of the works on Saturday, October 31, the Cabinet Secretary attributed the slippage to international raw material deficits rather than domestic liquidity problems.

![Road construction and tarmac laying along the Rironi-Mau Summit highway near the Ngarariga Interchange.](https://i.ibb.co/SDsNjTRR/225b3127aef7.webp)

Chirchir stated that procurement of bitumen—a petroleum-derived binding agent indispensable for asphalt concrete—had experienced acute bottlenecks over the preceding two months. He pinned the shortfall on broader geopolitical crosscurrents and shipping interruptions, though he maintained that the ministry had resolved the bottleneck and secured sufficient stock to support aggressive surfacing.

Under the ministry’s revised operational plan, the road builders were directed to lay roughly four kilometers of tarmac daily to recover lost ground ahead of the December deadline.

### Claims of an Election-Linked Credit Halt

That logistical explanation was publicly challenged on Sunday, November 1, by Democracy for Citizens Party (DCP) leader Rigathi Gachagua. Speaking during a church service in Kikuyu, Kiambu County, the former deputy president claimed the road is running out of money because bilateral financiers pulled back.

According to Gachagua, Chinese state financial institutions behind the Ksh200 billion undertaking declined to disburse additional tranches, allegedly reassessing the corridor as an exposed political liability in the lead-up to next year's general election.

"The contractors simply have no money to continue the work," Gachagua argued, dismissing Chirchir's reference to global bitumen markets and geopolitical friction. He maintained that site activity has slowed because the primary contractor’s funding taps were closed by lenders anxious over political continuity.

![Construction of the Rironi-Mau Highway, with an inset of DCP Party Leader Rigathi Gachagua.](https://i.ibb.co/PGXVgQ9p/91588020e160.webp)

He further demanded that the two Chinese contractors involved in the project account for state disbursements by October next year following the scheduled transition of power.

### Corridor Pressure and Unanswered Ledger Questions

The competing claims land on a transit corridor that remains one of the country's most heavily congested economic arteries, carrying bulk freight from the Port of Mombasa toward the Rift Valley, Western Kenya, and landlocked regional neighbors.

While Chirchir’s inspection team signaled confidence that an accelerated four-kilometer daily paving rate would salvage the 60-kilometer stretch before year-end, the funding mechanics of the overall mega-project remain under heavy public scrutiny.

Neither the Ministry of Roads and Transport nor the Chinese firms executing the expansion had issued a formal public response to the financing freeze claims by Sunday evening, leaving motorists and freight operators watching the pavement meters at Ngarariga Interchange to see whether equipment actually rolls at the accelerated pace promised by the state.
