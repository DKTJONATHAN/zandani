Kenya Power has engineered a Ksh 21.1 billion working capital turnaround, transforming a Ksh 19.21 billion liquidity deficit in June 2025 into a Ksh 1.90 billion surplus for the financial year ended June 30, 2026.

The liquidity reversal anchors the electricity distributor’s full-year financial statements published ahead of its 105th Annual General Meeting. Net profit for the period reached Ksh 36.01 billion, while total revenue grew 8.65 per cent to Ksh 238.24 billion, up from Ksh 219.28 billion recorded in the previous financial year.

![Kenya Power building in Nairobi CBD.](https://i.ibb.co/S4526wSQ/7e6c7f61b59e.webp)

The balance sheet recovery directly altered the utility’s market valuation. Kenya Power’s volume-weighted average share price rose from Ksh 11.50 in June 2025 to Ksh 17.15 in June 2026, marking a 49.13 per cent gain. On the back of improved liquidity and lower debt leverage, the Board of Directors recommended a final dividend of Ksh 1.20 per ordinary share, sustaining the dividend payouts that resumed in the 2023/2024 financial year.

Kenya Power Chairperson Joy Brenda Masinde attributed the financial trajectory to targeted efforts aimed at tightening operational costs, strengthening liquidity, and expanding network infrastructure to capture growing consumer demand.

However, the balance sheet improvement was tested by rising operational outlays. The cost of purchasing bulk electricity from power producers rose to Ksh 152.65 billion, up from Ksh 144.66 billion in the prior financial year. The company cited higher total electricity consumption and expanding peak demand across the nationwide network as the primary drivers of the elevated procurement bill.

![Kenya Power Managing Director and CEO Joseph Siror speaking at Stima Plaza on February 20, 2024](https://i.ibb.co/LdddX0Vx/fae9b6a816b4.webp)

Managing bulk power costs remains tightly linked to system stability. Managing Director and CEO Eng Joseph Siror previously cautioned that variable renewable energy sources—specifically solar and wind—accounted for 34 per cent of grid power during peak periods and 36 per cent during off-peak hours. Siror urged increased capital investment in firm base-load generation, such as geothermal and hydro assets, to prevent frequency instability as variable inputs grow.

Shareholders are scheduled to vote on the proposed Ksh 1.20 dividend during the 105th AGM on Friday, November 27, 2026. If approved, the payout will be executed on or about December 31, 2026, for investors registered by the close of business on November 27.
