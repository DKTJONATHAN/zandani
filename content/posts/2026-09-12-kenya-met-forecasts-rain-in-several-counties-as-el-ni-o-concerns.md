Nairobi, September 12, 2026 - The Kenya Meteorological Department has issued a 24-hour weather advisory as anxiety intensifies over the looming El Niño phenomenon, with the state placing the probability of the weather event at 81 percent.

While the acting director, Edward Muriuki, reports that most of the country will remain generally dry through this evening, active rainfall is expected across thirteen specific counties. The wet weather is concentrated in the western highlands-including Kericho, Kakamega, Vihiga, Nandi, Uasin Gishu, Bungoma, and Trans Nzoia-and along the coast in Mombasa, Lamu, Kilifi, and Kwale.

In Nairobi, residents are experiencing a cycle of morning cloud cover and isolated light showers, with temperatures oscillating between 14°C and 24°C. The Mount Kenya region, specifically Meru, faces the most consistent activity, with intermittent showers expected from overnight through Saturday afternoon. Further west, Kisii and Nyamira are bracing for afternoon thunderstorms, with thermometers hitting a high of 29°C in Kisii.

Confirmation of this localized rainfall comes as the government identifies 18 counties as high-risk zones for the anticipated El Niño surge. Meteorologists maintain a 97 percent probability that the effects of these extreme weather patterns will persist well into early 2027. Despite the current forecast for dry spells in northern and central regions, infrastructure preparedness remains the primary focus of national risk assessments.
