According to court filings and public confirmations released on Thursday, September 24, by legal advocacy lobby Sheria Mtaani, thousands of freehold and leasehold property owners in Kajiado County have secured immediate protection from county revenue collection after the Environment and Land Court suspended the implementation of the Kajiado County Finance Act, 2026.

The conservatory orders abruptly halt the devolved administration’s push to levy rates across titled land parcels, insulating rural ancestral holders and urban property developers who faced escalated financial assessments.

Lawyer Shadrack Wambui, who heads Sheria Mtaani and is seeking the Kajiado North parliamentary seat on a Democracy for Citizens Party (DCP) ticket, confirmed that the court stepped in to restrain the county from executing the levies until the substantive petition is fully argued and determined.

“The Kajiado Environment and Land Court has again stopped the imposition of land rates on all freehold and leasehold land in Kajiado. This order effectively suspends the Kajiado Finance Act, 2026 until our petition by Sheria Mtaani is heard and determined,” Wambui stated.

The immediate beneficiaries of the judicial intervention are landowners holding ancestral titles as well as urban plot owners who had been earmarked for revised property collections under the new fiscal legislation.

“No citizen should pay illegal and punitive rates on their own ancestral land. We went to court for you, and the court has agreed with us,” Wambui said.

### Repeated Statutory Hurdles

The judicial halt marks the second time in nine months that Kajiado County’s property tax apparatus has collapsed under legal scrutiny. In January, Justice M.D. Mwangi delivered a judgment holding that the county administration failed to follow mandatory statutory frameworks when introducing and altering land rates under the earlier Kajiado County Finance Act, 2023.

That earlier judgment declared the contentious rate provisions unconstitutional, null, and void. Justice Mwangi specifically ruled that the county lacked a lawful foundation to vary land rents upward from the figures formally specified in initial allotment letters.

The court also determined that the devolved unit bypassed obligatory property valuation procedures and failed to conduct the rigorous, specific public participation required prior to levying or altering charges on private land.

Following that January judgment, Sheria Mtaani organized an initiative in February where eligible landholders were guided to formally tender standard rates of Ksh1,500, anchoring their compliance strictly on validated legal terms rather than the county’s revised schedules.

### The April Policy Reversal

![An aerial view of Ongata Rongai town in Kajiado County.](https://i.ibb.co/JjXrr2r8/51136125d3a5.webp)

The dispute reignited in April when the Kajiado County Assembly enacted the Finance Act, 2026, which reintroduced land rates on freehold land parcels despite the prior judicial prohibitions.

The legislative maneuver drew immediate resistance from local communities and legal advocates, culminating in a town hall meeting in Matasia where residents voiced opposition to the recurrent levies.

Sheria Mtaani subsequently moved back to the Environment and Land Court, arguing that the county government had effectively reenacted discarded provisions without rectifying the baseline constitutional flaws identified by the bench—chiefly the absence of an authenticated valuation roll and genuine public concurrence.

### Revenue Machinery on Hold

The suspension of the Finance Act, 2026, creates an immediate fiscal challenge for the Kajiado County Government, which relies heavily on land rates to anchor its local revenue targets in rapidly growing peri-urban hubs such as Ngong, Kitengela, and Ongata Rongai.

By subjecting both freehold ancestral land and leasehold commercial properties to uniform collection drives, the county aimed to broaden its domestic tax yield. However, the court’s intervention deprives revenue officers of an enforcement mechanism, barring them from issuing compliance notices, imposing penalties, or demanding clearance certificates under the 2026 statute.

The matter now moves toward a full inter-partes hearing at the Kajiado Environment and Land Court, where county attorneys will be required to justify how the contested rates were drafted and whether the devolved government adhered to valuation laws before gazetting the 2026 measures.
