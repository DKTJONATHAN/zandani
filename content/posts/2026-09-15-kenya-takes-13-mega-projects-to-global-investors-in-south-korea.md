The Public-Private Partnership Directorate announced on Tuesday that Kenya has pitched a portfolio of 13 flagship infrastructure ventures in Seoul, positioning local motorists, industrial manufacturers, and tech entrepreneurs to benefit from fresh foreign direct investment.

### What we know

* Treasury is shifting decisively away from sovereign borrowing, banking on private concessions to deliver multi-billion-shilling transport corridors without straining fiscal ceilings.
* Seoul has cemented its role as Nairobi's preferred partner for high-tech ventures, giving Kenya access to advanced engineering capital for industrial digitisation.
* Local pension schemes are being courted alongside foreign financiers, potentially deploying dormant retirement savings into state utility assets for guaranteed yields.
* Re-tendering stalled legacy contracts under restructured partnership terms signals a pragmatic push to revive stranded state assets rather than abandoning past feasibility outlays.

Director General of the PPP Directorate, Kefa Seda, stated during the Global Infrastructure Cooperation Conference 2026 that the state is actively engaging global financiers to underwrite critical national utilities during the current fiscal cycle.

“Our country is taking a substantive pipeline of investment opportunities to the market across transport, energy, water, irrigation, ICT and other strategic sectors, with 13 priority projects identified for active market engagement in FY2026/27,” Seda noted in an official communique.

The government's pitch places heavy emphasis on dual transit arteries: the proposed Nairobi-Mombasa Expressway and the Mau Summit-Eldoret-Malaba highway corridor, both designated as commercial toll ventures aimed at unlocking transit logistics across East Africa.

Beyond road networks, the delegation presented plans for the Konza Technopolis cloud expansion and an adjacent 160-acre Digital Media City, an initiative structured with South Korean technological support to anchor creative and digital industry growth.

The energy and maritime portfolios feature the revival of the Kibuka Falls Hydropower and Irrigation Dam under a revised concession model, alongside a 1,200-megawatt liquefied natural gas power plant at Dongo Kundu in Mombasa. Global financiers were also invited to evaluate concessions across Kenya Ports Authority facilities, bulk power transmission lines, and the proposed Kenya Commercial Spaceport slated for the coastal Kipini zone between Lamu and Malindi.

State economic planners are concurrently operationalising the National Infrastructure Fund, a vehicle structured to de-risk these concessions by pooling domestic liquidity from local insurers and institutional pension managers alongside international equity. By anchoring these heavy outlays outside the consolidated national debt register, Nairobi is attempting to insulate state fiscal operations while accelerating long-deferred commercial masterplans.
