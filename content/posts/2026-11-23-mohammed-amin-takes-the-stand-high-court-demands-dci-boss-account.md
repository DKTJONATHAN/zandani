Director of Criminal Investigations Mohammed Amin finds himself at the centre of an escalating High Court push, personally summoned to brief Justice Alexander Muteti on the ongoing search for missing Tanzanian activist Twaha Mwaipaya. The directive, issued on Monday, November 23, 2026, places the DCI boss directly on the stand to provide concrete evidence regarding Mwaipaya's whereabouts by December 1.

Justice Muteti cited the gravity of the matter, underscoring the need for investigators to present tangible proof. This specific summons for Amin follows an application by Mwaipaya's legal team, who requested that lead investigators be compelled to testify. The activist’s lawyers also confirmed they have witnesses prepared to take the stand, including officials from the Independent Policing Oversight Authority (IPOA) and individuals under witness protection, whose identities will be shielded.

This judicial insistence on a direct report from the DCI's top office comes weeks after Inspector General of Police Douglas Kanja appeared before Justice Muteti on November 3. Kanja had denied any police involvement in Mwaipaya’s disappearance, maintaining that the National Police Service had neither arrested nor held the activist anywhere in the country. He acknowledged, however, that a missing person report had been filed at Ruai Police Station and was under active investigation.

Twaha Mwaipaya was reportedly abducted on October 25, 2026, in Kamulu, Machakos County. Following his disappearance, habeas corpus proceedings were initiated, compelling state authorities to account for him. On October 30, Justice Muteti had ordered police to produce Mwaipaya in court—alive or dead—or present valid detention warrants.

With the matter resuming on December 1, all eyes will be on DCI boss Mohammed Amin as he takes the stand to provide the court with an update on the high-stakes investigation.
