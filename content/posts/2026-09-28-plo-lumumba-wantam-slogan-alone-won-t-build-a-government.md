Public intellectual and constitutional lawyer PLO Lumumba has training sights on the rising wave of anti-incumbent rhetoric, warning that opposition hopefuls hammering the single-term exit strategy are offering Kenyans an intellectual blank cheque.

As political realignments gather pace ahead of the 2027 General Election, the catchphrase “Wantam”—demanding that President William Ruto serves only a single term—has gained traction among opposition circles. But Lumumba argues that reducing a national presidential bid to a simple removal exercise misses the fundamental point of governance.

Speaking on the evolving political landscape, the lawyer asserted that replacing the current occupant of State House without a documented, coherent blueprint for the economy, education, and infrastructure amounts to little more than political musical chairs. He noted that contemporary campaigns continue to lean heavily on familiar patterns of ethnic mobilisation and high-level coalition shifting, where influential figures swap political vehicles while the underlying governance philosophy remains untouched.

Lumumba challenged aspirants positioning themselves for the ultimate office to lay bare their development programmes. According to the public intellectual, voters deserve to evaluate competing, measurable manifestos rather than being herded into camps defined purely by who they oppose.

The sharp critique lands as various political factions test alliances and early strategies for 2027, highlighting a growing tension between populist slogans and the demand for substantive policy debate as the electoral calendar advances.
