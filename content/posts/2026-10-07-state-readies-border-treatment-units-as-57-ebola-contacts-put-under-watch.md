Kenya has activated treatment centres across border counties and stepped up screening at 15 points of entry as health officials begin managing an expanding tracing net triggered by the country’s first confirmed imported Ebola case.

The containment apparatus has placed 10 people in quarantine while tracking 57 documented contacts, with authorities warning that field tallies are expected to rise as investigations widen across county transit routes.

Speaking on Wednesday, October 7, 2026, during a multi-agency stakeholder briefing at Afya House in Nairobi, Public Health and Professional Standards Principal Secretary Mary Muthoni emphasized that the current response is calibrated to prevent an imported infection from establishing local transmission chains.

“We do not have an outbreak; we have an imported case which is under management,” Muthoni said, confirming that all national and devolved health surveillance officers have been moved to active response status.

![Public Health Principal Secretary Mary Muthoni speaks during a public health stakeholders’ forum in Nairobi, April 22, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/0bXDXSH/4acb622e9b53.webp)

### Decentralising Isolation and Border Screening

Rather than centralizing management entirely within Nairobi, the state’s emergency plan hinges on establishing clinical containment closer to frontier transit corridors. Specialized treatment units are currently being brought online in counties adjoining regional borders to ensure suspected cases can be isolated and managed without requiring long-distance transfers to the capital.

Director General for Health Dr. Patrick Amoth indicated that while primary quarantine centers are already receiving individuals, the ministry has established protocols to erect rapid makeshift isolation centers if contact numbers outpace designated institutional wards.

“Adequate quarantine capacity has been put in place, and makeshift facilities could be established in areas where the need arises as the response progresses,” Amoth said.

### County and Faith-Based Network Coordination

A critical pillar of the operational ramp-up involves integrating county government facilities and faith-based healthcare providers into a unified reporting network. Because peripheral faith-based hospitals often serve as the primary clinical intake point in remote border jurisdictions, standardizing their screening protocols remains essential to catching secondary cases early.

“The ministry is working closely with the county governments and faith-based health facilities to strengthen preparedness and ensure that the country responds effectively should the number increase,” Muthoni stated.

The Ministry of Health and devolved authorities have scheduled rolling multi-stakeholder review sessions in the coming days to assess field surveillance data, close emerging logistics gaps, and determine whether the perimeter of the 15 monitored border points requires further expansion.
