Kenyatta University became the focus of intense scrutiny this Sunday following the circulation of a viral video alleging procedural irregularities during a localized voter registration drive. The Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) has since moved to dismiss the claims, affirming that the exercise remains strictly within the bounds of the law.

The sequence of events began late Saturday when footage surfaced on social media platforms, suggesting unauthorized registration activity on the university grounds. Observers questioned the deployment of mobile kits, prompting a direct response from the commission. IEBC Chairperson Erastus Edung Ethekon maintained that the activity was a standard component of the Continuous Voter Registration (CVR) framework, designed specifically to capture the youth demographic at high-density institutions.

To dispel rumors of manipulation, Ethekon issued a formal clarification on the commission’s operational protocols:

*   **Registration Zones:** Mobile units are authorized for strategic outreaches at universities, supplementing the permanent services available at all 290 constituency offices, Huduma Centres, and the Anniversary Towers headquarters.
*   **Restricted Transfers:** All requests for voter transfers are prohibited at mobile desks; these must be processed exclusively at official constituency offices.
*   **Documentation:** Only new registrants are issued physical acknowledgment slips. Existing voters seeking transfers are already verified within the central database.

The commission has confirmed it is currently collaborating with law enforcement to identify the individuals behind the viral footage. IEBC officials have warned that any misinformation intended to undermine electoral integrity will be met with legal action.

At least 290 constituency offices remain the primary point of contact for voters seeking verification. The commission maintains that it will continue its outreach program across all strategic institutions as scheduled.
