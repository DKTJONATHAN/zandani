Contractors on the 60-kilometre Rironi–Naivasha corridor have been ordered to nearly double their civil engineering pace to lay one kilometre of road daily, as the Transport ministry attempts to open the dual carriageway ahead of the festive travel surge despite acknowledging that safety fixtures will remain incomplete.

The revised output schedule, confirmed by Transport Cabinet Secretary Davis Chirchir, requires China Road and Bridge Corporation to step up execution from an average rate of four kilometres a week to seven. The operational compression comes after the government breached an earlier pledge made in September to commission the section by October 30, forcing authorities to push public access into early December 2026.

To meet the condensed turnaround, the ministry is uncoupling surface asphalt works from final roadside installations, opting to let traffic onto the corridor before secondary infrastructure is fully in place.

### Machinery Surge at Mombasa Port

![An image of ongoing construction works on the Rironi-Mau Summit captured on September 23, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/Ng5ZHTPg/e249a7a31fd1.webp)

The ramped-up schedule relies on resolving severe material and plant deficits that stalled earthworks earlier in the cycle. Ten heavy asphalt pavers have landed at the Port of Mombasa to reinforce mechanical operations, accompanied by an easing of raw bitumen supply chain chokepoints that previously crippled surfacing schedules.

More than 90 individual work teams are currently stationed along the escarpment route. The ministry's revised engineering sequence prioritises finishing the compacted structural road base across the entire 60-kilometre alignment before running a high-speed top polishing layer over the subgrade.

According to Chirchir, stabilizing the base course allows the paving machinery to lay the final asphalt carpet continuously without having to stop for structural civil works. However, the ministry confirmed that while riding surfaces are projected to carry holiday traffic within weeks, peripheral road furniture—such as lane demarcations, crash barriers, and signage—will continue to be installed after the carriageway opens.

### Accountability Pressure Following Missed Milestones

![CS Chirchir](https://i.ibb.co/5XkMqF4k/ed3168ab4acb.webp)

The operational scramble follows an October 30 site audit conducted alongside the National Assembly’s Transport and Infrastructure Committee in Naivasha. During that inspection, parliamentary monitors questioned the timeline slippage after motorists spent weeks negotiating chronic gridlock along the construction corridor.

The Rironi–Naivasha stretch represents the first major operational segment of the broader Ksh200 billion Rironi–Mau Summit highway expansion. Implemented as a public-private partnership, the capital-intensive project is designed to widen the Northern Corridor transit artery to eliminate endemic transport chokepoints between Nairobi, the Rift Valley, and western transit borders.

While the state maintains that the first phase remains broadly aligned with overall contract parameters, the rapid pace now demanded on the Naivasha segment exposes the administrative pressure facing project managers to avert year-end travel gridlock on Kenya's primary trunk road.

Beyond the December operational target for the initial 60 kilometres, the wider corridor expansion to Mau Summit remains scheduled for overall completion by June 2027, when the entire artery will transition to direct toll charges for private and commercial transit.
