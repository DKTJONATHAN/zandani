A proposed regulatory overhaul at Kenya’s entry and exit points will directly link revenue collection databases with immigration and police tracking systems, creating a synchronized screening network across land, air, and sea borders.

The Border Control and Operations Coordination Committee opened a 14-day public participation window on Friday, October 2, inviting written submissions on the draft Kenya Citizenship and Immigration (Border Control and Operations Co-ordination) Regulations, 2026. Members of the public, commercial logistics providers, and trade representatives have until October 16, 2026, to file memoranda with the Border Management Secretariat located at Nyayo House along Uhuru Highway in Nairobi, or through official digital channels.

![Hundreds of Burundian nationals line up at the Burundi Embassy in Kenya to obtain emergency travel documents on Monday, September 7, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/Kx2bQFtr/b97364fe4105.webp)

Under the draft framework, the administrative boundaries that previously separated customs auditing from passenger clearance will give way to a structured, real-time intelligence exchange. The rules mandate coordinated monitoring among the Directorate of Immigration Services, the Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA), the National Police Service, and the Kenya Ports Authority (KPA). By integrating modern border tracking technology across all entry points, state agencies will be able to cross-reference cargo manifests, foreign travel histories, and tax compliance records instantaneously during border clearance.

This push for real-time inter-agency data sharing comes amid heightened executive focus on foreign business operations within the domestic market. The government recently issued a 90-day ultimatum requiring foreign nationals engaged in small-scale commerce to fully regularise their immigration permits, work authorisations, local business registrations, and tax documentation. Linking KRA datasets directly into border operational management ensures that commercial compliance and immigration status are evaluated simultaneously as individuals and goods enter or leave the country.

![Interior CS Kipchumba Murkomen and Deputy Inspector General of Police CDIG) Gilbert Masengeli during the launch of the Saboti Sub-County DCC Office on October 1, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/Q79MP12n/0ff5f0ec3637.webp)

Once the public consultation window closes at the end of business on October 16, 2026, the Border Control and Operations Coordination Committee will review the submitted memoranda before finalizing the statutory instrument for formal Parliamentary tabling and statutory enforcement.
