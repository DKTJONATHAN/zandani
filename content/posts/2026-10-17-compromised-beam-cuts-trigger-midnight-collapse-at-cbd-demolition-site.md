Emergency response teams and police investigators have pointed to compromised structural demolition procedures as the trigger behind a midnight building collapse along Moi Avenue that left four workers injured and sent rescue teams sifting through rubble in the heart of Nairobi's Central Business District.

The multi-storey structure, which formerly housed Club Avenida, caved in at approximately 12:00 a.m. on Saturday, October 17. The commercial property sits directly adjacent to Mount Kenya University's Union Towers and directly opposite Mateo Chambers, which houses the Magereza DT Sacco headquarters.

Preliminary site assessments indicate that contracted workers were carrying out teardown operations to clear the plot for planned commercial redevelopment when structural failure struck. Crews were cutting load-bearing metal support beams on a less stable wing of the building—directly adjacent to a compromised pavement section—when the remaining framework gave way, bringing the overhead masonry down in seconds.

![Emergency responders at the scene after a building collapsed along Moi Avenue, next to MKU Union Towers in Nairobi CBD, October 17, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/bMDcRWY7/9157daf567bd.webp)

### Twenty-Five Trapped in Active Demolition Zone

Witness statements established that an estimated 25 workers were inside the perimeter when the structural support failed. While night-shift demolition is frequently scheduled along busy transit corridors to mitigate vehicular disruption, the presence of more than two dozen workers inside a building undergoing structural steel cutting raised immediate concerns regarding site safety and progressive clearing protocols.

First responders from emergency medical services and National Police Service units arrived shortly after midnight to establish a cordon and initiate search operations through twisted metal and broken slabs. Paramedics treated four injured personnel extracted from the perimeter and transferred them to nearby medical facilities.

By approximately 1:00 a.m., field teams had accounted for 25 individuals who had been on site during the teardown. 

![Emergency responders at the scene after a building collapsed along Moi Avenue, next to MKU Union Towers in Nairobi CBD, October 17, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/PV5ZWHD/4ecb34d18e39.webp)

### Unresolved Search in Central District

Despite the initial headcount, emergency crews sustained active search operations through the early morning hours, responding to reports that an additional man remained trapped under the collapsed section.

The incident shifts immediate scrutiny onto Nairobi's urban planning and workplace safety regulators over oversight standards applied to nighttime demolition permits. Cutting primary structural supports while personnel operate directly underneath presents severe workplace hazards, particularly along dense urban corridors like Moi Avenue where adjacent commercial structures, vehicular lanes, and pedestrian pavements sit inches from the site footprint.

Rescue workers and police remained on site at daybreak to clear debris and conclude the search for the unaccounted-for individual, with an official technical review expected into the licensing and engineering approvals governing the teardown.
