Cargo handlers at the Port of Mombasa and the Inland Container Depot in Embakasi are shifting toward point-to-point logistics following the deployment of a dedicated single-carrier freight service designed to bypass intermediate sorting delays.

The inaugural CMA CGM block train departed the coast on Wednesday, carrying 108 twenty-foot equivalent units (TEUs) in a single uninterrupted movement. The launch event was overseen by Transport Principal Secretary Mohamed Daghar and France’s Ambassador to Kenya, Wadid Benaabou.

![A collage of the launch of the CMA CGM block train in Mombasa on October 28, with representatives of Kenya Railways and CMA CGM.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/kenya-railways-cma-cgm-group.jpg.webp?itok=8FH02nUU)

Unlike conventional freight runs that require complex sorting and mid-journey splitting, a block train transports common cargo bound for a single destination pool. Under the new arrangement negotiated between Kenya Railways, the Kenya Ports Authority (KPA), and shipping line CMA CGM, the dedicated rakes carry exclusively CMA CGM containers in pre-allocated blocks ranging between 50 and 100-plus TEUs per trip.

Coordinating the service requires synchronized oversight across vessel discharge, container segregation, rail transfer, and final unloading. KPA, Kenya Railways, and the shipping line share operational duties to streamline handling from maritime vessels directly onto the Standard Gauge Railway network.

Logistics planners note that bypassing intermediate handling significantly reduces port dwell times, a persistent bottleneck for regional supply chains. Additionally, moving the freight en masse by rail rather than long-distance trucking cuts greenhouse gas emissions by 50 to 67 per cent.

![An undated image of police officers at a Kenya Railways terminal](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/Police%20officers%20at%20a%20Kenya%20Railways%20terminal.jpg.webp?itok=w5lj1WBS)

The new service integrates into existing SGR and MGR Madaraka Freight operations, which currently handle between nine and ten block formations daily. Kenya joins an international roster utilizing similar dedicated rail corridors operated by CMA CGM, including networks in Mexico, India, China, Brazil, and Southern African routes linking Beira and Harare.

With the corridor now operational, Kenya Railways is pressing importers, exporters, and shipping lines to adopt the dedicated freight solution. State planners anticipate that tightening the Mombasa-Nairobi rail loop will lower operating overheads for transit cargo destined beyond Kenya's borders, reinforcing distribution channels into Uganda, South Sudan, Burundi, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo.
