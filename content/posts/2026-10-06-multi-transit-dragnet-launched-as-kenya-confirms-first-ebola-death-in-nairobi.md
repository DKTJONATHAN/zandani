A regional contact-tracing operation is underway across aviation, road transport, and Nairobi healthcare facilities after Kenya confirmed its first imported case of Ebola virus disease, involving a patient who cleared point-of-entry screening at Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (JKIA) before succumbing to the illness.

Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale announced on Tuesday that the patient, who died at Nairobi Hospital late Monday night, contracted the Ebola Bundibugyo virus strain after living and working in the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) for seven years.

### The Travel Footprint

The case highlights a complex multi-border transit sequence that spanned over a month. The patient first fell ill roughly four weeks ago while in the DRC and sought treatment at several medical facilities in the country before embarking on a journey back to Kenya.

According to the Ministry of Health, the individual traveled by road from the DRC into Uganda. On Saturday, October 3, the patient boarded a Jambojet flight to Nairobi, landing at JKIA at 1:10 pm with 23 other passengers and four crew members on board.

![A section of the Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (JKIA)](https://i.ibb.co/Lz7Y21Ry/d020d568f6a6.webp)

Upon landing, the traveler went through routine public health screening at the airport's health desks and was cleared through immigration control. Relatives and friends subsequently picked up the patient in a private Toyota Fielder and drove directly to Nairobi Hospital for medical attention.

### Diagnosis and Isolation

Medical staff at Nairobi Hospital immediately placed the patient in an isolated room after observing advanced clinical symptoms, including persistent fever, severe fatigue, muscular pain, painful swallowing, chills, and visible bleeding under the skin.

Recognizing the patient’s travel history and severe presentation, clinical teams managed the case as suspected viral hemorrhagic fever and drawn samples for laboratory analysis. Dual testing conducted independently by the National Virology Reference Laboratory under the National Public Health Institute and the Kenya Medical Research Institute (KEMRI) yielded positive results for Ebola Bundibugyo.

Despite receiving supportive clinical care in isolation, the patient died at 11:30 pm on Monday, October 5. Health officials confirmed that safe and dignified burial arrangements were set for Tuesday in strict compliance with international Ebola safety protocols.

### Tracking the Contacts

The Ministry of Health has initiated a multi-layered contact-tracing dragnet to prevent secondary transmission within the capital and along the transit route.

Surveillance teams have established a primary line of monitoring around 28 direct contacts. This group comprises family members who met the patient and healthcare personnel who provided direct care at Nairobi Hospital before and after the diagnosis.

Concurrently, public health officials are attempting to trace all 27 individuals who shared the Jambojet flight from Uganda—specifically the 23 fellow passengers and four crew members—to place them under mandatory health monitoring schedules.

To manage potential containment needs, Duale stated that the government has trained 4,971 health workers across both national and county levels on viral hemorrhagic fever management and officially notified the World Health Organization (WHO).

The primary focus remains on rapidly locating the remaining flight occupants and completing the initial 21-day observation window for all primary contacts.
