EALA MP Winnie Odinga has drawn a hard boundary for politicians planning to attend her late father’s upcoming first death anniversary, demanding an immediate end to high-table grandstanding and crowd-shouting rivalries inside the family’s ancestral compound.

Speaking on Wednesday, October 7, 2026, during the official handover and dedication of former Prime Minister Raila Odinga’s mausoleum at Kang’o Ka Jaramogi in Bondo, Siaya County, Winnie made it clear that the upcoming October 15 memorial service will not serve as a venue for factional political warfare.

As political groups attempt to claim ownership of the veteran leader’s movement and legacy, Winnie warned against turning the sacred family gathering into a public arena for political ego.

### 'Nobody Is Asked to Surrender Their Beliefs at the Gate'

The dedication of the mausoleum in Bondo provided the backdrop for Winnie’s formal plea to political leaders across the spectrum. With figures from opposing camps expected to travel to Siaya County for the anniversary, the lawmaker addressed the escalating tension directly, emphasizing that political differences should be set aside at the entrance of Kang’o Ka Jaramogi.

“We may hold political convictions, different ones. We may disagree strongly about the direction of our country. But you are welcome here,” Winnie stated during the dedication ceremony. “Nobody is being asked to surrender their beliefs at the gate. As his family, we call for a political ceasefire in his honour.”

The statement directly targets the friction brewing among national politicians as the October 15 date approaches. Rather than allowing leaders to use the gathering as a platform for political optics, the Odinga family is insisting on a temporary cessation of hostile posturing.

### Clamping Down on 'Crowd Ownership' and High-Table Optics

Winnie was particularly explicit about the behavioral expectations for political delegations and their entourages arriving in Bondo. She warned against turning the main memorial event into a spectacle designed to measure political clout through arranged applause or public embarrassment.

“Please let October 15 be a day of remembrance and celebration. It should not become a political showground, a competition over who owns the crowd, who receives the loudest cheers, or who can embarrass another guest,” she urged.

The warning underscores a growing concern within the family that competing factions seeking political dominance might exploit the media presence at the memorial for individual leverage, detracting from the primary focus of honoring Raila's memory.

### Prioritizing Grassroots Supporters Over Entourages

Shifting the focus away from political dignitaries and VIP escorts, Winnie redirected the memorial's protocol toward the ordinary citizens who formed the bedrock of her father's decades-long political career. She noted that while politicians often dominate high tables and media coverage, the core of Raila's backing came from unheralded supporters who stood by him without seeking official titles or state protection.

“Many of Baba’s people had no titles. They had no escorts, and they had no access to the high table. They stood in the rain. They travelled far. They prayed for him. They believed that their country could be better,” Winnie said.

Inviting grassroots supporters to bring their traditional songs, dances, poems, and personal memories to Kang’o Ka Jaramogi, she added, “We want the people who filled his life to help fill that day.”

Reflecting on her family’s personal loss as the October 15, 2026 anniversary approaches, Winnie concluded with a call to honor the fundamental democratic struggles that defined her father’s political career: “If freedom has a price, let us respect those who paid it.”
