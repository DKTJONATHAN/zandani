Kenya's push to transform Jomo Kenyatta International Airport into a 10-million-passenger mega-hub is moving beyond pouring concrete and building departure gates.

On Friday in Nairobi, the State Department for Aviation and Aerospace Development signed a multi-million-shilling technical contract with Stockholm-based Independent Business Group (IBG), formally assigning the Swedish aviation consultancy to overhaul the airport's air traffic management and flight navigation architecture.

The deal, witnessed by Transport Cabinet Secretary Davis Chirchir and Swedish Ambassador Håkan Åkesson, tackles a crucial operational reality: expanding terminal floor space without redesigning the congested airspace above Nairobi would only transfer passenger bottlenecks from airport lounges directly onto the tarmac and approach corridors.

### Re-engineering the Approach Corridors

Under the partnership, IBG will deliver technical expertise and specialized hardware aimed at modernizing Kenya’s air navigation infrastructure. The scope reaches across airport capacity planning, airspace redesign, instrument flight procedures, and the overhaul of communication, navigation, and surveillance systems.

![Transport Cabinet Secretary Davis Chirchir (left) and Ambassador of Sweden in Kenya, Håkan Åkesson, witnessing the signing of a contract between Swedish IBG and the State Departmen](https://i.ibb.co/n8BwKqMP/a80e6aa8c13c.webp)

Founded in 2007, the Swedish firm has carried out more than 90 airspace and airport engineering projects across Europe, Asia, the Middle East, and Africa. In Nairobi, its mandate centers on optimizing the delicate sequencing of arrivals and departures to squeeze higher aircraft throughput out of existing runway assets.

"I am delighted to witness the signing of a contract between Swedish IBG and the State Department for Aviation and Aerospace Development to support the work of extending JKIA," Ambassador Åkesson said following the bilateral signing.

The package also includes specialized personnel training for Kenyan air traffic controllers, intended to align local tower operations with international aviation standards as flight frequencies increase.

### The Civil Works Backdrop

The air traffic agreement forms the technical twin to the broader Ksh 154.2 billion JKIA modernization program awarded to China Road and Bridge Corporation (CRBC).

![An aerial view of the Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (JKIA) in Nairobi County.](https://i.ibb.co/PvmzrJDG/01d6a03cb892.webp)

Ground activity on that capital-intensive project started in June 2026, following the government's adoption of the comprehensive JKIA Master Plan earlier in February. While CRBC’s assignment focuses on heavy civil infrastructure—building a new terminal facility, cutting new taxiways, expanding aircraft parking aprons, and improving landside road access—the Swedish contract addresses the digital and regulatory systems required to direct aircraft in and out of those bays safely.

Without optimized arrival routes and enhanced surveillance radar, increased terminal gates risk sitting idle while incoming jets burn fuel in holding patterns over Machakos and Kajiado.

### Throughput Pressures on Regional Hub Status

JKIA has long operated near peak airfield capacity during prime morning and evening connection banks, leaving minimal margin for mechanical delays, weather disruptions, or flight diversions across East African routes.

The overarching master plan aims to absorb an additional 10 million passengers annually once the new terminal and airside expansions are fully commissioned. Meeting that target requires air traffic controllers to safely decrease aircraft spacing on approach paths while expediting runway clearance times on the ground.

With IBG now contracted to blueprint flight paths and install navigation surveillance, attention turns to how quickly the technical airspace adjustments can be certified and deployed before CRBC completes the first phases of the physical apron expansions.
