The National Land Commission has declared the land acquisition process for the Ksh82 billion Thwake Dam essentially concluded, confirming that 99.99 percent of Project Affected Persons have received statutory payouts for parcels acquired along the border of Makueni and Kitui counties.

In an operational update released on Saturday, October 24, the commission stated that the remaining 0.01 percent of unpaid claims involves unresolved succession rows and active court cases rather than administrative funding shortfalls.

"NLC remains committed to ensuring fair and transparent compensation of Project Affected Persons (PAPs) under the Thwake Dam Project," the commission said in its statement. "To date, the Commission has completed 99.9 per cent of PAP compensation, with the outstanding 0.1 per cent attributable to pending court cases and other disputes."

Removing the land acquisition bottleneck clears the final site encumbrances for the Ministry of Water and Sanitation, which reported the civil works at 94 percent completion as of October 2026. However, the operational benefits of the strategic reservoir will reach nearby residents through a sequenced schedule that defers direct water distribution until after initial impoundment.

### Impoundment Window and Household Connections

![thwake dam](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_style/public/images/media/thwake%20dam%20%282%29.jpg.webp?itok=35h0Dsab)

Under the ministry’s revised engineering schedule, hydrological impoundment—the actual gathering and storage of river waters behind the embankment—is slated to commence at the end of January 2027. State House has scheduled the formal commissioning of the primary civil structure for April 2027.

For households and farming clusters across Makueni and Kitui that were promised direct economic relief from the mega-dam, water access will not coincide with the filling of the basin. Civil engineers at the site confirmed that distribution infrastructure cannot be connected until the main dam wall and reservoir basin undergo full structural testing.

"We are targeting to store water by the end of January 2027 and commissioning by the end of April 2027," a site engineer stated during an October 16 technical review. "Then after that we will start doing the downstream works which includes water supply to neighbouring counties like Makueni and Kitui."

This sequence means that while the state resolves its legal obligations to displaced landholders, the broader population in the arid lower basin will continue waiting for raw water reticulation, treatment facilities, and pipeline networks to be built out in subsequent phases.

### Rescue Financing Resolves Cash Flow Halt

The April 2027 commissioning date represents an operational recalibration from an earlier target of December 2026, forced by liquidity pressures that stalled works on site earlier in the year. The works resumed following the disbursement of a Ksh10.6 billion supplementary financing package, drawn largely from external lenders.

![Ardhi House](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/ardhi-hse.jpg.webp?itok=WDj2n5MC)

The capital intervention includes a Ksh9.6 billion (€64.20 million) loan from the African Development Bank (AfDB) alongside Ksh630.8 million (€4.2 million) provided through the African Development Fund. President William Ruto confirmed the executive approval of the bailout funding, which allowed contractors to resume operations on the main embankment.

"We built Thwake Dam but it encountered a few challenges after the money ran out," President Ruto said regarding the financing intervention. "We have received Ksh10.6 billion which was approved last month to complete Thwake."

### Multi-Phase Delivery Trajectory to 2028

Initiated in 2018, Thwake was designed as a multi-purpose flagship facility to serve domestic consumption, regional irrigation, and clean power generation. While the clearance of land parcels and the 94 percent completion mark bring Phase One to its final structural stretch, the auxiliary sectors remain on a longer horizon.

According to project documentation from the African Development Bank, the comprehensive rollout—incorporating downstream irrigation infrastructure and a planned hydropower generation plant—carries an operational timeline extending through September 2028.

For the National Land Commission, resolving the remaining sliver of contested plots now depends on how swiftly court registries conclude ownership litigation, even as heavy plant machinery prepares the reservoir floor for the January 2027 inflow.
