A petition seeking the permanent liquidation of Directline Assurance Company has reached the High Court in Malindi, placing the underwriter of more than six out of every ten public service vehicles in Kenya under immediate threat of court-ordered dissolution.

The insolvency petition, brought by six creditors, targets an institution that holds a commanding 62 percent share of the country’s Public Service Vehicle (PSV) insurance market. A court notice released by the High Court's Deputy Registrar in Malindi confirmed the filing was formally lodged on August 27, 2026, under the provisions of the Insolvency Act and the repealed Companies Act.

The court set November 23, 2026, as the date for the main hearing, instructing creditors and contributories to appear either in person or through legal counsel to state their positions for or against the winding-up order.

### Sector Exposure and Regulatory History

![Matatus at a terminal in Nairobi in August 18, 2024](https://i.ibb.co/YBp9cdVD/1c88b6c76b9e.webp)

Directline’s position as the primary policy issuer for commercial passenger vehicles means any threat to its corporate existence carries widespread operational implications for matatu owners, long-distance bus operators, and commuters nationwide.

The filing marks the latest escalation in a series of legal, financial, and administrative battles that have dogged the insurer in recent years. In 2024, the Insurance Regulatory Authority (IRA) placed Directline under heightened surveillance following public operational disruptions.

That regulatory intervention came after media proprietor S.K. Macharia, acting through Royal Credit Limited, publicly declared that Directline had ceased trading, dissolved its board of directors, and dismissed its staff, with Royal Credit asserting control over the company's asset portfolio. 

The IRA swiftly rejected those assertions, clarifying that Directline remained a legally licensed operational entity and cautioning that any transfer or takeover of insurance assets without prior explicit regulatory consent was unlawful.

### Unresolved Pressure on Commercial Motorists

The push for permanent liquidation forces a legal reckoning for Directline's liabilities at a time when commercial transport operators rely heavily on valid statutory coverage to remain on the roads. Under Kenyan transport law, public service vehicles cannot operate legally without third-party passenger insurance, making continuity of underwriter coverage an essential operational requirement for the fleet.

Should the court grant the creditors' request for a winding-up order or appoint a liquidator, thousands of active policies across the public transport sector could face legal ambiguity or statutory cancellation, leaving vehicle owners scrambling to secure replacement coverage in a concentrated market.

The Malindi High Court will receive submissions from affected stakeholders, policyholders, and regulatory representatives when the matter comes up for hearing on November 23.
