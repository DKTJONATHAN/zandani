As public schools prepare for the October kickoff of the 2026 national assessment calendar, the Kenya Union of Post-Primary Education Teachers (KUPPET) has deployed its main operational lever—the invigilation, supervision, and marking of national examinations—to demand an immediate review of teacher allowances and a halt to parliamentary education legislation.

Speaking on Thursday, September 24, KUPPET Secretary-General Akello Misori called on the Salaries and Remuneration Commission (SRC) to upwardly revise compensation rates for educators deployed as examiners, supervisors, invigilators, and centre managers across all national evaluation levels.

"We want to remind the SRC to be on the frontline to safeguard the rights of the teachers or workers who undertake such projects," Misori said, arguing that current compensation scales fall short of professional standards. "Imagine a centre organiser, who is a professional, is being paid below what a construction worker is being paid."

The union warned that without an intervention by the remuneration agency, teachers will be directed to step down from duties across the entire national testing spectrum, starting with the Kenya Primary School Education Assessment (KPSEA) and extending to the Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education (KCSE).

### Operational Leverage Ahead of October Assessments

The threat comes at a critical juncture for the Ministry of Education and national examination bodies, with school administrators and logistics officials finalizing preparations for the 2026 testing period scheduled to begin in October. National examination administration relies almost entirely on public school teachers to serve as centre managers, invigilators, and field supervisors, followed by centralized marking exercises.

By conditioning teacher participation on allowance adjustments, KUPPET is targeting long-standing disputes over daily stipends and operational risk pay that educators receive during the multi-week assessment cycle.

### Resistance to Legislative Reforms

![Secondary School students sitting for their Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education (KCSE) examinations.](https://i.ibb.co/tw46NNkz/81bbcd1d5a82.webp)

Alongside the allowance dispute, KUPPET is mounting resistance against six education reform Bills currently progressing through Parliament, including the Basic Education Bill 2026. The union claims the proposed legal overhaul bypasses required policy frameworks.

"The current Education Bill being canvassed is being done outside the established law," Misori stated. "It is outside the sector plan, outside a known sessional paper. You cannot legislate before developing policy."

The union’s legislative pushback coincides with ongoing public consultations conducted by the National Assembly Departmental Committee on Education. The committee has extended its public participation hearings through October 2 to gather feedback across additional counties, despite KUPPET’s calls for the process to be suspended.

### What Comes Next

The dual standoff creates an immediate administrative challenge for the government as the October exam schedule approaches. The SRC has not yet issued a formal determination on the requested allowance adjustments, while the parliamentary education committee continues its county consultations through October 2 before drafting its final report on the Basic Education Bill 2026.
