Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP) leader Rigathi Gachagua has conditioned his backing in the united opposition on principals signing an open, public pledge to support the eventual nominee, aiming to strip aspirants of backroom leverage that threatens defection to President William Ruto.

The former Deputy President said during a broadcast interview with KTN on Wednesday, October 28, that the coalition cannot navigate the August 10, 2027, General Election while contenders permit their bases to weaponise split-ticket threats.

“I have told my colleagues that if you want my support, you must publicly commit that in case it is not you, you are willing to support others,” Gachagua said.

### Disarming the Defection Threat

The move directly challenges an established negotiation tactic in Kenyan coalition-building: contenders allowing their supporters to threaten a protest vote for the incumbent if the alliance passes over their candidate. Gachagua placed the responsibility for those ultimatums squarely on the silent assent of the principals themselves.

![rigathi-gachagua](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/rigathi-gachagua_0.jpg.webp?itok=YBehtlEu)

“This idea of your supporters saying, ‘If it is not so-and-so, then we will vote for Ruto,’ is unacceptable to me, and it only happens when you're quiet,” he said.

By demanding these assurances on the record, Gachagua seeks to neutralise quiet veto threats before formal nomination parameters are locked down. The stance benefits whichever candidate ultimately secures the ticket by guaranteeing a unified platform and shielding the bloc from post-nomination attrition.

To give the demand procedural weight, Gachagua presented himself as ready to abide by the same constraint, even while staking out his own claim to the coalition ticket.

“I think I am the most suitable candidate to be identified as the flag bearer for the United Opposition in terms of sheer energy, mobilisation of resources, and so on,” Gachagua stated. “But I have said many times: if the formula my colleagues agree on does not favour me, my supporters and I will support anyone unconditionally.”

### Pushback on Jubilee's 'Mobiliser' Distinction

The DCP leader also took aim at arguments circulating within the opposition umbrella—specifically from Jubilee figures—seeking to separate crowd mobilization from actual presidential viability.

![Wiper party leader Kalonzo Musyoka addressing the press alongside other united opposition leaders on Wednesday, June 10, 2026.](https://kenyans.co.ke/files/styles/article_inner/public/images/media/kalonzo-gachagua-matiang-i.jpg.webp?itok=cXI58bBT)

Pushing back against suggestions that certain principals should function as vote mobilisers rather than top-of-the-ticket contenders, Gachagua dismissed the distinction as an artificial shield for weak candidates.

“I heard someone in Jubilee saying some principals are not electable but can mobilise votes,” he said. “I want to tell that fellow that a president cannot be nominated; he needs to be voted in.”

### The Search for a Selection Formula

The demand for binding public loyalty arrives amid rapid bilateral consultations between top opposition figures. On Monday, October 26, Gachagua hosted Wiper Patriotic Front leader Kalonzo Musyoka for closed-door talks centred on harmonising their regional bases into a single presidential vehicle.

While both politicians publicly reaffirmed their commitment to presenting a joint candidate against Ruto, the coalition has yet to publish an agreed selection rubric, arbitration framework, or power-sharing formula.

With no flagbearer formalised as consultations intensify, Gachagua’s public condition effectively tests how much negotiating autonomy opposition principals are willing to surrender before the coalition's selection rules are officially written.
