Long-distance transport crews moving goods through eastern Congo are confronting a deepening operational crisis, marked by recurring rebel abductions, vehicle vandalism, and a stark vacuum of official state protection.

The vulnerability of regional supply lines was underscored when the Long-Distance Drivers and Conductors Association of Kenya (LoDCA) issued an urgent security alert. The warning followed reports that six truck drivers, including Kenyan nationals, had been seized by insurgent groups along the Mahagi-to-Kisangani transport corridor.

![Images of the aftermarth of attack on Kenyan drivers in DRC.](https://i.ibb.co/1YMWMVZY/57e5d0469211.webp)

While the association confirmed that all six individuals were subsequently recovered safe and sound, the circumstances surrounding their captivity remain opaque. The affected route snakes through volatile areas such as Pimbo, Djugu, Igabriel, and Bunia—territories long dominated by armed factions where security conditions remain precarious.

Visual evidence from the corridor highlights the severe hazards confronting drivers. Photographs obtained from the region depict heavy commercial vehicles left in ruins: windows shattered, tires deflated, and number plates stripped, with scattered remnants of cargo littering the ground and no security personnel in sight.

This incident is part of a worsening pattern of violence targeting East African transport logistics in the DRC. In January, unrest along the Kasumbalesa, Lubumbashi, and Kolwezi corridor resulted in torched cargo trailers and blocked trade routes. In April, Kenyan driver Edwin Njuguna was killed in Bunia, pushing LoDCA to threaten an operational shutdown. That threat was followed in August by the killing of four Kenyan drivers and their co-drivers along the Beni-to-Kisangani route, an attack that forced truckers to park their trailers indefinitely in Mambasa, Ituri Province.

![Images of trucks belonging to Kenyan truck drivers in dilapidated condition, with an inset of the M23 rebels.](https://i.ibb.co/TDq2Mk7T/1cb93cdfe3d7.webp)

In response to the latest flare-up, LoDCA has instructed drivers currently stationed in Mahagi to halt their journeys until verified security clearances are issued. Those already operating within Congolese territory have been advised to maintain constant communication with relatives, fleet colleagues, and association leadership, while seeking secure shelter away from active conflict zones.

Despite the escalating frequency of attacks and cargo destruction along critical regional trade arteries, Kenyan security agencies have yet to issue a formal statement or policy framework addressing the safety of citizens operating in the conflict-ridden zones.
