DJ Creme De La Creme has revealed that his infamous 2015 video leak was not a sudden crisis, but the breaking point of a four-year silent extortion campaign that shadowed his career long before the clip ever surfaced online.

Speaking in an interview with radio broadcaster Alex Mwakideu, the disc jockey and music producer revisited November 5, 2015—the morning the footage became public—explaining that the true psychological toll had been quietly mounting since 2011.

### The Four-Year Shadow

For nearly half a decade, Creme lived with the daily reality that an intimate recording was in hostile hands. Rather than a contemporaneous recording that found its way online immediately, the video had been weaponised as a persistent threat behind closed doors.

"My guy, me I carried the biggest weight of my life. Four years knowing that there’s someone who has something that they can use against you," Creme said during the conversation, shared on Instagram on Saturday, November 21, 2026.

When Mwakideu pressed him on the chronological gap between the recording and its internet emergence, the entertainer confirmed the timeline: "Hiyo tape, bro, for it to leak in 2015, bro, it had been there for four years."

The uncertainty of when, or if, the material would be released created a protracted period of tension. While the public only witnessed the aftermath on November 5, 2015, Creme had been navigating extortion demands long before the story broke.

### Extortion Demands and Public Accounting

When the footage finally circulated in early November 2015, it sent shockwaves through Kenya’s entertainment sector. Contemporaneous reports in *The Standard* documented that individuals had contacted the entertainer demanding cash payments in exchange for suppressing the video.

Creme later reiterated those blackmail attempts during a television appearance on K24’s *Talk Central*, detailing how the possessor of the file tried to leverage it for financial gain before ultimately leaking it.

Rather than mounting a public relations denial once the clip went viral, Creme issued a formal statement in 2015 accepting ownership of the incident. "I admit my wrong and take full responsibility for it," he stated at the time, opting for direct accountability over legal evasion.

### Surviving the Fallout at Home

The resilience forged during the 2015 fallout would later surface when his domestic life faced intense scrutiny in 2021. That year, Creme publicly confirmed that he and his wife had separated, briefly sparking widespread speculation about the stability of long-term celebrity partnerships.

During that period, he forcefully dismissed online narratives that infidelity or fluctuating finances had broken the home.

"My wife and I don’t fight about women, it was never about that," Creme noted, pointing directly to the fact that their foundation had already survived the intense scrutiny of the 2015 controversy. "If we ever fought about women, I would have been affected after the last scandal I had."

He equally rejected claims that money troubles had triggered the split, pointing out that his partner had been by his side long before commercial success arrived. "If my wife was to leave me because I’m broke she would have not even settled with me since I knew her when I had nothing," he said.

### The Reconciliation Turnaround

The separation proved temporary. Months after stepping away to co-parent, the couple resolved their differences and reunited away from the glare of social media commentary.

Reflecting on the reconciliation in a later interview with *Tuko*, Creme described the period as an exercise in mutual compromise rather than unrealistic standards of perfection.

"I learned the value of forgiveness as we both had to admit our mistakes. We had lots of unresolved issues, and they kept piling up," he said. "You can never be perfect for each other, but you can compromise."

By laying bare the four-year lead-up to the 2015 leak, Creme has contextualised one of the country's most sensationalised celebrity crises, framing the eventual online release not as the start of the nightmare, but as the public conclusion to years of quiet blackmail.
