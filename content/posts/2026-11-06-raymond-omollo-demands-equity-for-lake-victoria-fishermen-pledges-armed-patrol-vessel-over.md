Dr. Raymond Omollo took his regional tour to Migori County with a sharp economic argument for Nyanza's fishing hubs: Lake Victoria’s blue economy must be treated with the same weight, state funding, and structural protections long granted to Kenya’s traditional cash-crop strongholds.

Addressing Beach Management Units (BMUs) and local traders, the Internal Security and National Administration Principal Secretary framed the government's lake sector investment as an overdue rebalancing. He argued that fishermen earning their living from the water deserve the exact institutional focus given to coffee, tea, sugarcane, and dairy farmers in other regions.

The centerpiece of Omollo’s pitch rested on an explicit deadline for security reinforcement across the lake's volatile border zones. Acknowledging years of conflict, harassment, and security friction around Migingo Island and waters shared with Uganda and Tanzania, Omollo announced that enhanced security forces and dedicated patrol craft will be deployed before the end of 2026.

According to Omollo, consultations with the leadership of the Border Patrol Unit and the Kenya Coast Guard Service have locked in a strategy to phase out dependence on traditional wooden vessels. The security machinery will transition to modern, specialized patrol boats equipped with higher safety standards to protect both law enforcement officers and local fishermen operating near international boundaries.

"The commitment I’m giving before the end of this year, you will see increased number of police and security officers," Omollo told the assembly. "You will also see increased numbers of patrol boats so that we secure you as you undertake your fishing activities."

The security upgrade anchors a wider logistical network currently being constructed across four lake counties. In Migori, work is underway on the Muhuru Bay Pier, while in Siaya County, construction has moved to Usenge following the completion of the main Homa Bay pier infrastructure. Additional piers are slated for Mbita, Kendu Bay, and Sena on Mfangano Island, alongside a state-funded public ferry in Kisumu designed to streamline passenger transit and inter-county trade across the water.

To prevent financial leakage at the shore, the state is building nine modern fish landing sites outfitted with cold storage units, hygienic processing bays, and dedicated trading spaces. Omollo noted that post-harvest spoilage has historically left fishermen vulnerable to predatory pricing by middlemen. The cold-chain facilities are designed to allow catches to be preserved until fair market prices are reached, linked into a national network of more than 600 new municipal markets equipped with electricity and water connections.

Financial discipline at the community level formed another key pillar of the PS's address. Omollo pushed Beach Management Units to overhaul the governance of their savings and credit cooperative societies (SACCOs). He directed BMU leadership to seek professional training in financial accountability to safeguard member deposits and expand access to capital. For families not directly working on boats, Omollo advocated for economic diversification into integrated poultry farming to build stable household safety nets.

The broader logistics package is set for a high-profile rollout in the coming weeks, when President William Ruto is scheduled to tour the region to officially kick off construction on the 500-kilometre Lake Victoria Ring Road. The highway project, designed to span the shoreline from Migori to Busia, is intended to link primary landing sites directly to major commercial transit corridors across Western Kenya.

Omollo linked the economic push to social security, noting that over 33 million Kenyans have registered under the Social Health Authority (SHA) Taifa Care scheme—a safety net intended to prevent fishing households from liquidating assets or boats to cover emergency medical bills.
