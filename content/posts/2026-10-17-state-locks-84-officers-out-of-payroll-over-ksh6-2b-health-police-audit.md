Public Service Cabinet Secretary Geoffrey Ruku attributed an abrupt administrative lockout of 84 human resource officers across essential security and healthcare services on Friday, October 16, to an escalating criminal probe into Ksh6.2 billion in suspicious state wage distributions.

The intervention immediately insulates the exchequer from further unauthorized cash leakage, clearing a path for the National Treasury as it seeks to satisfy strict civil service reform conditions tied to pending World Bank development funding.

Under the directive, the affected personnel within the Ministry of Health, the Kenya Police Service, and the Kenya Prisons Service surrendered all credentials to the government payroll platform and proceeded on 30-day compulsory leave to facilitate unhindered forensic inspections by the Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI).

“Following ongoing investigations by the DCI and a subsequent preliminary report that have unearthed irregular transactions in the public service payroll, especially in the Ministry of Health, Kenya Police Service and Kenya Prison Service, I have directed the revocation of payroll system user rights of 84 human resources officers and the implementation of mandatory 30-day leave to allow further forensic investigations of the identified irregular transactions that have potentially led to loss of public funds,” Ruku stated.

### Ksh6.2 Billion Forensic Exposure

The targeted departments represent some of the public sector’s largest wage rolls, where manual entries and operational scale have historically created loopholes for off-ledger disbursements.

The clampdown stems from a wider executive audit presented to the Cabinet that scrutinized 53 state departments. That review flagged financial irregularities totaling Ksh6.2 billion across just 12 ministries and agencies, uncovering unverified adjustments to employee ledgers, payments outside established wage brackets, compromised statutory deductions, and weak internal supervisory controls.

To prevent disruptions in end-month salary disbursements for uniformed officers and medical personnel, the ministry instructed replacement human resource officers to take over the administration of the affected payroll units with immediate effect.

Ruku noted that personnel found culpable once the DCI concludes its inquiry will face disciplinary and legal proceedings, pointing to an executive mandate to cross-check all state personal numbers against verified civil service headcounts.

### Centralized Controls and Donor Benchmarks

Beyond disciplinary sanctions, the ministry instructed all ministries, departments, state agencies, and state corporations to transition entirely to the unified Human Resource Information System (HRIS).

The migration follows an August warning from Public Service Commission Chairperson Francis Meja, who cautioned that institutional accounting officers would face individual liability for executing salary disbursements outside approved state processing software.

The timing of the payroll purge coincides with ongoing talks between the National Treasury and the World Bank. Securing concessional financing under these negotiations depends heavily on demonstrable administrative progress in eliminating suspected ghost workers, terminating unauthorized off-platform compensation, and harmonizing disparate payroll registries between national ministries and county governments.

Investigators are currently verifying personnel identification records across the three affected security and health formations, with the DCI expected to deliver its final forensic accounting report before the 30-day suspension period lapses in mid-November.
