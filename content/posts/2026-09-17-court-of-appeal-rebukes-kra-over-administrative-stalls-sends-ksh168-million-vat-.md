NAIROBI - The Court of Appeal has delivered a sharp check on the Kenya Revenue Authority’s administrative practices, ruling that the revenue collector cannot unilaterally suspend statutory deadlines or freeze decisions simply because collateral proceedings remain pending before tribunals or courts.

Overturning a lower court decision that had dismissed a taxpayer’s challenge over delayed Value-Added Tax (VAT) refunds totaling more than Ksh168 million, the appellate bench held that administrative expediency cannot override clear timelines established by Parliament.

The ruling targets a persistent operational friction point between taxpayers and the state collector: KRA’s practice of withholding determinations on tax objections and refund claims on grounds that related legal battles are still playing out elsewhere. Under the Tax Procedures Act, where a taxpayer challenges an objection and the Commissioner fails to render a decision within 60 days, that objection is deemed allowed by operation of law.

“It is trite that a public authority, being a creature of the Constitution and/or statute, may exercise only those powers lawfully conferred upon it,” the appellate court stated. “Accordingly, where Parliament has prescribed mandatory timelines for the performance of statutory duties, those timelines cannot be varied, suspended or rendered nugatory by administrative expediency or by the Commissioner’s unilateral decision to await the outcome of collateral proceedings.”

The dispute at the center of the decision exposes severe administrative bottlenecks within the tax administration system. The Ksh168 million claim involved VAT refunds that had languished unresolved for up to seven years, spanning claims that had undergone full audit processes alongside those left completely un-audited. KRA defended its inaction by arguing that parallel disputes regarding the tax treatment of zero-rated exported services were active before the Tax Appeals Tribunal and the courts.

However, the appellate judges drew a firm line between substantive tax disputes and administrative compliance. The bench noted that when KRA sits on a matter past statutory windows without making a formal decision, it creates an administrative vacuum. Because the Tax Appeals Tribunal requires an actual administrative decision to entertain an appeal, KRA’s silence effectively leaves aggrieved taxpayers without access to the statutory appeals machinery.

In such instances, the court established, taxpayers are fully within their rights to approach the High Court for judicial review to challenge the revenue agency's failure to act lawfully and within set statutory timelines.

Crucially, while the Court of Appeal faulted KRA's administrative stall tactics, it maintained strict limits on judicial intervention. The judges declined to act as a primary tax adjudicator or directly order the payout of the disputed Ksh168 million refund, emphasizing that judicial review assesses the lawfulness of administrative action rather than replacing KRA's authority to determine substantive tax entitlements.

Instead, the appellate court remitted the outstanding complaints regarding statutory non-compliance and fair administrative action back to the High Court for determination on a priority basis.

The decision leaves KRA facing tighter operational discipline over its audit and objection queues, stripping the tax authority of its long-standing reliance on legal delays elsewhere to justify inaction at home.
