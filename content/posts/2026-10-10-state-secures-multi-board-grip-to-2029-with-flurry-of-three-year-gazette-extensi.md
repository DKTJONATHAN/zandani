A synchronized wave of gazette notices has locked down leadership tenures across Kenya’s critical public funds, utility watchdogs, and natural resource agencies through late 2029, anchoring administrative continuity across both the presidency and key ministerial portfolios.

The sweeping decisions, formalised in a Kenya Gazette notice dated Friday, October 9, 2026, distribute three-year mandates across six ministries, insulating the governance structures of bodies that manage public billions, police consumer utility tariffs, and direct regional investments.

### Pension Oversight and Devolved Funds

At the National Treasury, Cabinet Secretary John Mbadi moved to steady the stewardship of two major financial conduits: devolved constituency allocations and municipal worker retirement savings.

Mbadi reappointed former lawmaker Olago Aluoch as non-executive chairperson of the National Government Constituencies Development Fund (NG-CDF) Board, preserving established stewardship over the billions channeled annually to grassroots school and security infrastructure. Alongside Aluoch, Masitsa Shiyonga secured another three-year term as an NG-CDF board member.

![President William Ruto chairing a cabinet meeting on Friday, October 9, 2026.](https://i.ibb.co/Psg0gjHH/225571f8a70f.webp)

Concurrently, Mbadi altered the trustee composition of the Local Authorities Provident Fund (LAPFUND), appointing Kevin Onyango and Kenya Medical Practitioners, Pharmacists and Dentists Union (KMPDU) Deputy Secretary General Dennis Miskellah to three-year terms on the board. 

Miskellah’s entry into LAPFUND injects an active union negotiator directly into the oversight organ of a retirement scheme wrestling with historical county contribution remittance arrears and major asset portfolio management.

### Regulatory Anchors in Water, Energy, and Forestry

At State House, President William Ruto prioritized institutional stability within the country's environmental and water sectors by handing out immediate three-year extensions to sitting board leaders.

Job Chirchir was reappointed non-executive chairperson of the Water Services Regulatory Board (WASREB), effective October 14, keeping leadership steady at the primary watchdog tasked with vetting consumer water tariffs and licensing regional water service providers. 

![President William Ruto chairs a cabinet meeting on Tuesday February 10, 2026](https://i.ibb.co/7NVDGf7H/408f4b7aad7d.webp)

In the conservation docket, Ruto reappointed Titus Korir as non-executive chairperson of the Kenya Forest Service (KFS) for another three years. Korir, who first assumed the reins on October 13, 2023, maintains continuity over forest protection protocols and national tree-growing targets. The President also renewed the mandates of Francis Moroto, Habon Farah, Joseph Boit, and Rusiana Mitau on the NEPAD Kenya Governing Council.

Energy and Petroleum Cabinet Secretary Opiyo Wandayi matched the State House moves by extending Jane Masai’s tenure on the board of directors of the Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority (EPRA) for three years, also taking effect on October 14, as the regulator navigates volatile fuel and electricity tariff reviews.

### Professional Councils and Scientific Research

The ministerial notices also reached technical research, media training, and land-use governance:

* **Public Health Research:** Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale appointed Luka Chemwolo to the Kenya Medical Research Institute (KEMRI) Council for three years, refreshing oversight at the frontline infectious-disease research center.
* **Physical Planning Standards:** Lands Cabinet Secretary Alice Wahome appointed Geoffrey Moses as non-executive chairperson of the Physical Planners Registration Board for three years starting October 9, seating a new regulatory head over spatial planning certification.
* **Livestock Genetics:** Agriculture Cabinet Secretary Mutahi Kagwe reappointed John Karuru Mwangi to the board of the Kenya Animal Genetics Resource Centre for three years.
* **Media Training:** ICT Cabinet Secretary William Gitau renewed John Kieru Wambui’s tenure on the Council of the Kenya Institute of Mass Communication (KIMC).

The gazette directives collectively establish an unbroken three-year statutory horizon, leaving the reconstituted boards insulated until the final quarter of 2029 as their respective ministries push forward on delayed capital projects and tightened regulatory mandates.
