Kenyan youth groups looking to tap into a new Ksh434 million ($3.35 million) World Bank-backed health fund will not be able to submit standalone bids, facing a mandatory requirement to form multi-organisation consortiums before early next month.

The financing window, rolled out under the Global Financing Facility for Women, Children and Adolescents (GFF) alongside the Civil Society and Social Innovation Alliance (CIVIC), ties eligibility to joint applications involving two or three distinct entities, with at least one led by young people.

The structure is designed to pair community-level youth outfits with established civil society organisations to mount sustained public budget scrutiny and policy lobbying around reproductive, maternal, and adolescent nutrition.

### Coalition Architecture and Management

![Kenyans lining up at Wabera Street in Nairobi for a job interview at the Sarova Stanley in Nairobi.](https://i.ibb.co/pvCkbnpC/ac4be2014b93.webp)

Day-to-day oversight of the grant basket will not be handled directly from World Bank country offices. Instead, administrative management has been placed with the Voices Organising for Inclusive Civic Engagement and Sustainability (VOICES) consortium, working in tandem with the African Institute for Development Policy (AFIDEP) and the Organisation of African Youth.

Successful applicants will enter an 18-month programme that bundles direct grant funding with technical mentorship in national budget analysis, targeted advocacy training, and institutional capacity building.

By requiring mixed alliances, the facility aims to avoid fragmented community projects that struggle to influence national spending decisions, while preventing traditional non-governmental organisations from sidelining youth networks in governance discussions.

### Focus on Health Financing Gaps

The intervention comes amid continued fiscal pressure on primary healthcare programmes across sub-Saharan Africa. The grant’s core mandate focuses on equipping civic actors to track health spending allocations, press for domestic resource mobilization, and hold authorities accountable for frontline maternal and adolescent services.

![Kenyans seeking government services queue outside the Nakuru County government office on Tuesday, January 2021.](https://i.ibb.co/G4MyThJL/87ed51b30e70.webp)

Eligible entities include registered local civil society organisations, community-based organisations, and youth-led groups legally operating in participating jurisdictions.

Kenya is among 22 African countries cleared for the envelope. Regional participants include Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda, Ethiopia, Nigeria, Ghana, Zambia, Cameroon, Chad, Côte d’Ivoire, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Guinea, Liberia, Malawi, Mali, Mozambique, Senegal, Zimbabwe, Burkina Faso, and Sierra Leone.

### Tight Turnaround for Bidders

The primary logistical hurdle for prospective Kenyan bidders is the compressed preparatory timeline. Consortia must complete partner alignment, draft shared work plans, and coordinate submission documentation in under three weeks.

The World Bank confirmed that the portal for the GFF × CIVIC VOICES grants will close strictly on Wednesday, October 7, 2026, at 11:59 pm East Africa Time.
